## 1 John 1:7 to Leviticus 17.11

**CRITICAL**: John's assertion that "the blood of Jesus... cleanses us from all sin" synthesizes Levitical atonement theology (Leviticus 17:11) with purification rituals including Numbers 19.

### Texts

**Leviticus 17:11**
> "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it to you upon the altar to make atonement for your souls; for it is the blood that makes atonement for the soul."

**1 John 1:7**
> "But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin."

### Verbal Connections

**Blood Theology**:
- **Hebrew**: הַדָּם (haddam, "the blood") in Leviticus 17:11
- **Greek**: τὸ αἷμα (to haima, "the blood") in 1 John 1:7
- Both texts emphasize *the blood* as the divinely appointed means of dealing with sin

**Atonement/Cleansing**:
- **Leviticus 17:11**: יְכַפֵּר (yəḵappēr, "makes atonement"), from כָּפַר (kāpar, to cover, atone)
- **1 John 1:7**: καθαρίζει (katharizei, "cleanses"), from καθαρίζω (katharizō, to cleanse, purify)
- The shift from "atonement" (covering) to "cleansing" (purifying) reflects the move from type to fulfillment

**Comprehensive Scope**:
- **Leviticus 17:11**: "For your souls" (נַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם, napšōṯêḵem)—the life/person
- **1 John 1:7**: "From all sin" (ἀπὸ πάσης ἁμαρτίας, apo pasēs hamartias)—comprehensive cleansing

### How 1 John Uses Leviticus 17:11

**Foundational Blood Theology**:
Leviticus 17:11 establishes the theological foundation that blood makes atonement:
1. **Life principle**: "The life of the flesh is in the blood"
2. **Divine gift**: "I have given it to you"
3. **Atoning function**: "To make atonement for your souls"
4. **Efficacy explanation**: "It is the blood that makes atonement"

John applies this foundation to Christ:
- The life is in the blood → Christ's blood conveys His sinless life
- God gave it for atonement → Christ was "sent" by God (1 John 4:10, 14)
- Blood makes atonement → Christ's blood cleanses from sin
- For your souls → For "us" (believers)

**Synthesis with Purification Rituals**:
While Leviticus 17:11 focuses on *atonement* (כָּפַר, kāpar/covering guilt), 1 John 1:7 emphasizes *cleansing* (καθαρίζω, katharizō/purifying from defilement). This synthesizes:

1. **Leviticus 17:11** - Blood for atonement (guilt-covering)
2. **Numbers 19** - Ashes/water for purification (defilement-cleansing)
3. **1 John 1:7** - Christ's blood accomplishes both

The red heifer connection is evident in John's cleansing terminology:
- Numbers 19:12, 19: "he will be clean" (וְטָהֵר, wəṭāhēr)
- LXX Numbers 19:12: καθαρός ἔσται (katharos estai, "will be clean")
- 1 John 1:7: καθαρίζει (katharizei, "cleanses")—same root

**Continual Cleansing**:
The present tense καθαρίζει (katharizei, "cleanses") is theologically significant:
- **Not aorist** (past completed action): Christ's blood *cleansed* us (once)
- **Not perfect** (completed with ongoing results): Christ's blood *has cleansed* us
- **But present** (ongoing action): Christ's blood *cleanses* us continually

This parallels the red heifer's *continual availability*:
- Numbers 19:9: Ashes "kept" for preparing water "for purification from sin"
- 1 John 1:7: Christ's blood "cleanses" (present tense) "from all sin"

The red heifer was applied repeatedly (third day, seventh day); Christ's blood cleanses continually.

### Context in 1 John

**Immediate Context** (1 John 1:5-2:2):
John addresses fellowship with God and cleansing from sin:
- 1:5 - God is light; no darkness in Him
- 1:6 - False claim: walking in darkness while claiming fellowship
- 1:7 - True reality: walking in light → fellowship → cleansing by Christ's blood
- 1:8 - False claim: "we have no sin"
- 1:9 - True remedy: confession → forgiveness and cleansing
- 1:10 - False claim: "we have not sinned"
- 2:1 - True provision: Christ our advocate
- 2:2 - He is the propitiation for our sins

The blood's cleansing (1:7) and confession's cleansing (1:9) are complementary:
- 1:7 - Objective provision: Christ's blood cleanses
- 1:9 - Subjective appropriation: Confession accesses that cleansing

This mirrors the red heifer pattern:
- Objective provision: Ashes preserved for purification
- Subjective appropriation: Defiled person presents himself for sprinkling

**Broader Johannine Theology**:
John's Gospel and Epistles emphasize blood and water:
- **John 19:34** - "Blood and water" flow from Christ's pierced side
- **John 7:38-39** - "Living water" = the Spirit
- **1 John 5:6** - Christ came "by water and blood"
- **1 John 5:8** - "The Spirit and the water and the blood... agree as one"

This connects to Numbers 19's "living water" (מַיִם חַיִּים, mayim ḥayyîm) mixed with ashes. John sees in Christ the reality: His blood (sacrifice) and water/Spirit (application) provide comprehensive cleansing.

### Theological Significance

**Levitical Foundation**:
Leviticus 17:11 established blood's atoning necessity:
- **Life principle**: Blood = life (נֶפֶשׁ, nepeš)
- **Substitutionary**: Life given in place of sinner's life
- **Divine institution**: "I have given it to you"

This prohibits eating blood (Leviticus 17:10-12) because blood is sacred—set apart for atonement. The NT applies this:
- Acts 15:20, 29 - Gentile believers abstain from blood (ritual reason)
- 1 John 1:7 - Christ's blood cleanses (theological fulfillment)

**Cleansing from All Sin**:
The phrase "from all sin" (ἀπὸ πάσης ἁμαρτίας) is comprehensive:
- Not selective cleansing (some sins but not others)
- Not partial cleansing (weakened but not removed)
- But complete cleansing (all sin removed)

This surpasses OT provisions:
- Leviticus 17:11 - Atonement for sins
- Numbers 19 - Purification from corpse-defilement
- 1 John 1:7 - Cleansing from *all* sin

The red heifer cleansed only death-defilement; Christ's blood cleanses from all sin.

**Continual Application**:
The present tense "cleanses" indicates:
1. **Ongoing need**: Believers sin after conversion (1:8, 10)
2. **Ongoing provision**: Christ's blood remains efficacious
3. **Ongoing appropriation**: Continual walking in light and confession (1:7, 9)

This doesn't mean repeated atonement (Christ died "once for all," Hebrews 9:12, 26, 28) but continual *application* of that once-for-all atonement. The red heifer ashes illustrate this:
- The heifer was slain once; the ashes were applied repeatedly
- Christ died once; His blood cleanses continually

**Fellowship Requirement**:
The cleansing isn't automatic but conditional: "if we walk in the light" (1:7). This parallels the red heifer:
- Not automatic: The defiled person must present himself for sprinkling
- Not passive: Walking in light is active obedience
- Not merit-based: The cleansing power is in Christ's blood, not our walking

Walking in light is the *condition* for experiencing cleansing, not the *cause* of cleansing. The ashes' power was in the sacrifice, not the sprinkling; Christ's blood's power is in His sacrifice, not our walking.

### NT Intertextuality

**Related Texts on Blood's Cleansing Power**:

**Hebrews 9:13-14** - Explicitly connects Christ's blood to the red heifer:
> "If the ashes of a heifer... sanctify to the purifying of the flesh, how much more shall the blood of Christ... purge your conscience from dead works"

**Revelation 1:5** - "Washed us from our sins in his own blood" (λούσαντι ἡμᾶς... ἐν τῷ αἵματι αὐτοῦ)
- Aorist tense: Definitive cleansing (justification)
- Contrasts with 1 John 1:7's present tense (ongoing sanctification)

**Revelation 7:14** - "Washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb"
- The imagery of washing in blood echoes purification rituals
- White robes = complete, visible purity

**1 Peter 1:2** - "Sprinkling by the blood of Jesus Christ" (ῥαντισμὸν αἵματος Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ)
- Uses the exact sprinkling terminology from Numbers 19 (ῥαντίζω/ῥαντισμός)
- Applies it to believers' sanctification

**Red Heifer Typology in 1 John 1:7**:
Though 1 John doesn't explicitly cite Numbers 19, the connections are evident:

| Aspect | Numbers 19 | 1 John 1:7 |
|--------|------------|------------|
| **Provision** | Ashes preserved | Blood's efficacy perpetual |
| **Application** | Repeated sprinkling | Continual cleansing (present tense) |
| **Scope** | Corpse-defilement | All sin |
| **Result** | Ceremonial cleanness | Spiritual purity |
| **Access** | Through priestly administration | Through walking in light/confession |
| **Sphere** | External (flesh) | Internal (heart/conscience) |

### Practical Application

**For Original Readers**:
First-century believers familiar with Levitical sacrifices and purification rituals would understand:
- Blood's necessity (Leviticus 17:11)
- Cleansing's availability (Numbers 19's preserved ashes)
- Ongoing appropriation (repeated sprinkling)

John redirects them to Christ:
- His blood fulfills Leviticus 17:11's atonement
- His blood provides Numbers 19's purification
- His blood cleanses continually, not just ceremonially

**For Contemporary Believers**:
The present tense "cleanses" teaches:
1. **Justification is definitive**: Christ's blood *has* removed sin's guilt (Hebrews 10:10, 14)
2. **Sanctification is progressive**: Christ's blood *continually* cleanses from sin's defilement (1 John 1:7)
3. **Glorification is certain**: Christ's blood *will* present us faultless (Jude 24)

The red heifer typology applies:
- **Ashes preserved** → Christ's merit inexhaustible
- **Repeated sprinkling** → Daily appropriation through confession/faith
- **Ceremonial cleansing** → Spiritual transformation

**Assurance and Humility**:
The verse balances confidence and dependence:
- **Confidence**: "The blood of Jesus... cleanses us from all sin" (not some sins, but all)
- **Dependence**: "If we walk in the light" (ongoing obedience required)

This prevents:
- **Presumption**: Assuming cleansing without walking in light
- **Despair**: Thinking some sin exceeds Christ's cleansing power

**Continual Appropriation**:
Like the red heifer's ashes applied repeatedly, believers continually access Christ's cleansing:
- Through confession (1:9): Acknowledging specific sins
- Through faith (1:7): Trusting Christ's blood's efficacy
- Through obedience (1:7): Walking in the light

What the ashes typified *provisionally* (ongoing cleansing availability), Christ's blood fulfills *perfectly* (inexhaustible, comprehensive, effective cleansing). What required ceremonial administration now operates through spiritual appropriation. What cleansed externally now transforms internally. The type pointed to the need; Christ provides the reality.
