# 2 Timothy 4:17 to Psalm 22:21

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 4#2 Timothy 4 . 17|2 Timothy 4:17]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 21|Psalm 22:21]] (primary source: "Rescue me from the mouth of the lion")
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 6#Daniel 6 . 22|Daniel 6:22]] (Daniel's deliverance from lions' den)
- Background: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 17#1 Samuel 17 . 37|1 Samuel 17:37]] (David rescued from lion and bear); [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 7#Psalm 7 . 2|Psalm 7:2]]

**Type**: Metaphorical application with typological resonance

**Significance**: Paul's phrase "I was rescued from the lion's mouth" employs vivid OT deliverance imagery with multiple layers of meaning. Most directly, it echoes Psalm 22:21, the messianic psalm of the suffering righteous one whom God vindicates, creating christological resonance as Paul shares in Christ's sufferings and deliverance. Additionally, it evokes Daniel 6:22 where God sent his angel to shut the lions' mouths, delivering his faithful servant from unjust execution—establishing a typological pattern of God rescuing his servants from seemingly certain death when they remain faithful. Whether Paul faced literal threat of execution by wild beasts (damnatio ad bestias) or uses "lion's mouth" metaphorically for mortal danger in his legal proceedings, the OT imagery transforms his personal experience into a salvation history pattern. The hermeneutical move connects Paul's deliverance to the broader narrative of God's faithfulness to his servants throughout redemptive history, from David facing lions and bears (1 Sam. 17:37), through Daniel's miraculous rescue, to the suffering and vindication of the Messiah (Psalm 22), now extending to the apostle's own near-death experience. This provides pastoral encouragement for Timothy: the same God who rescued his servants throughout history will preserve his people through persecution and trial, even when facing the lion's mouth of Roman imperial power.
