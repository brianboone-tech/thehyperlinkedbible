# 2 Peter 3:12-13 to Isaiah 65:17

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 12|2 Peter 3:12-13]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 17|Isaiah 65:17]] (new heavens and new earth)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 22|Isaiah 66:22]] (new creation endures)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 1|Isaiah 60]] (glory of restored Zion)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 22|Isaiah 60:22]] (God will hasten it in its time)

**Type**: Echo/thematic parallel

**Significance**: Peter's appropriation of Isaiah's new creation language represents a crucial hermeneutical move from prophetic restoration imagery to cosmic eschatological fulfillment. Where Isaiah envisions a renewed Jerusalem and restored Israel, Peter universalizes this to cosmic dimensions—literally new heavens and earth—demonstrating how NT authors expand OT promises to their ultimate Christological and eschatological scope. The phrase "where righteousness dwells" (katoikei dikaiosyne) moves beyond mere forgiveness to a realm fundamentally constituted by justice, answering the problem of wickedness that necessitated both the flood (2 Peter 2:5) and the coming fire (3:7). This provides pastoral hope amid suffering: the present age's moral chaos is temporary, and God is preparing an eternal realm where righteousness permanently resides. Peter's eschatology thus grounds Christian ethics in cosmic hope, showing that godly living anticipates and participates in the righteousness that will characterize the new creation.
