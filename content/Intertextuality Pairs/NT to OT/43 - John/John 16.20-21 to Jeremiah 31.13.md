# John 16:20-21 to Jeremiah 31:13

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 16#John 16 . 20|John 16:20-21]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 13|Jeremiah 31:13]] ("turn their mourning into gladness")
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 61#Isaiah 61 . 1|Isaiah 61:1-3]] (comfort and joy instead of sorrow)
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 22#Jeremiah 22 . 10|Jeremiah 22:10]] (mourning terminology)
- [[Readable Bible/17 - Esther/Esther 9#Esther 9 . 22|Esther 9:22]] (sorrow turned to joy at Purim)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 13#Isaiah 13 . 8|Isaiah 13:8]] (woman in labor—extremely common in prophetic OT)
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 4#Jeremiah 4 . 31|Jeremiah 4:31]] (labor imagery)
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 12#Daniel 12 . 1|Daniel 12:1]] (day of Lord as "time of distress")
- 1QH XI, 8-12; Revelation 12:2-5 ("birth pains of the Messiah")
- Matthew 24:8, 21, 29 (Jesus' teaching: "beginning of birth pains," "great distress")

**Type**: Prophetic/Eschatological imagery

**Significance**: OT: God can "turn mourning into gladness," give "comfort and joy instead of sorrow" (Jeremiah 31:13; Isaiah 61:1-3). Purim celebrates "sorrow turned into joy" (Esther 9:22). Woman in labor image EXTREMELY common in prophetic OT, frequently applies to coming of end-time salvation through Messiah. Day of Lord regularly portrayed as "time of distress" (Daniel 12:1; Zephaniah 1:14-15). Second Temple Judaism coined phrase "birth pains of the Messiah" for tribulation period preceding final consummation (1QH XI, 8-12; cf. Revelation 12:2-5; Brownlee).
