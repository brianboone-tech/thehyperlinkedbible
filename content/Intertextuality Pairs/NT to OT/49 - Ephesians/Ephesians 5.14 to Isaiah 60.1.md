# Ephesians 5:14 to Isaiah 60:1

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 14|Ephesians 5:14]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 1|Isaiah 60:1]] (primary background - "Arise, shine, for your light has come")
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 26#Isaiah 26 . 19|Isaiah 26:19]] (possible background - resurrection language)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 51#Isaiah 51 . 17|Isaiah 51:17]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 1|Isaiah 52:1]] (possible background - wake up/arise language)

**Type**: Composite quotation / Early Christian hymnic tradition based on Isaiah

**Significance**: Paul cites an early Christian hymn drawing primarily on Isaiah 60:1 (eschatological light and restoration), introducing it with "therefore it says" to signal scriptural authority. The composite quotation combines awakening/arising with resurrection and light-shining, reflecting early Christian interpretation of Christ's resurrection as fulfilling Isaiah's prophecies. The hermeneutical move treats Spirit-inspired Christian tradition as authoritative scripture when it accurately interprets OT prophecy in light of Christ. Paul's three imperatives (wake up, rise, receive Christ's shining) correspond to conversion/baptism imagery used throughout early Christianity. The theological significance connects light/darkness dualism (recalling Genesis 1:3, anticipating Revelation 21:23) with resurrection reality (2:5-6), grounding ethics (walk as light children) in eschatological indicatives (already raised with Christ).
