**Type**: Composite typological warning (three OT villains as archetypes)

**Significance**: Jude's threefold typology demonstrates sophisticated use of OT narratives as paradigmatic categories of sin, each villain representing a distinct yet related form of apostasy. "The way of Cain" evokes not just murder but loveless religion—worship without love for God or neighbor (1 John 3:12 identifies Cain as belonging to the evil one). "Balaam's error" (plane) refers to his mercenary false prophecy, teaching for profit rather than truth (2 Peter 2:15 similarly uses Balaam). "Korah's rebellion" (antilogia) represents usurpation of God-ordained authority, with Korah's fate (swallowed by earth, consumed by fire) typifying divine judgment on unauthorized leadership. Together, these three OT figures create a composite portrait of the false teachers: they lack love, exploit believers for money, and reject legitimate spiritual authority. The hermeneutical move transforms historical villains into timeless categories, allowing readers across cultures to identify apostasy through OT typological patterns. This also establishes eschatological certainty: as Cain, Balaam, and Korah met judgment, so will their spiritual descendants.
