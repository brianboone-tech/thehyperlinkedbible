# Jude 1:9 to Deuteronomy 34:5-6

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 9|Jude 9]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 5|Deuteronomy 34:5-6]] (Moses died, Lord buried him, no one knows burial place)
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 10#Daniel 10 . 13|Daniel 10:13]] (Michael, one of the chief princes)
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 10#Daniel 10 . 21|Daniel 10:21]] (Michael, your prince)
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 12#Daniel 12 . 1|Daniel 12:1]] (Michael, great prince who protects your people)
- [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 3#Zechariah 3 . 1|Zechariah 3:1-2]] (Satan accusing Joshua, "The LORD rebuke you")
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 2#Exodus 2 . 11|Exodus 2:11-12]] (Moses killed Egyptian)

**Type**: Allusion to OT + extrabiblical tradition (*Assumption of Moses*)

**Significance**: Jude's citation of the *Assumption of Moses* represents apostolic freedom to use Jewish traditions illustratively without canonizing them, demonstrating that truth can be drawn from non-inspired sources when serving theological purposes. The hermeneutical principle is selective appropriation: Jude extracts a narrative detail that illustrates proper angelic restraint in spiritual conflict, using it to rebuke false teachers who "blaspheme all that they do not understand" (v. 10). The theological move connects Michael's deference to divine authority ("The Lord rebuke you," echoing Zechariah 3:2) with proper Christian engagement with evil—we do not rely on our own authority but appeal to God's. The mysterious burial of Moses (Deuteronomy 34:6) and Michael's role as Israel's protector (Daniel 10-12) provide canonical grounding for the tradition, though the specific dispute over Moses's body comes from extrabiblical sources. This establishes apostolic precedent for using common cultural/religious knowledge to make theological points, provided the teaching itself remains biblically sound.
