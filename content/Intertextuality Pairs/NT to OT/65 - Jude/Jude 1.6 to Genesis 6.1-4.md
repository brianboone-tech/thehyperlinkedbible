**Type**: Allusion to OT + Second Temple tradition (1 Enoch 6-19)

**Significance**: Jude's use of the angelic rebellion tradition represents a sophisticated hermeneutical move that validates the use of extrabiblical Jewish traditions when they authentically develop OT themes and serve apostolic theological purposes. The Genesis 6:1-4 narrative, understood through Second Temple interpretation as angelic transgression, becomes for Jude the supreme example of rebellion against divine order—beings of highest privilege and proximity to God who nevertheless abandoned their assigned place. The key theological point is escalation: if God did not spare angels, humanity's accountability is absolute. The detail of "everlasting chains" and "kept in darkness" emphasizes the irreversibility of judgment for cosmic rebellion, providing pastoral assurance that apostate teachers face certain doom. This also establishes Jude's pattern: each example increases in proximity to God's people (angels → Israelites → cities near Israel), building toward judgment on false teachers within the church itself.
