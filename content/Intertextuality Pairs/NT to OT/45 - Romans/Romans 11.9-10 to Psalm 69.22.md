# Romans 11:9-10 to Psalm 69:22

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 9|Romans 11:9]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 69#Psalm 69 . 22|Psalm 69:22]] (MT 69:23-24; LXX 68:23-24) (Davidic imprecation)
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 69#Psalm 69 . 6|Psalm 69:6]] (MT 69:7-9; LXX 68:7-9) (Extended context: hostility from household/family)
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 69#Psalm 69 . 30|Psalm 69:30]] (MT 69:31; LXX 68:31) (Extended context: hope of deliverance and praise)
- Exodus-Leviticus clean/unclean food laws (thematic connection to table purity)
- Intertestamental table fellowship texts (Joseph and Aseneth 7:1; 8:5; Jubilees 22:16; Additions to Esther 14:17; 3 Maccabees 3:4, 7; Letter of Aristeas 128-166)

**Type**: Direct quotation with extended contextual echoes and cultural-theological background

**Significance**: Paul applies David's curse on his enemies to unbelieving Israel. Shocking application: Israel's "table" (fellowship, Torah observance) becomes a trap. The psalm is messianic (see 15:3), so Christ pronounces this curse on those who reject him. Yet the curse is not final. Beale/Carson identify multiple layers of contextual significance. First, Psalm 69:6-8 reveals that "the hostility that the psalmist experiences comes from his own household and family." Paul may suggest that "Gentile believers in Rome may have to experience what both the Messiah and David before him faced: rejection from those close to them." Second, Psalm 69:30-31 points to vindication: "The psalmist's lament turns finally to the hope of deliverance. He thus announces, 'I will praise the name of God with song, and I will magnify him with thanksgiving.' The suffering of Christ anticipates his deliverance." This suffering-then-vindication pattern shapes Paul's theology of Israel's temporary hardening followed by eschatological salvation. Additionally, the "table" curse bears particular significance as it "reflects Israel's exclusive table fellowship"—"a dimension of Israel's pursuit of the law (9:31), an expression of a commitment to 'works' that set it apart from the Gentiles (11:6)." The intertestamental sources demonstrate that exclusive Jewish table fellowship was an expression of ethnic boundary maintenance. Paul treats the "table" curse as judgment on Israel's rejection of Christ through maintenance of ethnic separation.
