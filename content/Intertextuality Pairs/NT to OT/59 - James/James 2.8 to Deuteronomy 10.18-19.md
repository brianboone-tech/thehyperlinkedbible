# James 2:8 to Deuteronomy 10:18-19

**NT Text**: [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 2#James 2 . 8|James 2:8]]

**OT Source(s)**:
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 10#Deuteronomy 10 . 18|Deuteronomy 10:18-19]] (God's impartial justice for vulnerable)
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 15|Leviticus 19:15]] (no partiality in judgment)
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 17|Leviticus 19:17]] (rebuke your neighbor)
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 18|Leviticus 19:18]] (love your neighbor as yourself)
- [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 17|Numbers 20:17]] (request for passage)

**Type**: Echo/thematic parallel

**Significance**: James designates Leviticus 19:18 as the "royal law," elevating neighbor-love to supreme status within Torah ethics. This hermeneutical move mirrors Jesus's identification of this command as second greatest (Matt 22:39) and Paul's declaration that it fulfills the whole law (Gal 5:14). By framing partiality as violation of this royal law, James demonstrates how specific ethical failures contradict the fundamental principle of covenant love. The law is not fragmented commands but unified by the relational imperative to love neighbor impartially, reflecting God's own impartial character (Deut 10:17-19).
