# 4.1.12 - Intrusion - Promised Land II

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1 - The Delay - The Age of Preparation|← Back to 4.1]]

## Overview

**Biblical Scope**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 1|Judges 1:1]] - [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 22|Ruth 4:22]]

The period of the Judges represents one of the darkest and most cyclical intrusions in Old Testament history. After Joshua's generation dies, Israel enters a **repeating pattern of sin, oppression, repentance, and deliverance** that spans roughly 300-350 years. The refrain "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|Judges 21:25]]) summarizes the chaos of this era—Israel as corporate Adam fails repeatedly to fulfill their commission, falling into Canaanite idolatry and experiencing the judgment of foreign oppression.

Yet even in this period of repeated failure, God demonstrates His covenant faithfulness by raising up judges to deliver His people. These judges—military deliverers like Othniel, Ehud, Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah, and Samson—are **temporary saviors** who point forward to the ultimate Judge and King, Jesus Christ. As G.K. Beale argues, the Judges period demonstrates that Israel cannot save itself; they need a **permanent deliverer** who will not merely defeat external enemies but transform hearts.

Meredith Kline's framework helps us understand this era as an extended **Common Grace period** punctuated by repeated intrusions of both judgment (foreign oppression) and grace (the raising of judges). Yet none of these intrusions consummate the age—the cycle continues, escalating in severity, demonstrating that the law written on stone tablets cannot produce lasting transformation. The period ends pointing toward the need for a king, yet even the human kings will fail, ultimately directing hope toward the **eternal King** from David's line.

The book of Ruth stands as a **bright spot** in this dark period, showing God's sovereign grace to a Gentile woman who becomes part of the Messianic line. Ruth the Moabite finds redemption through her kinsman-redeemer Boaz, foreshadowing Christ the ultimate Redeemer who brings both Jew and Gentile into God's family.

### The Pattern of Intrusion

The Judges period follows the fivefold redemptive-historical pattern **repeatedly**, creating a downward spiral:

**Chaos**: Each cycle begins with Israel falling into idolatry, worshiping the Baals and Ashtaroth ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 11|Judges 2:11-13]]). They intermarry with Canaanites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 5|Judges 3:5-6]]), adopt pagan practices, and abandon the covenant. Rather than subduing the land fully as commissioned, they allow Canaanites to remain, creating ongoing spiritual danger ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 27|Judges 1:27-36]]).

**Creation**: When Israel cries out in oppression, God raises judges as instruments of new creation—deliverers who temporarily restore order. The Spirit comes upon judges like Othniel ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]]), Gideon ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 34|Judges 6:34]]), Jephthah ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|Judges 11:29]]), and Samson ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 6|Judges 14:6, 19]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 15#Judges 15 . 14|15:14]]), empowering them for deliverance.

**Commission**: Each judge is raised to deliver Israel, restore worship of Yahweh, and lead the people back to covenant faithfulness. They function as temporary mediators between God and His people, commissioned to bring shalom (peace) to the land.

**Sin**: Yet the cycle repeats—after each judge dies, Israel returns to idolatry, often "more corrupt than their fathers" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 19|Judges 2:19]]). The judges themselves increasingly exhibit moral compromise: Gideon makes an ephod that becomes a snare ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]]), Jephthah makes a rash vow ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 30|Judges 11:30-40]]), Samson repeatedly violates his Nazirite vow ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 1|Judges 14-16]]).

**Judgment**: Each cycle of sin brings foreign oppression—Mesopotamians ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 8|Judges 3:8]]), Moabites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 12|Judges 3:12-14]]), Canaanites under Jabin ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 2|Judges 4:2-3]]), Midianites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 1|Judges 6:1-6]]), Ammonites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 10#Judges 10 . 7|Judges 10:7-8]]), and Philistines ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 1|Judges 13:1]]). The period ends in civil war between tribes ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 20#Judges 20 . 1|Judges 20-21]]), demonstrating total breakdown of covenant order.

## Major Theological Themes

### 1. Israel as Failing Corporate Adam

The Judges period exposes Israel's corporate failure to fulfill their Adamic commission. God's original command after Joshua's death was clear: "**Go up** against the Canaanites first, to fight against them" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 1|Judges 1:1-2]]). Israel began well—Judah took Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 8|Judges 1:8]]), Caleb conquered Hebron ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 20|Judges 1:20]])—but they **failed to complete the conquest**.

G.K. Beale demonstrates that Israel's commission included all five elements of the Adamic mandate:

- **Subdue and rule**: Drive out all Canaanites, take dominion over the land ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 28|Judges 1:28]] - they put Canaanites to forced labor instead of driving them out)
- **Be fruitful and multiply**: Build families in covenant faithfulness (violated through intermarriage, [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 6|Judges 3:6]])
- **Serve and guard**: Keep God's covenant, maintain temple worship (violated through Baal worship, [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 11|Judges 2:11-13]])
- **Expand God's temple-presence**: Make Israel a light to the nations (instead they adopted pagan practices, [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 17|Judges 2:17]])
- **Reflect God's image**: Display His justice and holiness (violated through moral chaos, [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|Judges 21:25]])

The angel of the LORD's rebuke at Bochim captures this failure: "I said, 'I will never break my covenant with you, and you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall break down their altars.' **But you have not obeyed my voice**. What is this you have done?" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 1|Judges 2:1-2]]).

### 2. The Downward Spiral: Escalating Chaos

The Judges follow a **degenerating pattern**—each cycle grows worse, both in the severity of oppression and the moral quality of the judges:

**Early Judges** (Othniel, Ehud, Deborah): Relatively faithful, brief mentions of failure
- Othniel: "The Spirit of the LORD was upon him, and he judged Israel" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]])—40 years of rest
- Ehud: Assassinates Eglon, delivers Israel ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 15|Judges 3:15-30]])—80 years of rest
- Deborah: Prophetess and judge, leads with Barak ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 4|Judges 4:4-5]])—40 years of rest

**Middle Judges** (Gideon, Abimelech, Jephthah): Growing moral compromise
- Gideon: Starts strong ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 11|Judges 6:11-16]]) but ends making an idolatrous ephod ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]]), has 70 sons through many wives
- Abimelech: Gideon's son, murders 70 brothers ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 9#Judges 9 . 5|Judges 9:5]]), makes himself king—**not called a judge by God**
- Jephthah: Delivers Israel but makes a tragic vow ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 30|Judges 11:30-40]]), war between tribes ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 12#Judges 12 . 1|Judges 12:1-6]])

**Late Judge** (Samson): Maximum moral failure despite divine calling
- Nazarite from birth ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 5|Judges 13:5]]), empowered by the Spirit ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 6|Judges 14:6]])
- Yet repeatedly violates Nazirite vow: touches dead bodies, feasts with Philistines, sexual immorality with prostitutes and Delilah
- Dies defeated, blinded, enslaved—though God grants one final victory ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 28|Judges 16:28-30]])

The period ends with horrific chaos: a Levite's concubine gang-raped and murdered ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 19#Judges 19 . 22|Judges 19:22-30]]), civil war that nearly exterminates Benjamin ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 20#Judges 20 . 1|Judges 20-21]]), and mass kidnapping of women to provide wives for survivors ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 19|Judges 21:19-23]]).

### 3. The Cycle of Deliverance: God's Patient Grace

Despite Israel's repeated rebellion, God demonstrates **covenant faithfulness** through the cycle of deliverance:

**The Pattern** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 11|Judges 2:11-19]]):
1. Israel does evil in the sight of the LORD ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 11|v. 11]])
2. God's anger burns against Israel ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 14|v. 14]])
3. God gives them into the hand of oppressors ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 14|v. 14]])
4. Israel cries out to the LORD ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 9|Judges 3:9, 15]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 3|4:3]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 6|6:6]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 10#Judges 10 . 10|10:10]])
5. God raises a judge/deliverer ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 16|v. 16, 18]])
6. The land has rest for X years
7. The judge dies, Israel returns to evil ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 19|v. 19]])

This pattern repeats **at least seven major cycles** (some count 12-13 judges), demonstrating that:
- Israel cannot save themselves—deliverance always comes from **God's initiative**
- Temporary deliverers produce only temporary results—they need a **permanent Savior**
- External deliverance without heart transformation leads back to bondage—they need the **New Covenant**

God's patience is remarkable: "The LORD raised up judges, who saved them... Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, **the LORD was with the judge**, and he saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge. **For the LORD was moved to pity** by their groaning because of those who afflicted and oppressed them" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 16|Judges 2:16, 18]]).

### 4. The Spirit-Empowered Judges: Types of Christ

The judges are **Spirit-empowered deliverers**, pointing forward to the ultimate Spirit-anointed One:

**The Spirit's Pattern**:
- "The Spirit of the LORD was upon Othniel" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]])
- "The Spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 34|Judges 6:34]], Hebrew: *labash*—"clothed" or "came upon")
- "The Spirit of the LORD was upon Jephthah" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|Judges 11:29]])
- "The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon Samson" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 6|Judges 14:6, 19]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 15#Judges 15 . 14|15:14]])

These Spirit-empowerments prefigure Jesus, upon whom "the Spirit of the LORD shall rest" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 2|Isaiah 11:2]]), who is "anointed... with the Holy Spirit and with power" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]]).

Yet the judges' **failures** highlight their inadequacy:
- Gideon's ephod becomes a snare ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]])
- Jephthah's rash vow costs his daughter ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 35|Judges 11:35-39]])
- Samson's moral failures lead to his capture and death ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 21|Judges 16:21]])

Only Christ is the judge who **never fails**, empowered by the Spirit without measure ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 34|John 3:34]]), bringing eternal deliverance.

### 5. "No King in Israel": Pointing to the Need for a King

The refrain "In those days there was no king in Israel" appears four times ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 17#Judges 17 . 6|Judges 17:6]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 18#Judges 18 . 1|18:1]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 19#Judges 19 . 1|19:1]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|21:25]]), often with the addition "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 17#Judges 17 . 6|Judges 17:6]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|21:25]]).

This points to Israel's fundamental problem: they had rejected **Yahweh as their King** ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 7|1 Samuel 8:7]]—"they have rejected me from being king over them"). The judges were temporary, local deliverers, but Israel needed a **righteous king** from David's line who would:
- Rule with justice and righteousness
- Lead Israel in covenant faithfulness
- Defeat all enemies
- Establish God's kingdom permanently

The Judges period demonstrates that **judges are insufficient**—only a king, and ultimately the **King of kings**, can bring lasting peace.

### 6. Ruth: Grace to the Gentile, Hope for the Messiah

In the midst of the Judges' darkness, the book of Ruth shines as a **bright narrative of covenant love**. Set "in the days when the judges ruled" ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 1|Ruth 1:1]]), Ruth tells the story of:

**Chaos**: Famine in Bethlehem ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 1|Ruth 1:1]]), Elimelech's family goes to Moab, death of husband and sons ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 3|Ruth 1:3-5]]), Naomi returns empty ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 21|Ruth 1:21]])

**Creation**: Ruth's declaration of covenant loyalty—"Your people shall be my people, and your God my God" ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 16|Ruth 1:16-17]])—is a new creation confession, a Gentile Moabite embracing the God of Israel

**Commission**: Boaz, the kinsman-redeemer, is providentially positioned to redeem Naomi's family line ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 1|Ruth 2:1]], [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 20|20]])

**Sin**: While not prominently featured, the background of Judges-era chaos frames Ruth's story

**Judgment reversed**: Instead of judgment, Ruth experiences **redemption**—Boaz redeems her ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 9|Ruth 4:9-10]]), marries her, and she becomes the great-grandmother of David ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 17|Ruth 4:17, 22]])

**Theological Significance**:
- **Gentile inclusion**: Ruth the Moabite enters the covenant community, prefiguring the inclusion of Gentiles in Christ ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 11|Ephesians 2:11-19]])
- **Kinsman-redeemer**: Boaz as *go'el* (redeemer) points to Christ, our ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer who pays the price to redeem us ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]])
- **Davidic line**: Ruth is in the genealogy of David and thus of Jesus ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5]]), demonstrating God's sovereign grace in preserving the Messianic line
- **Covenant love (hesed)**: Ruth and Boaz both display *hesed*—covenant loyalty and loving-kindness ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 20|Ruth 2:20]]; [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 10|3:10]])—pointing to God's ultimate covenant faithfulness in Christ

### 7. The Incomplete Conquest: Canaanites Remaining

Israel's failure to **completely drive out the Canaanites** becomes a perpetual snare:

**The Command**: "You shall not make a covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall break down their altars" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 2|Judges 2:2]])

**The Failure**:
- Judah could not drive out the inhabitants of the plain ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 19|Judges 1:19]])
- Benjamin did not drive out the Jebusites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 21|Judges 1:21]])
- Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali all failed to complete the conquest ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 27|Judges 1:27-33]])

**The Consequence**: "I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become **thorns in your sides**, and their gods shall be **a snare to you**" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 3|Judges 2:3]])

This incomplete obedience echoes Adam and Eve's incomplete guarding of Eden—they allowed the serpent to remain, leading to their downfall. Israel's tolerance of Canaanite religion leads directly to their repeated apostasy.

The New Testament applies this principle to believers: "Make no provision for the flesh" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 13#Romans 13 . 14|Romans 13:14]]), "Cleanse out the old leaven" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]), "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 5|Colossians 3:5]]).

## Kline's Two-Register Framework

Meredith Kline's two-register cosmology illuminates the Judges period as an extended **Common Grace period** with repeated intrusions:

### Upper Register Intrusions: The Judges as Deliverers

Each raising of a judge is a **gracious intrusion** of the upper register into Israel's Common Grace judgment:

- The Spirit coming upon judges ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 34|6:34]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|11:29]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 6|14:6]]) is eschatological power invading history
- Military victories against overwhelming odds (Gideon's 300 vs. Midian's thousands, [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 7|Judges 7:7-22]]) demonstrate divine intervention
- Periods of rest (40, 80 years) are foretastes of the ultimate Sabbath rest

Yet these intrusions **do not consummate the age**—the judges die, Israel returns to sin, and the cycle repeats. The Common Grace period continues, allowing:
- Ongoing opportunity for repentance
- Preservation of the covenant line leading to David and Christ
- Demonstration that law cannot transform the heart
- Building anticipation for the ultimate Deliverer

### Lower Register Judgments: Foreign Oppression

The foreign oppressions are **lower-register judgments**—intrusions of eschatological wrath in measured form:

- 8 years under Cushan-rishathaim ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 8|Judges 3:8]])
- 18 years under Eglon of Moab ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 14|Judges 3:14]])
- 20 years under Jabin of Canaan ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 3|Judges 4:3]])
- 7 years under Midian ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 1|Judges 6:1]])
- 18 years under Ammonites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 10#Judges 10 . 8|Judges 10:8]])
- 40 years under Philistines ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 1|Judges 13:1]])

These are **previews of final judgment**, yet they are **temporary and reversible**. The ultimate judgment—exile to Babylon—still lies centuries in the future. God restrains His wrath, demonstrating patience and giving repeated opportunities for repentance.

## Christological Fulfillment: The True and Final Judge

The New Testament presents Jesus as the **ultimate Judge** who succeeds where all human judges failed:

### Jesus the Spirit-Anointed Deliverer

Where the Spirit came upon judges **temporarily** and **for specific tasks**, Jesus is **permanently anointed**:

"The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim liberty to the captives and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty those who are oppressed" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18-19]], quoting [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 61#Isaiah 61 . 1|Isaiah 61:1-2]]).

Where judges delivered Israel from **physical enemies** (Moabites, Midianites, Philistines), Jesus delivers from **sin, death, and Satan**:

"He disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them in him" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]).

### Jesus the Perfect Judge

Where human judges **failed morally** (Gideon's ephod, Jephthah's vow, Samson's lust), Jesus is the **righteous Judge** who never sins:

"For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, **yet without sin**" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]).

### Jesus the Eternal King

Where judges were **temporary** deliverers who died, Jesus is the **eternal King** who reigns forever:

"Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David and over his kingdom, to establish it and to uphold it with justice and with righteousness from this time forth and forevermore" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 7|Isaiah 9:7]]).

Where the Judges period ended with "no king in Israel" and "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|Judges 21:25]]), Christ's kingdom brings **righteousness and justice**:

"The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 15|Revelation 11:15]]).

### Jesus the Kinsman-Redeemer

Boaz's redemption of Ruth points to Christ's greater redemption:

Where Boaz redeemed Ruth by **paying the price** and **marrying her** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 9|Ruth 4:9-10]]), Christ redeems His bride the church by **His blood** and makes her His own:

"You were bought with a price" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 20|1 Corinthians 6:20]]).

Where Ruth the Gentile was brought into Israel's covenant, the church—composed of Jew and Gentile—is brought into the New Covenant:

"For he himself is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility... that he might create in himself one new man in place of the two, so making peace" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-15]]).

## Conclusion: The Inadequacy of Judges and the Hope of the True King

The Judges period demonstrates the tragic inadequacy of human deliverers. Israel needed more than temporary military heroes—they needed a **permanent Savior** who would transform hearts, defeat sin itself, and reign in righteousness forever. The repeating cycle of sin-oppression-deliverance-rest-sin shows that external deliverance without internal transformation cannot produce lasting change.

Yet God's patience throughout this dark period reveals His covenant faithfulness. He raised judges "because the LORD was moved to pity by their groaning" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 18|Judges 2:18]]), preserving the covenant line through which the Messiah would come. The book of Ruth shines as a beacon of hope, showing that God's redemptive plan includes Gentiles and that a kinsman-redeemer will come to save His people.

The fivefold pattern repeats throughout Judges—Chaos, Creation (through judges), Commission, Sin, Judgment—but never reaches final consummation. Each cycle points forward to the **ultimate cycle** when Christ will bring creation to its eschatological fulfillment. Where the judges brought temporary rest, Christ brings eternal rest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-11]]). Where Israel cried out and God sent judges, Christ is the Judge who **already sits at God's right hand**, interceding for His people ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]).

The period ends pointing to the need for a king, setting up the narrative of 1 Samuel and the eventual establishment of the Davidic dynasty—and ultimately, the **eternal King** who is David's greater Son.
