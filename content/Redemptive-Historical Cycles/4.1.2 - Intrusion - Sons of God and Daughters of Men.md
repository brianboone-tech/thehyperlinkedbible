# 4.1.2 - Intrusion - Sons of God and Daughters of Men

## Overview

The second intrusion in redemptive history traces the escalating corruption from Cain's exile through the announcement of the Flood judgment. This period reveals both God's patience in preserving a righteous remnant (the Sethite line) and humanity's total depravity when left to its own devices.

**Biblical Context**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 12|Genesis 4:12b-14]] through [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 1|Genesis 6:1-8]]

**Key Events**:
- Cain exiled as fugitive and wanderer ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 12|Genesis 4:12b]], [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 14|14]])
- God provides another seed in Seth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]])
- Men begin to call on the name of the LORD ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 26|Genesis 4:26]])
- Genealogy from Adam to Noah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 1|Genesis 5]])
- Men begin to multiply on the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 1|Genesis 6:1]])
- "Sons of God" take "daughters of men" as wives ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 2|Genesis 6:2]])
- Nephilim appear on the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 4|Genesis 6:4]])
- Wickedness becomes great on earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5-7]])
- God announces the Flood judgment ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 7|Genesis 6:7-8]])

## Critical Interpretive Question: Who Are the "Sons of God"?

The identity of the "sons of God" in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 2|Genesis 6:2]] is one of the most debated questions in biblical interpretation. Two primary views:

### View 1: Sethite Interpretation (Adopted Here)

**Position**: The "sons of God" are the godly descendants of Seth who intermarried with the ungodly descendants of Cain ("daughters of men").

**Supporting Evidence**:
1. **Contextual Flow**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 26|Genesis 4:26]] establishes Seth's line as those who "call upon the name of the LORD"
2. **Two-Line Pattern**: Genesis 4-5 carefully distinguishes Cain's line (cultural achievement without God) from Seth's line (worship and righteousness)
3. **Covenant Language**: "Sons of God" language used elsewhere for covenant people (cf. [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 14#Deuteronomy 14 . 1|Deuteronomy 14:1]]; [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 1#Hosea 1 . 10|Hosea 1:10]])
4. **Corporate Adam Typology**: Fits pattern of covenant community called to holy separation
5. **Temple Theology**: Beale's framework—godly seed commissioned to expand God's presence compromises by mixing with the profane
6. **Moral Culpability**: Makes better sense of human responsibility for the wickedness that follows
7. **Later Biblical Pattern**: Intermarriage with ungodly repeatedly condemned (cf. [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 3|Deuteronomy 7:3-4]]; [[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 1|Ezra 9:1-2]]; [[Readable Bible/46 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 14|2 Corinthians 6:14]])

**Theological Significance**: This view emphasizes covenant compromise—the breaking of holy separation that leads to total corruption.

### View 2: Angelic Interpretation

**Position**: The "sons of God" are fallen angels who took human wives.

**Supporting Evidence**:
1. **Job Usage**: "Sons of God" in [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 1#Job 1 . 6|Job 1:6]] and [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 2#Job 2 . 1|Job 2:1]] clearly refers to angelic beings
2. **Nephilim**: The offspring ("mighty men," "men of renown") suggest supernatural origin
3. **New Testament References**: [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 2#2 Peter 2 . 4|2 Peter 2:4]] and [[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 6|Jude 1:6]] speak of angels who sinned
4. **Severity of Judgment**: The Flood's universal scope suggests unprecedented evil
5. **Ancient Interpretation**: Early Jewish sources (1 Enoch, Jubilees) and church fathers favored this view

**Difficulties with Angelic View**:
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 30|Matthew 22:30]] indicates angels "neither marry nor are given in marriage"
- Places moral blame on supernatural beings rather than human covenant breaking
- Requires assuming information not clearly stated in Genesis
- Doesn't fit the contextual flow of Genesis 4-6's two-line emphasis

**This Framework's Position**: We adopt the **Sethite view** because:
1. It better fits the corporate Adam typology developed throughout this study
2. It aligns with Beale's temple theology and Kline's intrusion ethics
3. It maintains human moral responsibility
4. It establishes a pattern repeated throughout Scripture (covenant compromise through intermarriage)

## The Fivefold Pattern Applied

This intrusion follows the same redemptive-historical cycle seen in the Fall:

### [[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.2.1 - Chaos|1. Chaos]] - Cain's Exile
**Text**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 12|Genesis 4:12b, 14]]

Cain becomes a "fugitive and wanderer," exiled not only from Eden but "from the presence of the LORD." His line builds civilization ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 17|Genesis 4:17-24]]) but drifts further from God. Violence escalates (Lamech, [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]). The righteous line appears cut off—Abel murdered, Cain exiled. This is the chaos that sets up the need for new creation.

### [[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.2.2 - Creation|2. Creation]] - Seth Given
**Text**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]]

God graciously provides "another offspring instead of Abel." Eve recognizes this as divine appointment. With Seth's birth, worship is restored ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 26|Genesis 4:26]]—"people began to call upon the name of the LORD"). The genealogy of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 1|Genesis 5]] traces the righteous line from Adam to Noah, highlighting Enoch who "walked with God" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 22|Genesis 5:22, 24]]).

### [[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.2.3 - Commission|3. Commission]] - Multiplication
**Text**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 1|Genesis 6:1]]

"When man began to multiply on the face of the land"—the original commission to be fruitful and multiply ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]) continues. Population explodes. Two lines persist: Sethites (godly) and Cainites (ungodly). The commission includes maintaining holy separation, imaging God rightly, expanding the temple-garden through righteous worship.

### [[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.2.4 - Sin|4. Sin]] - Intermarriage and Wickedness
**Text**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 2|Genesis 6:2, 5-7]]

The "sons of God" (Sethite line) see the "daughters of men" (Cainite line) and "took as their wives any they chose." Holy separation breaks down. Lust, polygamy, covenant compromise follow. Wickedness escalates until "every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5]]). Total depravity on display.

### [[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.2.5 - Judgment|5. Judgment]] - Flood Announced
**Text**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 7|Genesis 6:7-8]]

God announces He will "blot out man" from the earth. The Flood will be cosmic de-creation, reversing [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1]]. Yet grace appears: "Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 8|Genesis 6:8]]). A remnant will be preserved. The pattern continues: sin → judgment → remnant → new creation.

## Theological Themes

### Pattern of Failure

Unlike the first intrusion (the Fall), where Adam and Eve were tested in perfection, this intrusion shows humanity's inability to maintain righteousness over generations. Even with:
- A righteous line established (Seth)
- Worship restored ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 26|Genesis 4:26]])
- Long lifespans allowing cultural development
- Direct knowledge of God's judgment on Cain

...the human heart still gravitates toward evil. The Sethite interpretation makes this failure most poignant: even those who "call upon the name of the LORD" eventually compromise with the world.

### Covenant Compromise Through Intermarriage

This theme will echo throughout redemptive history:
- Israel forbidden to intermarry with Canaanites ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 3|Deuteronomy 7:3-4]])
- Solomon's downfall through foreign wives ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 11#1 Kings 11 . 1|1 Kings 11:1-8]])
- Post-exilic crisis over intermarriage ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 1|Ezra 9-10]]; [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 23|Nehemiah 13:23-27]])
- New Testament warning against unequal yoking ([[Readable Bible/46 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 14|2 Corinthians 6:14-18]])

The principle: God's people must maintain holy distinctiveness. When the line between covenant community and world blurs, total corruption follows.

### Total Depravity

[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5]] provides the strongest Old Testament statement of human depravity:
- **Universal**: "every intention"
- **Internal**: "thoughts of his heart"
- **Comprehensive**: "only evil"
- **Constant**: "continually"

This text demolishes any notion of human goodness apart from grace. It sets the theological foundation for later biblical teaching on the depravity of the human heart (cf. [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 17#Jeremiah 17 . 9|Jeremiah 17:9]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 10|Romans 3:10-18]]).

### Common Grace and Patience

Between [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 3|Genesis 6:3]]'s announcement (120 years) and the Flood's arrival, God demonstrates remarkable patience. This restraint shows:
- God's reluctance to judge ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 33#Ezekiel 33 . 11|Ezekiel 33:11]])
- Opportunity for repentance ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]])
- Common grace sustaining even the wicked (Kline's framework)
- Pattern for final judgment—delay, then decisive action

### Remnant Theology

"But Noah found favor" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 8|Genesis 6:8]])—the crucial "but" that runs through redemptive history. Even in universal judgment, God preserves a remnant through whom:
- The Seed promise continues ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]])
- Covenant faithfulness is maintained
- New creation will emerge
- Redemptive history progresses toward Christ

This establishes the remnant pattern seen in Noah, Abraham, Israel in Egypt, the exile, the faithful Jews in Jesus' day, and ultimately the church.

### Beale's Temple Theology

From this framework:
- **Eden as Temple**: Adam commissioned to expand God's holy presence
- **Sethite Line as Priests**: Those who "call upon the name of the LORD" maintain worship
- **Intermarriage as Desecration**: Mixing holy seed with profane defiles the temple community
- **Flood as Temple Purification**: God cleanses the earth of corruption
- **Noah as New Adam**: Receives renewed commission to fill the earth with righteous image-bearers

The sons of God fail to maintain the temple's holiness by compromising with the profane world.

### Kline's Intrusion Ethics

From this framework:
- **Common Grace Period**: Time between Fall and Flood is marked by God's restraint
- **Two Seeds Coexisting**: Cain's and Seth's lines develop side-by-side (wheat/tares pattern)
- **Intrusion of Judgment**: The Flood intrudes as partial consummation—foretaste of final judgment
- **Ethical Demand**: During common grace, God's people must maintain separation while dwelling in the world
- **Eschatological Orientation**: This pattern points to final separation at Christ's return

The failure of Genesis 6 shows what happens when covenant people lose eschatological perspective and accommodate to the world.

## Christological Fulfillment

### Christ as the True Seed

Where Seth's line eventually compromised, Christ maintains perfect righteousness. He is:
- The ultimate "another offspring" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]] fulfilled in [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 16|Galatians 3:16]])
- The one who truly "walks with God" (Enoch typology)
- The righteous remnant compressed into one Man
- The Seed who crushes the serpent ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]])

### Christ as the Ark

Just as Noah's ark provided salvation through judgment waters:
- Christ is our refuge from God's wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]])
- Baptism connects to ark symbolism ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 20|1 Peter 3:20-21]])
- Only those "in Christ" survive final judgment
- The church is preserved through tribulation in Christ

### Christ Maintains Holy Separation

Where sons of God compromised:
- Christ remained "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]])
- He associated with sinners without being defiled ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 9#Matthew 9 . 10|Matthew 9:10-13]])
- He calls His church to be "in the world but not of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 14|John 17:14-16]])
- He provides power for holiness where Adam's sons failed ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 3|Romans 8:3-4]])

## Connection to Broader Redemptive Narrative

This intrusion sits between:
- **Previous**: The Fall (4.1.1)—established the problem of sin and promise of Seed
- **Next**: The Flood (4.1.3)—will execute judgment and establish new creation with Noah

The pattern emerging:
1. Creation/New Creation
2. Commission
3. Compromise/Sin
4. Judgment announced
5. Remnant preserved
6. New beginning (which becomes chaos for next cycle)

This fivefold pattern will repeat at:
- The Flood (Noah)
- The Patriarchs (Abraham)
- The Exodus (Moses)
- The Conquest (Joshua)
- The Monarchy (David)
- The Exile and Return
- The Incarnation (Christ)
- The Church Age
- The Consummation

Each cycle demonstrates human failure and God's faithfulness to preserve His redemptive purposes toward the ultimate fulfillment in Christ.

## Practical Application

### Warning Against Covenant Compromise

The church today faces the same temptation as the sons of God:
- Cultural accommodation that blurs distinctiveness
- Marriages that prioritize attraction over spiritual compatibility
- Partnerships with the world that compromise holiness
- Gradual drift from calling on the Lord's name toward self-reliance

[[Readable Bible/46 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 14|2 Corinthians 6:14-18]] applies the principle: "Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers."

### Reality of Total Depravity

[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5]] shatters illusions about human goodness. Apart from grace:
- Our thoughts are corrupt, not just our actions
- We are continually evil, not occasionally good
- Our intentions are only evil, with no mixed motives

This reality drives us to:
- Depend utterly on Christ's righteousness
- Recognize our need for the Spirit's transforming power
- Extend grace to others, knowing we're equally depraved
- Marvel at God's patience with our own wickedness

### Hope in the Remnant

"But Noah found favor"—the gospel in four words. Even when:
- The whole world falls into corruption
- The covenant community compromises
- Wickedness seems universal and unstoppable

...God preserves a remnant. This assures us:
- Christ will build His church ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 18|Matthew 16:18]])
- A people will be saved from every tribe and tongue ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]])
- Our labor in the Lord is not in vain ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 58|1 Corinthians 15:58]])
- God will complete what He started ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 1#Philippians 1 . 6|Philippians 1:6]])

### Preparation for Judgment

As the Flood came after long patience, so final judgment approaches:
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 37|Matthew 24:37-39]]: "As were the days of Noah, so will be the coming of the Son of Man"
- [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 3|2 Peter 3:3-7]]: The world will be destroyed by fire as it once was by water
- We must be ready, as Noah was ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 7|Hebrews 11:7]])
- We must warn others while patience lasts ([[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 23|Jude 1:23]])
