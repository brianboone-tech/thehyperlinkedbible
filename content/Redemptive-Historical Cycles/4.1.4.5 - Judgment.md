# 4.1.4.5 - Judgment - Plagues, Rebuke, and Expulsion (Genesis 12:17-20)

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.4 - Intrusion - Call of Abraham|← Back to 4.1.4]]

---

## Overview

God's judgment on Abraham's sin is both **merciful** and **ironic**. Merciful, because God intervenes to protect the seed promise before Sarai is violated. Ironic, because a **pagan king rebukes the patriarch**, exposing the reversal of roles. Pharaoh, who should be receiving blessing through Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]), instead receives plagues. Abraham, who should be a blessing to the nations, brings curses. The judgment culminates in **expulsion from Egypt**—a mini-exodus, foreshadowing Israel's later pattern. Yet even in judgment, God's **covenant faithfulness** prevails. The promise is preserved, not because Abraham is faithful, but because **God is faithful**.

**Key Theme**: Judgment falls, but in the form of divine rescue. God afflicts Pharaoh to protect Sarai, exposes Abraham's deception through a pagan's rebuke, and drives Abraham back to the Promised Land—demonstrating that covenant promises depend on God's faithfulness, not human performance.

## The First Judgment: God Afflicts Pharaoh (Genesis 12:17)

### Plagues on Pharaoh's House

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 17|Genesis 12:17]]**: "But **the LORD afflicted Pharaoh and his house with great plagues** because of Sarai, Abram's wife."

**Divine Intervention**: At the critical moment, when the seed promise is most threatened, **God acts**. The text does not record Abraham praying or repenting—God intervenes **sovereignly**, not in response to Abraham's merit but to preserve His covenant.

**Great Plagues** (נְגָעִים גְּדֹלִים, *nega'im gedolim*): The Hebrew word for "plagues" (נֶגַע, *nega*) refers to blows, strikes, or afflictions sent by God. This is the same word used for the **ten plagues** on Egypt during the Exodus ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 11#Exodus 11 . 1|Exodus 11:1]]—"Yet one plague more I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt").

**Foreshadowing the Exodus**: This is the **first plague on Egypt** in Scripture. The pattern is established:
1. **God's people in Egypt** (Abraham/Israel)
2. **Pharaoh oppresses/takes what belongs to God's people** (Sarai/Israel)
3. **God sends plagues** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 17|Genesis 12:17]] / Exodus 7-12)
4. **God's people leave with wealth** (Genesis 12:16, 20 / [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 35|Exodus 12:35-36]])
5. **Egypt is glad to see them go** (Genesis 12:20 / [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 33|Exodus 12:33]])

What happens to Abraham **typologically prefigures** what will happen to Israel. The personal becomes corporate.

### The Nature of the Plagues

The text does not specify what kind of afflictions Pharaoh and his house experienced, but several possibilities emerge from context and later parallels:

**Physical Illness**: The plagues likely involved some form of disease or barrenness that **prevented Pharaoh from consummating the marriage** with Sarai. God protects her by afflicting Pharaoh's household.

**Supernatural Sign**: The plagues were severe enough that Pharaoh **recognized** they were divine judgment related to Sarai. Somehow, God made clear that the afflictions were "because of Sarai, Abram's wife."

**Pattern**: This anticipates similar divine protection in later narratives:
- **Abimelech** is warned in a dream and afflicted with barrenness when he takes Sarah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 20#Genesis 20 . 3|Genesis 20:3-7, 17-18]])
- **Pharaoh** (Exodus) experiences ten plagues when he holds Israel captive ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 7#Exodus 7 . 14|Exodus 7:14–12:30]])

God **jealously guards** His covenant people and His promises. When the seed promise is threatened, He intervenes with power.

### Theological Significance of God's Intervention

**God's Faithfulness Despite Human Failure**: Abraham's deception **does not nullify God's covenant**. The promise depends on God's character, not Abraham's performance. This is **unconditional grace**—God saves despite our sin, not because of our righteousness.

**The Seed Promise Protected**: If Pharaoh had consummated the marriage with Sarai, the **lineage would be compromised**. Isaac must come from Abraham **and** Sarah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 19|Genesis 17:19]]—"No, but Sarah your wife shall bear you a son, and you shall call his name Isaac. I will establish my covenant with him"). God's plagues **preserve the purity of the seed line**.

**Kline's Framework**: This is an **intrusion of special grace** into the common grace world. While God allows general blessings on all people (Pharaoh's kingdom, Abraham's wealth), He **actively intervenes** to protect His covenant purposes. Special grace overrides natural consequences.

**[[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 3|Romans 3:3-4]]** applies: "What if some were unfaithful? Does their faithlessness nullify the faithfulness of God? By no means! Let God be true though every one were a liar." Abraham's faithlessness does not nullify God's faithfulness.

## The Second Judgment: Pharaoh Rebukes Abraham (Genesis 12:18-19)

### The Ironic Reversal

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 18|Genesis 12:18-19]]**: "So Pharaoh called Abram and said, **'What is this you have done to me?** Why did you not tell me that she was your wife? Why did you say, "She is my sister," so that I took her for my wife? Now then, here is your wife; take her, and go.'"

**The Reversal**: In one of the most shocking scenes in Genesis, the **pagan king rebukes the patriarch**. The man chosen by God to bless the nations ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]) is corrected by an **idolatrous Pharaoh**. The covenant mediator is exposed by an **unregenerate ruler**.

**Pharaoh's Righteousness**: Notice Pharaoh's indignation:
- **"What is this you have done to me?"**—Pharaoh recognizes he has been wronged
- **"Why did you not tell me that she was your wife?"**—Pharaoh appeals to honesty
- **"Why did you say, 'She is my sister'?"**—Pharaoh confronts the deception directly

Pharaoh acts with **more integrity** than Abraham in this moment. He:
- **Returns Sarai** without violating her (as far as the text indicates, God's plagues prevented consummation)
- **Rebukes the deception** (speaking truth to the covenant mediator)
- **Sends Abraham away** (protecting his own household from further divine judgment)

**Abraham's Silence**: The text records **no response** from Abraham. He does not defend himself, apologize, or explain. His silence is telling—there is **no defense** for his actions. He stands rebuked by a pagan, exposed as a deceiver.

### The Pattern of Rebuke

This is **not the only time** a pagan rebukes God's people for moral failure:

1. **Pharaoh rebukes Abraham** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 18|Genesis 12:18-19]])
2. **Abimelech rebukes Abraham** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 20#Genesis 20 . 9|Genesis 20:9-10]]—"What have you done to us? And how have I sinned against you, that you have brought on me and my kingdom a great sin? You have done to me things that ought not to be done")
3. **Abimelech rebukes Isaac** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 26#Genesis 26 . 10|Genesis 26:10]]—"What is this you have done to us? One of the people might easily have lain with your wife, and you would have brought guilt upon us")
4. **Cyrus delivers Israel** when they cannot deliver themselves ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 45#Isaiah 45 . 1|Isaiah 45:1]]—Cyrus called "his anointed")
5. **Paul confronts Peter** for compromising with the Judaizers ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 2#Galatians 2 . 11|Galatians 2:11-14]]—"I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned")

**Theological Principle**: God's covenant people are **not morally superior** by nature. They are chosen by grace, not merit. When they sin, God may use **outsiders** to expose and correct them. This is both **humbling** (God's people are rebuked by pagans) and **hopeful** (even pagans can speak truth and act justly, demonstrating God's common grace).

### The Exposure of Abraham's Witness

Abraham was called to be a **blessing** to the nations ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]). Instead, he brought **curses** (plagues). This reversal demonstrates the **corruption of sin**:
- Abraham **should** mediate blessing → Instead he mediates judgment
- Abraham **should** display God's character → Instead he displays fear and deception
- Abraham **should** be an example of faith → Instead he is rebuked for faithlessness

**Israel's Later Failure**: This pattern repeats with the nation:
- [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 24|Romans 2:24]]—"For, as it is written, 'The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you'"
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 20|Ezekiel 36:20-23]]—"But when they came to the nations, wherever they came, they profaned my holy name... But I had concern for my holy name"

When God's people fail to image Him rightly, the **nations blaspheme** rather than worship. This underscores the need for a **faithful representative**—one who will perfectly display God's character and truly bless the nations.

## The Third Judgment: Expulsion from Egypt (Genesis 12:20)

### Driven Out Under Guard

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 20|Genesis 12:20]]**: "And Pharaoh **gave men orders concerning him**, and **they sent him away** with his wife and all that he had."

**Forced Departure**: Pharaoh does not merely allow Abraham to leave—he **commands** it. The phrase "gave men orders concerning him" suggests that Abraham was escorted **under guard**, as if he were a criminal or threat. He is **expelled** from Egypt, driven out like an exile.

**Echoes of Eden**: This is the **fourth major expulsion** in Genesis:
1. **Adam and Eve exiled from Eden** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]—"He drove out the man")
2. **Cain exiled from God's presence** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 16|Genesis 4:16]]—"Cain went away from the presence of the LORD")
3. **Babel scattering** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 8|Genesis 11:8-9]]—"the LORD dispersed them")
4. **Abraham exiled from Egypt** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 20|Genesis 12:20]]—"they sent him away")

Each expulsion follows **sin**. Abraham, the new Adam, **repeats the pattern**—sin leads to exile.

**Foreshadowing Israel's Exodus**: Yet there is a **redemptive twist**. When Israel is later expelled from Egypt, it will be an act of **deliverance**, not judgment ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 31|Exodus 12:31-32, 39]]). What begins as judgment (Abraham's expulsion) becomes the pattern for salvation (Israel's exodus). God redeems even the consequences of sin.

### Abraham Leaves with Wealth

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 20|Genesis 12:20]]**: "And they sent him away **with his wife and all that he had**."

**The Wealth Retained**: Abraham leaves Egypt with:
- **Sarai** (the seed promise intact)
- **All that he had** (the livestock and servants acquired through deception, [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 16|Genesis 12:16]])

**Common Grace**: God allows Abraham to keep the wealth gained through his sin. This is **common grace**—God's general goodness even to the undeserving. Abraham does not deserve this wealth, but God permits it.

**Later Pattern**: Israel will also leave Egypt with **great wealth** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 35|Exodus 12:35-36]]—"They plundered the Egyptians"). What Abraham experiences individually, Israel will experience corporately.

**Ambiguity of Blessing**: The wealth is both a **blessing** (provision from God) and a **reminder of sin** (gained through deception). This ambiguity will continue—Abraham's riches will later cause conflict with Lot ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 5|Genesis 13:5-7]]).

## Grace in the Midst of Judgment

Though this section is titled "Judgment," it is simultaneously a display of **God's grace**:

### 1. The Seed Promise Preserved

**God's Primary Concern**: The plagues protect Sarai from violation, ensuring that Isaac will be born from Abraham and Sarah. The **seed line is secure**, not because Abraham protected it, but because **God guarded it**.

**[[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 29|Romans 11:29]]**: "For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable." God's covenant with Abraham **cannot fail** because it does not depend on Abraham's faithfulness but on God's unchanging character.

### 2. No Permanent Consequence

**Abraham is Not Destroyed**: Despite his sin, Abraham is not rejected, cursed, or abandoned. God does not revoke the covenant. The promises remain in effect:
- "I will make of you a great nation" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]])—still valid
- "I will bless you" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]])—still operative
- "In you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]])—still promised

This demonstrates **justification by faith alone**. Abraham is not justified by his perfect obedience (which he does not have) but by **faith** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]]—"And he believed the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness"). His failure in Egypt does not nullify his justification.

### 3. Abraham Returns to Worship

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 3|Genesis 13:3-4]]**: "And he journeyed on from the Negeb as far as Bethel to the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Ai, to the place where he had made an altar at the first. And there Abram **called upon the name of the LORD**."

**Restoration After Exile**: Abraham returns to the **place of worship**—the altar at Bethel where he had previously called on God's name ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 8|Genesis 12:8]]). This is **repentance**—returning to the Promised Land, returning to worship, returning to dependence on God.

**The Altar Still Stands**: The altar Abraham built before his failure is still there, waiting for him. This symbolizes God's **patience** and **readiness to restore**. Even after sin, the place of worship remains open.

**Pattern of Restoration**: This establishes the **redemptive cycle**:
1. **Blessing received** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1-3]], 7-8)
2. **Test faced** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 10|Genesis 12:10]])
3. **Sin committed** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 11|Genesis 12:11-16]])
4. **Judgment experienced** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 17|Genesis 12:17-20]])
5. **Restoration granted** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 3|Genesis 13:3-4]])

God does not leave His people in their failure. He **disciplines, exposes, and restores**.

## Christological Contrast: The Faithful Seed

Abraham's failure in Egypt highlights the need for a **faithful Seed** who will not waver:

### Christ's Egypt Experience

Where **Abraham fled to Egypt in fear**, **Christ went to Egypt in obedience**:
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 13|Matthew 2:13-15]]—"Rise, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt... This was to fulfill what the Lord had spoken by the prophet, 'Out of Egypt I called my son'"

Christ's sojourn in Egypt is **redemptive**—He goes not to escape famine but to fulfill prophecy, not in fear but in trust.

### Christ's Rebuke Experience

Where **Abraham was rebuked by Pharaoh**, **Christ rebuked the hypocrites**:
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 13|Matthew 23:13-33]]—Christ exposes the Pharisees' deception with authority and truth
- [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 22|1 Peter 2:22]]—"He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth"

Christ is **never** on the receiving end of a righteous rebuke because He **never sins**. He is the one who rightly exposes others, calling them to repentance.

### Christ's Bride Protection

Where **Abraham endangered Sarai**, **Christ protects the church**:
- [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 25|Ephesians 5:25-27]]—"Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that he might sanctify her... so that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing"
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11, 15]]—"I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep... I lay down my life for the sheep"

Christ does not **save Himself** at the bride's expense. He **sacrifices Himself** for her salvation.

### Christ's Expulsion Experience

Where **Abraham was expelled from Egypt in judgment**, **Christ was expelled from Jerusalem in redemptive suffering**:
- [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12-13]]—"So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood. Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured"

Christ's "expulsion" (crucifixion outside the city walls) is **voluntary suffering** for the sake of His people, the ultimate act of covenant faithfulness.

## Summary: Judgment as Mercy, Failure as Foreshadowing

The judgment on Abraham's sin demonstrates several truths:

1. **Covenant promises depend on God's faithfulness, not human performance**: Abraham fails, but God does not
2. **God jealously protects the seed promise**: Plagues on Pharaoh preserve Sarai's purity
3. **Even pagans can expose covenant people's sin**: Pharaoh's rebuke reveals Abraham's failure
4. **Expulsion can be redemptive**: Abraham's exile returns him to the Promised Land and worship
5. **Wealth gained through sin is tainted**: Abraham's prosperity in Egypt comes with moral cost
6. **Restoration is available after failure**: Abraham returns to the altar and calls on God's name
7. **The pattern foreshadows Israel's exodus**: Personal failure becomes corporate deliverance typology
8. **The need for a faithful Seed is evident**: Abraham's sin points to Christ, who never wavers

**The Cycle Continues**: This intrusion, like those before it, **does not break the pattern** of sin and judgment. Abraham is commissioned, Abraham sins, judgment follows. The cycle persists, pointing forward to the need for One who will perfectly fulfill the commission—**Jesus Christ**, the ultimate Seed of Abraham.

**Kline's Framework**: The intrusion of special grace (Abraham's call) occurs within the common grace world (Pharaoh's Egypt). Special grace **overrides** natural consequences (Abraham should have been destroyed, but God preserves him). Yet the intrusion does not consummate—Abraham is not the final Adam, not the ultimate fulfillment. The **already-but-not-yet** tension remains.

**Beale's Framework**: Abraham's return to the altar ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 3|Genesis 13:3-4]]) is a return to **temple-building**. Though he failed in Egypt (anti-temple behavior), he resumes the commission to expand God's presence. This demonstrates that **God's purposes persist** despite human failure. The temple-expansion project will continue through Abraham's seed, culminating in Christ, the true temple.

**The Gospel Preview**: This passage previews the gospel message:
- **We fail**, but **God remains faithful**
- **We endanger** the promise, but **God protects** it
- **We deserve judgment**, but **God provides mercy**
- **We are exposed** in our sin, but **God restores** us to worship
- **We cannot save ourselves**, but **God sends a Savior**

Abraham's expulsion from Egypt is both **judgment** and **grace**—judgment on his sin, grace in his preservation. This dual reality points to the **cross of Christ**, where God's judgment and mercy meet, where sin is punished and sinners are saved.
