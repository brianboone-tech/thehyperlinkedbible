# 4.1.7.3 - Commission

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7 - Intrusion - The Exodus to Mount Sinai|← Back to 4.1.7]]

## Overview: Israel as Royal Priesthood

The Commission phase of the Exodus intrusion (approximately [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 1|Exodus 19:1]] - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 18|Exodus 24:18]]) presents Israel receiving the **Adamic commission** in renewed form at Mount Sinai. God establishes Israel as a **"kingdom of priests and a holy nation"** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), giving them the mandate to be His image-bearing people who will mediate His presence to the nations and expand His temple-garden glory throughout the earth.

G.K. Beale emphasizes that the Sinai covenant **recapitulates the Adamic commission**: Israel is called to multiply, subdue, serve and guard, and reflect God's image as they expand God's kingdom from the tabernacle (portable Eden) outward to the nations. The giving of the Law defines **how** a redeemed people should live in covenant relationship with their Redeemer, while the tabernacle establishes **where** God will dwell among His people.

The theophany at Sinai ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16-25]]) is the most dramatic **upper-register intrusion** in the Old Testament—thunder, lightning, fire, earthquake, trumpet blast—manifesting eschatological glory in history. Yet Meredith Kline's framework reminds us that this intrusion, though massive, **does not consummate** the age. Israel will immediately fail (golden calf), demonstrating that external law cannot transform the heart. The Sinai commission points forward to the **New Covenant**, when God will write His law on hearts ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]) and send His Spirit ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 27|Ezekiel 36:27]]).

## 1. The Arrival at Sinai

Three months after leaving Egypt, Israel arrives at **Mount Sinai** (also called Horeb), the mountain of God.

### The Setting

"On the third new moon after the people of Israel had gone out of the land of Egypt, on that day they came into the wilderness of Sinai. They set out from Rephidim and came into the wilderness of Sinai, and they encamped in the wilderness. There Israel encamped before the mountain" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 1|Exodus 19:1-2]]).

Sinai is a **sacred mountain**, the place where God had first appeared to Moses in the burning bush ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 1|Exodus 3:1-6]]) and promised, "When you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall serve God on this mountain" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 12|Exodus 3:12]]). Now that promise is fulfilled—Israel has arrived at the mountain to receive God's covenant.

### Moses Ascends

"Moses went up to God, and the LORD called to him out of the mountain, saying, 'Thus you shall say to the house of Jacob, and tell the people of Israel: You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 3|Exodus 19:3-4]]).

The imagery of **eagles' wings** portrays God's tender care and sovereign power ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 32#Deuteronomy 32 . 11|Deuteronomy 32:11]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 40#Isaiah 40 . 31|Isaiah 40:31]]). God has delivered Israel **to Himself**—the ultimate goal is not merely freedom from Egypt but **relationship with God**.

## 2. The Covenant Proposal: Kingdom of Priests

God now proposes a **covenant** with Israel, defining their identity and mission.

### Conditional Blessings

"Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, you shall be my treasured possession among all peoples, for all the earth is mine; and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]).

Key elements of the covenant proposal:

**Conditional**: "**If** you will obey... **then** you shall be..." Blessings depend on covenant faithfulness
**Treasured possession**: Israel will be God's *segullah* (special treasure), set apart from all nations
**Kingdom of priests**: Israel corporately will serve as **mediators** between God and the nations
**Holy nation**: Set apart to God, reflecting His character to the world

### Israel's Adamic Commission

The language of "kingdom of priests and holy nation" **recapitulates the Adamic commission**:

**Image-bearing**: Priests represent God to the people, just as Adam represented God in creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-27]])
**Mediating God's presence**: Priests mediate between God and humanity; Israel will mediate God's presence to the nations
**Temple service**: Priests serve in God's sanctuary; Adam was to "serve and guard" the garden-temple ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]])
**Expanding God's glory**: Israel is to be a light to the nations ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|49:6]]), expanding God's temple-garden kingdom

Beale observes that Israel is called to what **Adam failed to do**: faithfully represent God, expand His presence, and bring the nations into covenant relationship with Yahweh.

### The People's Response

"All the people answered together and said, 'All that the LORD has spoken we will do.' And Moses reported the words of the people to the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 8|Exodus 19:8]]).

Israel's immediate and unanimous acceptance of the covenant seems promising. However, this verbal commitment will be tested almost immediately (golden calf), revealing that **external profession does not guarantee heart obedience**.

## 3. The Theophany: God Descends on Sinai

God prepares the people for the most dramatic **theophany** (visible manifestation of God) in the Old Testament.

### Preparation and Consecration

"And the LORD said to Moses, 'Go to the people and consecrate them today and tomorrow, and let them wash their garments and be ready for the third day. For on the third day the LORD will come down on Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 10|Exodus 19:10-11]]).

The people must **consecrate themselves**—washing garments, abstaining from sexual relations ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 15|Exodus 19:15]]), preparing to encounter the holy God. No one may **touch the mountain** on pain of death ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 12|Exodus 19:12-13]]), establishing God's transcendent holiness.

### The Glory Descends

"On the morning of the third day there were thunders and lightnings and a thick cloud on the mountain and a very loud trumpet blast, so that all the people in the camp trembled. Then Moses brought the people out of the camp to meet God, and they took their stand at the foot of the mountain. Now Mount Sinai was wrapped in smoke because the LORD had descended on it in fire. The smoke of it went up like the smoke of a kiln, and the whole mountain trembled greatly" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16-18]]).

The theophanic elements:

**Thunder and lightning**: Divine glory breaking through ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 18#Psalm 18 . 13|Psalm 18:13-14]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 4#Revelation 4 . 5|Revelation 4:5]])
**Thick cloud**: God's presence veiled in glory ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 15|Exodus 24:15-18]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-12]])
**Trumpet blast**: Eschatological signal ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 27#Isaiah 27 . 13|Isaiah 27:13]]; [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 52|1 Corinthians 15:52]]; [[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 4#1 Thessalonians 4 . 16|1 Thessalonians 4:16]])
**Fire**: Consuming holiness of God ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 4#Deuteronomy 4 . 24|Deuteronomy 4:24]]—"the LORD your God is a consuming fire")
**Earthquake**: Creation trembles before the Creator ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 68#Psalm 68 . 7|Psalm 68:7-8]]; [[Readable Bible/35 - Habakkuk/Habakkuk 3#Habakkuk 3 . 6|Habakkuk 3:6]])

Kline identifies this as **upper-register intrusion**—the glory of the Consummation age breaking into the Common Grace period. This is a preview of the **Day of the Lord**, when God will descend to judge and renew the earth.

### The People's Terror

The people are **terrified** by God's holiness and ask Moses to mediate: "You speak to us, and we will listen; but do not let God speak to us, lest we die" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 19|Exodus 20:19]]). This establishes the need for a **mediator** between God and humanity, a role Moses fills imperfectly and Christ fulfills perfectly ([[Readable Bible/54 - 1 Timothy/1 Timothy 2#1 Timothy 2 . 5|1 Timothy 2:5]]).

Hebrews contrasts the terror of Sinai with the welcoming glory of the heavenly Zion ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 18|Hebrews 12:18-24]]), showing that Christ's mediation brings us into God's presence with confidence rather than fear.

## 4. The Ten Commandments: Covenant Stipulations

God gives the **Ten Commandments** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 1|Exodus 20:1-17]]), also called the Decalogue, as the **core stipulations** of the covenant.

### Preamble: Grace Before Law

"I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 2|Exodus 20:2]]).

The Decalogue begins with **gospel** before law—God identifies Himself as the Redeemer who has already delivered Israel. The Law is given to a people already saved by grace, not as a means of earning salvation but as the **pattern for covenant life**.

### First Table: Duties to God (Commandments 1-4)

**No other gods** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 3|Exodus 20:3]]): Exclusive worship of Yahweh
**No idols** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 4|Exodus 20:4-6]]): No images or representations of God
**Do not take God's name in vain** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 7|Exodus 20:7]]): Honor God's name and reputation
**Remember the Sabbath** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 8|Exodus 20:8-11]]): Set apart the seventh day as holy to the Lord

The first table establishes **vertical relationship** with God—worship, reverence, rest.

### Second Table: Duties to Neighbor (Commandments 5-10)

**Honor parents** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 12|Exodus 20:12]]): Respect for God-given authority
**Do not murder** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 13|Exodus 20:13]]): Sanctity of human life
**Do not commit adultery** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 14|Exodus 20:14]]): Sanctity of marriage
**Do not steal** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 15|Exodus 20:15]]): Respect for property
**Do not bear false witness** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 16|Exodus 20:16]]): Truthfulness in community
**Do not covet** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 17|Exodus 20:17]]): Internal contentment, not just external compliance

The second table establishes **horizontal relationships**—family, life, marriage, property, truth, heart attitudes.

### Jesus' Summary

Jesus will summarize the Ten Commandments as **love for God** (first table) and **love for neighbor** (second table) ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 37|Matthew 22:37-40]]), demonstrating their abiding relevance.

## 5. The Book of the Covenant: Case Laws

Following the Ten Commandments, God gives additional **case laws** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 22|Exodus 20:22]]-[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 33|23:33]]), often called the "Book of the Covenant," which apply the principles of the Decalogue to specific situations.

### Categories of Law

The case laws cover:

**Worship regulations** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 22|Exodus 20:22-26]]): Altar construction, proper worship
**Slavery and servitude** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 21#Exodus 21 . 1|Exodus 21:1-11]]): Treatment of Hebrew servants
**Personal injury** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 21#Exodus 21 . 12|Exodus 21:12-36]]): Eye for eye, tooth for tooth—proportional justice
**Property laws** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 22#Exodus 22 . 1|Exodus 22:1-15]]): Restitution for theft or damage
**Social and moral laws** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 22#Exodus 22 . 16|Exodus 22:16]]-[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 9|23:9]]): Justice for the vulnerable, prohibition of sorcery
**Sabbath and festival laws** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 10|Exodus 23:10-19]]): Sabbath year, three annual feasts
**Promises and warnings** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 20|Exodus 23:20-33]]): God's angel will lead them, but they must remain faithful

### Theological Principles

The case laws reveal God's character:

**Justice**: Proportional punishment, no partiality
**Compassion**: Special concern for widows, orphans, foreigners ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 22#Exodus 22 . 21|Exodus 22:21-24]])
**Holiness**: Separation from pagan practices
**Sabbath rest**: Even the land gets rest ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 10|Exodus 23:10-11]])

These laws demonstrate that **all of life** is under God's lordship—worship, work, family, property, justice.

## 6. Covenant Ratification: Blood Ceremony

Moses reads the Book of the Covenant to the people, and they enter into a **blood covenant** with God.

### The People's Commitment

"Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read it in the hearing of the people. And they said, 'All that the LORD has spoken we will do, and we will be obedient'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 7|Exodus 24:7]]).

For the second time, Israel verbally commits to obey the covenant ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 8|Exodus 19:8]]; [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 7|24:7]]). Their double affirmation emphasizes their accountability—they have heard, understood, and agreed.

### The Blood of the Covenant

"And Moses took the blood and threw it on the people and said, 'Behold the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 8|Exodus 24:8]]).

The **sprinkling of blood** on the people seals the covenant. Half the blood was thrown on the altar ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 6|Exodus 24:6]]), half on the people—symbolizing the **union** between God and Israel through the covenant.

This ceremony prefigures the **New Covenant** ratified by Christ's blood:

**Jesus' words at the Last Supper**: "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 28|Matthew 26:28]])
**Hebrews' interpretation**: "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 22|Hebrews 9:22]])
**Christ as mediator**: Jesus mediates a **better covenant** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 6|Hebrews 8:6]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 15|9:15]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|12:24]])

### Covenant Meal

After the blood ceremony, Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu, and seventy elders ascend the mountain and **eat and drink** in God's presence ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 9|Exodus 24:9-11]]). They "saw the God of Israel" and "beheld God, and ate and drank"—a covenant meal in the presence of God, prefiguring the **messianic banquet** ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 6|Isaiah 25:6-9]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 8#Matthew 8 . 11|Matthew 8:11]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 9|Revelation 19:9]]).

## 7. The Tabernacle: God's Dwelling Place

God now gives detailed instructions for building the **tabernacle** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 1|Exodus 25-31]]), a portable sanctuary where He will dwell among His people.

### The Purpose

"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]).

God's ultimate goal is **presence**, not mere rules. The tabernacle reverses the curse of exile from Eden—God will once again dwell with humanity.

### Tabernacle as Portable Eden

Beale catalogs extensive parallels between the tabernacle and the **garden of Eden**:

**Eastward orientation**: Both face east ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]; [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 22|Exodus 26:22-30]])
**Cherubim guardians**: Cherubim guard Eden ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]), embroidered on tabernacle curtains ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 1|Exodus 26:1]], [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|31]])
**Lampstand as tree of life**: Seven-branched menorah ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 31|Exodus 25:31-40]]) with almond blossoms
**Gold and precious stones**: Eden rich in gold and gems ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 11|Genesis 2:11-12]]), as is the tabernacle
**Priestly garments**: Aaron's robe has pomegranates ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 33|Exodus 28:33-34]]), fruit imagery from Eden
**Seven-day creation**: Tabernacle construction described in seven speeches ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 1|Exodus 25-31]]), echoing Genesis 1
**God "sees" and blesses**: Like creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 31|Genesis 1:31]]), Moses "sees" the completed work ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 39#Exodus 39 . 43|Exodus 39:43]])

The tabernacle is **Eden restored**, a microcosm of the cosmic temple.

### Structure and Symbolism

The tabernacle has three areas corresponding to creation's structure:

**Outer Court**: The world, where sacrifices are offered
**Holy Place**: Eden, where priests minister before the lampstand, table, and incense altar
**Most Holy Place (Holy of Holies)**: God's throne room, the heavenly sanctuary

Only the High Priest enters the Most Holy Place, once a year on the Day of Atonement ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 1|Leviticus 16]]), demonstrating humanity's ongoing **exile from God's full presence**.

### Priesthood and Sacrifices

God appoints **Aaron and his sons** as priests ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 1|Exodus 28:1]]), who will mediate between God and Israel through **sacrifices and offerings**. The entire sacrificial system points forward to Christ as the **final High Priest** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 14|Hebrews 4:14-5:10]]) and **perfect sacrifice** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 11|Hebrews 9:11-14]]).

## 8. Typological Fulfillment in Christ and the Church

The Commission given at Sinai finds its ultimate fulfillment in **Christ and His church**.

### Jesus as True Israel

Jesus perfectly fulfills what Israel failed to do—He obeys the Law completely ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 17|Matthew 5:17]]), mediates the New Covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 15|Hebrews 9:15]]), and establishes God's kingdom.

### The Church as Royal Priesthood

Peter applies Exodus 19:6 to the **church**: "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).

The church inherits Israel's Adamic commission:

**Royal priesthood**: Believers serve as priests ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 6|Revelation 1:6]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 10|5:10]])
**Holy nation**: Set apart to God, reflecting His character
**Proclaiming God's excellencies**: Witness to the nations ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]])

### Christ as Tabernacle

John declares, "The Word became flesh and **dwelt** [literally 'tabernacled'] among us, and we have seen his glory" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). Jesus is the **true temple**, the place where God's glory dwells ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]).

### Believers as Temple

Paul teaches that **believers** are the temple of the Holy Spirit ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16|1 Corinthians 3:16-17]]; [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 19|6:19]]; [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 16|2 Corinthians 6:16]]), and the **church** is being built into a spiritual house ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 19|Ephesians 2:19-22]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 5|1 Peter 2:5]]).

### New Jerusalem as Final Temple

Revelation presents the **New Jerusalem** as the consummate temple-city-garden ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-22:5]]), where God dwells fully with His people forever. There is **no temple** in the New Jerusalem because "its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22|Revelation 21:22]])—God's presence fills all.

---

## Conclusion: Commissioned but Not Yet Consummated

The Commission phase at Sinai establishes Israel as God's **covenant people** with a renewed Adamic mandate: to be a kingdom of priests and a holy nation, to dwell with God in His tabernacle-temple, and to mediate His presence to the nations. The theophany at Sinai demonstrates God's awesome holiness and the seriousness of covenant relationship.

Yet this intrusion, though massive, **does not consummate** the age. Israel will immediately break the covenant (golden calf), revealing that **external law cannot transform the heart**. The Sinai covenant points forward to the **New Covenant** mediated by Christ, who perfectly obeys the Law, offers the final sacrifice, and sends the Holy Spirit to write God's law on hearts.

The church now stands as the **new Israel**, commissioned to be a royal priesthood proclaiming God's excellencies to the nations, indwelt by the Spirit, awaiting the final consummation when God will dwell with His people in the New Jerusalem forever. The Commission given at Sinai finds its ultimate fulfillment in Christ and will be fully realized in the new creation.
