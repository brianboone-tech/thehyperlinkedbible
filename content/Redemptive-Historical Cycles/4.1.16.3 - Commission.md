### 4.1.16.3 - Commission

**Biblical Context**: Be Faithful in Babylon, Await Promised Return, Hope in New Covenant (586-538 BC)

**Key Passages**: [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 4|Jeremiah 29:4-14]], [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 16|Ezekiel 3:16-21]], [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 1#Daniel 1 . 8|Daniel 1:8-21]], [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 6#Daniel 6 . 10|Daniel 6:10-23]]

---

#### Theological Framework: Commission in Exile

The Commission dimension of Israel's exile presents a profound paradox: how does a community that has failed its original mandate receive renewed commission? How do exiles, stripped of land, temple, and kingdom, fulfill God's purposes? How does a people experiencing covenant death become witnesses to covenant life?

The answer lies in the Intrusion pattern that defines this exile cycle. The Commission in exile is not organically developed from Israel's resources or circumstances but intrusively given through divine revelation, prophetic word, and providential preservation. God commissions the exiles not despite their failure but precisely through their experience of judgment and hope of restoration.

**The Nature of Exilic Commission**

Unlike earlier commissions (Adam to fill the earth, Abraham to be a blessing to nations, Israel at Sinai to be a kingdom of priests), the exilic commission operates in contexts of apparent divine absence and covenant termination:

1. **No Temple**: The central sanctuary, the place of God's name and presence, lies in ruins. Commission must function without the mediation of sacrificial worship.

2. **No Land**: The promised land, the arena for covenant obedience, is occupied by foreigners. Commission must operate in pagan territory.

3. **No Kingdom**: The Davidic dynasty, meant to mediate God's rule, is terminated. Commission must function without political power.

4. **No Autonomy**: Israel exists as captives and subjects of Babylon. Commission must operate under foreign domination.

Yet precisely in this stripped-down condition, the essential nature of commission becomes clear: faithful witness to Yahweh regardless of circumstances. The exilic commission reduces to fundamentals—maintain covenant identity, worship the true God, await promised restoration, witness to pagan nations. This is commission purified to its essence.

**Beale's Framework**: The exilic commission recapitulates Adam's commission but in reverse order. Where Adam was commissioned in Eden and failed leading to expulsion, Israel is expelled and then commissioned in exile to maintain hope of return. The commission is to preserve Adamic identity and mission even outside Eden, to remain God's image-bearers and witnesses even in Babylon, to maintain the temple-building project in hearts and communities even without physical temple.

**Kline's Framework**: The commission operates as upper-register intrusion into lower-register exile. The prophetic word comes from the divine realm, creating mission in contexts that naturally produce despair. The visions of restoration function as commissions—they don't merely predict future return but commission present faithfulness. The upper register's revelatory presence creates lower-register obedience.

**Jeremiah's Letter: Seek Babylon's Welfare**

[[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 4|Jeremiah 29:4-14]] contains the most counterintuitive commission in Scripture. Writing to the exiles in Babylon, Jeremiah conveys Yahweh's command: "Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat their produce. Take wives and have sons and daughters... multiply there, and do not decrease. But seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the LORD on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 5|Jeremiah 29:5-7]]).

**Commission Elements**:

1. **Settle In**: "Build houses... plant gardens." The exiles are not to adopt a posture of temporary encampment awaiting immediate return. They are commissioned to establish normal life in Babylon—housing, agriculture, families.

2. **Multiply**: "Have sons and daughters... multiply there, and do not decrease." The creation mandate ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]—"Be fruitful and multiply") continues in exile. Even in Babylon, Israel is to maintain demographic viability, preserving the covenant community through generations.

3. **Seek Babylon's Welfare**: The Hebrew shalom (welfare/peace) appears twice—"seek the welfare of the city... in its welfare you will find your welfare." This is radical: Israel is commissioned to work for the flourishing of the very empire that conquered them, destroyed their temple, and holds them captive.

4. **Pray for Babylon**: "Pray to the LORD on its behalf." Intercessory prayer for Babylon fulfills Abraham's commission to be a blessing to nations ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]), but now from a position of subjugation rather than strength.

5. **Await God's Timing**: "For thus says the LORD: When seventy years are completed for Babylon, I will visit you, and I will fulfill to you my promise and bring you back to this place" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 10|Jeremiah 29:10]]). The commission includes patient waiting—not passive resignation but active faithfulness while awaiting God's promised restoration.

**Theological Significance**:

**Creational Continuity**: The commission to multiply and seek shalom maintains creation ordinances even in exile. Marriage, family, agriculture, urban welfare—these creation structures continue to function as arenas for covenant faithfulness. Exile hasn't suspended creation or humanity's basic commissions within it.

**Missionary Preparation**: By seeking Babylon's welfare, Israel learns to function as blessing to the nations even from a position of weakness. This prepares for the ultimate missionary commission when Israel's greater Son will send disciples to all nations ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19-20]]). The exilic commission is proto-missionary: maintain faith in pagan context, witness through distinctive life, pray for enemies.

**Temporal Structuring**: The 70-year timeframe creates temporal order, transforming indefinite exile into limited judgment. This structures hope—the exile is severe but not permanent, just but not ultimate. The commission includes waiting faithfully through these numbered years.

**Providence Recognition**: "The city where I have sent you into exile" acknowledges that even Babylonian conquest operates under divine sovereignty. The commission presupposes that exile itself is God's act, not merely human politics. This transforms how exiles understand their circumstances—they are not random victims but covenant people experiencing prophesied judgment with promised end.

**Contrast with False Prophets**: Jeremiah's letter explicitly contradicts false prophets promising immediate return ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 8|Jeremiah 29:8-9]]). The true commission rejects wishful thinking in favor of patient obedience. False commission offers cheap hope; true commission requires costly faithfulness.

**Prophetic Commission: Ezekiel as Watchman**

Ezekiel receives explicit prophetic commission in exile: "Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel. Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 17|Ezekiel 3:17]]).

**Watchman Metaphor**: The watchman stands on the wall, seeing approaching danger before others, sounding the alarm to warn the city. Ezekiel's commission is to speak God's word whether people listen or not, warning of judgment and promising restoration.

**Commission Elements**:

1. **Speak God's Word**: "Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 17|Ezekiel 3:17]]). The prophet's authority derives entirely from received revelation. He is commissioned to speak not his opinions but God's word.

2. **Individual Responsibility**: "If I say to the wicked, 'You shall surely die,' and you give him no warning... his blood I will require at your hand" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 18|Ezekiel 3:18]]). The commission includes personal accountability—failure to speak incurs guilt.

3. **Audience Responsibility**: "But if you warn the wicked, and he does not turn from his wickedness... he shall die in his iniquity, but you will have delivered your soul" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 19|Ezekiel 3:19]]). The hearer's response determines their fate, not the prophet's eloquence.

4. **Persistent Proclamation**: Whether people "hear or refuse to hear (for they are a rebellious house)" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 11|Ezekiel 3:11]]), Ezekiel must speak. Commission is to faithfulness in proclamation, not to results.

5. **Sign-Acts**: Ezekiel is commissioned to perform symbolic acts—lying on his side for 390 days ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 4#Ezekiel 4 . 4|Ezekiel 4:4-8]]), eating rationed food ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 4#Ezekiel 4 . 9|Ezekiel 4:9-17]]), shaving his head and burning the hair ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 5#Ezekiel 5 . 1|Ezekiel 5:1-4]]). These dramatize coming judgment and serve as prophetic witness.

**Theological Significance**:

**Word-Centrality**: In exile without temple sacrifices, God's word becomes central. The prophetic commission establishes that revelation continues, that God still speaks, that his word creates and sustains his people even when visible institutions collapse.

**Individual Accountability**: Ezekiel 18 and 33 emphasize individual responsibility—each person answers for their own sin, righteousness is not transferable, repentance is always possible. This commission creates space for personal faith in contexts where corporate structures have failed.

**Visual Proclamation**: The sign-acts demonstrate that proclamation includes embodied witness. Ezekiel's entire life—his actions, his silence ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 3#Ezekiel 3 . 26|Ezekiel 3:26-27]]), his wife's death ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 24#Ezekiel 24 . 15|Ezekiel 24:15-24]])—becomes prophetic message. The commission is not merely verbal but existential.

**Suffering Servant Trajectory**: Ezekiel's commissioned suffering (lying immobile, eating defiled food, losing his wife) anticipates the Suffering Servant's commission to bear Israel's sins ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 13|Isaiah 52:13-53]]:12). Prophetic commission includes vicarious suffering that prefigures Christ's ultimate commission to bear humanity's sin.

**Priestly Commission: Maintain Holiness**

Though the temple is destroyed, the priestly commission to maintain holiness continues:

1. **Dietary Laws**: Daniel and his friends refuse the king's food, maintaining kashrut ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 1#Daniel 1 . 8|Daniel 1:8-16]]). This preserves covenant distinctiveness in pagan court.

2. **Sabbath Observance**: The exile enforces land Sabbaths Israel failed to observe ([[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 36#2 Chronicles 36 . 21|2 Chronicles 36:21]]), and personal Sabbath observance becomes marker of covenant identity.

3. **Circumcision**: Physical circumcision continues as covenant sign, distinguishing Israelites from Babylonians.

4. **Ritual Purity**: Though temple sacrifices are impossible, purity concerns continue—contact with unclean things, sexual ethics, avoidance of idolatry.

**Theological Significance**: These practices constitute commission to holiness—being set apart as Yahweh's people even in unclean land. Leviticus 18-20's call to holiness ("Be holy, for I am holy") continues to structure Israelite life in exile.

**Beale's Framework**: Priestly commission in exile maintains the sanctuary-building project at the community level. Each household becomes a micro-sanctuary, each family preserving holiness, each generation transmitting covenant identity. The commission is to be temple-people even without temple-place.

**Royal Commission: Daniel in Babylon's Court**

Daniel and his friends receive commission to serve in Babylon's royal court while maintaining covenant faithfulness. This represents royal commission in exile—exercising dominion not through political independence but through faithful service under foreign rule.

**Commission Elements**:

1. **Excellence in Service**: Daniel serves Babylonian and Persian kings with exceptional competence. He interprets dreams, administers provinces, counsels rulers. The commission includes diligent service to earthly authorities ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 13#Romans 13 . 1|Romans 13:1-7]] principle applied in exile).

2. **Wisdom and Understanding**: "In every matter of wisdom and understanding about which the king inquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters that were in all his kingdom" ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 1#Daniel 1 . 20|Daniel 1:20]]). The commission includes cultivating excellence that testifies to God's blessing.

3. **Unwavering Worship**: Despite serving pagan kings, Daniel maintains exclusive worship of Yahweh. He prays three times daily toward Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 6#Daniel 6 . 10|Daniel 6:10]]), refuses food offered to idols ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 1#Daniel 1 . 8|Daniel 1:8]]), refuses to worship imperial images ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 3#Daniel 3 . 12|Daniel 3:12-18]]).

4. **Bold Witness**: Daniel interprets dreams announcing Babylon's fall and God's eternal kingdom (Daniel 2, 4, 5). The commission includes prophetic witness even to emperors.

5. **Providential Preservation**: God delivers Daniel from lions and his friends from the furnace (Daniel 3, 6). The commission includes trust that God will preserve the faithful, though not exemption from trial.

**Theological Significance**:

**Joseph Typology**: Daniel in Babylon's court recapitulates Joseph in Egypt—faithful Israelite serving foreign power, interpreting dreams, administering empire, preserving covenant people. The commission is similar: exercise wisdom and maintain witness in pagan context.

**Adamic Dominion**: Daniel's administrative competence reflects Adam's original commission to exercise dominion ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Even in exile, under foreign rule, covenant people are commissioned to pursue excellence and order, reflecting God's image through competent stewardship.

**Eschatological Witness**: Daniel's proclamation of God's eternal kingdom to pagan emperors anticipates the gospel going to all nations. The commission includes announcing that earthly empires are temporary but God's kingdom is eternal ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 2#Daniel 2 . 44|Daniel 2:44]], 7:13-14).

**Community Commission: Preserve Covenant Identity**

The exilic community receives corporate commission to preserve covenant identity across generations:

1. **Torah Transmission**: "You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 7|Deuteronomy 6:7]]). This commission becomes critical in exile where institutional structures are absent.

2. **Narrative Memory**: Psalm 137 reveals the tension: "How shall we sing the LORD's song in a foreign land?" Yet the psalm itself preserves memory: "If I forget you, O Jerusalem, let my right hand forget its skill!" The commission includes maintaining historical memory, covenant narrative, theological identity.

3. **Synagogue Development**: Though not explicitly documented in exilic texts, synagogues likely developed during exile as community gathering places for prayer, Torah reading, and instruction—decentralized worship replacing the centralized temple.

4. **Scribal Activity**: The exile period saw extensive scribal activity—collecting, editing, and preserving sacred texts. This commission to preserve God's written word becomes crucial for post-exilic Judaism.

5. **Intermarriage Resistance**: The commission includes maintaining covenant boundaries by avoiding intermarriage with pagans, preserving distinct identity across generations in exile.

**Theological Significance**:

**Word Over Place**: With temple destroyed, God's word becomes the centering reality. The commission shifts from place-centered worship (Jerusalem temple) to word-centered worship (Torah reading, teaching, memory). This prepares for Christianity's even more radical decentralization.

**Generational Faithfulness**: The 70-year exile means some who enter will not return. The commission includes faithfulness not just for oneself but for future generations who will experience the return. This is commission to long-obedience in the same direction.

**Text-Centeredness**: Scribal preservation of texts creates the possibility of Judaism and Christianity as religions of the Book. The exilic commission to preserve, copy, teach, and transmit texts establishes patterns that continue through church history.

**Anticipated Return: Commission Toward Restoration**

The exilic commission is not merely to survive but to prepare for promised return:

1. **Maintain Hope**: [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 29#Jeremiah 29 . 11|Jeremiah 29:11]] promises: "For I know the plans I have for you, declares the LORD, plans for welfare and not for evil, to give you a future and a hope." The commission includes hope cultivation—remembering promises, trusting divine faithfulness, anticipating restoration.

2. **Prepare Hearts**: [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]]'s promise of new hearts and Spirit indwelling commissions the exiles to long for spiritual transformation, not just geographical return. The commission is toward internal preparation for new covenant realities.

3. **Await Messiah**: Daniel's seventy-weeks prophecy ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 9#Daniel 9 . 24|Daniel 9:24-27]]) structures hope toward Messiah's coming. The commission includes watching for God's anointed who will accomplish ultimate restoration.

4. **Corporate Repentance**: [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 9#Daniel 9 . 4|Daniel 9:4-19]] models corporate confession and intercession. The commission includes acknowledging sin, confessing covenant failure, pleading for divine mercy based on God's character rather than human merit.

5. **Vision-Shaped Life**: Ezekiel's temple vision (chapters 40-48) and new covenant promises commission the exiles to live in light of promised future. Though not yet realized, the vision shapes present faithfulness.

**Theological Significance**:

**Eschatological Orientation**: The exilic commission is fundamentally eschatological—living in light of promised but not-yet-realized restoration. This establishes pattern for Christian life between Christ's advents—already/not-yet, present exile/future glory.

**Repentance Commission**: The call to corporate repentance (Daniel 9, Nehemiah 9, Ezra 9) demonstrates that restoration requires acknowledging failure. The commission includes truth-telling about sin, not self-justification or blame-shifting.

**Promise-Trust**: The commission rests entirely on trusting God's promises, not visible circumstances. This is faith commission—believing God will accomplish what he has spoken despite all evidence to the contrary.

**Christological Fulfillment: The Ultimate Commission**

All exilic commissions find fulfillment in Christ and extension in his commission to the church:

1. **Jesus' Exile and Commission**: Christ bears ultimate exile ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]]—"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?") and receives ultimate commission to bring many sons to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]). He is faithful Israel who maintains perfect obedience in the wilderness of temptation ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 1|Matthew 4:1-11]]).

2. **Spirit Indwelling**: The promised new heart and Spirit ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]], [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]) is fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2), commissioning the church for witness through Spirit-empowerment.

3. **Royal-Prophetic-Priestly Commission**: Christ fulfills all three offices, and commissions his church to prophetic witness (proclamation), priestly service (intercession, spiritual sacrifices), and royal dominion (disciple-making among nations).

4. **Great Commission**: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]]) fulfills Abraham's commission to bless nations, Israel's commission to be light to Gentiles, and the exiles' commission to witness to Babylon. The church's commission includes the same elements: faithful witness in exile ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 1|1 Peter 1:1]]), excellence in service, maintaining holiness, seeking the city's welfare.

5. **Already/Not-Yet Commission**: Like the exiles, the church lives between promise and fulfillment—Christ has come (exile ended) but consummation awaits (full restoration future). The commission is to faithful presence in "Babylon" while awaiting New Jerusalem (Revelation 21-22).

**Synthesis: Commission Through Intrusion**

The exilic commission operates entirely through upper-register intrusion:

1. **Prophetic Word**: Jeremiah's letter, Ezekiel's watchman charge, Daniel's visions—all come as revealed word creating mission.

2. **Visionary Hope**: The new temple, new heart, new covenant, Son of Man kingdom—these visions commission present faithfulness by revealing future certainties.

3. **Providential Preservation**: God's preservation of Daniel, the three friends, the marked remnant (Ezekiel 9)—these demonstrate divine faithfulness that commissions continued trust.

4. **Temporal Structuring**: The 70 years, the seventy weeks—these divine time-structures commission patience and hope.

The commission is not generated from exilic circumstances (which produce only despair) but intrusively given from the divine realm. This is commission-by-revelation, mission-by-vision, obedience-through-trust in upper-register promises despite lower-register chaos.

**Conclusion: Faithful Presence in Exile**

The exilic commission reduces to essence: maintain covenant identity, worship the true God, witness to the nations, await promised restoration, trust divine timing. It is commission for faithful presence—living distinctly as God's people in contexts not of their choosing, serving excellently in circumstances not ideal, hoping firmly in promises not yet fulfilled.

This pattern becomes paradigmatic for all God's people living between the advents—commissioned to faithful witness in "exile," maintaining holiness in "Babylon," serving earthly authorities while worshiping the heavenly King, awaiting the return that will bring ultimate restoration.

The commission is neither to escape exile nor to embrace it fully, but to live faithfully within it—seeking the welfare of the city while never forgetting Jerusalem, serving pagan kings while praying toward God's temple, maintaining covenant identity while blessing the nations, enduring the numbered years while trusting the promised end.

This is commission through intrusion—divine word creating mission in contexts of human failure, revealed hope generating present faithfulness, upper-register promises producing lower-register obedience. It is commission not earned through success but given through grace, not merited through achievement but received through revelation, not generated from circumstances but intrusively bestowed from the throne-room of the sovereign God.
