##### 4.1.15.5 - Judgment

**The Final Reckoning: Vindication of the Righteous, Destruction of the Wicked**

The Wisdom Literature insists that despite present moral ambiguity, God will ultimately judge all humanity, vindicating the righteous and destroying the wicked. [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 14|Ecclesiastes 12:14]] concludes the Preacher's investigation: "For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every secret thing, whether good or evil." [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 1|Psalm 1]] contrasts the righteous and wicked, declaring the wicked "will not stand in the judgment" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 5|verse 5]]). [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 21|Proverbs 11:21]] asserts: "Be assured, an evil person will not go unpunished, but the offspring of the righteous will be delivered." [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 1|Job's]] restoration demonstrates God's vindication of the innocent sufferer, while numerous psalms celebrate divine judgment that overthrows wickedness and establishes justice.

**Ecclesiastes: The Final Accounting**

Despite cataloging life's vanities under the sun, [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 1#Ecclesiastes 1 . 1|Ecclesiastes]] repeatedly points to eschatological judgment as the resolution to moral chaos. [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 3#Ecclesiastes 3 . 17|Ecclesiastes 3:17]] declares: "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every matter and for every work." Where human justice fails, divine judgment will prevail.

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 8#Ecclesiastes 8 . 12|Ecclesiastes 8:12-13]] expresses confidence despite present anomalies: "Though a sinner does evil a hundred times and prolongs his life, yet I know that it will be well with those who fear God, because they fear before him, but it will not be well with the wicked, neither will he prolong his days like a shadow, because he does not fear before God." Final judgment, not present circumstances, determines ultimate fate.

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 11#Ecclesiastes 11 . 9|Ecclesiastes 11:9]] frames youthful joy with accountability: "Rejoice, O young man, in your youth, and let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth. Walk in the ways of your heart and the sight of your eyes. But know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment." Freedom exists under divine oversight; all actions will be evaluated.

The climactic statement comes in [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 13|Ecclesiastes 12:13-14]]: "The end of the matter; all has been heard. Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every secret thing, whether good or evil." No action, thought, or word escapes divine scrutiny. Hidden sins will be exposed; concealed righteousness will be revealed. This is the ultimate answer to life's vanity—transcendent judgment that gives eternal meaning to temporal choices.

Kline emphasizes that Ecclesiastes operates within the tension of Common Grace: present experience appears meaningless because immediate retribution is lacking, but eschatological judgment vindicates God's justice. The "under the sun" perspective is incomplete; one must look "above the sun" to final judgment for resolution.

**Psalm 1: The Two Ways and Their Ends**

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 1|Psalm 1]] functions as the Psalter's introduction, contrasting the righteous and wicked's destinies. The blessed person delights in God's Torah and meditates on it continually ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 2|verse 2]]), becoming like a tree planted by water streams that yields fruit and doesn't wither ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 3|verse 3]]). This images stability, life, and prosperity aligned with creation order.

By contrast, the wicked are like chaff driven by wind ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 4|verse 4]])—weightless, unstable, blown away. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 1#Psalm 1 . 5|Psalm 1:5-6]] announces judgment: "Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the congregation of the righteous; for the LORD knows the way of the righteous, but the way of the wicked will perish."

"Will not stand in the judgment" means they cannot endure God's scrutiny, will be exposed and condemned. The righteous are "known" by God—intimately, covenantally, approvingly. The wicked's way "perishes"—leads to destruction, ends in death. This binary framework governs the Psalter and Wisdom Literature: two paths, two destinies, determined by relationship with God and His Torah.

Beale notes this anticipates Jesus' teaching on the narrow and broad gates ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 7#Matthew 7 . 13|Matthew 7:13-14]]), the sheep and goats judgment ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 31|Matthew 25:31-46]]), and Paul's doctrine that God will render to each according to his works ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 6|Romans 2:6-11]]).

**Psalm 37: The Wicked Cut Off, the Righteous Inherit**

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 1|Psalm 37]] repeatedly asserts that evildoers will be cut off while the righteous will inherit the land—a theme appearing at least nine times (verses 9, 22, 28, 29, 34, 38). [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 9|Psalm 37:9-11]] declares: "For the evildoers shall be cut off, but those who wait for the LORD shall inherit the land. In just a little while, the wicked will be no more; though you look carefully at his place, he will not be there. But the meek shall inherit the land and delight themselves in abundant peace."

Jesus quotes [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 11|Psalm 37:11]] in the Beatitudes: "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 5|Matthew 5:5]]), extending the promise to the new creation.

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 12|Psalm 37:12-15]] describes the wicked plotting and gnashing teeth against the righteous, but "the Lord laughs at the wicked, for he sees that his day is coming." Their swords will enter their own hearts; their bows will be broken. Divine judgment reverses the oppressor's weapons against themselves.

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 20|Psalm 37:20]] announces: "But the wicked will perish; the enemies of the LORD are like the glory of the pastures; they vanish—like smoke they vanish away." This is total destruction—not temporary setback but final eradication. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 35|Psalm 37:35-36]] testifies: "I have seen a wicked, ruthless man, spreading himself like a green laurel tree. But he passed away, and behold, he was no more; though I sought him, he could not be found."

The psalm concludes with confidence: "Observe the blameless and behold the upright, for there is a future for the man of peace. But transgressors shall be altogether destroyed; the future of the wicked shall be cut off" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 37|verse 37-38]]). Future (*acharit*) is the decisive word—ultimate outcome, eschatological destiny. Present circumstances don't reveal the end; judgment will.

**Psalm 73: From Envy to Eschatological Perspective**

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 1|Psalm 73]] records Asaph's journey from near apostasy to renewed confidence through perceiving the wicked's end. After describing their present prosperity ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 3|verse 3-12]]) and questioning his own righteousness ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 13|verse 13-14]]), he enters God's sanctuary and perceives their end (*acharit*, [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 17|verse 17]]).

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 18|Psalm 73:18-20]] announces judgment: "Truly you set them in slippery places; you make them fall to ruin. How they are destroyed in a moment, swept away utterly by terrors! Like a dream when one awakes, O Lord, when you rouse yourself, you despise them as phantoms." Their prosperity is illusory, temporary, a dream that vanishes upon waking. God's awakening to judgment exposes their emptiness.

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 27|Psalm 73:27]] declares: "For behold, those who are far from you shall perish; you put an end to everyone who is unfaithful to you." Spatial distance from God (far from you) correlates with covenantal infidelity (unfaithful), resulting in perishing—eschatological destruction.

By contrast, [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 24|Psalm 73:24-26]] expresses confidence in the righteous's future: "You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will receive me to glory. Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever." Even if the righteous die (flesh and heart fail), they have eternal portion in God—implying resurrection hope.

Beale emphasizes that Psalm 73 presents one of the Old Testament's clearest expressions of resurrection faith, recognizing that vindication must extend beyond death if God's justice is to be fully manifest.

**Proverbs on the Wicked's Downfall**

Proverbs repeatedly asserts that wickedness leads to judgment and destruction. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 3|Proverbs 10:3]] contrasts: "The LORD does not let the righteous go hungry, but he thwarts the craving of the wicked." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 24|Proverbs 10:24-25]] declares: "What the wicked dreads will come upon him, but the desire of the righteous will be granted. When the tempest passes, the wicked is no more, but the righteous is established forever."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 27|Proverbs 10:27-30]] contrasts lifespans and security: "The fear of the LORD prolongs life, but the years of the wicked will be short. The hope of the righteous brings joy, but the expectation of the wicked will perish. The way of the LORD is a stronghold to the blameless, but destruction to evildoers. The righteous will never be removed, but the wicked will not dwell in the land."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 5|Proverbs 11:5-8]] presents the two paths: "The righteousness of the blameless keeps his way straight, but the wicked falls by his own wickedness... When the wicked dies, his hope will perish, and the expectation of wealth perishes too. The righteous is delivered from trouble, and the wicked walks into it instead."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 21|Proverbs 11:21]] gives strong assurance: "Be assured, an evil person will not go unpunished, but the offspring of the righteous will be delivered." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 31|Proverbs 11:31]] states: "If the righteous is repaid on earth, how much more the wicked and the sinner!" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 4#1 Peter 4 . 18|1 Peter 4:18]] quotes this, emphasizing judgment's certainty.)

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 12#Proverbs 12 . 7|Proverbs 12:7]] contrasts stability: "The wicked are overthrown and are no more, but the house of the righteous will stand." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 13#Proverbs 13 . 9|Proverbs 13:9]] uses light imagery: "The light of the righteous rejoices, but the lamp of the wicked will be put out."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 11|Proverbs 14:11]] declares: "The house of the wicked will be destroyed, but the tent of the upright will flourish." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 32|Proverbs 14:32]] contrasts deaths: "The wicked is overthrown through his evildoing, but the righteous finds refuge in his death"—suggesting hope beyond death for the righteous.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 15#Proverbs 15 . 25|Proverbs 15:25]] announces: "The LORD tears down the house of the proud but maintains the widow's boundaries." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 21#Proverbs 21 . 12|Proverbs 21:12]] observes: "The Righteous One observes the house of the wicked; he throws the wicked down to ruin."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 24#Proverbs 24 . 19|Proverbs 24:19-20]] counsels: "Fret not yourself because of evildoers, and be not envious of the wicked, for the evil man has no future; the lamp of the wicked will be put out." No future (*acharit*)—the decisive word indicating eschatological hopelessness.

**Job Vindicated and Restored**

Job's vindication demonstrates divine judgment favoring the righteous sufferer. After God's speeches from the whirlwind ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 38#Job 38 . 1|Job 38-41]]), Job repents in humility ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 6|Job 42:6]]). God then judges between Job and his friends, declaring to Eliphaz: "My anger burns against you and against your two friends, for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has" ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 7|Job 42:7]]).

The friends must offer sacrifices while Job intercedes for them ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 8|verse 8]])—Job functions as priestly mediator, prefiguring Christ's intercession. God accepts Job's prayer and restores his fortunes, giving him twice what he had before ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 10|verse 10]]): double the livestock ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 12|verse 12]]), restoration of family and community ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 11|verse 11]]), and long life seeing four generations ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 16|verse 16]]).

[[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 17|Job 42:17]] concludes: "And Job died, an old man, and full of days." This is covenant blessing language, echoing Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 8|Genesis 25:8]]). Job's vindication occurs within his lifetime, demonstrating God's justice while anticipating fuller eschatological vindication.

Beale notes that Job's suffering and restoration typologically point to Christ: the innocent sufferer vindicated by God, interceding for others, receiving inheritance with many sons ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]).

**Judgment Psalms: Divine Vengeance on Wickedness**

Numerous psalms cry out for divine judgment on the wicked. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 7#Psalm 7 . 1|Psalm 7:6-13]] calls for God to arise in anger, awake to judgment, and execute justice. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 7#Psalm 7 . 11|Psalm 7:11-13]] declares: "God is a righteous judge, and a God who feels indignation every day. If a man does not repent, God will whet his sword; he has bent and readied his bow; he has prepared for him his deadly weapons, making his arrows fiery shafts."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 9#Psalm 9 . 1|Psalm 9:5-8]] celebrates judgment executed: "You have rebuked the nations; you have made the wicked perish; you have blotted out their name forever and ever. The enemy came to an end in everlasting ruins... But the LORD sits enthroned forever; he has established his throne for justice, and he judges the world with righteousness; he judges the peoples with uprightness."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 11#Psalm 11 . 1|Psalm 11:4-7]] presents God's throne in heaven examining humanity: "His eyes see, his eyelids test the children of man. The LORD tests the righteous, but his soul hates the wicked and the one who loves violence. Let him rain coals on the wicked; fire and sulfur and a scorching wind shall be the portion of their cup. For the LORD is righteous; he loves righteous deeds; the upright shall behold his face."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 50#Psalm 50 . 1|Psalm 50:16-22]] pronounces judgment on the wicked who recite God's statutes yet hate discipline: "But to the wicked God says: 'What right have you to recite my statutes...?' ... 'Mark this, then, you who forget God, lest I tear you apart, and there be none to deliver!'"

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 75#Psalm 75 . 1|Psalm 75:2-3, 7-8]] declares God will judge at the appointed time: "At the set time that I appoint I will judge with equity... it is God who executes judgment, putting down one and lifting up another. For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup with foaming wine, well mixed, and he pours out from it, and all the wicked of the earth shall drain it down to the dregs."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 94#Psalm 94 . 1|Psalm 94:1-3]] cries: "O LORD, God of vengeance, O God of vengeance, shine forth! Rise up, O judge of the earth; repay to the proud what they deserve! O LORD, how long shall the wicked, how long shall the wicked exult?" The psalm expresses confidence that God will not forsake His people ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 94#Psalm 94 . 14|verse 14]]) and will bring back wickedness upon the wicked, destroying them ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 94#Psalm 94 . 23|verse 23]]).

**Imprecatory Psalms: Calling for Judgment**

The imprecatory psalms invoke divine judgment with startling intensity. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 58#Psalm 58 . 1|Psalm 58:6-11]] prays: "O God, break the teeth in their mouths; tear out the fangs of the young lions, O LORD! Let them vanish like water that runs away... Let them be like the snail that dissolves into slime, like the stillborn child who never sees the sun... The righteous will rejoice when he sees the vengeance; he will bathe his feet in the blood of the wicked. Mankind will say, 'Surely there is a reward for the righteous; surely there is a God who judges on earth.'"

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 109#Psalm 109 . 1|Psalm 109:6-15]] contains extensive imprecations: appointing an accuser over the wicked, condemning him, shortening his days, making his children fatherless and wife a widow, cutting off his memory from the earth. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 109#Psalm 109 . 17|Psalm 109:17-20]] justifies this: he loved to curse, so let curses come upon him; he clothed himself with cursing, so let it soak into his body.

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 137#Psalm 137 . 1|Psalm 137:7-9]], from Babylonian exile, calls for judgment on Edom and Babylon: "Remember, O LORD, against the Edomites the day of Jerusalem... O daughter of Babylon, doomed to be destroyed, blessed shall he be who repays you with what you have done to us! Blessed shall he be who takes your little ones and dashes them against the rock!"

These psalms shock modern sensibilities but express several biblical truths: (1) God's justice requires punishing wickedness; (2) victims may cry out for vindication without taking personal revenge ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 19|Romans 12:19]]); (3) covenant curses pronounced on covenant breakers are legitimate; (4) eschatological judgment will be thorough, not sentimental.

Beale notes these psalms find fulfillment in Christ's judgment on Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 1|Matthew 24]]) and the final judgment where Babylon (representing all opposition to God) falls ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 18#Revelation 18 . 1|Revelation 18]]).

**Song of Solomon: Love Strong as Death**

Even [[Readable Bible/22 - Song of Solomon/Song of Solomon 1#Song of Solomon 1 . 1|Song of Solomon]] contains judgment imagery. [[Readable Bible/22 - Song of Solomon/Song of Solomon 8#Song of Solomon 8 . 6|Song of Solomon 8:6]] declares love "strong as death," jealousy "fierce as the grave," its flashes "flashes of fire, the very flame of the LORD." Divine love and jealousy execute judgment on covenant unfaithfulness.

[[Readable Bible/22 - Song of Solomon/Song of Solomon 8#Song of Solomon 8 . 11|Song of Solomon 8:11-12]] mentions Solomon's vineyard and the beloved's own vineyard, suggesting accountability for what one has been entrusted—judgment based on stewardship.

**Theological Framework: Beale on Eschatological Vindication**

Beale emphasizes that Wisdom Literature's judgment theme anticipates New Testament eschatology. The righteous and wicked's destinies diverge ultimately, not always immediately. The resurrection of the dead—righteous to eternal life, wicked to eternal condemnation ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 12#Daniel 12 . 2|Daniel 12:2]])—resolves the tension that Ecclesiastes and Psalms wrestle with.

Jesus' teaching emphasizes final judgment: sheep and goats separated ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 31|Matthew 25:31-46]]), wheat and tares divided ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 24|Matthew 13:24-30, 36-43]]), good fish kept and bad thrown away ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 47|Matthew 13:47-50]]). [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 36|Matthew 12:36-37]] warns: "On the day of judgment people will give account for every careless word they speak, for by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned."

[[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 5|Romans 2:5-11]] declares God will render to each according to his works: eternal life to those who seek glory, honor, and immortality; wrath and fury to those who are self-seeking and do not obey truth. [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 10|2 Corinthians 5:10]] states: "We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or evil."

[[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 11|Revelation 20:11-15]] presents the great white throne judgment: books opened, dead judged by what is written according to their deeds, death and Hades thrown into the lake of fire, and anyone not found in the book of life cast into the lake of fire. This is Ecclesiastes' promised judgment fully manifest.

**Theological Framework: Kline on Intrusion and Delay**

Kline's framework explains why judgment is both certain and delayed. In the Common Grace administration, final judgment is postponed, allowing wheat and tares to grow together ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 30|Matthew 13:30]]). This delay is mercy, providing opportunity for repentance ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]]).

However, judgment intrudes proleptically in historical acts: the Flood ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 1|Genesis 6-9]]), Sodom and Gomorrah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 19#Genesis 19 . 1|Genesis 19]]), the Exodus plagues ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 7#Exodus 7 . 1|Exodus 7-12]]), conquest of Canaan ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 1|Joshua 6-11]]), exile of Israel and Judah ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 17#2 Kings 17 . 1|2 Kings 17]], [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 1|25]]), and supremely the cross where God judged sin in Christ ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 3|Romans 8:3]]).

These historical judgments are types of final judgment, previews of the ultimate reckoning. The fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 was intrusive judgment on covenant-breaking Israel, foreshadowing the final judgment ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 1|Matthew 24]]). Christ's second coming will consummate what His first advent inaugurated: complete destruction of wickedness, full vindication of righteousness.

Kline also emphasizes that believers undergo judgment now in Christ. [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 24|John 5:24]] promises: "Whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." Christ bore believers' judgment at the cross ([[Readable Bible/53 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 5#1 Thessalonians 5 . 9|1 Thessalonians 5:9]]); they will not face condemnation ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]).

**Christological Fulfillment: Jesus as Judge**

Jesus is revealed as the eschatological Judge whom Wisdom Literature anticipated. The Father "has given all judgment to the Son" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 22|John 5:22]]), and "has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 27|John 5:27]]). [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 17#Acts 17 . 31|Acts 17:31]] declares God "has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by a man whom he has appointed; and of this he has given assurance to all by raising him from the dead."

Jesus Himself taught extensively on final judgment. [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 31|Matthew 25:31-46]] presents the Son of Man sitting on His glorious throne, separating sheep from goats, sending the righteous to eternal life and the cursed to eternal fire. [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 28|John 5:28-29]] promises: "An hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out, those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment."

Jesus' resurrection vindicates God's justice. The one who suffered innocently is raised and exalted, receiving the name above every name ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9-11]]). Job's vindication typologically anticipated this: the innocent sufferer restored and honored, functioning as intercessor.

[[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 11|Revelation 19:11-16]] depicts Christ returning as conquering King: eyes like flame of fire, name written that no one knows, robe dipped in blood, called "The Word of God," leading heaven's armies, striking down nations, ruling with rod of iron, treading the winepress of God's wrath, bearing the name "King of kings and Lord of lords." This is the Judge whom Wisdom Literature proclaimed would vindicate righteousness and destroy wickedness.

**Application for God's People**

The judgment theme in Wisdom Literature provides multiple applications for believers:

**Comfort in Suffering**: Like Job and the psalmists, believers may suffer unjustly in the present while awaiting future vindication. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 19|Romans 12:19]] counsels: "Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God, for it is written, 'Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord.'" Christians don't need to take revenge because God will execute perfect justice.

**Motivation for Holiness**: Knowing all will be judged motivates godly living. [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 10|2 Corinthians 5:10-11]] states: "We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ... Therefore, knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade others." [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 17|1 Peter 1:17]] exhorts: "Conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile, knowing that you were ransomed... with the precious blood of Christ."

**Evangelistic Urgency**: Final judgment makes evangelism urgent. [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 17#Acts 17 . 30|Acts 17:30-31]] declares God "commands all people everywhere to repent, because he has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness." [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 20|2 Corinthians 5:20-21]] pleads: "We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God. For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."

**Confidence in God's Justice**: The wicked's prosperity need not cause despair. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 6#Revelation 6 . 10|Revelation 6:10]] records martyrs crying, "How long before you will judge?" [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 2|Revelation 19:2]] celebrates: "His judgments are true and just; he has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth with her immorality, and has avenged on her the blood of his servants."

**Hope Beyond Death**: Like Psalm 73's confidence of being received to glory ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 24|verse 24]]) and Proverbs' teaching that the righteous find refuge in death ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 32|Proverbs 14:32]]), believers trust that death is gain ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 1#Philippians 1 . 21|Philippians 1:21]]) because they will be with Christ ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 1#Philippians 1 . 23|verse 23]]) and will be raised on the last day ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 24|John 11:24-26]]).

**Living in Light of Eternity**: Ecclesiastes' conclusion—fear God, keep commandments, know judgment is coming ([[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 13|Ecclesiastes 12:13-14]])—remains the Christian's duty. Believers live not "under the sun" alone but in light of eternity, making choices with eternal weight, storing up treasures in heaven ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 6#Matthew 6 . 19|Matthew 6:19-20]]), and eagerly awaiting the righteous Judge who will make all things right.

The Wisdom Literature's judgment theme finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ's return when every knee will bow ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 10|Philippians 2:10]]), every tongue confess Him Lord ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 11|verse 11]]), the righteous shine like the sun in the Father's kingdom ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 43|Matthew 13:43]]), and God will be all in all ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 28|1 Corinthians 15:28]]).
