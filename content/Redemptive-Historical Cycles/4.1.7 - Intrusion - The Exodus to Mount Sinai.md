# 4.1.7 - Intrusion - The Exodus to Mount Sinai

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1 - The Delay - The Age of Preparation|← Back to 4.1]]

---

## Overview

**Biblical Scope**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 1#Exodus 1 . 8|Exodus 1:8]] - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 18|Exodus 24:18]]

The Exodus to Mount Sinai represents the **most significant Old Testament intrusion** of the eschatological age into the period of the Delay. This is not merely a historical rescue operation but a **new creation event**, where God births a nation through the waters of the Red Sea, establishes them as a corporate Adam with a renewed Adamic commission, and makes them His dwelling place through the tabernacle. As G.K. Beale argues, this intrusion serves as the **greatest Old Testament type of Christ's redemptive work**, prefiguring baptism, new creation, resurrection, and the building of God's temple-people.

Yet this massive intrusion—complete with the most dramatic theophany in the Old Testament at Mount Sinai—**does not consummate the age**. Meredith Kline's two-register cosmology helps us understand this: the Exodus is a powerful intrusion of the upper register (Consummation) into the lower register (Common Grace), but it remains **preparatory**. Israel sins immediately with the golden calf, demonstrating that even the greatest Old Testament deliverance cannot solve the fundamental problem of the human heart. The Exodus points forward to the **greater Exodus** that Christ will accomplish through His death, resurrection, and sending of the Spirit.

### The Pattern of Intrusion

The Exodus-to-Sinai narrative follows the **fivefold redemptive-historical pattern** with remarkable clarity:

**[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7.1 - Chaos|4.1.7.1 - Chaos]]**: Egypt becomes an anti-Eden, a place of slavery, oppression, and death. Pharaoh orders the murder of Hebrew boys ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 1#Exodus 1 . 15|Exodus 1:15-22]]), and Israel groans under brutal forced labor ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 1#Exodus 1 . 11|Exodus 1:11-14]]). The ten plagues function as **de-creation judgments**, reversing the order of Genesis 1 and demonstrating that Egypt is a land under divine curse.

**[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7.2 - Creation|4.1.7.2 - Creation]]**: The passage through the Red Sea ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 21|Exodus 14:21-31]]) echoes Genesis creation imagery—waters divided, dry ground appears, the Spirit leads through the pillar of cloud and fire. Israel emerges on the other side as a **newborn nation**, God's "firstborn son" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22|Exodus 4:22-23]]). The Song of Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 1|Exodus 15:1-21]]) celebrates this new creation with worship.

**[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7.3 - Commission|4.1.7.3 - Commission]]**: At Sinai, God commissions Israel as a **"kingdom of priests and holy nation"** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]), giving them the Adamic task of being His image-bearing people. The Ten Commandments ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 1|Exodus 20:1-17]]) and covenant stipulations define their mission, while the tabernacle instructions ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 1|Exodus 25-31]]) establish them as a temple-people with God dwelling in their midst.

**[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7.4 - Sin|4.1.7.4 - Sin]]**: While Moses is on the mountain receiving the law, Israel builds the golden calf ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 1|Exodus 32:1-6]]), breaking the first two commandments before the covenant is even ratified. This corporate rebellion demonstrates that **redemption from Egypt does not automatically produce heart transformation**. The pattern is established: law given, law broken immediately.

**[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7.5 - Judgment|4.1.7.5 - Judgment]]**: Three thousand die by the sword ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 28|Exodus 32:28]]), an intrusion of final judgment into the Common Grace period. Yet Moses intercedes ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 30|Exodus 32:30-34]]), and God renews the covenant ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 1|Exodus 34:1-9]]). The nation is preserved, but their entry into the land is delayed—they will wander in the wilderness.

## Major Theological Themes

### 1. Israel as Corporate Adam

The Exodus narrative presents Israel as a **new humanity**, a corporate Adam receiving a renewed creation commission. Just as Adam was placed in the garden-temple to serve and guard it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]]), Israel is called to be God's dwelling place and to expand His glory to the nations. The language of [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]—"you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation"—recapitulates the Adamic commission to rule as God's image-bearers and to mediate His presence.

G.K. Beale observes that Israel's commission includes all five elements of the original Adamic mandate:

- **Being fruitful and multiplying**: God promises to make them numerous ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 1#Exodus 1 . 7|Exodus 1:7]])
- **Subduing and ruling**: They are to take dominion over the promised land
- **Serving and guarding**: They are to keep the covenant and guard God's holiness
- **Expanding the temple-garden**: The tabernacle is to be the center from which God's glory spreads
- **Reflecting God's image**: As a holy nation, they are to display God's character to the nations

This corporate Adamic identity explains why Israel's failure is so catastrophic—they repeat Adam's sin, breaking covenant immediately after receiving it.

### 2. New Creation Through the Red Sea

The Exodus through the Red Sea is not simply a rescue from slavery but a **new creation event**. The narrative deliberately echoes Genesis 1:

- **Waters divided**: Just as God separated the waters in creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 6|Genesis 1:6-7]]), so He divides the Red Sea ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 21|Exodus 14:21]])
- **Dry ground appears**: Creation produces dry land ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 9|Genesis 1:9]]), and so does the Red Sea crossing ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 22|Exodus 14:22]])
- **Spirit hovers**: The Spirit hovers over waters in creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]]), and God leads Israel through cloud and fire (symbols of His Spirit) ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 21|Exodus 13:21-22]])
- **De-creation for enemies**: The waters destroy Egypt ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 28|Exodus 14:28]]), just as they will in the flood ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 21|Genesis 7:21-23]])

Paul explicitly identifies the Red Sea crossing as a **type of baptism** ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 1|1 Corinthians 10:1-2]]), and the early church understood baptism as participation in Christ's death and resurrection—a new creation reality ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 12|Colossians 2:12]]). The Exodus thus becomes the paradigmatic Old Testament image of **salvation as new creation**.

### 3. Temple Theology and the Tabernacle

The tabernacle instructions in [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 1|Exodus 25-31]] reveal that God's ultimate purpose is not merely to deliver Israel from Egypt but to **dwell among them** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]). The tabernacle is a **mobile Eden**, a temple-garden where God's presence rests.

Beale catalogs multiple connections between the tabernacle and Eden:

- **Eastward orientation**: Both the garden ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]) and the tabernacle face east
- **Cherubim guardians**: Cherubim guard Eden ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]) and are embroidered on the tabernacle curtains ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 1|Exodus 26:1]])
- **Lampstand as tree of life**: The golden lampstand ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 31|Exodus 25:31-40]]) with almond blossoms recalls the tree of life
- **Menorah and river**: The seven-branched lampstand may symbolize the river flowing from Eden
- **Priestly garments**: Aaron's robe has pomegranates ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 33|Exodus 28:33-34]]), fruit imagery connecting to Eden
- **Gold and precious stones**: Eden is rich in gold and precious stones ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 11|Genesis 2:11-12]]), as is the tabernacle

The tabernacle is thus a **sanctuary-in-miniature**, a portable version of the cosmic temple that is ultimately the entire creation. God is reversing the curse of exile from Eden by coming to dwell with His people again.

### 4. Covenant Pattern: Deliverance → Law → Blessing/Curse

The Exodus establishes the classic covenant pattern that will be repeated throughout Scripture:

**Grace precedes law**: God delivers Israel from Egypt **before** giving them the law at Sinai. The Ten Commandments begin with "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 2|Exodus 20:2]])—redemption is the foundation for obedience, not the result of it.

**Law defines covenant relationship**: The commandments ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 1|Exodus 20:1-17]]) and Book of the Covenant ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 22|Exodus 20:22-23:33]]) stipulate how a redeemed people should live in relationship with their Redeemer.

**Blessing and curse**: Israel's covenant includes both promises of blessing for obedience and threats of curse for disobedience ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 20|Exodus 23:20-33]]).

**Ratification ceremony**: The covenant is sealed with blood ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 3|Exodus 24:3-8]]), prefiguring Christ's blood of the new covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 18|Hebrews 9:18-22]]).

This pattern will be repeated at Moab (Deuteronomy), at Shechem ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 1|Joshua 24]]), and ultimately in the New Covenant ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]]).

### 5. Moses as Mediator and Type of Christ

Moses functions as the **mediator** between God and Israel, a role that prefigures Christ's perfect mediation. Key parallels include:

**Rescued from death as infants**: Moses escapes Pharaoh's infanticide ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 2#Exodus 2 . 1|Exodus 2:1-10]]), just as Jesus escapes Herod's slaughter ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 13|Matthew 2:13-18]]).

**Called out of Egypt**: God calls Moses from Egypt to deliver Israel; God calls Jesus out of Egypt ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 15|Matthew 2:15]], quoting [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 11#Hosea 11 . 1|Hosea 11:1]]).

**Forty days and nights**: Moses is on the mountain forty days ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 18|Exodus 24:18]]), and Jesus fasts forty days in the wilderness ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 2|Matthew 4:2]]).

**Intercession**: Moses intercedes for sinful Israel, offering himself as a substitute ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 32|Exodus 32:32]]): "But now, if you will forgive their sin—but if not, please blot me out of your book." Christ becomes the ultimate substitute, actually bearing the curse ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]).

**Face-to-face communion**: Moses speaks with God "face to face" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 11|Exodus 33:11]]), prefiguring the perfect communion Christ has with the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 18|John 1:18]]).

**Yet Moses fails**: Moses cannot enter the promised land because of his own sin ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 12|Numbers 20:12]]). The book of Hebrews emphasizes that while Moses was faithful as a **servant** in God's house, Christ is faithful as a **Son** over God's house ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 1|Hebrews 3:1-6]]). Moses points to the need for a better mediator.

### 6. The Ten Plagues as De-Creation Judgments

The ten plagues ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 7#Exodus 7 . 14|Exodus 7:14-12:30]]) are not arbitrary displays of power but **systematic de-creation judgments** on Egypt, demonstrating that Egypt is an anti-Eden under divine curse.

Meredith Kline observes that the plagues reverse the order of creation:

- **Darkness** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 10#Exodus 10 . 21|Exodus 10:21-23]]) undoes the creation of light ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 3|Genesis 1:3]])
- **Hail destroys vegetation** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 9#Exodus 9 . 22|Exodus 9:22-26]]), undoing the third day of creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 11|Genesis 1:11-13]])
- **Locusts consume crops** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 10#Exodus 10 . 12|Exodus 10:12-15]]), continuing the destruction of plant life
- **Death of livestock** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 9#Exodus 9 . 3|Exodus 9:3-7]]) undoes the creation of animals ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 24|Genesis 1:24-25]])
- **Death of firstborn** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 29|Exodus 12:29-30]]) is the ultimate de-creation, undoing the creation of humanity ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-27]])

The plagues also **judge Egypt's gods**, demonstrating Yahweh's supremacy over all false deities ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 12|Exodus 12:12]]). The Nile turns to blood, striking at the heart of Egypt's water god; the sun is darkened, humiliating Ra, the sun god. Each plague demonstrates that Egypt's gods are **no-gods**, powerless before the Creator.

### 7. Passover and Substitutionary Atonement

The Passover ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 1|Exodus 12:1-28]]) establishes the principle of **substitutionary atonement** that will find its ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Key elements:

**Selection of the lamb**: A lamb without blemish is chosen ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5]]), prefiguring Christ as the "Lamb of God without blemish" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]).

**Shed blood**: The lamb's blood is shed and applied to the doorposts ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 7|Exodus 12:7]]), protecting the household from judgment. Christ's blood protects believers from final judgment ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]).

**Death of firstborn or substitute**: Either the firstborn dies, or the lamb dies in his place—there is no third option. Christ dies in the place of His people ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 18|2 Peter 3:18]]; [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]).

**Memorial feast**: Passover becomes an annual memorial ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 14|Exodus 12:14]]), just as the Lord's Supper memorializes Christ's death ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 11#1 Corinthians 11 . 24|1 Corinthians 11:24-26]]).

**Redemption of firstborn**: Israel is God's firstborn son ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22|Exodus 4:22-23]]), redeemed through the blood of the lamb. Christ is God's true Firstborn ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15|Colossians 1:15]], [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 18|18]]), through whom many sons are brought to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]).

Paul explicitly identifies Christ as "our Passover lamb" who has been sacrificed ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]).

### 8. Wilderness Provision: Manna, Water, and Sabbath

God's provision in the wilderness demonstrates His faithfulness and teaches Israel dependence:

**Manna from heaven** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 4|Exodus 16:4-36]]): God provides bread daily, teaching Israel to trust Him for their daily bread ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 6#Matthew 6 . 11|Matthew 6:11]]). Jesus identifies Himself as the **true bread from heaven** who gives eternal life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 32|John 6:32-35]]).

**Water from the rock** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 17#Exodus 17 . 6|Exodus 17:6]]): God provides water from the rock at Horeb. Paul identifies the rock as **Christ** ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 4|1 Corinthians 10:4]]), from whom living water flows ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 37|John 7:37-39]]).

**Sabbath rest** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 22|Exodus 16:22-30]]): The pattern of manna collection establishes the Sabbath principle, pointing forward to the ultimate rest found in Christ ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]]).

These provisions teach that **God sustains His people supernaturally**, and all point forward to Christ as the ultimate provision for every human need.

## Kline's Two-Register Framework

Meredith Kline's **two-register cosmology** helps us understand the Exodus as a dramatic but still-preparatory intrusion:

### Upper Register Intrusions at Sinai

The theophany at Mount Sinai ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16-25]]) is the most dramatic **intrusion of the upper register** (Consummation) into the lower register (Common Grace) in the Old Testament:

- **Thunder, lightning, thick cloud**: Divine glory breaks through ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16]])
- **Trumpet blast**: The sound grows louder and louder ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 19|Exodus 19:19]])
- **Fire and smoke**: The mountain is wrapped in fire ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 18|Exodus 19:18]])
- **Mountain quakes**: The whole mountain trembles ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 18|Exodus 19:18]])
- **Danger of death**: Anyone who touches the mountain dies ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 12|Exodus 19:12-13]])

This is **eschatological glory invading history**, a preview of the Day of the Lord when God will descend to judge and renew the earth. Hebrews contrasts this terrifying theophany with the welcoming glory of Mount Zion, the heavenly Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 18|Hebrews 12:18-24]]).

### Yet Common Grace Continues

Despite this massive intrusion, the **Common Grace period continues**:

- **Israel sins immediately**: The golden calf ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 1|Exodus 32:1-6]]) demonstrates that even direct revelation of God's glory doesn't transform the heart
- **Death is partial**: Only 3,000 die ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 28|Exodus 32:28]]), not the whole nation—judgment is restrained
- **Covenant renewed**: Despite sin, God renews the covenant ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 10|Exodus 34:10-28]]), showing ongoing grace
- **Wilderness wandering**: Immediate entry into the land is delayed, but not permanently cancelled

The Exodus demonstrates both the **glory of God's redemption** and the **insufficiency of external law** to change the heart. It points forward to the New Covenant, when God will write His law on hearts ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]) and pour out His Spirit ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 27|Ezekiel 36:27]]).

## Christological Fulfillment: The Greater Exodus

The New Testament presents Jesus as accomplishing the **greater Exodus** to which the Old Testament Exodus pointed:

### Jesus Fulfills the Exodus Types

**Greater deliverance**: Jesus delivers not from Egypt but from sin and death ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 6|Romans 6:6-11]])

**Greater Passover**: Christ is our Passover lamb, sacrificed for us ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]])

**Greater baptism**: The Red Sea crossing prefigures baptism into Christ's death and resurrection ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 1|1 Corinthians 10:1-2]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 3|Romans 6:3-4]])

**Greater provision**: Jesus is the true bread from heaven ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 35|John 6:35]]), the living water ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 38|John 7:38]]), the rock from which living water flows ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 4|1 Corinthians 10:4]])

**Greater covenant**: Jesus mediates a new and better covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 6|Hebrews 8:6]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 15|9:15]])

**Greater tabernacle**: Jesus tabernacles among us ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]), and believers become the temple of the Holy Spirit ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16|1 Corinthians 3:16-17]])

**Greater intercession**: Jesus intercedes perfectly as our great High Priest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]])

### Luke's Gospel: The New Exodus

Luke's Gospel is saturated with Exodus typology:

**Transfiguration**: Moses and Elijah discuss Jesus' "exodus" (Greek: *exodon*) which He is about to accomplish in Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 9#Luke 9 . 31|Luke 9:31]]). Jesus' death and resurrection are the **new Exodus**, the ultimate deliverance.

**Last Supper**: Jesus reinterprets the Passover meal, identifying His body and blood as the new Passover sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 14|Luke 22:14-20]]).

**Ascension**: Jesus ascends into heaven like Moses ascending Sinai, but He enters the heavenly throne room itself ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 9|Acts 1:9-11]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]]).

### Revelation: The Final Exodus

The book of Revelation presents the **consummation of the Exodus**:

**Song of Moses and the Lamb**: The redeemed sing "the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 15#Revelation 15 . 3|Revelation 15:3]]), uniting the Exodus with Christ's victory.

**Plagues on Babylon**: The judgments on Babylon echo the plagues on Egypt, as God delivers His people from the final anti-Eden ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 16#Revelation 16 . 1|Revelation 16:1-21]]).

**New Jerusalem**: The final state is not a return to Eden but a glorious **city-temple-garden**, the tabernacle of God with humanity ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]), fulfilling the Exodus promise: "I will dwell among them."

## Conclusion: Intrusion and Hope

The Exodus to Mount Sinai is the **paradigmatic Old Testament intrusion**, demonstrating God's power to deliver, create anew, and dwell with His people. Yet this intrusion **does not consummate the age**—Israel sins, the covenant is broken, and the wilderness wandering delays their entry into rest. The Exodus points forward to the **greater Exodus** accomplished by Christ, who perfectly fulfills the Law, mediates the New Covenant, and brings His people into eternal rest.

The fivefold pattern of the Exodus—Chaos, Creation, Commission, Sin, Judgment—is the pattern of all history, repeated at every level from individuals to nations to the cosmos itself. And at every level, the pattern points to **Christ**, the true Israel who succeeds where Old Testament Israel failed, the true Moses who intercedes perfectly, the true Passover Lamb who takes away the sin of the world.
