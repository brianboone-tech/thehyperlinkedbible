## The Adamic Mandate in Conquest Context

The commission given to Israel through Joshua recapitulates and advances the original Adamic mandate of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]. Just as Adam was commanded to <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it,"</span> so Israel is commanded to fill the land of Canaan, subdue the Canaanite nations, and establish God's rule throughout the territory. The conquest is not merely a military campaign but a <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">theological mission</span>—the restoration of creation order in a land corrupted by idolatry and wickedness.

G.K. Beale has identified five elements in the Adamic commission that structure biblical theology: (1) extend sacred space, (2) subdue the earth, (3) rule over creation, (4) be fruitful and multiply, and (5) observe the Sabbath. Each of these elements finds expression in Israel's commission to conquer Canaan.

First, Israel is to <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">extend sacred space</span>. Just as Adam was placed in the garden-sanctuary with the implicit commission to expand Eden throughout the earth, transforming the entire world into a temple where God dwells with His people, so Israel is to transform Canaan into holy ground where Yahweh's presence dwells. The conquest is fundamentally about sanctification—setting apart profane space for sacred use.

Second, Israel is to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">subdue the land</span>. The language of conquest in Joshua echoes the language of subduing in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]. The Hebrew word *kabash* (subdue) connotes forceful subjugation, bringing something under control. Israel is to bring the Canaanite nations under subjugation, not for exploitation but for elimination, removing the corrupting influence of idolatry from the land.

Third, Israel is to <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">rule over the territory</span>. The land is not to be ruled by Canaanite kings who serve false gods, but by Yahweh through His covenant people. The conquest establishes theocratic rule—government under God's revealed Law. Joshua functions as God's vice-regent, administering divine judgment and distributing the land according to God's command.

Fourth, Israel is to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">be fruitful and multiply</span> in the land. The promise of descendants as numerous as the stars (Genesis 15:5) begins to find fulfillment as Israel settles in Canaan. The land itself is described as "flowing with milk and honey" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 8|Exodus 3:8]])—a picture of agricultural abundance that will support population growth.

Fifth, Israel is to <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">observe the Sabbath</span>, entering into rest in the land ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 9|Deuteronomy 12:9-10]]). The conquest will eventually bring "rest from all your enemies" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 10|Deuteronomy 12:10]]), a sabbatical rest that typologically points toward the ultimate eschatological rest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8-11]]).

The commission to Joshua and Israel is therefore not an arbitrary military assignment but a <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">recapitulation of the original human vocation</span>. Israel is the corporate Adam figure, called to succeed where the first Adam failed—to resist the serpent's lies, remain faithful to God's word, and extend God's kingdom throughout the earth.

## Primary Command: Possess the Land

The foundational element of Israel's commission is the command to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">possess the land</span> that God promised to their fathers. God declares to Joshua: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you, just as I promised to Moses. From the wilderness and this Lebanon as far as the great river, the river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites to the Great Sea toward the going down of the sun shall be your territory"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 3|Joshua 1:3-4]]).

This commission has several crucial dimensions. First, it is grounded in <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">prior promise</span>. The land is not a new idea but the fulfillment of God's covenant with Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 18|Genesis 15:18-21]]), Isaac ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 26#Genesis 26 . 3|Genesis 26:3]]), and Jacob ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 13|Genesis 28:13]]). The conquest validates God's faithfulness across multiple generations.

Second, the commission involves <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">specific boundaries</span>. The land is not vaguely "somewhere in Canaan" but precisely delimited—from the wilderness (Negev desert) to Lebanon, from the Euphrates to the Mediterranean. These boundaries define the scope of Israel's mission. They are to possess all the land within these parameters, not merely establish a foothold in part of it.

Third, possession is described as both <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">already accomplished and yet to be achieved</span>. God says "I have given to you"—the land is already theirs by divine decree. Yet they must still "tread upon" it—they must take physical possession through conquest. This tension between the already and the not-yet structures the entire conquest narrative. The land is theirs by promise, but they must appropriate that promise by faith-filled obedience.

Fourth, the promise includes divine presence: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Just as I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not leave you or forsake you"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 5|Joshua 1:5]]). Possession of the land is inseparable from the presence of God. Without Yahweh's presence, Israel cannot conquer. With His presence, they cannot be defeated. The promise of divine accompaniment transforms the conquest from an impossible human endeavor into an assured divine victory.

The command to possess the land thus encapsulates <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">covenant theology</span> in its entirety: God's sovereign election (choosing to give the land), human responsibility (the need to walk in and take possession), the means of grace (God's empowering presence), and eschatological hope (the land as type of eternal inheritance).

## Torah Obedience: The Foundation of Success

Immediately after the promise of divine presence, God commands Joshua to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">meditate on the Law day and night</span>: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night, so that you may be careful to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 8|Joshua 1:8]]).

This is one of the most important verses in the book of Joshua, for it establishes that <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">success in the conquest is conditioned on Torah obedience</span>, not military superiority. The phrase "Book of the Law" (*sefer ha-torah*) refers to the Pentateuch, particularly Deuteronomy, which Moses had recently delivered as his farewell covenant renewal address. Joshua is to internalize this Law, allowing it to shape his thinking, speaking, and acting.

The command to meditate "day and night" indicates <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">comprehensive orientation toward God's word</span>. This is not merely academic study or religious ritual performed at appointed times. It is constant mental engagement with divine revelation, allowing the Law to inform every decision and action. Joshua cannot afford to compartmentalize his life into "religious" and "secular" spheres. As covenant mediator leading Israel in holy war, his entire existence must be saturated with God's word.

The promise attached to this command is remarkable: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success."</span> The Hebrew words translated "prosperous" (*tsalach*) and "good success" (*sakal*) denote effective accomplishment of one's goals. But the accomplishment is explicitly conditioned on Torah obedience. The promise is not "if you develop superior military tactics, then you will succeed," but "if you keep my Law, then you will succeed."

This establishes a principle that will be dramatically illustrated throughout the conquest: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">covenant faithfulness determines military outcomes</span>. When Achan sins by taking devoted things at Jericho, Israel is defeated at Ai despite overwhelming military superiority ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 3|Joshua 7:3-5]]). When Achan's sin is purged, Ai is easily conquered ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 1|Joshua 8:1-29]]). The battles are won or lost at the altar of covenant obedience, not on the battlefield of military tactics.

The commission to Torah obedience also distinguishes Israel's conquest from pagan holy war. Ancient Near Eastern nations also engaged in warfare supposedly sanctioned by their gods. But those campaigns were driven by imperial ambition, economic opportunism, or ethnic hatred. Israel's conquest, by contrast, is to be governed by <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">revealed divine Law</span>. The conquest is not autonomous but theonomous—conducted according to God's explicit stipulations.

This creates both limitation and empowerment. It limits Israel by constraining how they may conduct warfare—they cannot use any tactics they choose but only those consonant with Torah. But it also empowers Israel by assuring them that if they obey, God Himself will fight for them. The commission to Torah obedience thus protects against both autonomous presumption and faithless despair.

## Utterly Destroy: The Herem Command

The most controversial and difficult aspect of Israel's commission is the command to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">utterly destroy</span> the Canaanite nations. God commands through Moses: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"When the LORD your God brings you into the land that you are entering to take possession of it, and clears away many nations before you, the Hittites, the Girgashites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, seven nations more numerous and mightier than you, and when the LORD your God gives them over to you, and you defeat them, then you must devote them to complete destruction. You shall make no covenant with them and show no mercy to them"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 1|Deuteronomy 7:1-2]]).

The Hebrew word translated "devote to complete destruction" is *herem*, which means to set apart for God by complete destruction. Objects or persons under *herem* cannot be used for ordinary purposes—they must be either destroyed or, if valuable (like gold or silver), dedicated to the sanctuary treasury. The *herem* command appears repeatedly in the conquest narrative: at Jericho ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 17|Joshua 6:17]]), at Ai ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 26|Joshua 8:26]]), and in the campaigns against the southern and northern coalitions ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 40|Joshua 10:40]]).

This command raises profound moral and theological questions. How can a good God command the extermination of entire populations, including women and children? Is this not genocide, and therefore intrinsically evil regardless of who commands it?

Several theological considerations are essential for understanding the *herem* command within its redemptive-historical context. First, <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">the Canaanite nations are under divine judgment for persistent and egregious sin</span>. God had told Abraham that his descendants would not possess the land immediately because <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet complete"</span> ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 16|Genesis 15:16]]). Four hundred years later, that iniquity had reached its fullness. The Canaanites practiced child sacrifice, ritual prostitution, bestiality, and pervasive idolatry ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 18#Leviticus 18 . 21|Leviticus 18:21-30]]). Their culture had become thoroughly devoted to wickedness.

Second, <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">the conquest is an intrusion of eschatological judgment into history</span>. As Meredith Kline argues, certain pivotal moments in redemptive history witness the temporary breaking through of final judgment. The Flood, Sodom and Gomorrah, and the conquest of Canaan are all examples of God executing in time and space the judgment that ultimately awaits all unrepentant sinners at the last day. The Canaanites are not being treated uniquely unfairly; they are experiencing temporally what all who persist in rebellion will experience eternally.

Third, <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">the judgment is religious, not ethnic</span>. God is not commanding ethnic cleansing based on biological descent but religious purging based on spiritual allegiance. This is proven by Rahab, a Canaanite who is spared because she abandons Canaanite religion and aligns herself with Yahweh ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 25|Joshua 6:25]]). Conversely, Achan, an ethnic Israelite, is destroyed because he violates the *herem* ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 25|Joshua 7:25]]). The criterion is covenant faith, not ethnicity.

Fourth, <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">the *herem* is not normative for all times but specific to this unique redemptive-historical moment</span>. The conquest of Canaan is a once-for-all event in which God's people take possession of the land promised to Abraham. It is not a pattern for Christian holy war in the church age. The New Testament makes clear that believers' warfare is spiritual, not physical ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 6#Ephesians 6 . 12|Ephesians 6:12]]), and that the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but spiritual ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 10#2 Corinthians 10 . 4|2 Corinthians 10:4]]).

Fifth, <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">the *herem* protects Israel from spiritual contamination</span>. The rationale given for the command is explicitly theological: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"You shall not intermarry with them, giving your daughters to their sons or taking their daughters for your sons, for they would turn away your sons from following me, to serve other gods"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 3|Deuteronomy 7:3-4]]). The Canaanites' presence in the land posed an existential threat to Israel's covenant faithfulness. History proved this assessment correct—Israel's failure to fully execute the *herem* resulted in centuries of syncretism and apostasy.

Sixth, <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">God's command is morally authoritative precisely because He is the Creator who gives and takes life</span>. All human beings exist under a death sentence because of sin ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 23|Romans 6:23]]). That God allows any of us to live is sheer mercy. When God brings temporal judgment, whether through disease, natural disaster, or human agency, He is simply executing the sentence that all deserve. The question is not "How can a good God command the Canaanites' destruction?" but "How can a just God allow any sinners to live?"

The *herem* command is thus a <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">terrible but necessary</span> aspect of Israel's commission. It demonstrates the seriousness of sin, the reality of divine judgment, and the incompatibility of holiness with wickedness. It also foreshadows the ultimate judgment when Christ will return to destroy all who have rejected God's grace and rebelled against His rule ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 15|Revelation 19:15]]).

## Make No Covenant: The Prohibition Against Treaties

Closely related to the *herem* command is the prohibition against <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">making covenants with the inhabitants of the land</span>: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Take care, lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land to which you go, lest it become a snare in your midst"</span> ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 12|Exodus 34:12]]).

This prohibition guards against the temptation to compromise the conquest through diplomatic treaties. In the ancient Near East, treaty-making was a common method of establishing peace without warfare. Vassal treaties allowed weaker nations to preserve their existence by submitting to stronger powers. But for Israel, such treaties were forbidden because they would <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">preserve the very corruption that the conquest was designed to eliminate</span>.

The rationale is theological: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"You shall tear down their altars and break their pillars and cut down their Asherim (for you shall worship no other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God), lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and when they whore after their gods and sacrifice to their gods and you are invited, you eat of his sacrifice, and you take of their daughters for your sons, and their daughters whore after their gods and make your sons whore after their gods"</span> ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 13|Exodus 34:13-16]]).

The concern is that covenants with Canaanites would inevitably lead to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">religious syncretism</span>. Peaceful coexistence with idolaters would create social pressure to participate in their religious festivals. Intermarriage would produce children who followed their mothers' gods. Within a generation, the distinctiveness of Yahweh worship would be compromised, and Israel would become indistinguishable from the nations around them.

This prohibition will be tested dramatically in Joshua 9, when the Gibeonites deceive Israel into making a treaty with them ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 9#Joshua 9 . 14|Joshua 9:14-15]]). The text notes that Joshua made peace with them <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"without asking counsel from the LORD"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 9#Joshua 9 . 14|Joshua 9:14]]). This failure to consult Yahweh results in a permanent Canaanite presence within Israel, confirming the wisdom of the no-covenant command.

The prohibition against treaty-making underscores that <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">Israel's commission is not to integrate with Canaanite culture but to replace it entirely</span>. They are not to be pluralistic, tolerating various religious traditions. They are to be exclusively devoted to Yahweh, eliminating all rival claims to worship.

This exclusivism is grounded in the nature of Yahweh as <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"a jealous God"</span> ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 14|Exodus 34:14]]). The term "jealous" (*qanna*) does not denote petty envy but righteous zeal for exclusive devotion. Just as a husband rightly demands exclusive sexual fidelity from his wife, so Yahweh rightly demands exclusive spiritual fidelity from His covenant people. Idolatry is not merely a religious mistake but spiritual adultery—covenant unfaithfulness that violates the marriage bond between God and Israel.

The no-covenant command thus protects the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">exclusive claim of Yahweh</span> on Israel's worship. Any compromise on this point would undermine the entire purpose of the conquest—to create a holy people in a holy land devoted entirely to the worship of the one true God.

## Destroy Idolatrous Apparatus: Purge the Land

Integral to the commission is the command to <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">destroy all physical manifestations of Canaanite religion</span>: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"You shall surely destroy all the places where the nations whom you shall dispossess served their gods, on the high mountains and on the hills and under every green tree. You shall tear down their altars and dash in pieces their pillars and burn their Asherim with fire. You shall chop down the carved images of their gods and destroy their name out of that place"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 2|Deuteronomy 12:2-3]]).

Canaanite religion was fundamentally tied to sacred spaces—high places (*bamot*), where altars were erected; sacred pillars (*masseboth*), standing stones representing deities; Asherah poles (*asherim*), wooden symbols of the fertility goddess; and carved images (*pesilim*), idols representing various gods. These physical objects were not merely cultural artifacts but active instruments of idolatry, designed to facilitate worship of false gods.

Israel's commission includes the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">complete eradication of this apparatus</span>. Altars must be torn down, pillars smashed, poles burned, idols destroyed. The goal is to "destroy their name out of that place"—to eliminate every trace of Canaanite religion so that the land is purified for Yahweh worship.

This purging is necessary because <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">physical objects can become spiritual snares</span>. The command warns: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"Take care that you be not ensnared to follow them, after they have been destroyed before you, and that you do not inquire about their gods, saying, 'How did these nations serve their gods?—that I also may do the same'"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 30|Deuteronomy 12:30]]). The curiosity about pagan worship practices, even if initially innocent, can lead to experimentation and eventually adoption of those practices.

The command to destroy idolatrous apparatus reflects a biblical understanding of <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">the power of the environment to shape worship</span>. Human beings are not merely rational intellects but embodied creatures who are influenced by our physical surroundings. Sacred spaces, ritual objects, and visual symbols all shape how we think about and relate to the divine. If Israel allows Canaanite altars and images to remain, they create constant temptation toward syncretism.

This principle explains why God is so specific about the construction of the tabernacle and later the temple. Every detail—the materials, dimensions, furnishings, and rituals—is divinely prescribed because <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">how we worship shapes whom we worship</span>. Allowing Canaanite worship forms to remain would inevitably corrupt Israel's understanding of Yahweh, transforming Him into a fertility god like Baal.

The command to destroy idolatrous apparatus also anticipates the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">positive replacement</span> that will follow. Deuteronomy 12 continues: <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"But you shall seek the place that the LORD your God will choose out of all your tribes to put his name and make his habitation there. There you shall go, and there you shall bring your burnt offerings and your sacrifices"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 5|Deuteronomy 12:5-6]]). Canaanite altars scattered throughout the land will be replaced by one central sanctuary where Yahweh's name dwells. Decentralized idolatry will be replaced by centralized, authorized worship.

The destruction of idolatrous apparatus is therefore not mere vandalism but <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">spiritual cleansing</span>. The land must be purged of demonic influence so that it can become holy ground where Yahweh dwells with His people. This commission anticipates the ultimate cleansing when Christ will purge the entire cosmos of evil, making all things new ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]]).

## Be Holy: The Call to Covenant Distinctiveness

Underlying all the specific commands is the foundational commission to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">be holy</span>. God declares to Israel: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. The LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession, out of all the peoples who are on the face of the earth"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 6|Deuteronomy 7:6]]).

Holiness (*qodesh*) means <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">set apart for God's exclusive use</span>. A holy object or person is one withdrawn from common use and dedicated to sacred purposes. Israel's holiness is not something they achieve through moral effort but something they receive through divine election. God has chosen them, set them apart, made them His treasured possession. Their holiness is derivative, flowing from their relationship with the Holy One.

But election to holiness brings obligation to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">live distinctively</span>. Israel cannot worship Yahweh while simultaneously participating in Canaanite culture. The call to holiness demands separation from idolatrous practices: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"You shall not do as they do in the land of Egypt, where you lived, and you shall not do as they do in the land of Canaan, to which I am bringing you. You shall not walk in their statutes. You shall follow my rules and keep my statutes and walk in them. I am the LORD your God"</span> ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 18#Leviticus 18 . 3|Leviticus 18:3-4]]).

The commission to holiness governs every aspect of Israel's life in the land—their worship, their social structures, their economic practices, their family relationships, their treatment of the poor, their administration of justice. Every sphere of life must be ordered according to <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">Torah principles</span>, not Canaanite customs.

This comprehensive holiness is the antithesis of syncretism. Syncretism attempts to blend different religious traditions, taking elements from multiple sources to create a hybrid faith. But Yahweh demands <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">exclusive devotion</span>. The first commandment brooks no compromise: "You shall have no other gods before me" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 3|Exodus 20:3]]). Holiness requires rejecting all rival claimants to divine authority and ordering life entirely around Yahweh's revealed will.

The commission to holiness also has a <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">missional dimension</span>. Israel's distinctiveness is not an end in itself but a means of witness to the nations. When Israel lives in faithful obedience to Torah, displaying justice, righteousness, and wisdom, the surrounding nations will recognize Yahweh's greatness: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"Keep them and do them, for that will be your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the peoples, who, when they hear all these statutes, will say, 'Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people'"</span> ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 4#Deuteronomy 4 . 6|Deuteronomy 4:6]]).

The conquest of Canaan is therefore not merely about securing real estate but about establishing a <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">model covenant community</span> that demonstrates God's character to the world. Israel is to be a "light to the nations" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]]), showing what human society looks like when it is governed by divine wisdom and saturated with divine presence.

## Expand God's Kingdom: The Ultimate Goal

All the specific elements of Israel's commission converge on one ultimate goal: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">expand God's kingdom from the garden-sanctuary into the wider world</span>. The conquest of Canaan is a critical stage in the unfolding of God's redemptive plan to restore all creation to its original glory.

Adam was placed in Eden with the implicit commission to extend the boundaries of the garden until the entire earth became a temple filled with God's glory. Israel is placed in Canaan with the explicit commission to transform the land into holy space where God dwells. The pattern is consistent: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">God establishes a sacred center and commands His people to expand it</span>.

This expansion requires both <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">destruction and construction</span>—destroying the works of darkness and building the structures of light. The Canaanite nations and their idolatrous apparatus must be eliminated because they represent the kingdom of Satan, the serpent who corrupted Eden. In their place, Israel must establish Yahweh worship, Torah governance, and covenant community.

The conquest is therefore fundamentally about <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">the conflict between two kingdoms</span>—the kingdom of God and the kingdom of Satan. The Canaanites are not religiously neutral people who happen to worship different gods. They are, whether consciously or not, servants of the serpent-dragon who seeks to corrupt God's creation and usurp His glory. Their religions are demonic ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 20|1 Corinthians 10:20]]), their practices abominable, their cultures corrupting.

Israel's commission to conquer Canaan is therefore a commission to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">advance the kingdom of God against the kingdom of darkness</span>. This is spiritual warfare as much as physical warfare. The battles are fought with swords and spears, but the real conflict is between Yahweh and the demonic powers that enslave the nations through idolatry.

This understanding elevates the conquest from mere territorial acquisition to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">cosmic significance</span>. Every Canaanite city conquered is a victory for God's kingdom. Every altar torn down is a blow against Satan's dominion. Every Israelite family settled in the land is an outpost of God's presence. The conquest is a microcosm of the ultimate conquest when Christ returns to destroy all His enemies and establish His kingdom over the entire cosmos.

## Theological Analysis: Beale's Escalation Toward Christ

G.K. Beale's framework of <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">escalation toward Christ</span> illuminates how Israel's commission in Joshua advances the Adamic mandate while simultaneously revealing its insufficiency. Israel succeeds where Adam failed in many respects—they enter the land, defeat enemies, establish covenant community. But their success is partial and temporary, demonstrating the need for the ultimate Adam who will accomplish what neither Adam nor Israel could.

Israel's commission to subdue the land echoes Adam's commission to subdue the earth. But Israel's subduing is military and regional, not global and cultural. They conquer Canaan, not the world. They establish theocratic rule in one geographical area, not worldwide kingdom. This represents <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">redemptive-historical progress</span> beyond Eden (a more developed expression of the Adamic commission) but also <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">incompleteness</span> (falling short of the ultimate goal).

Moreover, Israel's obedience to the commission is partial. They fail to fully execute the *herem*, leaving Canaanite enclaves within the land ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 13#Joshua 13 . 13|Joshua 13:13]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 19|Judges 1:19-36]]). They make unauthorized treaties (the Gibeonites in Joshua 9). They eventually fall into the very idolatry that the conquest was designed to prevent (as Judges demonstrates). Their failure proves that <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">a better mediator is needed</span>—one who will perfectly fulfill the Adamic commission.

This pattern of partial success revealing the need for ultimate fulfillment is characteristic of Old Testament redemptive history. Each successive stage—Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Joshua, David—represents advancement toward the goal while simultaneously demonstrating inadequacy to fully achieve it. The progression is <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">eschatological</span>, pointing forward to the one who will finally and fully accomplish what all the types could only foreshadow.

Jesus Christ is the ultimate Adam who perfectly fulfills the commission that God gave humanity. He subdues all enemies, including sin, death, and Satan ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 25|1 Corinthians 15:25-26]]). He establishes God's kingdom, first through His incarnation, death, and resurrection, and ultimately at His return ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 15|Revelation 11:15]]). He creates a holy people drawn from every nation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 9|Revelation 5:9]]). He transforms the entire cosmos into a temple where God dwells with redeemed humanity ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]).

Israel's commission in Joshua thus functions as a <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">type</span>—a historical pattern that points forward to greater fulfillment in Christ. The conquest of Canaan foreshadows Christ's conquest of the spiritual forces of evil. The destruction of Canaanite idolatry foreshadows Christ's destruction of sin and death. The establishment of covenant community in the land foreshadows the church as God's new covenant people. The partial rest achieved under Joshua foreshadows the eternal rest believers will enter through Christ.

## Theological Analysis: Kline's Intrusion Ethics

Meredith Kline's framework helps explain why the commission given to Israel in Joshua includes elements (like the *herem*) that are not normative for all times. According to Kline, the conquest represents an <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">intrusion of eschatological judgment</span> into the common grace era.

Under normal common grace conditions, God exercises patience with sinners, allowing time for repentance and restraining full judgment. The wicked and righteous coexist, and believers are commanded to love enemies, not destroy them ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 44|Matthew 5:44]]). But in intrusion events, God brings forward the judgment that properly belongs to the last day, executing temporal-physical judgment that foreshadows eternal-spiritual judgment.

The conquest is one such intrusion event. The commission to destroy the Canaanites is <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">not a perpetual mandate for believers to execute judgment on unbelievers</span>. It is a unique, unrepeatable commission tied to a specific redemptive-historical moment—the establishment of Israel in the Promised Land as a type of the eschatological kingdom.

This explains why <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">New Testament ethics are so different from conquest ethics</span>. Jesus commands believers to love enemies, pray for persecutors, and turn the other cheek ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 38|Matthew 5:38-48]]). The apostles teach submission to governing authorities ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 13#Romans 13 . 1|Romans 13:1]]) and patient endurance of persecution ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 20|1 Peter 2:20-23]]). Believers wage spiritual warfare, not physical ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 6#Ephesians 6 . 12|Ephesians 6:12]]).

The difference is <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">redemptive-historical location</span>. The conquest occurred during an intrusion moment when eschatological judgment broke into history. The church age is a common grace period when God exercises patience, "not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance" ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]]). Final judgment awaits Christ's return, when He will execute comprehensive judgment on all who have rejected God's grace.

Israel's commission in Joshua thus cannot be directly applied to Christians today. We are not commanded to physically destroy unbelievers or tear down their temples. Our commission is to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">proclaim the gospel</span>, make disciples, and establish the church—spiritual warfare resulting in spiritual conquest ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19-20]]).

But the theological principles underlying the commission remain relevant. God still demands <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">exclusive devotion</span>—no idols, no syncretism. Believers are still called to be <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">holy</span>, set apart from the world's values and practices. The church is still commissioned to <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">expand God's kingdom</span>, making disciples of all nations. And ultimate judgment still awaits those who persist in rebellion against God.

The conquest commission, rightly understood within its redemptive-historical context, points forward to the ultimate fulfillment when <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">Christ the Warrior-King returns</span> to destroy all evil and establish His kingdom forever. The sword Joshua wielded typologically corresponds to the sword that proceeds from Christ's mouth—the word of judgment that will finally and fully subdue all enemies ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 15|Revelation 19:15]]).

## The Christological Fulfillment

Every dimension of Israel's commission finds ultimate fulfillment in Jesus Christ. Jesus is the true Israel who perfectly obeys God's word ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 4|Matthew 4:4]]), destroys the works of the devil ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 8|1 John 3:8]]), purges idolatry from God's people ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 16|John 2:16]]), and establishes a holy nation ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).

Jesus' commission from the Father parallels Joshua's commission from Yahweh. Just as God said to Joshua, "Be strong and courageous, for I am with you," so the Father sent the Son with divine power and presence ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 30|John 10:30]]). Just as Joshua meditated on the Law day and night, so Jesus perfectly embodied Torah, fulfilling it down to the smallest letter ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 17|Matthew 5:17-18]]).

The *herem* command finds fulfillment in <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Christ's destruction of sin and death</span> through His cross and resurrection. What Joshua accomplished partially and typologically—destroying God's enemies—Christ accomplished fully and finally. He "disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]).

The prohibition against covenants with idolaters finds fulfillment in the church's call to <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">separation from the world</span>. Believers are not to be "unequally yoked with unbelievers" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 14|2 Corinthians 6:14]]) or participate in idolatrous practices ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 14|1 Corinthians 10:14]]). The church must maintain covenant distinctiveness in the midst of pagan culture.

The commission to be holy finds fulfillment in the church as <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">a holy nation, a people for God's own possession</span> ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]). Believers are set apart through Christ's sanctifying work, made holy by His blood and Spirit ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 10|Hebrews 10:10]]; [[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 5#1 Thessalonians 5 . 23|1 Thessalonians 5:23]]).

Most comprehensively, the commission to expand God's kingdom finds fulfillment in the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">Great Commission</span>: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]]).

Just as Joshua was commissioned to take possession of Canaan, so the church is commissioned to make disciples of all nations. Just as God promised to be with Joshua, so Christ promises to be with the church. Just as Israel's success depended on Torah obedience, so the church's effectiveness depends on teaching obedience to Christ's commands. The pattern is consistent: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">God commissions His people to extend His kingdom through faithful obedience empowered by His presence</span>.

The conquest under Joshua was glorious but incomplete. The conquest under Jesus is comprehensive and eternal. Joshua conquered one land; Jesus conquers the cosmos. Joshua destroyed physical enemies; Jesus destroys spiritual powers. Joshua brought temporary rest; Jesus brings eternal rest. Joshua's commission anticipated Christ; Christ's commission fulfills all that Joshua foreshadowed.
