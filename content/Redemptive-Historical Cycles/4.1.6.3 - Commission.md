## The Commission: From Jacob to Israel

The commission phase of Jacob's intrusion occurs in two major moments: first at Bethel, where God confirms the Abrahamic covenant promises ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 13|Genesis 28:13-15]]), and second at Peniel, where Jacob wrestles with God and receives a new name—Israel ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 28|Genesis 32:28]]). Together, these encounters commission Jacob to be the father of God's covenant people, bearer of the promise to bless all nations, and embodiment of persistent faith that strives with God and prevails.

---

## Primary Biblical Texts

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 13|Genesis 28:13-15]]**: God's covenant confirmation at Bethel
**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 22|Genesis 32:22-32]]**: Wrestling with God at Peniel and receiving the name Israel
**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 35#Genesis 35 . 9|Genesis 35:9-12]]**: Covenant renewal at Bethel after return from Haran

## First Commission: Bethel and the Covenant Promises

### 1. The Identity of the Covenant God

God begins His commission by identifying Himself in relation to Jacob's forefathers:

> "I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 13|Genesis 28:13]])

**Theological Significance**:

- **Covenant Continuity**: The same God who appeared to Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1]]) and Isaac ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 26#Genesis 26 . 24|Genesis 26:24]]) now appears to Jacob
- **Generational Faithfulness**: God keeps covenant across generations
- **Personal Relationship**: Not merely "the God of your ancestors" but entering into direct relationship with Jacob
- **Divine Name**: "I am the LORD" (YHWH) reveals the covenant name of God

This establishes that Jacob's commission is not a new initiative but the continuation of God's plan to redeem humanity through Abraham's line.

### 2. The Land Promise

> "The land on which you lie I will give to you and to your offspring." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 13|Genesis 28:13]])

**Elements of the Promise**:

- **Immediate Connection**: "On which you lie"—the very ground beneath Jacob belongs to his descendants
- **Personal and Corporate**: "To you and to your offspring"—both Jacob and his descendants
- **Unconditional Gift**: "I will give"—not "you will earn" or "you might obtain"
- **Territorial Inheritance**: The land promised to Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]) now confirmed to Jacob

**Edenic Restoration Theme**:

The land promise represents Edenic restoration—a place where God's people dwell in His presence, cultivating the earth under His blessing. This looks backward to:

- **Eden**: The garden where Adam was placed to work and keep ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]])
- **Creation Mandate**: "Fill the earth and subdue it" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])

And forward to:

- **Canaan Conquest**: Israel inheriting the land under Joshua ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 21#Joshua 21 . 43|Joshua 21:43-45]])
- **Davidic Kingdom**: Territorial expansion under David and Solomon ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 21|1 Kings 4:21]])
- **New Creation**: "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 5|Matthew 5:5]])
- **New Heaven and Earth**: The ultimate inheritance ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1]])

### 3. The Multiplication Promise

> "Your offspring shall be like the dust of the earth, and you shall spread abroad to the west and to the east and to the north and to the south." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 14|Genesis 28:14a]])

**Imagery and Scope**:

- **Dust of the Earth**: Innumerable multitude, echoing God's promise to Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 16|Genesis 13:16]])—"if one can count the dust of the earth, your offspring also can be counted"
- **Four Directions**: Comprehensive expansion—west, east, north, south—covering all the earth
- **Spreading Abroad**: Active expansion, not passive existence

**Creation Mandate Fulfillment**:

This promise explicitly echoes the creation mandate: "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Through Jacob's descendants, God will accomplish the original Adamic commission to fill the earth with His image-bearers.

**Fulfillment in Stages**:

- **Twelve Tribes**: Jacob's twelve sons become the twelve tribes of Israel ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 28|Genesis 49:28]])
- **Egyptian Multiplication**: "The people of Israel were fruitful and increased greatly" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 1#Exodus 1 . 7|Exodus 1:7]])
- **Conquest and Kingdom**: Nation established in the land
- **Gospel Expansion**: "In Christ Jesus you are all sons of Abraham" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 26|Galatians 3:26-29]])
- **Revelation's Multitude**: "A great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]])

### 4. The Universal Blessing Promise

> "In you and your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 14|Genesis 28:14b]])

**The Missional Core**:

This is the theological heart of the commission—Jacob and his descendants exist not merely for their own benefit but to channel blessing to all nations. Key elements:

- **In You and Your Offspring**: Both corporate (the nation Israel) and singular (the promised Seed, Christ)
- **All Families of the Earth**: Universal scope—every ethnic group, tribe, and nation
- **Shall Be Blessed**: Passive voice—they will receive blessing mediated through Jacob's line

**Galatians 3 Commentary**:

Paul interprets this promise Christologically:

> "The promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' who is Christ." ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 16|Galatians 3:16]])

The ultimate "offspring" is Jesus Christ, through whom all nations receive the blessing of justification by faith ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 8|Galatians 3:8-9]]).

**Israel's Missional Identity**:

This commission establishes Israel's identity as a:

- **Priestly Kingdom**: Mediating God's blessing to the nations ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]])
- **Light to the Nations**: "I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|Isaiah 49:6]])
- **Servant of the LORD**: Through whom God's righteousness and justice come to the world ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 1|Isaiah 42:1-4]])

### 5. The Presence Promise

> "Behold, I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land. For I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 15|Genesis 28:15]])

**New Element in Covenant**:

While Abraham and Isaac received promises of land, offspring, and blessing, this explicit promise of divine presence is emphasized particularly for Jacob. This is appropriate because:

- **Jacob is Leaving the Land**: He needs assurance that God goes with him into exile
- **Jacob Faces Danger**: From Laban's potential exploitation and Esau's murderous intent
- **Jacob Needs Transformation**: God's presence will shape him from deceiver to Israel

**Three-Part Presence Promise**:

a) **"I am with you"**: Present-tense companionship—God is currently with Jacob
b) **"Will keep you wherever you go"**: Future protection in all locations—Haran, return journey, etc.
c) **"Will bring you back to this land"**: Promise of return—exile is temporary, not permanent

**Absolute Certainty**:

"For I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 15|Genesis 28:15]])

- **Divine Commitment**: God stakes His faithfulness on completing His promises
- **Irrevocable Purpose**: Nothing can prevent God from accomplishing His will
- **Personal Assurance**: Jacob can trust completely in God's word

**Emmanuel Pattern**:

This presence promise establishes a pattern throughout Scripture:

- **Moses**: "I will be with you" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 12|Exodus 3:12]])
- **Joshua**: "I will be with you. I will not leave you or forsake you" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 5|Joshua 1:5]])
- **Jeremiah**: "I am with you to deliver you" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 1#Jeremiah 1 . 8|Jeremiah 1:8]])
- **Disciples**: "I am with you always, to the end of the age" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 20|Matthew 28:20]])
- **Church**: "I will never leave you nor forsake you" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 5|Hebrews 13:5]])

## Second Commission: Peniel and the Name Change

### 1. The Context: Wrestling in the Night

Years later, as Jacob returns to the promised land, he faces the terror of meeting Esau. In his fear, he wrestles all night with a mysterious figure:

> "And Jacob was left alone. And a man wrestled with him until the breaking of the day." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 24|Genesis 32:24]])

**Identity of the Wrestler**:

- **Appears as a Man**: Physical, tangible struggle
- **Supernatural Power**: Touches Jacob's hip and dislocates it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 25|Genesis 32:25]])
- **Divine Authority**: Changes Jacob's name and blesses him
- **God Himself**: Jacob declares, "I have seen God face to face" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 30|Genesis 32:30]])
- **Angel of the LORD**: Likely a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ (theophany)

**Theological Significance of Wrestling**:

The wrestling match is both physical and spiritual, representing:

- **Persistent Faith**: Jacob refuses to let go until blessed ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 26|Genesis 32:26]])
- **Honest Struggle**: Wrestling with God is permitted, even expected
- **Human Limitation**: Jacob's hip is dislocated—human strength must be broken
- **Divine Condescension**: God allows Himself to be "overcome" by Jacob's persistence
- **Transformative Encounter**: Jacob emerges permanently changed (limping) and renamed

### 2. The Question: "What is Your Name?"

> "Then he said to him, 'What is your name?' And he said, 'Jacob.'" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 27|Genesis 32:27]])

**Significance of the Question**:

- **Confession of Identity**: Jacob must acknowledge who he is—"heel-grabber," "supplanter," "deceiver"
- **Recognition of Need**: Admitting his true character before transformation can occur
- **Covenant Pattern**: Name-giving indicates relationship and authority

This echoes Adam naming the animals ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 19|Genesis 2:19-20]]) and God naming Abram as Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 5|Genesis 17:5]])—naming indicates dominion and covenant relationship.

### 3. The New Name: Israel

> "Then he said, 'Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel, for you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed.'" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 28|Genesis 32:28]])

**Etymology and Meaning**:

- **Jacob** (יַעֲקֹב, Ya'akov): "heel-grabber" or "supplanter"—recalls his birth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 26|Genesis 25:26]]) and his deception
- **Israel** (יִשְׂרָאֵל, Yisra'el): "he strives with God" or "God strives"—from שָׂרָה (sarah, "to strive/contend") + אֵל (El, "God")

**Explanation Given**:

"For you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed"

- **Striven with God**: The all-night wrestling at Peniel
- **Striven with Men**: Conflicts with Esau, Laban, and others
- **Prevailed**: Not through deceit but through persistent faith—refusing to release God without blessing

**Theological Depth**:

The name "Israel" encapsulates the identity of God's people:

- **Wrestling in Faith**: Believers struggle with God in prayer and faith, not passive acceptance
- **Persistence Rewarded**: "The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working" ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 5#James 5 . 16|James 5:16]])
- **Transformation Through Encounter**: Brokenness (dislocated hip) leads to blessing
- **Corporate Identity**: Jacob becomes Israel, and his descendants are "the children of Israel"

**Pattern of Name Changes in Scripture**:

- **Abram → Abraham**: "Father of many nations" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 5|Genesis 17:5]])
- **Sarai → Sarah**: "Princess" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 15|Genesis 17:15]])
- **Jacob → Israel**: "Strives with God" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 28|Genesis 32:28]])
- **Simon → Peter**: "Rock" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 42|John 1:42]])
- **Saul → Paul**: Apostle to the Gentiles ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 9|Acts 13:9]])
- **Believers**: Given new name in Christ ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 2#Revelation 2 . 17|Revelation 2:17]])

### 4. The Blessing and the Limp

> "Then Jacob asked him, 'Please tell me your name.' But he said, 'Why is it that you ask my name?' And there he blessed him." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 29|Genesis 32:29]])

God refuses to give His name (mystery remains), but grants the blessing Jacob sought. Yet Jacob emerges from the encounter permanently marked:

> "The sun rose upon him as he passed Penuel, limping because of his hip." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 32#Genesis 32 . 31|Genesis 32:31]])

**The Permanent Limp**:

- **Physical Reminder**: Every step reminds Jacob of his encounter with God
- **Brokenness and Blessing**: Blessing comes through brokenness, not human strength
- **Humility**: The deceiver who once relied on his own cunning now walks with visible weakness
- **Divine Power in Human Weakness**: Prefigures Paul's "thorn in the flesh" and God's declaration, "My power is made perfect in weakness" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 12#2 Corinthians 12 . 9|2 Corinthians 12:9]])

## Third Commission: Bethel Renewal

### 1. Return to Bethel

After the violent episode at Shechem ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 34#Genesis 34 . 25|Genesis 34:25-31]]), God commands Jacob to return to Bethel:

> "God said to Jacob, 'Arise, go up to Bethel and dwell there. Make an altar there to the God who appeared to you when you fled from your brother Esau.'" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 35#Genesis 35 . 1|Genesis 35:1]])

Jacob responds by purifying his household:

> "So Jacob said to his household and to all who were with him, 'Put away the foreign gods that are among you and purify yourselves and change your garments. Then let us arise and go up to Bethel, so that I may make there an altar to the God who answers me in the day of my distress and has been with me wherever I have gone.'" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 35#Genesis 35 . 2|Genesis 35:2-3]])

**Purification Elements**:

- **Remove Foreign Gods**: Rachel's stolen household gods ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 31#Genesis 31 . 19|Genesis 31:19]]) and others acquired in Haran
- **Purify Yourselves**: Ritual cleansing before worship
- **Change Garments**: External sign of internal transformation

This establishes covenant renewal pattern throughout Scripture (Joshua 24, Josiah's reform, Nehemiah 9-10).

### 2. Covenant Reaffirmation

At Bethel, God appears again and renews the covenant:

> "God appeared to Jacob again, when he came from Paddan-aram, and blessed him. And God said to him, 'Your name is Jacob; no longer shall your name be called Jacob, but Israel shall be your name.' So he called his name Israel." ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 35#Genesis 35 . 9|Genesis 35:9-10]])

God then reaffirms all the covenant promises:

> "And God said to him, 'I am God Almighty: be fruitful and multiply. A nation and a company of nations shall come from you, and kings shall come from your own body. The land that I gave to Abraham and Isaac I will give to you, and I will give the land to your offspring after you.'" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 35#Genesis 35 . 11|Genesis 35:11-12]])

**New Elements**:

- **"Be Fruitful and Multiply"**: Direct echo of the Adamic commission ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])
- **"A Nation and Company of Nations"**: Not just one nation but multiple peoples from Jacob's line
- **"Kings Shall Come from Your Body"**: Royal lineage—anticipates David and ultimately Christ

## Theological Analysis

### Commission as Covenant Mediation

Jacob's commission establishes him as the mediator of the Abrahamic covenant to the third generation. Through him:

- **God's Promises Continue**: Unbroken line from Abraham to Isaac to Jacob
- **Corporate People Formed**: The twelve tribes emerge from Jacob's sons
- **Missional Purpose Maintained**: Universal blessing remains the goal

### Wrestling as the Pattern of Faith

The Peniel encounter reveals that true faith involves:

- **Honest Struggle**: Believers can wrestle with God in prayer and doubt
- **Persistent Seeking**: "Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 7#Matthew 7 . 7|Matthew 7:7]])
- **Transformation Through Brokenness**: Human strength must be disabled for divine strength to operate
- **Blessing Through Encounter**: Real faith means meeting God, not merely believing facts about God

### Israel as Corporate Identity

The name "Israel" becomes the identity of the entire covenant people:

- **Children of Israel**: Jacob's descendants corporately identified
- **Nation Israel**: Political and ethnic entity
- **Spiritual Israel**: Those who share Abraham's faith ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 9#Romans 9 . 6|Romans 9:6]]—"not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel")
- **True Israel**: Jesus Christ, who perfectly fulfills Israel's calling ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 15|Matthew 2:15]])

## Christological Fulfillment

### Christ as the True Israel

Where Jacob strove with God and prevailed through persistence, Christ perfectly obeyed the Father:

- **Wrestling in Gethsemane**: "Not my will, but yours, be done" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 42|Luke 22:42]])
- **Perfect Obedience**: "Although he was a son, he learned obedience through what he suffered" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 8|Hebrews 5:8]])
- **Fulfilling Israel's Mission**: Bringing blessing to all nations through His death and resurrection

### Believers as Israel in Christ

Through union with Christ, believers receive:

- **New Name**: "Christian"—belonging to Christ
- **Covenant Promises**: All God's promises are "Yes" in Christ ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 1#2 Corinthians 1 . 20|2 Corinthians 1:20]])
- **Missional Calling**: "You will be my witnesses... to the end of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 8|Acts 1:8]])

---

## Application for the People of God

### Persistent Prayer

Jacob's wrestling teaches believers to persist in prayer, refusing to let go until God answers ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 18#Luke 18 . 1|Luke 18:1-8]]—parable of the persistent widow).

### Transformation Through Brokenness

The limp reminds us that God often works through our weaknesses, not our strengths. Brokenness becomes the path to blessing.

### Identity in Christ

Just as Jacob became Israel, believers receive new identity in Christ—no longer defined by past failures but by God's grace.
