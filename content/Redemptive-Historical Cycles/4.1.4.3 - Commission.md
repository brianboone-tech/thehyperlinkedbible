# 4.1.4.3 - Commission - Adamic Mandate Restated to Abraham (Genesis 12:1-3, 7-8)

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.4 - Intrusion - Call of Abraham|← Back to 4.1.4]]

---

## Overview

God's call to Abraham is not merely a personal summons but a **corporate commission**—a restatement of the Adamic mandate given to a new representative head. Just as Adam was commissioned to be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth, and extend God's temple-presence, Abraham receives a parallel commission with three primary dimensions: **land** (a place to dwell), **seed** (descendants to multiply), and **blessing** (a mission to the nations). The commission is both particular (go to a specific land) and universal (bless all families of the earth), establishing the pattern for all subsequent redemptive intrusions.

**Key Theme**: Abraham inherits the Adamic commission as a new corporate head, called to multiply a people, possess a land, expand God's worship, and mediate blessing to all nations—ultimately fulfilled in Christ.

---

## Primary Texts

### Genesis 12:1-3 - The Threefold Commission

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1-3]]**: "Now the LORD said to Abram, 'Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house to the land that I will show you. And I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. I will bless those who bless you, and him who dishonors you I will curse, and in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed.'"

### Genesis 12:7-8 - Land Promise and Altar Building

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7-8]]**: "Then the LORD appeared to Abram and said, 'To your offspring I will give this land.' So he built there an altar to the LORD, who had appeared to him. From there he moved to the hill country on the east of Bethel and pitched his tent, with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east. And there he built an altar to the LORD and called upon the name of the LORD."

---

## The Threefold Commission: Land, Seed, Blessing

The Abrahamic commission can be understood through three interlocking promises, each echoing aspects of the original Adamic mandate:

### 1. Land: "To the Land That I Will Show You"

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1]]**: "Go from your country and your kindred and your father's house **to the land that I will show you**."

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]**: "To your offspring **I will give this land**."

**Parallel to Adam's Commission**: Just as Adam was placed in Eden—a specific geographic location where God's presence dwelt ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 8|Genesis 2:8]]—"the LORD God planted a garden in Eden")—Abraham is called to a specific land. Eden was the **prototype sanctuary**, and Canaan will become the **typological sanctuary** where God's people dwell in His presence.

**The Land as Gift**: The land is not earned but **given** ("I will give"). Abraham must go by faith, not knowing the destination ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 8|Hebrews 11:8]]—"He went out, not knowing where he was going"). This establishes the pattern: **God's promises precede human obedience**.

**The Land as Place of Blessing**: The land is where the commission will be fulfilled—where Abraham's descendants will multiply, where they will worship, where God's presence will dwell in tabernacle and temple. The land is not merely real estate but **sacred space**, the theater of redemption.

**Connection to Dominion Mandate**: Adam was to "subdue" the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Abraham's descendants will be called to "possess" the land ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 7|Genesis 15:7]]—"I am the LORD who brought you out from Ur of the Chaldeans to give you this land to possess"). Both involve exercising God-given authority over territory.

**The Land Occupied**: When Abraham arrives, "the Canaanites were in the land" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 6|Genesis 12:6]]). The promise is real, but **not yet fulfilled**. Abraham must walk through the land by faith, trusting God's future fulfillment. This creates the **already-but-not-yet** tension that characterizes all redemptive intrusions.

**Typological Fulfillment**: The land promise finds its ultimate fulfillment not in a geographic territory but in **Christ and the new creation**:
- [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 13|Romans 4:13]]—"For the promise to Abraham and his offspring that he would be heir of **the world** did not come through the law but through the righteousness of faith"
- [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 16|Hebrews 11:16]]—"But as it is, they desire **a better country, that is, a heavenly one**. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city"
- [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-3]]—"Then I saw **a new heaven and a new earth**... And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man'"

The **land** promised to Abraham expands to encompass the entire **renewed creation**, where God's people dwell in His presence forever.

### 2. Seed: "I Will Make of You a Great Nation"

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]]**: "And **I will make of you a great nation**, and I will bless you and make your name great."

**Parallel to Adam's Commission**: Adam was commanded to "be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Abraham receives the same commission: multiply into a **great nation**.

**The Impossibility**: This promise is given to a man whose wife is **barren** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]]—"Now Sarai was barren; she had no child"). The commission, from the outset, requires **miraculous intervention**. The seed promise cannot be fulfilled by human power but only by divine grace.

**Corporate Multiplication**: The promise is not just for many descendants but for a **nation** (גּוֹי, *goy*)—an organized people with identity, government, and purpose. Abraham will become the **corporate head** of a covenant community, a new humanity.

**Later Amplifications**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 16|Genesis 13:16]]—"I will make your offspring as the dust of the earth, so that if one can count the dust of the earth, your offspring also can be counted"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 5|Genesis 15:5]]—"Look toward heaven, and number the stars, if you are able to number them... So shall your offspring be"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 6|Genesis 17:6]]—"I will make you exceedingly fruitful, and I will make you into nations, and kings shall come from you"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 17|Genesis 22:17]]—"I will surely bless you, and I will surely multiply your offspring as the stars of heaven and as the sand that is on the seashore"

**The Narrowing of the Seed**: While the promise speaks of **many** descendants, the singular "offspring" (זֶרַע, *zera*) also appears ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]—"To your **offspring** I will give this land"). This singular form points to a specific individual who will fulfill the promise.

**[[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 16|Galatians 3:16]]** makes this explicit: "Now the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' **who is Christ**."

The seed promise has **dual fulfillment**:
1. **Corporate**: Abraham's physical descendants (Israel) and spiritual descendants (the church)—[[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 29|Galatians 3:29]]—"And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise"
2. **Individual**: The ultimate Seed is **Jesus Christ**, through whom the nations are blessed

**Beale's Framework**: The seed promise is the continuation of the protoevangelium—the "seed of the woman" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]) who will crush the serpent's head. Abraham's seed narrows the promise: the Seed will come through this specific lineage.

### 3. Blessing: "In You All the Families of the Earth Shall Be Blessed"

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]**: "And **in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed**."

This is the **universal scope** of the commission. Though God narrows His focus to one man, the purpose is **global redemption**. Abraham is blessed not for his sake alone but to be a **channel of blessing** to all peoples.

**Parallel to Adam's Commission**: Adam was to fill the **entire earth** with God's image-bearers, extending God's rule and presence globally. Abraham inherits this universal mission: through his seed, **all nations** will be brought into covenant relationship with God.

**The Reversal of Babel**: At Babel, the nations were **scattered** in judgment ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 8|Genesis 11:8-9]]). The phrase "all the families of the earth" (כֹּל מִשְׁפְּחֹת הָאֲדָמָה, *kol mishpachot ha'adamah*) echoes the **Table of Nations** in Genesis 10, where humanity is divided into families, languages, and territories.

Now, through Abraham's seed, the nations **scattered in curse** will be **gathered in blessing**. What Babel fragmented, Abraham's seed will reunite.

**The Nature of the Blessing**:
- **Spiritual**: Justification by faith ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 8|Galatians 3:8-9]]—"And the Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'In you shall all the nations be blessed.' So then, those who are of faith are blessed along with Abraham, the man of faith")
- **Eschatological**: Eternal life in the new creation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 1|Revelation 22:1-5]]—nations walking in the light of the New Jerusalem)
- **Covenantal**: Adoption into God's family ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 12|Ephesians 2:12-13]]—Gentiles, once "alienated from the commonwealth of Israel," are now "brought near by the blood of Christ")

**Missional Dimension**: Abraham's commission includes a **priestly** function—mediating God's blessing to the world. This anticipates:
- **Israel's calling**: "You shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]])
- **The church's mission**: "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood... that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]])

**Fulfillment in Christ**: Jesus is the ultimate **blessing** to the nations:
- [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 32|Luke 2:32]]—"a light for revelation to the Gentiles, and for glory to your people Israel"
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 16|John 3:16]]—"For God so loved **the world**, that he gave his only Son"
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]]—"Go therefore and make disciples of **all nations**"
- [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]—"a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages"

Through Christ, Abraham's seed, the **blessing** reaches to the ends of the earth.

## Altar Building: Expanding the Temple-Presence

### First Altar at Shechem (Genesis 12:7)

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]**: "Then the LORD appeared to Abram and said, 'To your offspring I will give this land.' **So he built there an altar to the LORD**, who had appeared to him."

**Significance of the Altar**:
- **Worship**: The altar is a place of sacrifice and prayer, signifying Abraham's devotion to Yahweh
- **Claim**: By building an altar, Abraham is symbolically **claiming the land** for God—this is sacred territory
- **Testimony**: In a land full of Canaanite idolatry, the altar is a **public declaration** of allegiance to the true God
- **Priestly Function**: Abraham acts as a **priest**, offering sacrifices and mediating between God and the land

**Beale's Framework**: The altar functions as a **proto-temple**—a localized place where heaven and earth meet, where God's presence is invoked. Adam was placed in Eden, the original temple-garden, to "work and keep" it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]]—language of priestly service). Now Abraham builds altars as **mini-sanctuaries**, beginning the process of extending God's temple-presence into Canaan.

**Pattern Throughout Abraham's Life**:
- **Shechem**: First altar upon entering the land ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]])
- **Bethel**: Second altar, "called on the name of the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 8|Genesis 12:8]])
- **Hebron**: Third altar after separating from Lot ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 18|Genesis 13:18]])
- **Moriah**: The climactic altar where Isaac is offered ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 9|Genesis 22:9]])

Each altar marks a **stage of faith**, a deepening of covenant relationship, and a further extension of God's presence.

### Second Altar at Bethel (Genesis 12:8)

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 8|Genesis 12:8]]**: "From there he moved to the hill country on the east of **Bethel** and pitched his tent, with Bethel on the west and Ai on the east. And there **he built an altar to the LORD and called upon the name of the LORD**."

**Bethel**: The name means "house of God" (בֵּית־אֵל, *Beit-El*). Later, Jacob will have a vision of the heavenly ladder at this very place and rename it Bethel ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 28#Genesis 28 . 19|Genesis 28:19]]). Abraham, without knowing it, is building an altar at a site that will become **central to Israel's worship**.

**Calling on the Name of the LORD**: The phrase קָרָא בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה (*qara b'shem YHWH*) indicates **public, vocal worship**. Abraham is not worshiping in secret but **proclaiming** Yahweh's name in a land of idolaters. This is bold faith and missionary witness.

This phrase echoes [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 26|Genesis 4:26]]—"At that time people began to call upon the name of the LORD"—indicating that Abraham is continuing the **line of true worshipers** (Seth's line) in contrast to the idolatrous Canaanites.

**Tent and Altar**: Abraham **pitches his tent** (temporary dwelling, pilgrim status) and **builds an altar** (permanent worship). This paradox defines the life of faith:
- **Tent**: Abraham is a **sojourner**, not settling permanently, recognizing that this world is not his final home ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 9|Hebrews 11:9]]—"By faith he went to live in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents")
- **Altar**: Abraham commits to **worship** wherever he goes, establishing God's presence even as a pilgrim

This establishes the pattern for God's people: **in the world but not of it**, living as exiles yet establishing outposts of worship.

**Beale's Insight**: Abraham's altars are the beginning of the **temple-expansion project**. Just as Adam was to extend Eden's borders by multiplying image-bearers who worship God, Abraham begins to establish **sacred sites** throughout Canaan, preparing the land for God's dwelling. These altars anticipate:
- The **tabernacle** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]—"Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst")
- The **temple** ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]]—"the glory of the LORD filled the house")
- **Christ himself** ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]—"The Word became flesh and **dwelt** among us")
- The **church** ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 21|Ephesians 2:21-22]]—"the whole structure... grows into a holy temple in the Lord")
- The **new creation** ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]—"Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man")

Abraham's commission to build altars is the **first step** in God's plan to fill the earth with His glory.

## Christological Fulfillment

Where Abraham **begins** to fulfill the commission, **Christ completes** it:

### Land: Christ Inherits the Earth

Abraham was promised **Canaan**, but the ultimate fulfillment is **cosmic**:
- [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 13|Romans 4:13]]—"For the promise to Abraham and his offspring that he would be **heir of the world** did not come through the law but through the righteousness of faith"
- [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 5|Matthew 5:5]]—"Blessed are the meek, for they shall **inherit the earth**"
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 2#Psalm 2 . 8|Psalm 2:8]]—"Ask of me, and I will make the nations your heritage, and the ends of the earth your possession"

Christ receives **all authority in heaven and on earth** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18]]) and will reign over the **new creation** ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-5]]).

### Seed: Christ Multiplies Spiritual Children

Abraham was promised **physical descendants**, but Christ produces **spiritual offspring**:
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 12|John 1:12-13]]—"But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become **children of God**, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God"
- [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 29|Galatians 3:29]]—"And if you are Christ's, then you are **Abraham's offspring**, heirs according to promise"
- [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]—"a great multitude that no one could number, **from every nation**, from all tribes and peoples and languages"

Christ's seed is **innumerable**, drawn from every corner of the earth, fulfilling the promise to Abraham in superabundance.

### Blessing: Christ Blesses All Nations

Abraham was called to **mediate** blessing, but Christ is the **source** of all blessing:
- [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 3|Ephesians 1:3]]—"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with **every spiritual blessing** in the heavenly places"
- [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 14|Galatians 3:14]]—"so that in Christ Jesus **the blessing of Abraham** might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith"
- [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 3#Acts 3 . 25|Acts 3:25-26]]—"And you are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with your fathers, saying to Abraham, 'And in your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed.' God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, **to bless you** by turning every one of you from your wickedness"

Through Christ, the **blessing** reaches to the ends of the earth, and **all families** are invited into covenant relationship with God.

### Worship: Christ Builds the True Temple

Abraham built **altars**, but Christ is the **ultimate temple**:
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]—"Jesus answered them, 'Destroy **this temple**, and in three days I will raise it up'... But he was speaking about **the temple of his body**"
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 23|John 4:23-24]]—"But the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father **in spirit and truth**"
- [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22|Revelation 21:22]]—"And I saw no temple in the city, for **its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb**"

Christ fulfills the altar-building commission by **becoming** the meeting place between God and man, the ultimate sacrifice, and the eternal dwelling of God's presence.

## Summary: Commission Given, Partially Fulfilled, Awaiting Christ

Abraham receives the **Adamic commission** in threefold form:
1. **Land**: Go to Canaan, receive it as a gift, dwell in God's presence
2. **Seed**: Multiply into a great nation, produce descendants beyond number
3. **Blessing**: Be a channel of blessing to all families of the earth

This commission is **real** and Abraham **begins** to fulfill it:
- He goes to Canaan ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 4|Genesis 12:4-5]])
- He builds altars and worships ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7-8]])
- He trusts God for offspring despite barrenness ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]])

Yet the commission is **not completed**:
- Abraham lives as a **sojourner**, never fully possessing the land ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 13|Hebrews 11:13]]—"These all died in faith, not having received the things promised")
- He has one son (Isaac) in his lifetime, not the multitude promised
- The blessing to the nations is still **future**, awaiting the ultimate Seed

The commission, like all Old Testament intrusions, **points beyond itself** to the One who will fully and finally accomplish it—**Jesus Christ**, the true Seed of Abraham, through whom all the families of the earth are blessed.
