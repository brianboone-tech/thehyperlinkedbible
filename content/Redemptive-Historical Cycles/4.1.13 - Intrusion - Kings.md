# 4.1.13 - Intrusion - Kings

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1 - The Delay - The Age of Preparation|← Back to 4.1]]

---

## Overview

**Biblical Scope**: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 1|1 Samuel 8:1]] - [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 30|2 Kings 25:30]]

The monarchy period represents Israel's most dramatic attempt to establish God's kingdom on earth through human kingship. Beginning with Israel's demand for "a king... like all the nations" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 5|1 Samuel 8:5]]) and culminating in the destruction of Jerusalem and exile to Babylon ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 1|2 Kings 25:1-21]]), this era demonstrates both the **glory of God's covenant with David** and the **tragic failure of human kingship** apart from heart transformation.

The period divides into two main phases:

**United Monarchy** (c. 1050-930 BC): Saul, David, Solomon—three kings ruling over all twelve tribes. This period begins in failure (Saul's rejection), reaches its zenith under David (the Davidic covenant, [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 1|2 Samuel 7]]), and culminates in both glory and decline under Solomon (temple built, but idolatry introduced through foreign wives).

**Divided Kingdom** (930-586 BC): Israel (Northern Kingdom, ten tribes) and Judah (Southern Kingdom, two tribes) separate under Rehoboam and Jeroboam. Israel spirals into idolatry, never having a godly king, and is destroyed by Assyria in 722 BC ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 17#2 Kings 17 . 6|2 Kings 17:6]]). Judah has occasional reforming kings (Hezekiah, Josiah) but ultimately follows Israel into exile, conquered by Babylon in 586 BC ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 1|2 Kings 25:1-21]]).

G.K. Beale demonstrates that the monarchy was intended to fulfill Israel's **corporate Adamic commission**—the king was to be a new Adam, leading God's people in temple expansion, subduing enemies, and bringing the nations under God's rule. David's reign and the building of Solomon's temple represent the **high point of Old Testament temple expansion**, with Israel's borders reaching their greatest extent and God's glory filling the temple ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]]). Yet even this glorious intrusion **does not consummate the age**—Solomon sins, the kingdom divides, and both kingdoms slide into apostasy and exile.

Meredith Kline's framework helps us see the monarchy as a series of **upper-register intrusions** (David's covenant, Solomon's temple, reforming kings) that fail to transform Israel's heart. The Davidic covenant in [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 1|2 Samuel 7]] becomes the most significant Old Testament promise pointing forward to Christ—God pledges an **eternal king** from David's line whose kingdom will never end. The failure of every Davidic king demonstrates that only the **greater David**, Jesus Christ, can fulfill this promise.

### The Pattern of Intrusion

The monarchy period follows the fivefold pattern, but with increasing escalation toward final judgment:

**Chaos**: Israel demands a king "like all the nations" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 5|1 Samuel 8:5]]), rejecting Yahweh as their true King ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 7|1 Samuel 8:7]]). Saul is anointed but quickly disobeys ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 13#1 Samuel 13 . 8|1 Samuel 13:8-14]]; [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 1|1 Samuel 15:1-23]]), leading to his rejection. After the kingdom divides, Jeroboam establishes golden calves at Dan and Bethel ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 28|1 Kings 12:28-33]]), corrupting worship from the start. Subsequent kings follow "the sin of Jeroboam" ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 15#1 Kings 15 . 34|1 Kings 15:34]], etc.).

**Creation**: God raises David as His chosen king ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 1|1 Samuel 16:1-13]]), anointed by the Spirit. David defeats Goliath ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 17#1 Samuel 17 . 45|1 Samuel 17:45-50]]), conquers Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 5#2 Samuel 5 . 6|2 Samuel 5:6-10]]), brings the ark to Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 6#2 Samuel 6 . 12|2 Samuel 6:12-15]]), and receives the covenant promise of an eternal dynasty ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 11|2 Samuel 7:11-16]]). Solomon builds the temple ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 1|1 Kings 6-8]]), and God's glory fills it ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]])—the **zenith of Old Testament temple theology**.

**Commission**: The king is commissioned to lead Israel in covenant faithfulness, expand God's kingdom, rule with justice, and build/maintain the temple. Solomon is given wisdom to judge ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 3#1 Kings 3 . 9|1 Kings 3:9-12]]), and the nations come to hear his wisdom ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 34|1 Kings 4:34]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 10#1 Kings 10 . 1|1 Kings 10:1-13]])—a preview of the nations streaming to Zion.

**Sin**: Yet every king fails. Saul disobeys ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 22|1 Samuel 15:22-23]]). David commits adultery and murder ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 11#2 Samuel 11 . 1|2 Samuel 11-12]]). Solomon multiplies wives and turns to idolatry ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 11#1 Kings 11 . 1|1 Kings 11:1-8]]). The northern kings are universally wicked. Even Judah's reforming kings (Hezekiah, Josiah) cannot prevent the nation's slide toward exile.

**Judgment**: Saul dies on the battlefield ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 31#1 Samuel 31 . 1|1 Samuel 31:1-6]]). David's house is struck with violence ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 10|2 Samuel 12:10-12]]). The kingdom divides ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 16|1 Kings 12:16-20]]). Israel falls to Assyria (722 BC, [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 17#2 Kings 17 . 6|2 Kings 17:6-23]]). Judah falls to Babylon (586 BC, [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 1|2 Kings 25:1-21]]), the temple is destroyed, and the Davidic line appears to end.

## Major Theological Themes

### 1. Israel's Rejection of Yahweh as King

Israel's demand for a king is fundamentally a **rejection of God's kingship**:

"And the LORD said to Samuel, 'Obey the voice of the people in all that they say to you, for **they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me from being king over them**'" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 7|1 Samuel 8:7]]).

Samuel warns of the consequences: kings will take their sons for military service, their daughters for domestic service, their fields and vineyards, and a tenth of their produce ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 10|1 Samuel 8:10-18]]). Yet the people insist: "No! But there shall be a king over us, that we also may be **like all the nations**" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 19|1 Samuel 8:19-20]]).

This desire to be "like all the nations" contradicts Israel's calling to be a **holy nation, set apart** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]; [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 20#Leviticus 20 . 26|Leviticus 20:26]]). Yet paradoxically, God **grants their request** and uses the monarchy to advance His redemptive plan, ultimately pointing to Christ the **true King**.

### 2. Saul: The King After the Flesh

Saul represents the king chosen according to **human standards**—tall, handsome, impressive in appearance ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 9#1 Samuel 9 . 2|1 Samuel 9:2]]; [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 10#1 Samuel 10 . 23|1 Samuel 10:23-24]]). He is "a choice young man... there was not a man among the people of Israel more handsome than he."

Yet Saul's reign demonstrates the **inadequacy of external qualifications**. He quickly fails through:

**Impatience and presumption**: Saul offers sacrifice without waiting for Samuel ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 13#1 Samuel 13 . 8|1 Samuel 13:8-14]]), usurping the priestly role. Samuel declares: "You have done foolishly... your kingdom shall not continue."

**Partial obedience**: Saul is commanded to "devote to destruction" all the Amalekites ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 3|1 Samuel 15:3]]), but he spares King Agag and the best of the spoil ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 8|1 Samuel 15:8-9]]). Samuel's rebuke is severe: "**To obey is better than sacrifice**, and to listen than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of divination, and presumption is as iniquity and idolatry. **Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, he has also rejected you from being king**" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 22|1 Samuel 15:22-23]]).

**Jealousy and murder**: Saul becomes consumed with jealousy of David ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 18#1 Samuel 18 . 7|1 Samuel 18:7-9]]), repeatedly attempts to kill him ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 18#1 Samuel 18 . 10|1 Samuel 18:10-11]]; [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 19#1 Samuel 19 . 1|1 Samuel 19]]), and massacres 85 priests at Nob ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 22#1 Samuel 22 . 17|1 Samuel 22:17-19]]).

Saul's tragic end—consulting a medium ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 28#1 Samuel 28 . 7|1 Samuel 28:7-19]]) and dying by suicide ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 31#1 Samuel 31 . 4|1 Samuel 31:4]])—demonstrates that **the flesh profits nothing** ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 63|John 6:63]]).

### 3. David: The King After God's Heart

In contrast to Saul, David is chosen according to God's standard: "The LORD sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but **the LORD looks on the heart**" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 7|1 Samuel 16:7]]).

David is called "a man after [God's] own heart" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 13#1 Samuel 13 . 14|1 Samuel 13:14]]; [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 22|Acts 13:22]]), not because he is sinless, but because he demonstrates:

**Faith in God's power**: David defeats Goliath not with sword and spear but "in the name of the LORD of hosts" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 17#1 Samuel 17 . 45|1 Samuel 17:45]]), declaring: "The battle is the LORD's" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 17#1 Samuel 17 . 47|1 Samuel 17:47]]).

**Reverence for God's presence**: When Uzzah touches the ark and dies ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 6#2 Samuel 6 . 6|2 Samuel 6:6-7]]), David fears and then brings the ark to Jerusalem with proper reverence ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 6#2 Samuel 6 . 12|2 Samuel 6:12-15]]), dancing before the LORD ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 6#2 Samuel 6 . 14|2 Samuel 6:14]]).

**Humble repentance**: When confronted by Nathan over his sin with Bathsheba ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 7|2 Samuel 12:7-13]]), David immediately confesses: "I have sinned against the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 13|2 Samuel 12:13]]). Psalm 51 expresses his deep contrition.

**Desire for God's house**: David's desire to build a temple for God ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 2|2 Samuel 7:2]]) demonstrates his heart for worship, even though God reserves this task for Solomon.

Yet David also fails—his adultery with Bathsheba and murder of Uriah ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 11#2 Samuel 11 . 1|2 Samuel 11]]) bring lasting consequences: "The sword shall never depart from your house" ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 10|2 Samuel 12:10]]). His family is plagued by violence—Amnon rapes Tamar ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 13#2 Samuel 13 . 1|2 Samuel 13:1-22]]), Absalom murders Amnon ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 13#2 Samuel 13 . 28|2 Samuel 13:28-29]]), Absalom rebels ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 15#2 Samuel 15 . 1|2 Samuel 15-18]]).

David's failure demonstrates that even the best human king is **insufficient**—only a **sinless king** can establish the eternal kingdom.

### 4. The Davidic Covenant: Promise of an Eternal King

The centerpiece of the monarchy narrative is God's covenant with David in [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 1|2 Samuel 7]]:

"When your days are fulfilled and you lie down with your fathers, I will raise up your offspring after you... And **I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever**. I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son... And **your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before me. Your throne shall be established forever**" ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]]).

This covenant promises:

**Eternal dynasty**: David's line will never end (violated only if the king forsakes God completely, [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 89#Psalm 89 . 30|Psalm 89:30-37]])

**Father-son relationship**: God will be a father to David's son—ultimately fulfilled in Christ, God's eternal Son ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 5|Hebrews 1:5]])

**Eternal kingdom**: The kingdom will never end—only fulfilled in Christ's eternal reign ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 32|Luke 1:32-33]])

**Temple-builder**: Solomon will build the temple ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 13|2 Samuel 7:13]]), but ultimately Christ builds the true temple—the church ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 18|Matthew 16:18]]; [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 19|Ephesians 2:19-22]])

Psalm 89 expands this covenant: "I have made a covenant with my chosen one... I will establish his offspring forever and his throne as the days of the heavens" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 89#Psalm 89 . 3|Psalm 89:3-4]]).

The Davidic covenant becomes the **foundational Old Testament promise of the Messiah**, pointing to Christ who is "the son of David" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 3|Romans 1:3]]).

### 5. Solomon: Glory and Decline

Solomon's reign represents both the **peak of Israel's glory** and the beginning of its decline:

**Peak Glory**:
- Wisdom from God ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 3#1 Kings 3 . 12|1 Kings 3:12]])—Solomon's wisdom exceeds all ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 29|1 Kings 4:29-34]])
- Temple construction ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 1|1 Kings 6-7]])—a magnificent structure, seven years in building
- God's glory fills the temple ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]])—"The glory of the LORD filled the house"
- Nations come to Solomon ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 10#1 Kings 10 . 1|1 Kings 10:1-13]])—Queen of Sheba visits, prefiguring Gentile inclusion
- Peace and prosperity ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 25|1 Kings 4:25]])—"Judah and Israel lived in safety, from Dan even to Beersheba"

**Beginning of Decline**:
- Multiplies wives (700 wives, 300 concubines, [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 11#1 Kings 11 . 3|1 Kings 11:3]])—violating Deuteronomy 17:17
- Turns to idolatry ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 11#1 Kings 11 . 4|1 Kings 11:4-8]])—"His wives turned away his heart after other gods"
- Heavy taxation and forced labor ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 5#1 Kings 5 . 13|1 Kings 5:13-18]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 4|1 Kings 12:4]])—burdens that lead to the kingdom's division

God's judgment is announced: "I will surely tear the kingdom from you" ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 11#1 Kings 11 . 11|1 Kings 11:11-13]]), though delayed until after Solomon's death for David's sake.

Solomon's pattern—beginning in wisdom, ending in folly—demonstrates that **human wisdom cannot prevent the heart's corruption** apart from God's transforming grace.

### 6. The Divided Kingdom: Escalating Apostasy

When Rehoboam refuses to lighten the tax burden ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 13|1 Kings 12:13-14]]), ten tribes rebel and make Jeroboam king ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 20|1 Kings 12:20]]). The kingdom divides into:

**Israel (Northern Kingdom)**:
- Capital: Samaria (later, after Tirzah)
- 19 kings from 9 dynasties (all evil)
- **Jeroboam's sin**: Sets up golden calves at Dan and Bethel ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 12#1 Kings 12 . 28|1 Kings 12:28-29]])—"This is your god, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt"
- Subsequent kings follow "the sin of Jeroboam" (repeated 19 times in Kings)
- **Ahab and Jezebel**: Worst apostasy—Baal worship, persecution of prophets ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 16#1 Kings 16 . 29|1 Kings 16:29-33]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 1|1 Kings 18]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 21#1 Kings 21 . 1|1 Kings 21]])
- Falls to Assyria in 722 BC ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 17#2 Kings 17 . 6|2 Kings 17:6-23]])—"Because they did not obey the voice of the LORD their God"

**Judah (Southern Kingdom)**:
- Capital: Jerusalem
- 20 kings from David's line (8 good, 12 evil)
- Occasional reformers: Asa ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 15#1 Kings 15 . 11|1 Kings 15:11-15]]), Jehoshaphat ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 22#1 Kings 22 . 43|1 Kings 22:43]]), Joash ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 12#2 Kings 12 . 2|2 Kings 12:2]]), Hezekiah ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 3|2 Kings 18:3-7]]), Josiah ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 22#2 Kings 22 . 2|2 Kings 22:2]]; [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 23#2 Kings 23 . 25|2 Kings 23:25]])
- Yet even good kings cannot reverse the nation's trajectory
- Falls to Babylon in 586 BC ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 1|2 Kings 25:1-21]])—temple destroyed, people exiled

The divided kingdom demonstrates that **political division leads to spiritual apostasy**, and even occasional reforms cannot prevent final judgment when the people's hearts remain hard.

### 7. The Prophets and the Kings

Throughout the monarchy, God sends **prophets** to confront kings and call the nation back to covenant:

**Samuel**: Anoints both Saul and David, confronts Saul's disobedience ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 1|1 Samuel 15]])

**Nathan**: Confronts David's adultery ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 1|2 Samuel 12:1-14]]), delivers the Davidic covenant ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 1|2 Samuel 7]])

**Elijah**: Confronts Ahab and Jezebel ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 17|1 Kings 18:17-40]]; [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 21#1 Kings 21 . 17|1 Kings 21:17-24]]), demonstrates Yahweh's supremacy at Mount Carmel ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 36|1 Kings 18:36-39]])

**Elisha**: Continues Elijah's ministry with double portion of his spirit ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 2#2 Kings 2 . 9|2 Kings 2:9-14]]), performs numerous miracles

**Isaiah**: Ministers to Judah, confronts Ahaz ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 7#Isaiah 7 . 1|Isaiah 7]]) and counsels Hezekiah ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 19#2 Kings 19 . 1|2 Kings 19]]), prophesies Messiah ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 6|Isaiah 9:6-7]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 1|Isaiah 53]])

**Jeremiah**: Warns Judah of coming exile ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 25#Jeremiah 25 . 1|Jeremiah 25]]), prophesies New Covenant ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]])

The prophets function as God's **covenant lawsuit attorneys**, calling Israel back to the covenant, yet they are largely ignored or persecuted ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 37|Matthew 23:37]]).

### 8. Temple Theology: From Glory to Destruction

The temple represents the **zenith of Old Testament temple theology**:

**Solomon's Temple** ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 1|1 Kings 6-8]]):
- Patterned after the tabernacle but more permanent and glorious
- Seven years in construction ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 38|1 Kings 6:38]])
- Overlaid with gold, decorated with cherubim, pomegranates, palm trees—Eden imagery
- God's glory fills the temple ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]])—"The glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD"
- Solomon's prayer ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 22|1 Kings 8:22-53]]) acknowledges God's cosmic transcendence yet gracious condescension

**Temple Desecration**:
- Rehoboam allows high places to remain ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 14#1 Kings 14 . 22|1 Kings 14:22-24]])
- Ahaz introduces Assyrian altar ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 16#2 Kings 16 . 10|2 Kings 16:10-16]])
- Manasseh places idols in the temple ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 21#2 Kings 21 . 4|2 Kings 21:4-7]])

**Temple Destruction**:
- Nebuchadnezzar burns the temple ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 9|2 Kings 25:9]]), breaks temple vessels ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 25#2 Kings 25 . 13|2 Kings 25:13-17]])
- Ezekiel sees God's glory depart ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 10#Ezekiel 10 . 18|Ezekiel 10:18-19]]; [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 11#Ezekiel 11 . 23|11:23]])—the ultimate tragedy

The temple's destruction demonstrates that **buildings cannot contain God's presence** if the people reject Him. Only the **true temple**, Jesus Christ, can permanently dwell among humanity ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]).

## Kline's Two-Register Framework

Meredith Kline's two-register cosmology illuminates the monarchy as a series of **upper-register intrusions** that fail to consummate the age:

### Upper Register Intrusions

**David's Anointing**: The Spirit rushing upon David ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 13|1 Samuel 16:13]]) is an eschatological empowerment, a foretaste of the Spirit-anointed Messiah.

**Davidic Covenant**: God's promise of an eternal kingdom ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]]) is an upper-register intrusion into history—eschatological promise invading the Common Grace period.

**Solomon's Temple and God's Glory**: The glory cloud filling the temple ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]]) is the greatest Old Testament theophany since Sinai, an intrusion of God's eschatological presence.

**Hezekiah's Deliverance**: The angel of the LORD strikes down 185,000 Assyrians ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 19#2 Kings 19 . 35|2 Kings 19:35]]), a dramatic divine intervention, yet it only delays Judah's final judgment.

**Josiah's Reform**: Josiah's discovery of the Law ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 22#2 Kings 22 . 8|2 Kings 22:8]]) and subsequent reforms ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 23#2 Kings 23 . 1|2 Kings 23]]) temporarily revive covenant faithfulness, yet cannot reverse the nation's ultimate trajectory.

### Common Grace Continues

Despite these glorious intrusions, the **Common Grace period continues**—kings die, reforms fail, and the people return to idolatry. The intrusions demonstrate:

- God's patience in delaying judgment
- Preservation of the Davidic line despite failure
- Ongoing opportunity for repentance
- Building anticipation for the **true and eternal King**

The destruction of the temple and exile to Babylon appear to **end** the Davidic covenant, yet God's promise remains: "I will not violate my covenant or alter the word that went forth from my lips" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 89#Psalm 89 . 34|Psalm 89:34]]). The covenant awaits fulfillment in Christ.

## Christological Fulfillment: The Greater David

The New Testament presents Jesus as the **true King** who fulfills the Davidic covenant and succeeds where all human kings failed:

### Jesus the Son of David

The Gospels emphasize Jesus' Davidic descent:

"The book of the genealogy of **Jesus Christ, the son of David**, the son of Abraham" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]]).

The angel announces to Mary: "The Lord God will give to him **the throne of his father David**, and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and **of his kingdom there will be no end**" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 32|Luke 1:32-33]]).

### Jesus the Eternal King

Where every Davidic king died, Jesus reigns **forever**:

"Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David and over his kingdom" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 7|Isaiah 9:7]]).

After His resurrection, Jesus declares: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18]]).

### Jesus the Sinless King

Where David committed adultery and murder, Solomon turned to idolatry, and all kings failed, Jesus is **without sin**:

"He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 22|1 Peter 2:22]]).

Only a **sinless king** can establish an eternal kingdom on the foundation of righteousness.

### Jesus the True Temple

Where Solomon built a physical temple that was eventually destroyed, Jesus is the **true temple**:

"Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up... He was speaking about the temple of his body" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]).

Believers become the new temple: "You are being built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 22|Ephesians 2:22]]).

### Jesus the Wisdom of God

Where Solomon possessed great wisdom but ended in folly, Jesus is **God's wisdom incarnate**:

"Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 24|1 Corinthians 1:24]]).

"In whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 3|Colossians 2:3]]).

### Jesus the King of All Nations

Where Solomon received the Queen of Sheba, prefiguring Gentile inclusion, Jesus is the **King of all nations**:

"I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|Isaiah 49:6]]).

"Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9-10]]).

---

## Conclusion: From Failed Kings to the Eternal King

The monarchy period demonstrates both the **glory of the Davidic covenant** and the **tragic failure of human kingship**. Every king—even the best, David—falls short, demonstrating that only a **perfect, sinless, eternal King** can fulfill God's promise. The temple is built in glory, yet is destroyed in judgment. The kingdom reaches its peak under Solomon, yet divides and ultimately falls into exile.

Yet through all the failure, God's covenant promise stands firm: David's line will produce an **eternal King** whose kingdom will never end. The fivefold pattern repeats—Chaos (demand for king), Creation (David anointed, temple built), Commission (king to rule righteously), Sin (every king fails), Judgment (division, exile)—yet this pattern points forward to the **ultimate King** who will bring the pattern to its final fulfillment.

Jesus Christ is the **greater David**, the King who defeats not merely Philistines and Moabites but sin, death, and Satan. He builds not a temple of stone but a temple of living stones—His people, the church. His kingdom is not limited to Israel but extends to all nations. And His reign is not temporary but **eternal**—"The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 15|Revelation 11:15]]).

The monarchy period sets up the longing for a true King that finds its answer in the incarnation: "Behold, your king is coming to you... humble, and mounted on a donkey" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 9#Zechariah 9 . 9|Zechariah 9:9]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 5|Matthew 21:5]]). The eternal King has come, and He will reign forever.
