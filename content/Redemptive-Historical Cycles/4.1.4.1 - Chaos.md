# 4.1.4.1 - Chaos - Babel Scattering, Idolatry, and Barrenness (Genesis 11:9, 30-32)

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.4 - Intrusion - Call of Abraham|← Back to 4.1.4]]

---

## Overview

The chaos from which Abraham is called is **threefold**: the scattering at Babel, the idolatry of Terah's family, and the barrenness of Sarai. Each element represents a different dimension of the human predicament apart from God's gracious intervention. Together, they create a situation of **absolute hopelessness**—a chaos so complete that only sovereign, miraculous grace can bring new creation.

**Key Theme**: The chaos of judgment (Babel scattering), spiritual darkness (idolatry), and physical impossibility (barrenness) creates the context for God's surprising call of Abraham. Out of global chaos, God chooses one man; out of spiritual death, God creates faith; out of physical barrenness, God promises a great nation.

## First Element of Chaos: Babel Scattering (Genesis 11:9)

### Global Judgment and Division

The previous intrusion climaxed with humanity's rebellion at Babel—building a city and tower to **make a name for themselves** and avoid being **scattered** across the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 4|Genesis 11:4]]). This was direct defiance of God's command to "fill the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 1|Genesis 9:1, 7]]).

God's response was swift: **"Come, let us go down and there confuse their language"** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 7|Genesis 11:7]]). The result was the **scattering** of humanity into distinct peoples, languages, and territories (as cataloged in Genesis 10—the Table of Nations).

**The Chaos This Creates**:
- **Linguistic Fragmentation**: Humanity can no longer communicate as one. The unity they sought in rebellion is shattered.
- **Geographic Dispersion**: Families and clans are scattered "over the face of all the earth," separated from one another.
- **Cultural Division**: Distinct nations, languages, and cultures emerge, often in competition and conflict.
- **Spiritual Darkness**: Each nation develops its own religious systems, idolatries, and false worship.

**Beale's Insight**: Babel is the **anti-temple**—instead of humanity expanding God's presence from a sacred center (as Adam was commissioned to do), they centralize human power in defiance of God. The scattering is God's judgment, but it also prevents humanity from uniting in corporate rebellion again (at least until the end of history, cf. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 13#Revelation 13 . 7|Revelation 13:7-8]]).

### The Pattern: Exile Repeats

The scattering at Babel is the **third major exile** in redemptive history:
1. **Adam exiled from Eden** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]])—expelled from God's presence
2. **Cain exiled from the presence of the LORD** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 16|Genesis 4:16]])—driven out as a wanderer
3. **Babel scattering** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 9|Genesis 11:9]])—humanity dispersed across the earth

Each exile represents a **deepening of judgment** and a **widening of the chaos**. What began with one couple expelled from one garden has now become the entire human race scattered across the earth, fragmented and alienated.

### The Hopelessness of the Situation

From the scattering at Babel comes a world characterized by:
- **Division**: Nations at enmity with one another (anticipating the conflicts throughout Genesis and beyond)
- **Idolatry**: Each nation "exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 23|Romans 1:23]])
- **Darkness**: Humanity "became futile in their thinking, and their foolish hearts were darkened" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 21|Romans 1:21]])
- **Judgment**: God "gave them up" to their sinful desires ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 24|Romans 1:24, 26, 28]])

Into this chaos of scattered, idolatrous nations, God will call **one man** to become the source of blessing for **all the families of the earth** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]). The nations scattered in judgment will be the nations gathered through Abraham's seed.

## Second Element of Chaos: Terah's Idolatry (Genesis 11:31-32; Joshua 24:2)

### Abraham's Family Background

[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 27|Genesis 11:27-32]] provides the genealogy and family background of Abram (later renamed Abraham). His father is **Terah**, a descendant of Shem (one of Noah's three sons). Terah's family lives in **Ur of the Chaldeans**, a major city in ancient Mesopotamia.

**Historical Context**: Ur was a wealthy, advanced civilization with sophisticated culture, architecture, and religion. But it was also a center of **paganism**. The people of Ur worshiped the moon god **Sin** and other deities. Archaeological evidence reveals ziggurats (temple towers), idol worship, and elaborate religious rituals.

### The Explicit Testimony: "They Served Other Gods"

Centuries later, Joshua will reveal the spiritual state of Abraham's family: **"Your fathers lived beyond the Euphrates, Terah, the father of Abraham and of Nahor; and they served other gods"** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 2|Joshua 24:2]]).

This is a shocking statement. Abraham's own father, **Terah**, was an **idolater**. The man who would become the father of faith, the friend of God, the one through whom all nations would be blessed—he came from a family that worshiped false gods.

**Key Implications**:

**No Inherent Righteousness**: Abraham was not called because he was more righteous than others. He was part of an idolatrous family, indistinguishable from the pagan nations around him.

**Sovereign Grace**: God's call of Abraham is **pure grace**. There is no merit, no seeking on Abraham's part, no spiritual superiority. God simply chooses him out of the mass of fallen humanity.

**Pattern for All Redemption**: This establishes the gospel pattern: God does not save the righteous but **makes righteous those He calls**. "While we were still sinners, Christ died for us" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 8|Romans 5:8]]).

### The Incomplete Journey

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 31|Genesis 11:31]]** records that Terah "went forth together from Ur of the Chaldeans **to go into the land of Canaan**, but when they came to Haran, **they settled there**."

This is significant: Terah **intended** to go to Canaan but **stopped halfway**. Haran, like Ur, was a center of moon-god worship. Terah's partial obedience (if God had indeed called him) results in settling short of the goal.

**Theological Significance**: This may suggest that God had called Terah first, but Terah failed to complete the journey. Now God will call **Abram** to finish what Terah started. The son will succeed where the father faltered.

**Pattern**: This anticipates Israel's pattern—called to enter Canaan, but stopping short due to unbelief ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 1|Numbers 13-14]]). Only the next generation (under Joshua) will complete the journey.

**Application**: Many begin the journey of faith but settle in Haran—halfway to the promise, comfortable in a halfway commitment. True faith presses on to Canaan.

### Terah Dies in Haran

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 32|Genesis 11:32]]**: "The days of Terah were 205 years, and Terah died in Haran."

Before the new creation can begin, the **old must die**. Terah, the idolater, dies in Haran. This clears the way for God's call to Abram in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1]]—"Go from your country and your kindred and **your father's house**."

**Kline's Framework**: The death of the old generation represents the **end of the common grace period** for Terah. He had his chance but stopped short. Now God will work through the next generation. This pattern of **generational transition** repeats throughout redemptive history (cf. the generation that dies in the wilderness, [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 29|Numbers 14:29-35]]).

## Third Element of Chaos: Sarai's Barrenness (Genesis 11:30)

### The Stark Declaration

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]]**: "Now Sarai was barren; she had no child."

This simple, devastating statement is placed **immediately before** God's call of Abraham in Genesis 12. The juxtaposition is deliberate. Before God promises to make Abraham into a **great nation** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]]), the narrative emphasizes the **impossibility** of that promise.

**Hebrew**: The word for "barren" (עֲקָרָה, *aqarah*) indicates not just childlessness but **sterility**—the inability to conceive. Sarai's condition is not temporary but chronic.

### Barrenness as Curse and Chaos

In the ancient world, **barrenness was considered a curse**. Children were seen as a blessing from God ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 127#Psalm 127 . 3|Psalm 127:3]]—"Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward"). To be barren was to be cut off from the future, from legacy, from hope.

**Theological Significance of Barrenness**:
- **Symbol of Death**: Barrenness represents the inability to produce life, echoing the death sentence pronounced in Eden ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 17|Genesis 2:17]]—"in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die").
- **Failure of Adamic Commission**: God commanded humanity to "be fruitful and multiply" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Barrenness is the failure of this commission.
- **Chaos**: In Genesis 1, God creates order and life out of formless void. Barrenness is a return to that void—emptiness, futility, hopelessness.

### The Impossibility Magnified

Sarai's barrenness is not just a medical condition but a **theological crisis**. God promises to make Abraham into a great nation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]]), to multiply his offspring like the stars of heaven ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 5|Genesis 15:5]]), and to give him the land through his descendants ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]). But **Sarai is barren**.

How can Abraham become a great nation if his wife cannot conceive? The promise seems impossible from the start.

**Pattern of Barren Matriarchs**:
- **Sarai**: Barren until age 90 ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 17|Genesis 17:17]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-2]])
- **Rebekah**: Barren until Isaac prays ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 21|Genesis 25:21]])
- **Rachel**: Barren while Leah bears children ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 29#Genesis 29 . 31|Genesis 29:31]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 22|Genesis 30:22-24]])
- **Hannah**: Barren until God opens her womb ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 5|1 Samuel 1:5-20]])

**Theological Point**: The **seed promise** cannot be fulfilled by human power. Barrenness forces reliance on **divine intervention**. The promised seed will not come through natural generation but through **miraculous gift**.

### Foreshadowing the Ultimate Birth

Sarai's barrenness foreshadows the **virgin birth of Christ**. Just as Isaac is born to a barren woman beyond childbearing age (humanly impossible), so Jesus is born to a virgin (divinely miraculous). Both births demonstrate that **salvation comes from God, not human effort**.

**[[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 28|Galatians 4:28-29]]**: "Now you, brothers, like Isaac, are children of promise. But just as at that time he who was born according to the flesh persecuted him who was born according to the Spirit, so also it is now."

The children of promise are always born **according to the Spirit**, not according to the flesh. Sarai's barrenness ensures that Isaac's birth will be a **miracle of grace**, pointing forward to the ultimate miracle—Christ born of a virgin by the power of the Holy Spirit.

## The Threefold Chaos Summarized

The chaos from which Abraham is called involves:

1. **Global Scattering** (Babel): Humanity dispersed, divided, alienated—judgment on corporate rebellion
2. **Spiritual Darkness** (Idolatry): Abraham's family worships false gods—no knowledge of the true God apart from revelation
3. **Physical Impossibility** (Barrenness): Sarai cannot conceive—the promise seems humanly impossible

**Each element represents a different kind of death**:
- **Scattering** = relational death (division, alienation)
- **Idolatry** = spiritual death (darkness, deception)
- **Barrenness** = physical/generational death (no future, no life)

**Only God can create life out of this threefold death**. The call of Abraham is an act of **resurrection**—calling the dead to life, bringing light into darkness, creating unity out of division, producing offspring from barrenness.

## Beale's Framework: From Anti-Temple to True Temple

**G. K. Beale** emphasizes that Babel represents humanity's attempt to build a **false temple**—a center of human autonomy and self-glorification. The tower "with its top in the heavens" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 4|Genesis 11:4]]) is a parody of the true temple where heaven and earth meet.

The scattering of Babel creates the chaos—nations without God, worshiping idols, building their own kingdoms. Into this chaos, God calls Abraham to be the beginning of the **true temple community**:
- Abraham builds **altars** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7-8]])—proto-temples, places of worship
- Abraham's descendants become **priests** to the nations ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]—"a kingdom of priests")
- Through Abraham's seed, the **true temple** (Christ) will come ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]—"Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up")

The chaos of Babel's anti-temple is reversed through Abraham's seed, culminating in Christ, who is the ultimate temple—the place where God's presence dwells fully ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]—"The Word became flesh and dwelt among us").

---

## Kline's Framework: Common Grace and Special Grace

**Meredith Kline** distinguishes between **common grace** (God's general preservation of humanity despite sin) and **special grace** (God's particular redemption of the elect).

**Common Grace Context**: After Babel, humanity exists under common grace. God does not immediately destroy the rebellious nations. He allows them to flourish, develop cultures, multiply. The Noahic covenant ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 8|Genesis 9:8-17]]) remains in effect—seasons continue, the earth is not destroyed.

**Special Grace Intrusion**: The call of Abraham is the **intrusion of special grace** into the common grace world. Out of all the scattered nations, God chooses **one man** to be the vehicle of redemption. This is **election**—God's sovereign choice to save a people for Himself.

**Pattern**: Throughout the Old Testament, special grace operates within the framework of common grace. Israel exists among the nations, distinct but not isolated. The covenant community is called to be separate yet missional—a light to the nations ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|Isaiah 49:6]]).

The chaos of Babel's scattering creates the **nations** that will eventually be blessed through Abraham's seed. What appears as judgment (scattering) becomes the framework for redemption (the nations gathered through the gospel).

## Summary: Absolute Hopelessness Requires Sovereign Grace

The chaos into which God speaks His call to Abraham is **comprehensive**:
- **Globally**: Nations scattered and divided (Babel)
- **Spiritually**: Abraham's family in idolatry (Terah)
- **Physically**: Sarai barren and unable to conceive

This threefold chaos creates a situation of **absolute hopelessness**. There is no natural solution, no human remedy, no grounds for optimism. The promise of a great nation through Abraham seems absurd given the circumstances.

**But this is precisely the point**: Salvation must come from **outside the system**. It cannot emerge from human effort, human wisdom, or human power. It must be a **divine intrusion**—God speaking into the void, calling light out of darkness, bringing life out of death.

**[[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 17|Romans 4:17]]** describes God as the one "who gives life to the dead and calls into existence the things that do not exist." This is the God who calls Abraham. Out of scattering, God will create unity. Out of idolatry, God will create faith. Out of barrenness, God will create a great nation.

The chaos is not an obstacle to God's plan—it is the **canvas** on which His glory is displayed. The darker the chaos, the brighter the light of grace. The deeper the death, the more glorious the resurrection.

This is the chaos from which Abraham is called. This is the void over which the Spirit hovers, preparing to create anew.
