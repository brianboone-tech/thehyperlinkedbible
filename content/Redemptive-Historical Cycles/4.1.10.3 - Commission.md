# 4.1.10.3 - Commission - Deuteronomy and the Renewed Covenant

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.10 - Intrusion - Wilderness Wanderings II|← Back to 4.1.10]]

## Overview

The book of Deuteronomy represents the commission phase for the second generation—a comprehensive restatement and application of the covenant given at Sinai, now addressed to those who will actually enter and possess the Promised Land. The Hebrew title *Devarim* ("these are the words") signals that this is Moses' final address, his magisterial exposition of the Law for a new generation. The setting is crucial: Israel stands on the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 5|Deuteronomy 1:5]]), ready to cross into Canaan. The first generation is dead; Moses himself will soon die. The commission given here will determine whether this generation succeeds or repeats the catastrophic failure of their parents.

Deuteronomy structures the commission around three major sermons (chapters 1-4, 5-28, 29-30) followed by Moses' final acts: the installation of Joshua, the Song of Moses, the Blessing of the Tribes, and his death on Mount Nebo. The central theme throughout is covenant faithfulness: love the LORD with total devotion, obey His commands, and receive blessing; turn to other gods, and face curse and exile. The commission is not merely legal obligation but relational summons—God has chosen Israel in love ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 7|Deuteronomy 7:7-8]]), redeemed them from slavery, and now calls them to exclusive loyalty. The language of Deuteronomy shaped Israel's faith for centuries, becoming the most-quoted Old Testament book in the New Testament and the book Jesus Himself cited when tempted in the wilderness ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 1|Matthew 4:1-11]]).

## The Great Commandment: Love as Covenant Obligation

The heart of Deuteronomy's commission appears in the *Shema*, Israel's foundational confession of faith: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 4|Deuteronomy 6:4-5]]). This is not sentimental affection but covenantal allegiance—wholehearted, undivided commitment of the entire person to the covenant LORD. The command to love God "with all your heart" demands inner affection and loyalty. The command to love "with all your soul" (*nephesh*) calls for dedication of one's entire life, even unto death if necessary. The command to love "with all your might" (*meod*) requires maximal strength and resources—nothing held back.

This love must permeate every dimension of life. Moses commands: "And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise. You shall bind them as a sign on your hand, and they shall be as frontlets between your eyes. You shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 6|Deuteronomy 6:6-9]]). The commission extends to family life (teaching children), daily routine (sitting, walking, lying down, rising), physical reminders (signs on hands and foreheads), and domestic space (doorposts and gates). Every sphere of existence must be saturated with covenant consciousness.

The demand for exclusive worship is absolute: "You shall fear only the LORD your God. You shall serve him and swear by his name. You shall not go after other gods, the gods of the peoples who are around you— for the LORD your God in your midst is a jealous God—lest the anger of the LORD your God be kindled against you, and he destroy you from off the face of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 13|Deuteronomy 6:13-15]]). The language of jealousy is not petty emotion but rightful claim—God's covenant relationship with Israel is exclusive like marriage, tolerating no rivals. The warning that God will "destroy you from off the face of the earth" anticipates the exile that will eventually come when Israel violates this commission through persistent idolatry.

## Historical Rehearsal and Covenant Renewal

Moses begins Deuteronomy by rehearsing Israel's history from Horeb (Sinai) to the plains of Moab ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 6|Deuteronomy 1:6—4:40]]). This is not mere nostalgia but pedagogical strategy—the second generation must learn from the first generation's failures. Moses recounts the rebellion at Kadesh Barnea: "Yet you would not go up, but rebelled against the command of the LORD your God. And you murmured in your tents and said, 'Because the LORD hated us he has brought us out of the land of Egypt, to give us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us'" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 26|Deuteronomy 1:26-27]]). The accusation that "the LORD hated us" inverts reality—it is Israel's unbelief, not God's hatred, that prevented entry into the land.

Moses describes God's response: "Not one of these men of this evil generation shall see the good land that I swore to give to your fathers, except Caleb the son of Jephunneh" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 35|Deuteronomy 1:35-36]]). Significantly, Moses includes himself in this judgment: "Even with me the LORD was angry on your account and said, 'You also shall not go in there. Joshua the son of Nun, who stands before you, he shall enter. Encourage him, for he shall cause Israel to inherit it'" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 37|Deuteronomy 1:37-38]]). The historical rehearsal establishes a sobering reality: disobedience brings death, even for the greatest leaders. But it also establishes hope: God remains faithful to His covenant promises and will accomplish His purposes through the next generation and new leadership.

The commission is formally renewed in chapters 29-30 where Moses gathers "all Israel" to "enter into the sworn covenant of the LORD your God, which the LORD your God is making with you today, that he may establish you today as his people, and that he may be your God, as he promised you, and as he swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 29#Deuteronomy 29 . 12|Deuteronomy 29:12-13]]). This covenant renewal includes both "whoever is standing here with us today before the LORD our God, and... whoever is not here with us today" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 29#Deuteronomy 29 . 15|Deuteronomy 29:15]])—a provision that includes future generations. The commission Moses delivers binds not only his immediate audience but all subsequent Israelites.

## The Law Restated: Worship, Justice, and Holiness

Deuteronomy's central section (chapters 12-26) elaborates the commission through specific stipulations governing worship, civil justice, family life, warfare, and social ethics. The foundational principle appears in chapter 12: centralized worship at the place God chooses. "You shall seek the place that the LORD your God will choose out of all your tribes to put his name and make his habitation there. There you shall go, and there you shall bring your burnt offerings and your sacrifices" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 5|Deuteronomy 12:5-6]]). This command prohibits the multiplicity of high places and altars that characterized Canaanite worship. Israel must gather at one central sanctuary (eventually Jerusalem) to worship the one true God.

The purpose is explicitly stated: "You shall not worship the LORD your God in the way that they serve their gods. For every abominable thing that the LORD hates they have done for their gods, for they even burn their sons and their daughters in the fire to their gods" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 31|Deuteronomy 12:31]]). The commission includes radical separation from Canaanite religious practice, especially child sacrifice. Israel's worship must be pure, reflecting God's holy character rather than imitating pagan abominations. The prohibition against adding to or subtracting from God's commands ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 32|Deuteronomy 12:32]]) establishes the sufficiency and authority of revealed law.

Justice and compassion permeate the social legislation. Israel must appoint judges who "judge the people with righteous judgment" without partiality or bribery: "Justice, and only justice, you shall follow, that you may live and inherit the land that the LORD your God is giving you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 16#Deuteronomy 16 . 20|Deuteronomy 16:20]]). The commission extends to protection of the vulnerable: "You shall not pervert the justice due to the sojourner or to the fatherless, or take a widow's garment in pledge, but you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt and the LORD your God redeemed you from there; therefore I command you to do this" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 24#Deuteronomy 24 . 17|Deuteronomy 24:17-18]]). Israel's experience of redemption from oppression must shape their treatment of the oppressed—covenant ethics flow from covenant grace.

The commission includes family integrity. Children who persistently rebel against parental authority are to be judged by the elders ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 21#Deuteronomy 21 . 18|Deuteronomy 21:18-21]]). Sexual purity is required—adultery, rape, and fornication all carry severe penalties ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 22#Deuteronomy 22 . 13|Deuteronomy 22:13-30]]). Even seemingly minor regulations (like not wearing garments of mixed materials or not muzzling an ox while threshing) teach the principle of respecting God's created order ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 22#Deuteronomy 22 . 10|Deuteronomy 22:10-11]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 25#Deuteronomy 25 . 4|Deuteronomy 25:4]]). The commission is comprehensive—no area of life falls outside covenant obligation.

## Blessings and Curses: The Two Ways

The commission reaches its climax in chapters 27-28 with the recitation of blessings for obedience and curses for disobedience. Israel is to set up large stones on Mount Ebal, plaster them, and write "all the words of this law" on them ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 27#Deuteronomy 27 . 2|Deuteronomy 27:2-3]]). Six tribes stand on Mount Gerizim to bless the people, and six on Mount Ebal to pronounce curses ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 27#Deuteronomy 27 . 12|Deuteronomy 27:12-13]]). The Levites proclaim twelve curses against specific sins (idolatry, dishonoring parents, moving boundary stones, perverting justice, sexual immorality, murder, bribery), and the people respond "Amen" to each ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 27#Deuteronomy 27 . 15|Deuteronomy 27:15-26]]). The corporate "Amen" signifies acceptance of covenant responsibility—Israel binds itself under oath to these terms.

The blessings for obedience are expansive: "And if you faithfully obey the voice of the LORD your God, being careful to do all his commandments that I command you today, the LORD your God will set you high above all the nations of the earth. And all these blessings shall come upon you and overtake you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 1|Deuteronomy 28:1-2]]). The blessings cover urban and rural life, reproduction and agriculture, defense against enemies, economic prosperity, and international status: "The LORD will establish you as a people holy to himself, as he has sworn to you, if you keep the commandments of the LORD your God and walk in his ways. And all the peoples of the earth shall see that you are called by the name of the LORD, and they shall be afraid of you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 9|Deuteronomy 28:9-10]]). Obedience results in Israel becoming the head and not the tail, lending to nations but not borrowing ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 12|Deuteronomy 28:12-13]]).

But the curses for disobedience are devastating and detailed. "But if you will not obey the voice of the LORD your God or be careful to do all his commandments and his statutes that I command you today, then all these curses shall come upon you and overtake you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 15|Deuteronomy 28:15]]). The curses reverse the blessings: urban and rural life cursed, reproduction and agriculture cursed, military defeat, economic collapse, disease, drought, and ultimately exile. The description of siege conditions is horrific: "You shall eat the fruit of your womb, the flesh of your sons and daughters, whom the LORD your God has given you, in the siege and in the distress with which your enemies shall distress you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 53|Deuteronomy 28:53]]). This gruesome prophecy was literally fulfilled during the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/25 - Lamentations/Lamentations 4#Lamentations 4 . 10|Lamentations 4:10]]).

The final curse is expulsion from the land: "And the LORD will scatter you among all peoples, from one end of the earth to the other, and there you shall serve other gods of wood and stone, which neither you nor your fathers have known. And among these nations you shall find no respite, and there shall be no resting place for the sole of your foot, but the LORD will give you there a trembling heart and failing eyes and a languishing soul" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 64|Deuteronomy 28:64-65]]). This is the ultimate reversal of the Exodus—instead of rest in the land, restless wandering among the nations; instead of freedom, slavery; instead of covenant blessing, covenant curse. Yet even this devastating curse is not the final word.

## The Gospel in Deuteronomy: Circumcised Hearts

After describing the inevitable cycle of sin, curse, and exile, Moses proclaims a stunning promise of restoration. "And when all these things come upon you, the blessing and the curse, which I have set before you, and you call them to mind among all the nations where the LORD your God has driven you, and return to the LORD your God, you and your children, and obey his voice in all that I command you today, with all your heart and with all your soul, then the LORD your God will restore your fortunes and have mercy on you, and he will gather you again from all the peoples where the LORD your God has scattered you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 1|Deuteronomy 30:1-3]]). Even after exile, repentance will lead to restoration.

But the most remarkable promise concerns the inner transformation necessary for obedience: "And the LORD your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your offspring, so that you will love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul, that you may live" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 6|Deuteronomy 30:6]]). This is the gospel embedded in the Law—recognition that external commands cannot produce the heart obedience they require. Only divine grace, circumcising the heart by the Spirit, can enable the love and faithfulness the covenant demands. This promise anticipates the New Covenant where God writes His law on hearts of flesh rather than tablets of stone ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]; [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]]).

Moses sets before Israel the ultimate choice: "See, I have set before you today life and good, death and evil. If you obey the commandments of the LORD your God that I command you today, by loving the LORD your God, by walking in his ways, and by keeping his commandments and his statutes and his rules, then you shall live and multiply, and the LORD your God will bless you in the land that you are entering to take possession of it" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 15|Deuteronomy 30:15-16]]). The commission culminates in this stark binary: "I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Therefore choose life, that you and your offspring may live, loving the LORD your God, obeying his voice and holding fast to him, for he is your life and length of days, that you may dwell in the land that the LORD swore to your fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give them" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 19|Deuteronomy 30:19-20]]).

## Theological Analysis: Kline's Covenant Structure and Beale's Adamic Commission

Meredith Kline's groundbreaking analysis of Deuteronomy's structure as an ancient Near Eastern suzerainty treaty illuminates its function as covenant commission. The book follows the standard treaty format: preamble (1:1-5), historical prologue (1:6—4:49), stipulations (5-26), sanctions (27-28), and succession arrangements (29-34). This structure demonstrates that Israel's covenant with Yahweh follows the pattern of vassal treaties between a great king and subject peoples, but with this crucial difference: Israel's Suzerain is not a human emperor but the Creator of heaven and earth who redeemed them from slavery and binds Himself by oath to bless them.

Kline's emphasis on the covenant's intrusion character is evident throughout Deuteronomy. The blessings and curses are not merely natural consequences but supernatural intrusions—God actively blessing obedience and actively cursing rebellion in ways that transcend normal historical causation. The vivid descriptions in chapter 28 function as covenant lawsuit sanctions, invoking cosmic powers of blessing and curse. The ultimate curse of exile represents God's withdrawal of His presence, removing the intrusion of special grace that constituted Israel as His people. Yet the promise of restoration in chapter 30 reveals that even judgment is not final—God's ultimate purpose is eschatological blessing, achieved through the circumcision of hearts.

G.K. Beale's framework helps us see Deuteronomy's commission as renewal of the Adamic mandate. Just as Adam was placed in Eden with the commission to "work it and keep it" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]])—vocabulary also used for priestly service—Israel is commissioned to enter Canaan and "keep" God's commands while "working" the land. The blessing of fruitfulness, agricultural abundance, and dominion over enemies echoes Genesis 1:28's command to "be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it." Israel is to function as a corporate Adam, expanding Eden-like holiness and order throughout the Promised Land by faithful covenant keeping.

Beale's emphasis on the already/not yet tension is evident in Deuteronomy's eschatological outlook. The land Israel is about to possess is already a partial fulfillment of the Abrahamic promise, but the curses and restoration prophecies reveal it is not the ultimate fulfillment. The promise of heart circumcision points beyond Sinai to the New Covenant, beyond Canaan to the new creation. Deuteronomy's commission is simultaneously legal (do this and live) and gracious (God will give you a new heart to obey), reflecting the tension between Israel's current status as a redeemed but not yet perfected people and their future glorification.

## Christological Fulfillment

Jesus Christ is the perfect fulfillment of Deuteronomy's commission. Where Israel failed to love God with all heart, soul, and strength, Jesus succeeded. When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, He responded with three quotations from Deuteronomy: "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 8#Deuteronomy 8 . 3|Deuteronomy 8:3]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 4|Matthew 4:4]]); "You shall not put the Lord your God to the test" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 16|Deuteronomy 6:16]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 7|Matthew 4:7]]); "You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 13|Deuteronomy 6:13]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 10|Matthew 4:10]]). Jesus recapitulated Israel's wilderness testing and, unlike Israel, obeyed perfectly.

When asked about the greatest commandment, Jesus quoted the Shema: "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 37|Matthew 22:37]]). Jesus not only taught this command but embodied it, loving the Father with perfect obedience unto death ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 8|Philippians 2:8]]). He is the true Israel who fulfilled the covenant commission, earning the blessings promised for obedience.

The curses of Deuteronomy 28 fell on Jesus at the cross. He experienced the curse of hanging on a tree ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 21#Deuteronomy 21 . 23|Deuteronomy 21:23]]; [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]), becoming a curse for us so that "the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 14|Galatians 3:14]]). Every curse Israel deserved for covenant violation—defeat, disease, exile, death—Jesus bore in His body on the cross, exhausting divine wrath so that covenant blessing might flow to His people.

The promise of circumcised hearts is fulfilled through the Spirit whom Christ sends. "This circumcision was not done by hands... having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 11|Colossians 2:11-12]]). The New Covenant in Christ's blood accomplishes what the Mosaic covenant commanded but could not produce—hearts that love God through the indwelling Spirit ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 5|Romans 5:5]]). The choice between life and death, blessing and curse, finds ultimate resolution in the gospel: "whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 36|John 3:36]]).
