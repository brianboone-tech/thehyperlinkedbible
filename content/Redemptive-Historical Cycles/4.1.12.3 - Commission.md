# 4.1.12.3 - Commission: The Judges Period and Ruth - Covenant Obligations and the Call to Faithfulness

## Introduction: The Enduring Call Despite Failure

Even in the darkest period of Israel's history, the covenant commission remains unchanged. God's expectations for His people do not fluctuate based on their performance. The commands given at Sinai, renewed in Deuteronomy, and reiterated by Joshua at Shechem continue to define Israel's identity and obligations throughout the Judges era.

The commission theme in Judges and Ruth includes:
1. **Covenant obligations**: Worship Yahweh alone, destroy idolatry, remain separate from the nations
2. **Judges' calling**: Deliver Israel, judge righteously, lead in covenant faithfulness
3. **Teaching the next generation**: Pass on the knowledge of Yahweh and His works
4. **Boaz's redemptive commission**: Redeem Ruth, raise up seed for the dead, preserve the family line
5. **Corporate identity**: Be a holy people, distinct from the surrounding nations

G.K. Beale's framework shows that the commission in Judges is essentially the Adamic commission applied to Israel: be fruitful (multiply), subdue enemies (extend God's rule), guard the sanctuary (protect covenant holiness). However, Israel's repeated failure to fulfill this commission demonstrates the need for the ultimate Adam who will perfectly obey.

Meredith Kline's intrusion theology explains that the judges are temporarily commissioned as types of the coming King. Their limited success reveals that the ultimate commission—establishing eternal righteousness and Sabbath-rest—requires the eschatological Judge-King who will come at the fullness of time.

The book of Ruth shows covenant commission fulfilled at the personal level: Ruth embraces Yahweh and His people, Boaz fulfills the kinsman-redeemer obligations, and together they demonstrate what covenant faithfulness looks like in the midst of a faithless generation.

## Joshua's Farewell: The Commission Delivered

The transition from Joshua to Judges is marked by Joshua's farewell address, which functions as a covenant renewal ceremony and a solemn warning to the next generation.

### Joshua 23: Warnings and Blessings

In his first farewell speech, Joshua rehearses the mighty acts of Yahweh and warns Israel about the dangers of covenant compromise:

> **"Therefore, be very strong to keep and to do all that is written in the Book of the Law of Moses, turning aside from it neither to the right hand nor to the left, that you may not mix with these nations remaining among you or make mention of the names of their gods or swear by them or serve them or bow down to them, but you shall cling to the LORD your God just as you have done to this day."** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 6|Joshua 23:6-8]])

This passage contains several key commission elements:

**1. Keep the Law**: The covenant commission is rooted in obedience to the written Torah. This echoes Deuteronomy 5-26, where the Law defines Israel's covenant obligations.

**2. Do not mix with the nations**: Israel is called to maintain separation from Canaanite culture and religion. This is not ethnic superiority but theological distinctness—Yahweh alone is God.

**3. Do not worship foreign gods**: The first and second commandments ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 3|Exodus 20:3-6]]) remain central. Idolatry is the covenant violation that leads to all other sins.

**4. Cling to the LORD**: Positive covenant loyalty is not merely avoiding evil but actively embracing Yahweh as the only God. The Hebrew word *dabaq* (cling) is the same word used of marriage in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 24|Genesis 2:24]]—covenant faithfulness is marital fidelity.

Joshua then warns of the consequences of disobedience:

> **"Know for certain that the LORD your God will no longer drive out these nations before you, but they shall be a snare and a trap for you, a whip on your sides and thorns in your eyes, until you perish from off this good ground that the LORD your God has given you."** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 13|Joshua 23:13]])

This is precisely what happens in Judges: the nations become a snare, leading Israel into idolatry and oppression.

### Joshua 24: Choose This Day Whom You Will Serve

In his second farewell speech, Joshua calls for explicit covenant commitment:

> **"Now therefore fear the LORD and serve him in sincerity and in faithfulness. Put away the gods that your fathers served beyond the River and in Egypt, and serve the LORD. And if it is evil in your eyes to serve the LORD, choose this day whom you will serve, whether the gods your fathers served in the region beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you dwell. But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 14|Joshua 24:14-15]])

This is one of the most famous declarations in Scripture. Joshua frames the commission as a choice: serve Yahweh or serve idols. There is no middle ground, no syncretism, no "both/and" option. It is "either/or."

The people respond: **"Far be it from us that we should forsake the LORD to serve other gods, for it is the LORD our God who brought us and our fathers up from the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery, and who did those great signs in our sight and preserved us in all the way that we went, and among all the peoples through whom we passed. Therefore we also will serve the LORD, for he is our God."** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 16|Joshua 24:16-18]])

Joshua warns them that this is not a casual commitment:

> **"You are not able to serve the LORD, for he is a holy God. He is a jealous God; he will not forgive your transgressions or your sins. If you forsake the LORD and serve foreign gods, then he will turn and do you harm and consume you, after having done you good."** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 19|Joshua 24:19-20]])

This is a stunning statement: **"You are not able to serve the LORD."** Joshua recognizes the inherent weakness of Israel as corporate Adam. They are unable, in their own strength, to maintain covenant faithfulness. Yet he calls them to commit anyway, establishing a covenant that will expose their inability and drive them to seek divine grace.

Beale observes that this speech anticipates the entire Judges narrative: Israel will repeatedly prove unable to serve Yahweh, yet God will repeatedly save them in grace. The cycle reveals the need for a Mediator who can enable obedience—the Last Adam who will succeed where Israel fails.

The people affirm their commitment: **"The LORD our God we will serve, and his voice we will obey"** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 24|Joshua 24:24]]). Joshua makes a covenant with them and writes these words in the Book of the Law of God ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 25|Joshua 24:25-26]]).

Yet within one generation, this covenant commitment is forgotten.

## The Angel of the LORD at Bochim: Commission Violated

After Joshua's death and the incomplete conquest recorded in Judges 1, the Angel of the LORD appears at Bochim to confront Israel's covenant failure:

> **"Now the angel of the LORD went up from Gilgal to Bochim. And he said, 'I brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that I swore to give to your fathers. I said, "I will never break my covenant with you, and you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land; you shall break down their altars." But you have not obeyed my voice. What is this you have done? So now I say, I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you.'"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 1|Judges 2:1-3]])

This divine confrontation reveals the core commission violations:

### 1. Covenant Faithfulness Required

God says, **"I will never break my covenant with you"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 1|Judges 2:1]])—divine covenant faithfulness is unconditional. But Israel's covenant faithfulness is conditional: **"you shall make no covenant with the inhabitants of this land."**

The commission is clear: **no covenants with Canaanites**. Israel is to be separate, distinct, holy. Making covenants with the nations would compromise Israel's exclusive loyalty to Yahweh and expose them to idolatrous influence.

This echoes [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 32|Exodus 23:32-33]]: **"You shall make no covenant with them and their gods. They shall not dwell in your land, lest they make you sin against me; for if you serve their gods, it will surely be a snare to you."**

Israel has violated this command. Rather than driving out the Canaanites, they have coexisted with them. Rather than destroying the altars, they have tolerated them. The commission has been ignored.

### 2. Destroy Canaanite Worship

God commands: **"You shall break down their altars"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 2|Judges 2:2]]). This is not optional. The altars represent rival worship, false gods, and theological corruption. They must be destroyed.

This echoes [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 5|Deuteronomy 7:5]]: **"But thus shall you deal with them: you shall break down their altars and dash in pieces their pillars and chop down their Asherim and burn their carved images with fire."**

The commission is not merely to avoid worshiping false gods personally but to actively eliminate false worship from the land. Israel is called to be a priestly kingdom ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), guarding the holiness of God's sanctuary-land.

Beale's creation-temple theology explains why this matters: the land of Canaan is to be a new Eden, a holy sanctuary where Yahweh dwells with His people. Allowing Canaanite altars is like allowing the serpent into Eden—it invites corruption and judgment.

### 3. Consequences of Disobedience

Because Israel has failed to obey, God announces the consequences: **"I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 3|Judges 2:3]]).

This is covenant curse language, echoing Joshua's warning in [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 13|Joshua 23:13]]. The Canaanites, whom Israel was commissioned to dispossess, will now become oppressors. The gods, which Israel was commissioned to destroy, will now become temptations.

The people's response: **"And the people lifted up their voices and wept"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 4|Judges 2:4]]). They name the place Bochim (meaning "weeping"). Yet the text does not record genuine repentance—only weeping. Emotion without transformation is insufficient. The cycle of apostasy continues.

Kline's intrusion theology helps explain God's response: the incomplete conquest is not merely Israel's failure but part of God's larger redemptive plan. The presence of enemies in the land creates the need for judges, who prefigure the ultimate King. The ongoing struggle reveals that the final Sabbath-rest requires a greater Deliverer than human judges can provide.

## The Commission to Teach the Next Generation

One of the most significant commission failures in Judges is the failure to teach the next generation:

> **"And all that generation also were gathered to their fathers. And there arose another generation after them who did not know the LORD or the work that he had done for Israel."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 10|Judges 2:10]])

This is a catastrophic failure. The generation that experienced the conquest and saw God's mighty acts has died without passing on their knowledge to their children. The result is spiritual amnesia—the next generation does not know Yahweh or His works.

### The Shema: Commission to Teach

This failure violates one of the most central commandments in the Torah—the Shema and its accompanying teaching mandate:

> **"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise. You shall bind them as a sign on your hand, and they shall be as frontlets between your eyes. You shall write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates."** ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 4|Deuteronomy 6:4-9]])

The commission is comprehensive: teach the Law continually, in every context, at every opportunity. Parents are to model love for Yahweh and instruct their children in His commandments.

Furthermore, [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 20|Deuteronomy 6:20-25]] anticipates that children will ask about the commandments, and parents are to rehearse the redemptive history:

> **"When your son asks you in time to come, 'What is the meaning of the testimonies and the statutes and the rules that the LORD our God has commanded you?' then you shall say to your son, 'We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt. And the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand... And the LORD commanded us to do all these statutes, to fear the LORD our God, for our good always, that he might preserve us alive, as we are this day.'"**

This did not happen. The generation in Judges failed to tell the story, failed to teach the Law, failed to model covenant faithfulness. The result is apostasy.

Beale observes that this teaching commission is part of the Adamic mandate. Adam was to guard the garden and teach Eve the command not to eat from the forbidden tree ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 16|Genesis 2:16-17]]). He failed, and corruption entered. Similarly, the Judges-era parents were to guard their children from idolatry by teaching them the Torah. They failed, and corruption entered again.

### Psalm 78: The Necessity of Intergenerational Teaching

The failure to teach is addressed explicitly in Psalm 78:

> **"He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers to teach to their children, that the next generation might know them, the children yet unborn, and arise and tell them to their children, so that they should set their hope in God and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments; and that they should not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast, whose spirit was not faithful to God."** ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 78#Psalm 78 . 5|Psalm 78:5-8]])

The psalm goes on to recount Israel's repeated rebellions, starting with the Exodus generation and continuing through the Judges period. The refrain is clear: each generation that forgets God becomes stubborn and rebellious.

The solution is not merely teaching facts but cultivating **hope in God**. Covenant faithfulness is sustained not by bare knowledge but by confidence in God's promises and fear of His judgments.

Kline's intrusion theology emphasizes that this hope must be eschatological: the next generation must be taught not only what God has done in the past but what He has promised to do in the future. They must look forward to the coming King who will establish righteousness forever.

## The Judges' Commission: Deliver and Judge Righteously

Each judge is commissioned by God to deliver Israel from oppression and to judge righteously. However, the execution of this commission is mixed.

### Othniel: Model Commissioning

Othniel's commissioning is straightforward: **"The Spirit of the LORD was upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]]).

The commission has three elements:
1. **Spirit-empowerment**: Divine enablement for the task
2. **Judgment**: Administering justice and leading Israel in covenant faithfulness
3. **Warfare**: Delivering Israel from oppression

Othniel fulfills all three. He is the paradigmatic judge.

### Deborah: Prophetic and Judicial Commission

Deborah is described as **"a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth"** who **"was judging Israel at that time"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 4|Judges 4:4]]). She sat under a palm tree, and **"the people of Israel came up to her for judgment"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 5|Judges 4:5]]).

Deborah's commission includes:
1. **Prophetic word**: She speaks the word of Yahweh to Barak ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 6|Judges 4:6-7]])
2. **Judicial leadership**: She administers justice for the tribes
3. **Military strategy**: She accompanies Barak to ensure obedience

Deborah fulfills her commission with integrity. The text records no moral failure on her part. Her song in Judges 5 celebrates Yahweh's victory and calls the people to covenant faithfulness.

### Gideon: Commission with Hesitation

Gideon's commissioning is more complex. The Angel of the LORD appears to him and says: **"The LORD is with you, O mighty man of valor"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 12|Judges 6:12]]).

Gideon responds with doubt: **"Please, sir, if the LORD is with us, why then has all this happened to us? And where are all his wonderful deeds that our fathers recounted to us, saying, 'Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt?' But now the LORD has forsaken us and given us into the hand of Midian"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 13|Judges 6:13]]).

The LORD commissions him: **"Go in this might of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian; do not I send you?"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 14|Judges 6:14]])

Gideon protests his inadequacy: **"Please, Lord, how can I save Israel? Behold, my clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 15|Judges 6:15]]).

The LORD reassures him: **"But I will be with you, and you shall strike the Midianites as one man"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 16|Judges 6:16]]).

Gideon's hesitation echoes Moses' hesitation at the burning bush ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 11|Exodus 3:11]]; [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 10|Exodus 4:10]]). The pattern is consistent: God chooses unlikely deliverers, they protest their inadequacy, and God promises His presence.

Gideon eventually obeys. His first commission is not military but theological: **"Tear down the altar of Baal that your father has, and cut down the Asherah that is beside it and build an altar to the LORD your God on the top of the stronghold here"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 25|Judges 6:25-26]]).

This is the heart of the commission: destroy false worship, restore true worship. Gideon obeys, though he does it at night out of fear ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 27|Judges 6:27]]). Nevertheless, this act of covenant faithfulness qualifies him for military leadership.

After the victory, Gideon refuses to be made king: **"I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you; the LORD will rule over you"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 23|Judges 8:23]]). This is theologically correct—Yahweh is Israel's king.

However, Gideon then makes a golden ephod from the spoils of war, which becomes **"a snare to Gideon and to his family"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]]). He fulfills the commission to deliver Israel militarily but fails to guard against idolatry. This foreshadows the downward trajectory of subsequent judges.

### Jephthah: Misguided Commission Fulfillment

Jephthah's commission is explicit: **"Then the Spirit of the LORD was upon Jephthah, and he passed through Gilead and Manasseh... And Jephthah made a vow to the LORD and said, 'If you will give the Ammonites into my hand...'"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|Judges 11:29-30]]).

Jephthah is Spirit-empowered and delivers Israel from the Ammonites. But his rash vow—to sacrifice whatever comes out of his house first—leads to the apparent sacrifice of his daughter ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 30|Judges 11:30-40]]). This is a gross violation of the Law, which forbids human sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 18#Leviticus 18 . 21|Leviticus 18:21]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 31|Deuteronomy 12:31]]).

Jephthah fulfills the commission to deliver Israel but violates the commission to obey the Torah. His zeal is misguided, his theology is syncretistic, and his judgment is tragically flawed.

### Samson: Commissioned from Birth, Failed in Life

Samson's commission is announced before his birth: **"For behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. No razor shall come upon his head, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 5|Judges 13:5]]).

Samson is commissioned to:
1. **Be a Nazirite**: Consecrated to God, set apart for holiness ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 6#Numbers 6 . 1|Numbers 6:1-8]])
2. **Begin to save Israel**: Initiate deliverance (not complete it)

Samson repeatedly violates the Nazirite vow (touching a carcass - [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 8|Judges 14:8-9]], attending a drinking feast - [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 10|Judges 14:10]], allowing his hair to be cut - [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 19|Judges 16:19]]). Yet the Spirit of the LORD repeatedly empowers him for deliverance.

Samson fulfills the commission to "begin to save Israel" but does not complete it. He is a partial deliverer, pointing forward to the complete Deliverer who will come after him.

Beale observes that Samson's commissioned-but-compromised life illustrates the tension of the entire Judges period: God uses flawed instruments to accomplish His purposes, but their flaws reveal the need for a perfect instrument—the Messiah.

## Boaz's Commission: Kinsman-Redeemer Obligations

The book of Ruth presents a different kind of commission—the personal, familial obligation of the kinsman-redeemer (*go'el*).

### The Law of Kinsman-Redemption

The background for Boaz's commission is found in the Torah's kinsman-redemption laws:

**[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 25#Leviticus 25 . 25|Leviticus 25:25]]**: **"If your brother becomes poor and sells part of his property, then his nearest redeemer shall come and redeem what his brother has sold."**

**[[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 25#Deuteronomy 25 . 5|Deuteronomy 25:5-6]]**: **"If brothers dwell together, and one of them dies and has no son, the wife of the dead man shall not be married outside the family to a stranger. Her husband's brother shall go in to her and take her as his wife and perform the duty of a husband's brother to her. And the first son whom she bears shall succeed to the name of his dead brother, that his name may not be blotted out of Israel."**

These laws establish the *go'el* system:
1. **Redeem property**: Prevent family land from being permanently lost
2. **Raise up seed**: Preserve the family name and inheritance
3. **Protect the vulnerable**: Widows and orphans are to be cared for

Boaz recognizes his kinsman-redeemer obligation when Naomi and Ruth return to Bethlehem.

### Boaz's Faithful Fulfillment

When Ruth gleans in Boaz's field, he shows her extraordinary favor ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 8|Ruth 2:8-16]]). When she comes to the threshing floor and requests that he spread his wing over her (a metaphor for marriage and protection), Boaz responds with covenant faithfulness:

> **"May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have made this last kindness greater than the first in that you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich. And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you ask, for all my fellow townsmen know that you are a worthy woman."** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 10|Ruth 3:10-11]])

Boaz acknowledges that there is a nearer kinsman who has first right of redemption ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 12|Ruth 3:12]]). He goes to the city gate and publicly negotiates the redemption ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 1|Ruth 4:1-12]]).

When the nearer kinsman declines (because marrying Ruth would complicate his own inheritance), Boaz fulfills the commission:

> **"Then Boaz said to the elders and all the people, 'You are witnesses this day that I have bought from the hand of Naomi all that belonged to Elimelech and all that belonged to Chilion and to Mahlon. Also Ruth the Moabite, the widow of Mahlon, I have bought to be my wife, to perpetuate the name of the dead in his inheritance, that the name of the dead may not be cut off from among his brothers and from the gate of his native place. You are witnesses this day.'"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 9|Ruth 4:9-10]])

Boaz's commission fulfillment is complete:
1. **Redeems the land**: Eliminates the debt and restores the inheritance
2. **Marries Ruth**: Provides for the widow and honors the dead
3. **Raises up seed**: Perpetuates Elimelech's name and line

The result is the birth of Obed, who becomes the grandfather of King David ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 17|Ruth 4:17-22]]).

### Typology of Christ's Commission

Boaz's fulfillment of the kinsman-redeemer commission is one of the clearest types of Christ's work:

| **Boaz's Commission** | **Christ's Commission** |
|----------------------|------------------------|
| Must be near kinsman | Must be truly human ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]]) |
| Must be willing | Lays down His life willingly ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 18|John 10:18]]) |
| Must be able to pay | His blood is sufficient ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]]) |
| Redeems land and person | Redeems creation and humanity ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 21|Romans 8:21-23]]) |
| Marries the widow | Christ and the Church ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 25|Ephesians 5:25-27]]) |
| Raises up seed | Resurrection life ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 20|1 Corinthians 15:20-22]]) |

Beale observes that Boaz's commission fulfillment is a microcosm of the Adamic commission. He guards (protects Ruth), subdues (overcomes legal obstacles), and is fruitful (raises up offspring). He functions as a faithful Adam in miniature, pointing forward to the Last Adam who will fulfill the commission on a cosmic scale.

## Ruth's Commission: Covenant Faithfulness as an Outsider

Ruth herself receives a commission, though it is implicit rather than explicit: she must choose between returning to Moab or cleaving to Naomi and Yahweh.

### Ruth's Declaration: Embracing the Covenant

Ruth's famous declaration is a personal covenant commitment:

> **"Do not urge me to leave you or to return from following you. For where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God. Where you die I will die, and there will I be buried. May the LORD do so to me and more also if anything but death parts me from you."** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 16|Ruth 1:16-17]])

This is a self-imposed commission. Ruth voluntarily embraces:
1. **Naomi's path**: Geographical and relational loyalty
2. **Naomi's people**: Identification with Israel
3. **Naomi's God**: Worship of Yahweh alone
4. **Permanence**: Lifetime commitment, sealed by oath

Ruth's commission is identical to Israel's commission at Sinai: worship Yahweh, be His people, forsake all other gods. The difference is that Ruth, a Moabite, fulfills this commission while Israel, the covenant people, violates it.

Beale notes the profound irony: the Gentile outsider demonstrates covenant faithfulness while the covenant insiders commit apostasy. This anticipates the inclusion of the Gentiles in the people of God through the gospel ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 17|Romans 11:17-24]]).

### Ruth's Diligence: Faithful Work

Ruth's commission includes practical faithfulness. She gleans in the fields to provide for Naomi ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 2|Ruth 2:2]]), works diligently from morning to evening ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 7|Ruth 2:7]]), and submits to Naomi's guidance ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 5|Ruth 3:5]]).

Her diligence is noticed by Boaz: **"All that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband has been fully told to me, and how you left your father and mother and your native land and came to a people that you did not know before. The LORD repay you for what you have done, and a full reward be given you by the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge!"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 11|Ruth 2:11-12]])

Ruth's commission is not merely theological (worshiping Yahweh) but also practical (caring for Naomi, working diligently, acting with wisdom and propriety). She embodies the comprehensive nature of covenant faithfulness.

Kline's intrusion theology sees Ruth as a proleptic fulfillment of the eschatological promise that the nations will come to worship Yahweh ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 2#Isaiah 2 . 2|Isaiah 2:2-3]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 3|Isaiah 60:3]]). She is a foretaste of the ingathering of the Gentiles that will occur through the Messiah.

## The Commission to Be a Holy People

Throughout the Judges period, the fundamental commission remains unchanged: **"You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy"** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 2|Leviticus 19:2]]).

This holiness commission has several dimensions:

### 1. Separation from the Nations

Israel is called to be distinct: **"I am the LORD your God, who has separated you from the peoples. You shall therefore separate the clean beast from the unclean... You shall be holy to me, for I the LORD am holy and have separated you from the peoples, that you should be mine"** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 20#Leviticus 20 . 24|Leviticus 20:24-26]]).

This separation is not ethnic superiority but theological identity. Israel is Yahweh's treasured possession ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5]]), a kingdom of priests ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), a light to the nations ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]]).

Judges records Israel's repeated failure to maintain separation. Instead of being distinct, they intermarry with Canaanites ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 6|Judges 3:6]]), adopt Canaanite worship ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 13|Judges 2:13]]), and become indistinguishable from the nations around them.

### 2. Worship Yahweh Alone

The first commandment is clear: **"You shall have no other gods before me"** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 3|Exodus 20:3]]). The second commandment expands: **"You shall not make for yourself a carved image... You shall not bow down to them or serve them, for I the LORD your God am a jealous God"** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 4|Exodus 20:4-5]]).

The entire Judges cycle is driven by Israel's violation of this commission. They repeatedly serve Baals, Ashtaroth, Asherim, and other gods ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 11|Judges 2:11-13]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 10#Judges 10 . 6|Judges 10:6]]).

Yahweh's jealousy is not petty but covenantal. He is the husband, Israel is the bride, and idolatry is adultery ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 3#Jeremiah 3 . 1|Jeremiah 3:1-10]]; [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 1#Hosea 1 . 2|Hosea 1:2-3]]). The commission to worship Yahweh alone is the commission to be faithful in the covenant marriage.

### 3. Justice and Righteousness

Holiness includes ethical conduct: **"You shall do no injustice in court. You shall not be partial to the poor or defer to the great, but in righteousness shall you judge your neighbor"** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 15|Leviticus 19:15]]).

The judges are commissioned to judge righteously, but the period is marked by increasing injustice. The Levite's concubine is raped and murdered, and no one seeks justice until the Levite himself takes extreme action ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 19#Judges 19 . 25|Judges 19:25-30]]). The civil war against Benjamin is brutal and excessive ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 20#Judges 20 . 35|Judges 20:35-48]]). The abduction of virgins to provide wives for the surviving Benjaminites is morally reprehensible ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 20|Judges 21:20-23]]).

The holiness commission is violated comprehensively. Israel is no longer distinguishable from Sodom and Gomorrah.

Beale's corporate Adam framework explains the failure: Israel, like Adam, was commissioned to be holy image-bearers, reflecting God's character to the world. But just as Adam failed and became corrupt, so Israel fails and becomes corrupt. The commission remains, but human ability to fulfill it is lost. Only a new Adam, empowered by the Spirit and perfectly obedient, can restore holiness.

## Theological Synthesis: The Enduring Commission Despite Human Failure

The commission theme in Judges reveals both divine faithfulness and human failure. God's expectations do not change—His people are called to worship Him alone, remain separate from idolatry, teach the next generation, and judge righteously. Yet Israel repeatedly fails.

### Beale's Corporate Adam Framework

The commission in Judges is the Adamic commission applied to corporate Israel:
- **Be fruitful**: Teach the next generation (failed)
- **Subdue**: Defeat enemies and dispossess Canaanites (partial success)
- **Guard**: Protect the land from idolatry (catastrophic failure)

Each judge is temporarily commissioned to fulfill these tasks, but their mortality, moral compromise, and limited scope reveal the need for an eternal, sinless, universal Judge-King.

### Kline's Intrusion Eschatology

The judges are commissioned as temporary intrusions of the coming King. Their limited success demonstrates that the ultimate commission—establishing eternal righteousness and Sabbath-rest—requires the eschatological Deliverer.

The commission remains, but its fulfillment is deferred until the coming of the Messiah. The judges are types, not the reality.

### Ruth and Boaz: Commission Fulfilled Personally

In contrast to Israel's corporate failure, Ruth and Boaz demonstrate personal commission fulfillment:
- **Ruth**: Embraces Yahweh and His people
- **Boaz**: Redeems the widow and raises up seed
- **Both**: Demonstrate covenant faithfulness in a faithless generation

Their story is a microcosm of the gospel: Gentiles are included, redemption is accomplished, new life is created, and the Messianic line continues.

## Christological Fulfillment: Jesus, the Commissioned One Who Does Not Fail

The commission theme in Judges points forward to Jesus Christ, who perfectly fulfills every aspect of the commission:

### 1. Jesus Fulfills the Commission to Worship God Alone

Where Israel repeatedly turned to idols, Jesus perfectly worships the Father. When tempted by Satan to worship him, Jesus responds: **"You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve"** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 10|Matthew 4:10]]).

### 2. Jesus Fulfills the Commission to Teach

Where the Judges-era parents failed to teach the next generation, Jesus faithfully teaches His disciples: **"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations... teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you"** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19-20]]).

### 3. Jesus Fulfills the Commission to Judge Righteously

Where the judges were flawed and compromised, Jesus judges with perfect righteousness: **"The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son"** ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 22|John 5:22]]). His judgment is always just ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 30|John 5:30]]).

### 4. Jesus Fulfills the Commission to Redeem

Where Boaz redeemed Ruth, Jesus redeems His people:
- **Near kinsman**: Jesus took on flesh ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]])
- **Willing redeemer**: Jesus laid down His life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 18|John 10:18]])
- **Able redeemer**: His blood is sufficient ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]])
- **Raises up seed**: Jesus gives eternal life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 25|John 11:25-26]])

### 5. Jesus Fulfills the Commission to Be Holy

Where Israel failed to be a holy people, Jesus is the Holy One of God ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 34|Luke 4:34]]). He is **"holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners"** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]).

Through union with Christ, believers are commissioned and empowered to fulfill what Israel could not: **"You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light"** ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).

---

## Conclusion: The Commission Awaits the Perfect Fulfiller

The commission in Judges remains constant: worship Yahweh alone, destroy idolatry, teach the next generation, judge righteously, be a holy people. Yet Israel repeatedly fails. The judges themselves are commissioned to deliver and lead, but their temporary success and moral compromise reveal the need for a permanent, sinless Judge-King.

Ruth and Boaz demonstrate what covenant faithfulness looks like at the personal level, but their story only highlights the broader failure of corporate Israel. The commission cannot be fulfilled by human effort—it requires divine intervention.

The entire Judges period cries out for the coming Messiah, who will perfectly fulfill every commission, establish eternal righteousness, and enable His people to walk in holiness. The commission awaits the Perfect Fulfiller—Jesus Christ, the Last Adam, the Eternal Judge, the Ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer, and the Holy One of God.
