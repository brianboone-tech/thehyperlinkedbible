# 4.1.10.2 - Creation - The Second Generation and Balaam's Oracles

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.10 - Intrusion - Wilderness Wanderings II|← Back to 4.1.10]]

---

## Overview

After the chaos of death and judgment, a new creation emerges: the second generation born in the wilderness who will possess the Promised Land. The narrative of [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 22#Numbers 22 . 1|Numbers 22-27]] records both external validation (Balaam's oracles blessing Israel) and internal preparation (the second census, Joshua's commissioning). This is not a return to Eden's innocence but the formation of a new covenant community that will succeed where the first generation failed. The creation theme appears in multiple forms: a new people numbered and organized ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 1|Numbers 26]]), new leadership commissioned ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 18|Numbers 27:18-23]]), and prophetic affirmation that God's purpose to bless Abraham's seed cannot be thwarted by human or demonic opposition.

The centerpiece of this creation phase is the remarkable account of Balaam, the pagan prophet hired by Balak king of Moab to curse Israel ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 22#Numbers 22 . 5|Numbers 22:5-6]]). What emerges instead is a series of four oracles that magnify Israel's blessed status, preview their conquest of the land, and climax in a messianic prophecy of stunning clarity. These oracles demonstrate that even when a foreign king employs spiritual power to curse God's people, the LORD transforms every attempted curse into blessing ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 8|Numbers 23:8]]; [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 20|23:20]]). The creation motif appears in God's sovereign decree: "I have received a command to bless; he has blessed, and I cannot revoke it" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 20|Numbers 23:20]]).

## Balaam's First Oracle: Israel as Peculiar People

When Balak brings Balaam to overlook the Israelite camp from Bamoth-baal ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 22#Numbers 22 . 41|Numbers 22:41]]), expecting curses, Balaam instead pronounces blessing: "How can I curse whom God has not cursed? How can I denounce whom the LORD has not denounced?" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 8|Numbers 23:8]]). The first oracle establishes Israel's unique status as a people "dwelling alone, and not counting itself among the nations" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 9|Numbers 23:9]]). This echoes God's original intent for Israel to be "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), set apart from the nations not by their own merit but by divine election.

Balaam describes Israel's multitude: "Who can count the dust of Jacob or number the fourth part of Israel?" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 10|Numbers 23:10]]). The language deliberately recalls God's promise to Abraham that his offspring would be "as the dust of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 16|Genesis 13:16]]) and "as the stars of heaven and as the sand that is on the seashore" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 17|Genesis 22:17]]). Despite forty years of death in the wilderness, God's creative word to Abraham continues to be fulfilled. The second generation, though born under judgment, inherits the Abrahamic promise. Balaam's oracle concludes with a personal confession: "Let me die the death of the upright, and let my end be like his!" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 10|Numbers 23:10]]). Even this pagan prophet recognizes that Israel possesses something worth dying for—covenant relationship with the living God.

## Balaam's Second Oracle: God's Faithfulness

Frustrated by the first blessing, Balak brings Balaam to a different location (the field of Zophim on the top of Pisgah) hoping for different results ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 13|Numbers 23:13-14]]). The second oracle ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 18|Numbers 23:18-24]]) emphasizes God's unchanging nature: "God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind. Has he said, and will he not do it? Or has he spoken, and will he not fulfill it?" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 19|Numbers 23:19]]). This is a creation declaration—God's word possesses inherent creative and covenantal power. What God has spoken over Israel in blessing cannot be undone by human curse or divine caprice.

The oracle continues: "Behold, I received a command to bless; he has blessed, and I cannot revoke it. He has not beheld misfortune in Jacob, nor has he seen trouble in Israel. The LORD their God is with them, and the shout of a king is among them" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 20|Numbers 23:20-21]]). This is remarkable given Israel's actual history—the golden calf, the rebellion at Kadesh, Korah's revolt, ongoing grumbling and unbelief. Yet Balaam sees Israel not as they are in themselves but as they are in God's electing grace. The phrase "he has not beheld misfortune in Jacob" doesn't deny Israel's sin but affirms that God's covenant commitment is not based on their performance. The "shout of a king" anticipates Israel's monarchy but ultimately points to the messianic King who would reign over Jacob forever.

The oracle climaxes with an image of Israel's irresistible power under God's blessing: "God brings them out of Egypt and is for them like the horns of the wild ox. For there is no enchantment against Jacob, no divination against Israel" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 22|Numbers 23:22-23]]). The very attempt to curse Israel through divination is futile because God Himself is their deliverer and defender. The oracle concludes with prophetic description of Israel's conquest: "Behold, a people! As a lioness it rises up and as a lion it lifts itself; it does not lie down until it has devoured the prey and drunk the blood of the slain" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 24|Numbers 23:24]]). Far from being vulnerable victims in the wilderness, Israel is portrayed as a conquering lion that will devour the Canaanite nations.

## Balaam's Third Oracle: Beauty and Dominion

The third oracle ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 3|Numbers 24:3-9]]) opens with Balaam's eyes being opened to see Israel as God sees them: "How lovely are your tents, O Jacob, your encampments, O Israel! Like palm groves that stretch afar, like gardens beside a river, like aloes that the LORD has planted, like cedar trees beside the waters" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 5|Numbers 24:5-6]]). The imagery evokes Eden—gardens, rivers, planted trees. Israel's camp becomes a reconstituted Eden, a space where God's creative ordering is reasserted against the chaos of wilderness and rebellion. The comparison to "gardens beside a river" recalls the "river that flowed out of Eden to water the garden" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 10|Genesis 2:10]]), suggesting that Israel's covenantal existence represents a new Edenic reality.

The oracle continues with imagery of abundance and dominion: "Water shall flow from his buckets, and his seed shall be in many waters; his king shall be higher than Agag, and his kingdom shall be exalted" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 7|Numbers 24:7]]). The reference to Agag (king of the Amalekites) anticipates Saul's victory over Amalek ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 8|1 Samuel 15:8]]) but points beyond to the ultimate King whose kingdom will be exalted above all earthly powers. The concluding refrain echoes God's word to Abraham: "Blessed are those who bless you, and cursed are those who curse you" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 9|Numbers 24:9]]; cf. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]). Balak had hoped to curse Israel and thereby gain military advantage; instead, he receives the solemn warning that cursing Israel brings curse upon oneself.

## Balaam's Fourth Oracle: The Star and the Scepter

Balak's anger burns against Balaam ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 10|Numbers 24:10]]), but before departing, Balaam delivers a fourth, unsolicited oracle that peers into the distant future. The messianic prophecy is explicit: "I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near: a star shall come out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of Israel; it shall crush the forehead of Moab and break down all the sons of Sheth" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 17|Numbers 24:17]]). The "star" and "scepter" are royal symbols pointing to the Davidic king and, ultimately, to the Messiah. The "star" imagery evokes Genesis 15:5 where God shows Abraham the stars as a picture of his innumerable offspring, but here the star is singular—one descendant who will embody Israel's hope.

The oracle predicts military victories over Israel's enemies: Moab, Edom, Seir, and Amalek ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 17|Numbers 24:17-20]]). These conquests occurred partially under David's reign ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 8#2 Samuel 8 . 2|2 Samuel 8:2, 14]]), but the ultimate fulfillment awaits the Messiah who will "crush" (the same verb used in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]) the head of all opposition. The phrase "break down all the sons of Sheth" likely refers to all humanity in rebellion (Sheth being another name for Seth, father of all living humans after Cain's line), pointing to cosmic victory over all God's enemies. This creation oracle affirms that from Israel will come the ultimate restorer of Edenic peace and divine rule.

## The Second Census

Following Balaam's oracles, [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 1|Numbers 26]] records a second census of Israel, mirroring the first census in Numbers 1 ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 1#Numbers 1 . 1|Numbers 1:1-46]]). The LORD commands Moses and Eleazar to "take a census of all the congregation of the people of Israel, from twenty years old and upward, by their fathers' houses, all in Israel who are able to go to war" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 2|Numbers 26:2]]). This census counts the new generation—those born in the wilderness or under twenty years old at the time of the Kadesh rebellion. Significantly, the narrative notes: "But among these there was not one of those listed by Moses and Aaron the priest, who had listed the people of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. For the LORD had said of them, 'They shall die in the wilderness.' Not one of them was left, except Caleb the son of Jephunneh and Joshua the son of Nun" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 64|Numbers 26:64-65]]).

The second census is an act of divine creation—not creation ex nihilo, but the formation of a new covenant community from the remnant that survived judgment. The total number counted is 601,730 ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 51|Numbers 26:51]]), slightly fewer than the 603,550 counted in the first census ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 1#Numbers 1 . 46|Numbers 1:46]]). This decrease reflects the reality of God's judgment—a generation died in the wilderness. Yet the fact that the numbers are comparable demonstrates God's faithfulness to maintain His people despite judgment. The census serves practical purposes (dividing the land, organizing military units) but carries theological significance: God is creating a new assembly capable of receiving the promised inheritance.

The census also includes provisions for inheritance rights, notably the daughters of Zelophehad who petition for the right to inherit their father's portion since he died without sons ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 1|Numbers 27:1-11]]). The LORD's ruling in their favor demonstrates that God's creative purposes include justice and provision even for those who might be marginalized by human custom. The land distribution described in Numbers 26 anticipates the actual possession described in Joshua—a creation hope that the wilderness generation never realized but the second generation will inherit.

## Joshua's Commissioning

The creation of new leadership is essential for the second generation's success. Moses, excluded from entering the land, asks the LORD to "appoint a man over the congregation who shall go out before them and come in before them, who shall lead them out and bring them in, that the congregation of the LORD may not be as sheep that have no shepherd" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 16|Numbers 27:16-17]]). The LORD responds by designating Joshua, "a man in whom is the Spirit" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 18|Numbers 27:18]]), to succeed Moses. The commissioning ceremony involves Moses laying his hand on Joshua and giving him "some of [his] authority" before Eleazar the priest and all the congregation ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 20|Numbers 27:20-21]]).

The language of Joshua being filled with "the Spirit" echoes the creation account where God breathed His Spirit into Adam ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 7|Genesis 2:7]]), constituting him as living image-bearer. Joshua is being created/commissioned as the new covenant mediator who will bring Israel into their inheritance. Unlike Moses who was barred from entry, Joshua will "go in and out" before the people, a phrase denoting leadership authority. The requirement that Joshua inquire of the Urim before Eleazar the priest ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 21|Numbers 27:21]]) establishes accountability and maintains the priestly role in discerning God's will. This is not autonomous human leadership but Spirit-empowered, priest-mediated, theocratic governance.

The commissioning is later reinforced in [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 31#Deuteronomy 31 . 7|Deuteronomy 31:7-8]] where Moses publicly charges Joshua: "Be strong and courageous, for you shall go with this people into the land that the LORD has sworn to their fathers to give them, and you shall put them in possession of it. It is the LORD who goes before you. He will be with you; he will not leave you or forsake you. Do not fear or be dismayed." The promise of God's presence ("I will be with you") echoes the promise given to Moses at the burning bush ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 12|Exodus 3:12]]) and to the patriarchs before him. Joshua is not replacing God's presence but serving as the human instrument through whom God will fulfill His covenant promises.

## Theological Analysis: Kline's Common Grace and Beale's New Creation

Meredith Kline's theology of common grace and special grace illuminates the Balaam narrative. Balak represents the world of common grace—pagan nations who receive rain, harvest, and general blessings from God's providence ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 45|Matthew 5:45]]). Yet Balak seeks to manipulate spiritual power through divination to curse Israel, attempting to use common-grace knowledge (that spiritual forces influence events) against the people of special grace. God's sovereignty over Balaam's oracles demonstrates that no power in the common-grace sphere can thwart God's special-grace purposes. The oracles themselves function as intrusions of eschatological reality into historical time—Balaam sees the consummation ("I see him, but not now") and declares it as certain as if already accomplished.

Kline's two-register cosmology explains how Balaam, a pagan diviner, can deliver true prophecy. Balaam inhabits the lower register (earthly, common grace) but is temporarily granted access to heavenly revelation (upper register, special grace). His oracles are not products of his own spiritual virtue but sovereign acts of God overriding and redirecting pagan divination toward divine purposes. The fact that Balaam later counsels Moab to seduce Israel into idolatry ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 31#Numbers 31 . 16|Numbers 31:16]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 2#Revelation 2 . 14|Revelation 2:14]]) shows that receiving true revelation doesn't necessarily transform the heart—a distinction between common grace (truth available to all) and saving grace (transformation of the will).

G.K. Beale's new creation theology explains the census and Joshua's commissioning as acts of divine recreation. Just as God formed Adam from the dust and breathed life into him, God forms the second generation from the remnant of judgment and breathes His Spirit into their leader Joshua. The census functions like the naming of animals in Genesis 2—an act of ordering chaos and asserting dominion. By numbering the tribes and assigning inheritance portions, God is preparing Israel to fulfill the Adamic mandate to "fill the earth and subdue it" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]), now applied specifically to filling and subduing Canaan.

Beale's emphasis on temple expansion illuminates Balaam's description of Israel's camp as gardens by waters. The tabernacle at the center of the camp represents Eden restored, with the camp radiating outward as expanding sacred space. The conquest of Canaan will be the expansion of this temple-garden reality, pushing back the chaos of Canaanite idolatry and establishing Yahweh's rule. Joshua's commission as "a man in whom is the Spirit" anticipates the ultimate Spirit-anointed conqueror who will not merely conquer a land but "make all things new" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]]).

## Christological Fulfillment

Balaam's prophecy of the star and scepter finds ultimate fulfillment in Jesus Christ. The "star" imagery is picked up in the Magi's visit to worship the infant King: "Where is he who has been born king of the Jews? For we saw his star when it rose" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 2|Matthew 2:2]]). Jesus identifies Himself as "the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 16|Revelation 22:16]]). The "scepter" represents royal authority, fulfilled when Jesus claims "all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18]]). The promise that this ruler will "crush" enemies connects directly to Genesis 3:15's promise that the woman's seed would crush the serpent's head—a promise Jesus fulfills through His death and resurrection ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 16#Romans 16 . 20|Romans 16:20]]; [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]).

Balaam's declaration that "God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 23#Numbers 23 . 19|Numbers 23:19]]) takes on new meaning in light of the Incarnation. God indeed is not man—yet the Son of God became the Son of Man ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]) to fulfill every covenant promise without change or shadow of turning ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 1#James 1 . 17|James 1:17]]). In Christ, God's immutable faithfulness is wedded to genuine humanity, so that the promises spoken over Israel find their "Yes" in Him ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 1#2 Corinthians 1 . 20|2 Corinthians 1:20]]).

Joshua's commissioning as the Spirit-filled leader who brings Israel into rest is fulfilled in Jesus (whose name in Greek is "Joshua"). The book of Hebrews contrasts the two: "For if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day later on" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8]]). Joshua brought Israel into Canaan, a temporal and typological rest; Jesus brings the people of God into eternal Sabbath rest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]]). Where Moses could only take Israel to the border, and Joshua could only give earthly inheritance, Jesus leads His people through death into the true Promised Land—resurrection life in the new creation.

The second census, counting a new generation prepared to enter the land, prefigures the "great multitude that no one could number" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]) gathered from every tribe and nation who will inherit the new heavens and new earth. Just as the first generation died in wilderness because of unbelief, so all who refuse to believe in Christ will perish outside the rest of God ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 19|Hebrews 3:19]]). But those born of the Spirit (the new generation) will receive the inheritance promised to Abraham's true seed ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 29|Galatians 3:29]]).
