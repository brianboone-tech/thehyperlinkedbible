##### 4.1.15.4 - Sin

**Folly Revealed: The Way of Death and the Wicked's Prosperity**

Having issued wisdom's commission, the Wisdom Literature exposes sin's destructive reality through the personification of Folly, the description of the fool's path to death, and the troubling observation that the wicked often prosper while the righteous suffer. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 13|Proverbs 9:13-18]] presents Folly as Wisdom's dark counterpart, calling the simple to her house where stolen water is sweet and bread eaten in secret is pleasant, while those who heed her go to Sheol. The Psalter wrestles with the scandal of wickedness flourishing ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 1|Psalm 73]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 1|Psalm 37]]). [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 1#Ecclesiastes 1 . 1|Ecclesiastes]] observes repeatedly that under the sun, the righteous and wicked meet the same fate ([[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 9#Ecclesiastes 9 . 2|Ecclesiastes 9:2]]), creating theological perplexity in the Common Grace administration.

**The Fool Identified**

The Hebrew term *kesil* (fool) and related words (*nabal*, *evil*) describe not intellectual deficiency but moral-spiritual rebellion. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 14#Psalm 14 . 1|Psalm 14:1]] defines the fool fundamentally: "The fool says in his heart, 'There is no God.' They are corrupt, they do abominable deeds, there is none who does good." ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 53#Psalm 53 . 1|Psalm 53:1]] repeats this nearly verbatim.) This is not theoretical atheism but practical godlessness—living as if God doesn't exist, denying His moral governance.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 7|Proverbs 1:7]] defines the fool by contrast: "Fools despise wisdom and instruction." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 22|Proverbs 1:22]] characterizes fools as hating knowledge, refusing Wisdom's invitation. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 12#Proverbs 12 . 15|Proverbs 12:15]] states: "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but a wise man listens to advice." Folly is unteachable self-sufficiency, rejecting correction.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 18#Proverbs 18 . 2|Proverbs 18:2]] exposes the fool's motivation: "A fool takes no pleasure in understanding, but only in expressing his opinion." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 12|Proverbs 26:12]] warns: "Do you see a man who is wise in his own eyes? There is more hope for a fool than for him"—the self-deluded are beyond even the fool's reach.

The book of Proverbs presents fools as incorrigible. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 17#Proverbs 17 . 10|Proverbs 17:10]] observes: "A rebuke goes deeper into a man of understanding than a hundred blows into a fool." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 11|Proverbs 26:11]] uses repulsive imagery: "Like a dog that returns to his vomit is a fool who repeats his folly." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 27#Proverbs 27 . 22|Proverbs 27:22]] declares: "Crush a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with crushed grain, yet his folly will not depart from him."

**Lady Folly's Invitation**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 13|Proverbs 9:13-18]] personifies Folly as a loud, seductive woman sitting at the door of her house, calling to passersby: "Whoever is simple, let him turn in here!" (echoing Wisdom's words from [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 4|verse 4]]). She promises: "Stolen water is sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant" ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 17|verse 17]]). This is sin's allure—the forbidden appears desirable, transgression exciting.

But [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 18|Proverbs 9:18]] reveals the truth: "But he does not know that the dead are there, that her guests are in the depths of Sheol." Folly's house is a grave, her table a death feast. This parallels the adulteress imagery throughout Proverbs 5-7, where the strange woman's path leads to Sheol ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 5#Proverbs 5 . 5|Proverbs 5:5]], [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 27|Proverbs 7:27]]).

Beale notes the typological connection: Folly represents all sin's seduction, promising life while delivering death, appearing sweet while being poisonous. This echoes the serpent's temptation in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 1|Genesis 3]], where eating the forbidden fruit promised wisdom but brought death.

**The Strange Woman: Sexual Sin as Paradigm**

Proverbs dedicates extensive attention to warning against the adulteress (strange woman, forbidden woman). [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 2#Proverbs 2 . 16|Proverbs 2:16-19]] introduces her: she forsakes the companion of her youth, forgets her covenant with God, and her house sinks down to death. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 5#Proverbs 5 . 3|Proverbs 5:3-6]] describes her seductive speech (lips dripping honey) but ultimate bitterness (end is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a two-edged sword), leading to death and Sheol.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 5#Proverbs 5 . 9|Proverbs 5:9-14]] details the consequences: loss of honor, years given to the merciless, wealth consumed by strangers, body wasted, final groaning, public shame, near ruin before the assembly. Sexual sin destroys comprehensively—physically, economically, socially, spiritually.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 32|Proverbs 6:32-35]] declares: "He who commits adultery lacks sense; he who does it destroys himself. He will get wounds and dishonor, and his disgrace will not be wiped away. For jealousy makes a man furious, and he will not spare when he takes revenge."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 1|Proverbs 7]] provides extended narrative: a young man lacking sense walks near the adulteress's corner at twilight ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 7|verse 7-9]]). She seizes him with seductive words ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 13|verse 13-20]]), and he follows "as an ox goes to the slaughter... till an arrow pierces his liver; as a bird rushes into a snare; he does not know that it will cost him his life" ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 22|verse 22-23]]).

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 26|Proverbs 7:26-27]] concludes: "For many a victim has she laid low, and all her slain are a mighty throng. Her house is the way to Sheol, going down to the chambers of death." Sexual immorality functions as paradigmatic sin—pleasurable in the moment, destructive eternally; promising life, delivering death.

Beale notes that the strange woman also represents spiritual adultery—Israel's idolatry described as harlotry throughout the prophets (Hosea, Jeremiah, Ezekiel). To pursue false gods or worldly wisdom is to embrace death disguised as life.

**The Sluggard's Sin**

The sluggard (*atsel*) represents another form of folly. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 6|Proverbs 6:6-11]] sends him to the ant to learn wisdom: the ant works without supervision, preparing food in summer, gathering in harvest. By contrast, the sluggard sleeps excessively, folds his hands to rest, and poverty comes like a robber ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 10|verse 10-11]]).

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 4|Proverbs 10:4-5]] contrasts: "A slack hand causes poverty, but the hand of the diligent makes rich. He who gathers in summer is a prudent son, but he who sleeps in harvest is a son who brings shame." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 13#Proverbs 13 . 4|Proverbs 13:4]] states: "The soul of the sluggard craves and gets nothing, while the soul of the diligent is richly supplied."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 19#Proverbs 19 . 15|Proverbs 19:15]] warns: "Slothfulness casts into a deep sleep, and an idle person will suffer hunger." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 20#Proverbs 20 . 4|Proverbs 20:4]] observes: "The sluggard does not plow in the autumn; he will seek at harvest and have nothing." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 21#Proverbs 21 . 25|Proverbs 21:25-26]] diagnoses the problem: "The desire of the sluggard kills him, for his hands refuse to labor. All day long he craves and craves, but the righteous gives and does not hold back."

Sluggardry is practical atheism—living as if creation's order of sowing/reaping doesn't apply, ignoring God's command that humans work and keep the garden ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]]), rejecting the pattern that "if anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat" ([[Readable Bible/53 - 2 Thessalonians/2 Thessalonians 3#2 Thessalonians 3 . 10|2 Thessalonians 3:10]]).

**The Wicked Prosper: Psalm 73's Crisis**

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 1|Psalm 73]] presents the theological crisis of the wicked prospering. Asaph nearly loses faith ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 2|verse 2-3]]) envying the arrogant when he sees their prosperity. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 4|Psalm 73:4-5]] describes their health and ease: "For they have no pangs until death; their bodies are fat and sleek. They are not in trouble as others are; they are not stricken like the rest of mankind."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 6|Psalm 73:6-12]] intensifies the picture: pride adorns them like a necklace, violence clothes them, their eyes bulge with fatness, they scoff and speak with malice, setting their mouths against heaven. They think God doesn't know ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 11|verse 11]]). "Behold, these are the wicked; always at ease, they increase in riches" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 12|verse 12]]).

This creates the crisis of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 13|Psalm 73:13-14]]: "All in vain have I kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence. For all the day long I have been stricken and rebuked every morning." Why pursue righteousness if the wicked prosper and the righteous suffer? This is the problem of moral disorder in the Common Grace administration.

The resolution comes when Asaph enters God's sanctuary ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 17|verse 17]]) and perceives the wicked's end: they stand on slippery places ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 18|verse 18]]), will be destroyed in a moment ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 19|verse 19]]), are like a dream when one awakes ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 20|verse 20]]). Present prosperity doesn't reflect ultimate reality; eschatological judgment will set all right.

**Psalm 37: Don't Fret Over Evildoers**

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 1|Psalm 37:1-2]] commands: "Fret not yourself because of evildoers; be not envious of wrongdoers! For they will soon fade like the grass and wither like the green herb." The psalm repeatedly urges trust in the LORD while observing that the wicked plot ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 12|verse 12]]), borrow without repaying ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 21|verse 21]]), and attack the righteous ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 32|verse 32]]).

Yet the psalm affirms repeatedly that the wicked will be cut off ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 9|verse 9]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 22|22]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 28|28]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 34|34]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 38|38]]), while the righteous will inherit the land ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 9|verse 9]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 11|11]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 22|22]], [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 29|29]]). [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 35|Psalm 37:35-36]] testifies: "I have seen a wicked, ruthless man, spreading himself like a green laurel tree. But he passed away, and behold, he was no more; though I sought him, he could not be found."

This psalm operates within Kline's Common Grace framework: the wicked's present prosperity is temporary, not ultimate. Final judgment is eschatologically delayed, creating a mixed world where external circumstances don't immediately correspond to moral standing.

**Ecclesiastes on Wickedness**

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 3#Ecclesiastes 3 . 16|Ecclesiastes 3:16]] observes: "Moreover, I saw under the sun that in the place of justice, even there was wickedness, and in the place of righteousness, even there was wickedness." Even judicial systems meant to uphold justice become corrupted. [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 4#Ecclesiastes 4 . 1|Ecclesiastes 4:1]] laments: "Again I saw all the oppressions that are done under the sun. And behold, the tears of the oppressed, and they had no one to comfort them! On the side of their oppressors there was power."

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 7#Ecclesiastes 7 . 15|Ecclesiastes 7:15]] presents the perplexing observation: "In my vain life I have seen everything. There is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked man who prolongs his life in his evildoing." This violates the simple retribution theology that suffering always indicates sin and prosperity indicates righteousness.

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 8#Ecclesiastes 8 . 11|Ecclesiastes 8:11-14]] explains one reason wickedness persists: "Because the sentence against an evil deed is not executed speedily, the heart of the children of man is fully set to do evil." Delayed judgment emboldens sin. The Preacher observes righteous people treated according to the deeds of the wicked, and wicked people treated according to the deeds of the righteous ([[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 8#Ecclesiastes 8 . 14|verse 14]])—"this also is vanity."

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 9#Ecclesiastes 9 . 2|Ecclesiastes 9:2-3]] presents the ultimate scandal from an "under the sun" perspective: "It is the same for all, since the same event happens to the righteous and the wicked, to the good and the evil, to the clean and the unclean, to him who sacrifices and him who does not sacrifice. As the good one is, so is the sinner... This is an evil in all that is done under the sun, that the same event happens to all." Death equalizes all, seemingly erasing moral distinctions.

**Sins of the Tongue**

Proverbs extensively catalogs speech sins. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 16|Proverbs 6:16-19]] lists seven abominations to the LORD, four involving speech: a lying tongue, a false witness who breathes out lies, one who sows discord among brothers. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 18|Proverbs 10:18]] warns: "The one who conceals hatred has lying lips, and whoever utters slander is a fool."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 9|Proverbs 11:9]] states: "With his mouth the godless man would destroy his neighbor, but by knowledge the righteous are delivered." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 12#Proverbs 12 . 17|Proverbs 12:17-19]] contrasts: "Whoever speaks the truth gives honest evidence, but a false witness utters deceit... Truthful lips endure forever, but a lying tongue is but for a moment."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 16#Proverbs 16 . 27|Proverbs 16:27-28]] describes speech as destructive fire: "A worthless man plots evil, and his speech is like a scorching fire. A dishonest man spreads strife, and a whisperer separates close friends." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 18#Proverbs 18 . 8|Proverbs 18:8]] observes gossip's insidious appeal: "The words of a whisperer are like delicious morsels; they go down into the inner parts of the body."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 20|Proverbs 26:20-28]] presents extended teaching on destructive speech: gossip keeps fire burning ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 20|verse 20-21]]), the quarrelsome kindle strife ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 21|verse 21]]), the smooth tongue conceals hatred ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 23|verse 23-26]]), and lying tongues bring ruin ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 26#Proverbs 26 . 28|verse 28]]).

**Pride and Arrogance**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 16|Proverbs 6:16-17]] lists pride first among seven abominations: "haughty eyes." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 13|Proverbs 8:13]] declares: "The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil. Pride and arrogance and the way of evil and perverted speech I hate." Pride opposes the fear of the LORD fundamentally.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 2|Proverbs 11:2]] warns: "When pride comes, then comes disgrace, but with the humble is wisdom." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 16#Proverbs 16 . 5|Proverbs 16:5]] states: "Everyone who is arrogant in heart is an abomination to the LORD; be assured, he will not go unpunished." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 16#Proverbs 16 . 18|Proverbs 16:18]] gives the famous warning: "Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 21#Proverbs 21 . 4|Proverbs 21:4]] identifies pride as sin's essence: "Haughty eyes and a proud heart, the lamp of the wicked, are sin." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 29#Proverbs 29 . 23|Proverbs 29:23]] promises: "One's pride will bring him low, but he who is lowly in spirit will obtain honor."

**Injustice and Oppression**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 1|Proverbs 11:1]] condemns economic injustice: "A false balance is an abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is his delight." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 31|Proverbs 14:31]] declares: "Whoever oppresses a poor man insults his Maker, but he who is generous to the needy honors him."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 17#Proverbs 17 . 15|Proverbs 17:15]] identifies judicial corruption: "He who justifies the wicked and he who condemns the righteous are both alike an abomination to the LORD." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 17#Proverbs 17 . 23|Proverbs 17:23]] warns: "The wicked accepts a bribe in secret to pervert the ways of justice."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 22#Proverbs 22 . 22|Proverbs 22:22-23]] commands: "Do not rob the poor, because he is poor, or crush the afflicted at the gate, for the LORD will plead their cause and rob of life those who rob them." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28#Proverbs 28 . 3|Proverbs 28:3]] observes paradoxically: "A poor man who oppresses the poor is a beating rain that leaves no food"—even the oppressed can become oppressors.

**Violence and Bloodshed**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 10|Proverbs 1:10-19]] warns against joining violent men who lie in wait for blood, swallow victims alive like Sheol. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 18|Proverbs 1:18-19]] observes that those who are greedy for unjust gain lay ambushes for their own lives—violence recoils on the violent.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 17|Proverbs 6:17]] lists among abominations "hands that shed innocent blood." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28#Proverbs 28 . 17|Proverbs 28:17]] declares: "If one is burdened with the blood of another, he will be a fugitive until death; let no one help him."

**Job's Friends: The Sin of False Theology**

Job's comforters represent a subtle sin: defending God with false theology. They assume Job's suffering proves hidden sin, applying strict retribution doctrine inappropriately. [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 4#Job 4 . 7|Job 4:7-8]] presents Eliphaz's theology: "Remember: who that was innocent ever perished? Or where were the upright cut off? As I have seen, those who plow iniquity and sow trouble reap the same."

This sounds pious but is false in Job's case. [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 8#Job 8 . 3|Job 8:3-4]] shows Bildad's cruel insinuation: "Does God pervert justice? Or does the Almighty pervert the right? If your children have sinned against him, he has delivered them into the hand of their transgression." He implies Job's children deserved death for sin.

[[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 22#Job 22 . 5|Job 22:5-11]] records Eliphaz falsely accusing Job of oppressing widows, refusing water to the weary, withholding bread from the hungry—none of which is true. These friends sin by bearing false witness, adding to Job's suffering, and misrepresenting God's character.

[[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 42#Job 42 . 7|Job 42:7-8]] vindicates Job and condemns the friends: "My anger burns against you... for you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has." They must offer sacrifices while Job intercedes for them. False theology, even when well-intentioned, is sin.

**Theological Framework: Beale on Sin's Deception**

Beale emphasizes that Wisdom Literature exposes sin as fundamentally deceptive—promising life while delivering death, appearing sweet while being poisonous. This continues the serpent's pattern from [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 1|Genesis 3]], where the forbidden fruit was desirable to make one wise but actually brought death.

Folly personified represents sin's seductive power. The strange woman images spiritual adultery—idolatry and false worship that appear attractive but lead to Sheol. The prosperity of the wicked demonstrates that outward circumstances don't reveal spiritual reality; only eschatological judgment will expose all hearts.

The Wisdom Literature's treatment of sin prepares for the New Testament's revelation that all have sinned ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 23|Romans 3:23]]), there is none righteous ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 10|Romans 3:10-12]]), and the wages of sin is death ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 23|Romans 6:23]]). The fool who says "there is no God" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 14#Psalm 14 . 1|Psalm 14:1]]) is every person in their natural state, suppressing truth in unrighteousness ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 18|Romans 1:18]]).

**Theological Framework: Kline on Sin in Common Grace**

Kline's framework explains why the wicked prosper and the righteous suffer in the present age. Common Grace administration delays final judgment, allowing wheat and tares to grow together ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 30|Matthew 13:30]]). The wicked receive undeserved temporal blessings (rain, harvest, health) while the righteous may suffer temporal afflictions.

This is not moral randomness but eschatological patience. [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]] explains: "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance." The delay allows opportunity for salvation.

However, Kline emphasizes that Common Grace restrains sin's full manifestation. Without divine restraint, sin would destroy all order immediately. The fact that creation continues, that diligence generally produces fruit, that justice sometimes prevails—all demonstrate God's common grace preserving creation until the final judgment.

The Wisdom Literature's wrestling with moral ambiguity in the present age anticipates the New Testament's "already-not yet" tension: Christ has defeated sin definitively ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]) but its full eradication awaits His return ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 4|Revelation 21:4]]).

**Christological Fulfillment: Jesus and Sin**

Jesus confronted sin at every level. He exposed the Pharisees' hypocrisy—whitewashed tombs full of dead bones ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 27|Matthew 23:27]]), blind guides straining gnats while swallowing camels ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 24|Matthew 23:24]]). He called the scribes fools for focusing on external purity while neglecting justice, mercy, and faithfulness ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 23|Matthew 23:23]]).

Jesus identified sin's root as internal: evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false witness, and slander all come from the heart ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 15#Matthew 15 . 19|Matthew 15:19]]). This deepens Proverbs' teaching: the heart must be guarded ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 23|Proverbs 4:23]]) because it's the source of life or death.

On the cross, Jesus bore sin's consequences. He who knew no sin became sin for us ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]), experiencing the death that Folly's guests receive ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 18|Proverbs 9:18]]). He descended to the depths of Sheol that the adulteress's path leads to ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 27|Proverbs 7:27]]), but rose victorious, defeating death ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 55|1 Corinthians 15:55-57]]).

Jesus resolved the prosperity problem of Psalm 73. Though wicked leaders condemned Him and wicked crowds crucified Him, His resurrection vindicated righteousness and exposed wickedness. [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 17#Acts 17 . 31|Acts 17:31]] declares God "has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by a man whom he has appointed; and of this he has given assurance to all by raising him from the dead."

**Application for God's People**

Wisdom Literature's exposure of sin warns believers against self-deception. Sin promises pleasure but delivers death; folly appears desirable but ends in Sheol. Christians must guard their hearts ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 23|Proverbs 4:23]]), flee sexual immorality ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 18|1 Corinthians 6:18]]), tame the tongue ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 3#James 3 . 8|James 3:8]]), and humble themselves before God ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 4#James 4 . 10|James 4:10]]).

The prosperity of the wicked should not cause envy or doubt. Believers must not fret over evildoers ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 37#Psalm 37 . 1|Psalm 37:1]]) but trust in eschatological justice. Present circumstances don't reflect ultimate reality; final judgment will vindicate the righteous and condemn the wicked.

Awareness of sin's pervasiveness drives believers to Christ, the only Righteous One, whose blood cleanses from all sin ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]). Confessing sin and receiving forgiveness ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]) replaces the fool's path to death with the wise person's path to life in Christ.
