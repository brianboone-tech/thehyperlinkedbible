# 4.1.7.2 - Creation

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.7 - Intrusion - The Exodus to Mount Sinai|← Back to 4.1.7]]

## Overview: The Red Sea as New Creation Event

The Creation phase of the Exodus intrusion (approximately [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 37|Exodus 12:37]] - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 21|Exodus 15:21]]) presents the passage through the Red Sea as a **new creation event**. This is not merely a miraculous rescue but a **birth of a nation** through the waters, echoing the creation narratives of Genesis 1 and the flood of Genesis 6-8. Israel emerges from the depths as God's "firstborn son" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22|Exodus 4:22-23]]), a new humanity called to fulfill the Adamic commission.

G.K. Beale emphasizes that the Red Sea crossing is the **paradigmatic Old Testament new creation event**, prefiguring Christian baptism, resurrection, and the final new creation. Just as the Spirit hovered over the waters in Genesis 1 ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]]), so God's presence in cloud and fire leads Israel through the divided waters. Just as Noah emerged from the flood into a cleansed world ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 15|Genesis 8:15-19]]), so Israel emerges from the Red Sea into new life.

Meredith Kline's two-register framework helps us understand this as a **massive intrusion** of upper-register (Consummation) glory into the lower-register (Common Grace) period. The Red Sea crossing reveals God's power to **de-create His enemies** (Egypt) while simultaneously **creating His people** (Israel) through the same waters. This pattern will find its ultimate fulfillment when Christ passes through the waters of death and emerges in resurrection, bringing many sons to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]).

## 1. The Passover: Substitutionary Atonement

Before the Exodus itself, God institutes the **Passover** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 1|Exodus 12:1-28]]), establishing the principle of substitutionary atonement that will define redemption throughout Scripture.

### The Lamb Without Blemish

"Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male a year old. You may take it from the sheep or from the goats, and you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, when the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it at twilight" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5-6]]).

The **lamb without blemish** prefigures Christ as the perfect, sinless sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]—"a lamb without blemish or spot"; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]—"without blemish"). The slaughter "at twilight" on the fourteenth day corresponds to the timing of Christ's crucifixion ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]]; [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]).

### The Blood Applied

"Then they shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and the lintel of the houses in which they eat it... The blood shall be a sign for you, on the houses where you are. And when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will befall you to destroy you, when I strike the land of Egypt" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 7|Exodus 12:7]], [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 13|13]]).

The **applied blood** is the only protection from judgment. Either the firstborn dies, or the lamb dies in his place—there is no third option. This establishes the core principle: **without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 22|Hebrews 9:22]]).

Christ's blood, once applied by faith, protects believers from the final judgment ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]]; [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]).

### Israel as God's Firstborn Son

God identifies Israel as His **"firstborn son"** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22|Exodus 4:22-23]]), and the death of Egypt's firstborn is explicitly judgment for refusing to release God's son. The redemption of Israel's firstborn through the Passover lamb establishes the pattern: **the firstborn belongs to God and must be redeemed through substitution** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 11|Exodus 13:11-16]]).

Christ is God's true **Firstborn** ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15|Colossians 1:15]], [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 18|18]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 6|Hebrews 1:6]]), the beloved Son who is sacrificed so that many sons might be brought to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]).

### Memorial Feast

"This day shall be for you a memorial day, and you shall keep it as a feast to the LORD; throughout your generations, as a statute forever, you shall keep it as a feast" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 14|Exodus 12:14]]).

The Passover becomes an **annual memorial**, prefiguring the Lord's Supper, which memorializes Christ's once-for-all sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 11#1 Corinthians 11 . 23|1 Corinthians 11:23-26]]). Both are covenant meals that proclaim redemption accomplished.

## 2. The Exodus: Birth of a Nation

The departure from Egypt marks the **birth of Israel as a nation**.

### The Great Company

"The people of Israel journeyed from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides women and children. A mixed multitude also went up with them, and very much livestock, both flocks and herds" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 37|Exodus 12:37-38]]).

The number—approximately **600,000 men**, suggesting a total population of 2-3 million—demonstrates the fulfillment of God's promise to multiply Abraham's seed ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 5|Genesis 15:5]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 17|22:17]]). What entered Egypt as **70 people** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 46#Genesis 46 . 27|Genesis 46:27]]) emerges as a **great nation** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 2|Genesis 12:2]]).

The "mixed multitude" indicates that **non-Israelites** joined the Exodus, anticipating the inclusion of Gentiles in the people of God ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 28|Galatians 3:28-29]]; [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 11|Ephesians 2:11-22]]).

### Fulfillment of Covenant Timing

"The time that the people of Israel lived in Egypt was 430 years. At the end of 430 years, on that very day, all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 40|Exodus 12:40-41]]).

The precision of "on that very day" emphasizes God's **faithfulness to His covenant word** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 13|Genesis 15:13-16]]). God had predicted Israel's enslavement and deliverance, and He brings it to pass exactly on schedule. This establishes confidence that **all of God's promises will be fulfilled** at their appointed time ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 4|Galatians 4:4]]—"when the fullness of time had come").

## 3. The Red Sea Crossing: New Creation Through Water

The crossing of the Red Sea ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 10|Exodus 14:10-31]]) is the **central new creation event** of the Exodus, deliberately echoing Genesis 1 creation imagery.

### God's Presence: Cloud and Fire

"And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them along the way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light, that they might travel by day and by night" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 21|Exodus 13:21]]).

The **pillar of cloud and fire** represents God's manifest presence, recalling the Spirit hovering over the waters at creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]]). Cloud and fire are theophanic symbols of God's glory ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16-18]]; [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 15|24:15-18]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 17#Matthew 17 . 5|Matthew 17:5]]).

The pillar moves to stand **between Israel and Egypt** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 19|Exodus 14:19-20]]), providing light for Israel and darkness for Egypt. God is both **protector of His people** and **judge of His enemies**.

### The Waters Divided

"Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided. And the people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 21|Exodus 14:21-22]]).

The language deliberately echoes **Genesis 1**:

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 6|Genesis 1:6-7]]** — God separates the waters above from the waters below, creating dry ground
**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 9|Genesis 1:9]]** — "Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear"
**[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 21|Exodus 14:21-22]]** — The waters are divided, dry ground appears, and Israel passes through on foot

Beale notes that the same Hebrew root (*baqa'*, "to divide/split") is used in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 6|Genesis 1:6]] (separating waters) and [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 16|Exodus 14:16]], 21 (dividing the Red Sea). This linguistic connection confirms that the Red Sea crossing is a **new creation event**.

### De-Creation for Egypt, Re-Creation for Israel

The **same waters** that bring deliverance to Israel bring destruction to Egypt:

"The waters returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen; of all the host of Pharaoh that had followed them into the sea, not one of them remained. But the people of Israel walked on dry ground through the sea, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 28|Exodus 14:28-29]]).

This parallels the **flood narrative**:

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 21|Genesis 7:21-23]]** — The flood waters destroy all flesh outside the ark
**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1]]** — God "remembers" Noah and makes the waters recede
**[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 28|Exodus 14:28-31]]** — The Red Sea waters destroy Egypt but deliver Israel

Kline observes this as the **two-register pattern**: the same divine act brings **de-creation judgment** on God's enemies (upper-register intrusion) and **new creation life** to God's people (Common Grace continues for the redeemed).

## 4. Israel's Response: The Song of Moses

After the crossing, Israel's first act is **worship**—the Song of Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 1|Exodus 15:1-21]]), the first recorded song in Scripture.

### Celebrating God's Triumph

"I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea. The LORD is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation; this is my God, and I will praise him, my father's God, and I will exalt him" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 1|Exodus 15:1-2]]).

The song celebrates **God's victory** over Pharaoh and Egypt. Key themes:

**The LORD is a warrior** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 3|Exodus 15:3]]) — God fights for His people
**Yahweh's incomparability** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 11|Exodus 15:11]]) — "Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods?"
**Redemption and guidance** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 13|Exodus 15:13]]) — "You have led in your steadfast love the people whom you have redeemed"
**God's dwelling place** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 17|Exodus 15:17]]) — "You will bring them in and plant them on your own mountain... the sanctuary, O Lord, which your hands have established"

### Miriam and the Women

"Then Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron, took a tambourine in her hand, and all the women went out after her with tambourines and dancing. And Miriam sang to them: 'Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 20|Exodus 15:20-21]]).

The participation of **Miriam and the women** in worship demonstrates that the whole community—men and women—celebrate God's salvation. Miriam is identified as a **prophetess**, highlighting the role of women in preserving and proclaiming God's works (cf. the midwives, Moses' mother, Pharaoh's daughter).

### Eschatological Fulfillment

The Song of Moses is recalled in **Revelation 15:3**, where the redeemed sing "the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb." The Red Sea deliverance prefigures the **final deliverance** when God's people will stand victorious over the beast and his image, singing of God's triumph ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 15#Revelation 15 . 1|Revelation 15:1-4]]).

## 5. Baptism Typology: Paul's Interpretation

The New Testament explicitly interprets the Red Sea crossing as a **type of baptism**.

### Baptized into Moses

"For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 1|1 Corinthians 10:1-2]]).

Paul identifies the Red Sea crossing as **baptism into Moses**—the people are united to their mediator through the waters. This prefigures **baptism into Christ** ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 3|Romans 6:3-4]]; [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 27|Galatians 3:27]]), where believers are united to Christ through the waters of baptism, participating in His death and resurrection.

### Death and Resurrection Pattern

"We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 4|Romans 6:4]]).

The Red Sea pattern:

- **Egypt** = Bondage to sin and death
- **Passover/Red Sea** = Death of the old life
- **Emergence on far shore** = Resurrection to new life
- **Journey to Promised Land** = Sanctification/pilgrimage

Christian baptism proclaims this same pattern: **death to sin, resurrection to new life in Christ** ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 12|Colossians 2:12]]).

## 6. Wilderness Provision: God Sustains His New Creation

After the Red Sea, Israel enters the **wilderness**, where God provides supernaturally, demonstrating His ongoing care for His new creation.

### Water from the Rock

At Rephidim, the people grumble for water, and God commands Moses: "Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb, and you shall strike the rock, and water shall come out of it, and the people will drink" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 17#Exodus 17 . 6|Exodus 17:6]]).

Paul identifies the rock as **Christ**: "For they drank from the spiritual Rock that followed them, and the Rock was Christ" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 4|1 Corinthians 10:4]]). Jesus will later identify Himself as the source of **living water** ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 10|John 4:10]], [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 14|14]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 37|7:37-39]]), the Spirit-giving Rock from whom eternal life flows.

The **striking of the rock** prefigures Christ being struck in judgment so that living water (the Spirit) might flow to believers ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 38|John 7:38-39]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|19:34]]).

### Manna from Heaven

When Israel grumbles for food, God provides **manna**:

"Then the LORD said to Moses, 'Behold, I am about to rain bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go out and gather a day's portion every day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law or not'" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 4|Exodus 16:4]]).

Key theological elements:

**Daily dependence**: Israel must gather fresh manna each day (except the Sabbath), teaching trust in God's daily provision ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 6#Matthew 6 . 11|Matthew 6:11]]—"give us this day our daily bread")

**Sabbath rest**: Double portions on the sixth day preserve the Sabbath ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 22|Exodus 16:22-30]]), establishing the pattern of work and rest

**Testing**: The manna tests whether Israel will obey God's word ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 8#Deuteronomy 8 . 2|Deuteronomy 8:2-3]]—"man does not live by bread alone")

**Christ as true bread**: Jesus identifies Himself as the **true bread from heaven** who gives eternal life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 32|John 6:32-35]], [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 48|48-51]])

### Quail

God also provides **quail** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 13|Exodus 16:13]]), demonstrating His abundant provision. Later, when Israel demands meat in unbelief ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 4|Numbers 11:4-6]]), God provides quail but also sends judgment ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 31|Numbers 11:31-34]]), showing the difference between **provision in faith** and **provision in grumbling**.

## 7. Theological Themes: New Creation in Christ

The Creation phase of the Exodus points forward to the **ultimate new creation** accomplished in Christ.

### Christ as the True Israel

Where Israel passed through the Red Sea as God's "son" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22|Exodus 4:22-23]]), Christ is the **true Son** called out of Egypt ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 15|Matthew 2:15]], quoting [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 11#Hosea 11 . 1|Hosea 11:1]]). Christ succeeds where Israel failed, perfectly fulfilling the Law and accomplishing the greater Exodus.

### Baptism as Participation in Christ's Death and Resurrection

The Red Sea typology finds fulfillment in **Christian baptism**, which unites believers to Christ's death and resurrection ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 3|Romans 6:3-5]]; [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 12|Colossians 2:12]]). Just as Israel passed through death (the waters) into new life, believers pass through death to sin into resurrection life in Christ.

### The Spirit as Life-Giver

The cloud and fire that led Israel prefigure the **Holy Spirit**, who leads believers ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 14|Romans 8:14]]; [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 18|Galatians 5:18]]). The Spirit is the **down payment** of the final new creation ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 1#2 Corinthians 1 . 22|2 Corinthians 1:22]]; [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 5|5:5]]; [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 13|Ephesians 1:13-14]]).

### New Creation Already and Not Yet

The Exodus demonstrates the **already/not yet** tension of redemption:

**Already**: Israel is delivered, a new nation born, God dwells among them
**Not Yet**: They are still in the wilderness, not yet in the Promised Land

Similarly, believers are **already** new creations in Christ ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]) but **not yet** fully glorified ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 23|Romans 8:23-25]]; [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 2|1 John 3:2]]). We live between the Red Sea (conversion) and the Jordan (glorification).

## 8. Kline's Framework: Upper-Register Intrusion

Meredith Kline's two-register cosmology illuminates the Red Sea as a **dramatic intrusion** of eschatological glory into history.

### De-Creation and Re-Creation Simultaneous

The Red Sea event demonstrates God's **dual sovereignty**:

**De-creation for enemies**: Egypt is uncreated, swallowed by the waters—a preview of final judgment
**Re-creation for His people**: Israel is born through the waters—a preview of final resurrection

This simultaneous de-creation/re-creation pattern will reach its climax at the **final judgment**, when the old creation passes away and the new creation is fully revealed ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-5]]).

### Theophanic Glory

The pillar of cloud and fire, the dividing of the waters, the destruction of enemies—these are **theophanies**, visible manifestations of God's glory. They are intrusions of the upper register into the lower register, previews of the Day of the Lord when God's glory will be fully revealed ([[Readable Bible/35 - Habakkuk/Habakkuk 2#Habakkuk 2 . 14|Habakkuk 2:14]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 23|Revelation 21:23]]).

### Common Grace Continues

Yet despite this massive intrusion, **Common Grace continues**—Israel does not immediately enter the Promised Land but wanders in the wilderness for 40 years. The intrusion is real but **not yet consummated**. Full rest awaits ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8-11]]).

---

## Conclusion: From Death to Life

The Creation phase of the Exodus—Passover, Red Sea crossing, wilderness provision—demonstrates God's power to bring **life from death**, to create a new people through the waters of judgment. Egypt represents the old world under curse; Israel on the far shore represents the new creation under blessing.

This pattern is the gospel in miniature: **Christ passes through the waters of death** (crucifixion) and emerges in resurrection, bringing many sons to glory. Believers are united to Christ through baptism, dying to sin and rising to new life. The Spirit sustains them through the wilderness of this present age, providing daily bread and living water, until they cross the final Jordan into the Promised Land of eternal rest.

The Song of Moses will be joined by the **Song of the Lamb** ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 15#Revelation 15 . 3|Revelation 15:3]]), as the redeemed stand on the sea of glass, having conquered through the blood of the Lamb. The Red Sea was just the beginning—the ultimate new creation awaits, when God will dwell with His people forever in the new heavens and new earth ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3-4]]).
