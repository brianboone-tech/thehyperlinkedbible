# 4.1.1.4 - Sin - of Murder of Abel (Genesis 4:8)

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.1 - Intrusion - Cain and Abel|← Back to 4.1.1]]

---

## Text

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-8]]**: "In the course of time Cain brought to the LORD an offering of the fruit of the ground, and Abel also brought of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions. And the LORD had regard for Abel and his offering, but for Cain and his offering he had no regard. So Cain was very angry, and his face fell. The LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it.' Cain spoke to Abel his brother. And when they were in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel and killed him."

---

## The Sin Unfolds: From Rejected Offering to Murder

The movement from **Commission** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 2|Genesis 4:2-4]]—work, worship, imaging God) to **Sin** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5-8]]—anger, murder, rebellion) marks the tragic failure of this intrusion. What began with hope—new life, new opportunity, acceptable worship—ends in violence and death.

The sin unfolds in stages:

1. **God rejects Cain's offering** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5]])
2. **Cain becomes angry** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5]])
3. **God warns Cain** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 6|Genesis 4:6-7]])
4. **Cain murders Abel** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 8|Genesis 4:8]])
5. **Cain lies to God** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 9|Genesis 4:9]])

This is not a sudden outburst but a **progression**. Cain's sin begins in the heart (anger, jealousy, pride) and culminates in action (murder, deception). This pattern—**internal sin → external sin**—will repeat throughout Scripture.

---

## Stage 1: The Rejected Offering

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5]]**: "But for Cain and his offering he had no regard. So Cain was very angry, and his face fell."

God accepts Abel's offering but **rejects** Cain's. The Hebrew says literally "he [God] did not look upon" (לֹא שָׁעָה, *lo sha'ah*) Cain's offering—implying a **turning away**, a refusal to regard, a divine rejection.

### Why Was Cain's Offering Rejected?

As explored in the Commission section, the reasons for rejection include:

- **Lack of faith** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]])
- **Generic offering** (not firstfruits, not the best)
- **No blood** (no recognition of need for atonement)
- **Wrong heart attitude** (pride, presumption, self-righteousness)

Cain approaches God on his own terms. He offers what he chooses, when he chooses, how he chooses. He assumes God will accept him. When God does not, Cain is outraged.

This reveals the fundamental problem: **Cain does not believe he needs grace**. He thinks his works are sufficient. He expects acceptance based on his effort, not God's mercy. This is the essence of **self-righteousness**—approaching God with a sense of entitlement rather than humility.

### The Pattern of Works-Based Religion

Cain becomes the **archetype** of works-based religion throughout Scripture:

- **Pharisees** approach God trusting in their own righteousness ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 18#Luke 18 . 9|Luke 18:9-14]])
- **Judaizers** insist on circumcision and law-keeping for salvation ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 2#Galatians 2 . 16|Galatians 2:16-21]])
- **False teachers** promote self-effort and human achievement over grace ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 2|Philippians 3:2-9]])

The New Testament explicitly contrasts **Abel** (justified by faith) with **Cain** (condemned by works):

- **[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]]**: "By faith Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain"
- **[[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]]**: "We should not be like Cain, who was of the evil one"
- **Jude 11**: "Woe to them! For they walked in the way of Cain"

The "way of Cain" is the way of self-justification, human merit, and rejection of grace. It is the way of the serpent—autonomy, rebellion, the desire to be "like God" on one's own terms ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 5|Genesis 3:5]]).

---

## Stage 2: Anger and Jealousy

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5]]**: "So Cain was very angry, and his face fell."

Cain's response to rejection is not **repentance** but **anger**. He does not ask, "What did I do wrong? How can I make this right?" Instead, he becomes enraged. His "face fell"—indicating sullen resentment, brooding bitterness.

### Anger as the Root of Murder

**Jesus** later teaches that **anger is the seed of murder**: "Everyone who is angry with his brother will be liable to judgment... whoever says, 'You fool!' will be liable to the hell of fire" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 22|Matthew 5:22]]). Murder begins in the heart. The physical act is merely the outward expression of inner hatred.

Cain's anger reveals several sinful attitudes:

1. **Pride**: "How dare God reject me?"
2. **Jealousy**: "Why is Abel accepted and not me?"
3. **Self-pity**: "I am the victim here."
4. **Blame-shifting**: "This is God's fault for being unfair."

None of these attitudes acknowledge **personal responsibility**. Cain does not consider that the problem might be with his own heart, his own offering, his own approach to God. Instead, he externalizes blame and nurses his grievance.

### The Destructive Power of Envy

**[[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]]** identifies the root of Cain's sin: "Why did he murder him? Because his own deeds were evil and his brother's righteous."

Cain's envy is not merely about the **offering**. It is about **righteousness**. Abel is righteous; Cain is not. Abel's very existence is a rebuke to Cain. Abel's acceptance highlights Cain's rejection. Rather than repent, Cain seeks to **eliminate the source of his shame**.

This dynamic will repeat throughout Scripture:

- **Saul envies David** and seeks to kill him ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 18#1 Samuel 18 . 7|1 Samuel 18:7-9]])
- **Religious leaders envy Jesus** and plot his death ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 18|Matthew 27:18]]; [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 15#Mark 15 . 10|Mark 15:10]])
- **World hates the church** because the church exposes the world's sin ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 18|John 15:18-21]])

The righteous always provoke the wicked. Light exposes darkness. Truth confronts lies. And the wicked, rather than repent, often seek to silence the witness.

## Stage 3: God's Warning—Opportunity for Repentance

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 6|Genesis 4:6-7]]**: "The LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it.'"

God does not immediately judge Cain. Instead, he **intervenes** with a warning and an invitation. This demonstrates God's patience, his desire for repentance, his willingness to give Cain another chance.

### "If You Do Well, Will You Not Be Accepted?"

God makes clear that **the door is still open**. Cain is not permanently rejected. If he repents, if he offers the right sacrifice, if he approaches God in faith, he will be accepted. The problem is not God's arbitrary will but Cain's hard heart.

This establishes the biblical principle: **God is eager to accept those who come to him in faith**. "Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you" ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 4#James 4 . 8|James 4:8]]). "Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 10#Romans 10 . 13|Romans 10:13]]).

God's question—"Why are you angry?"—is an invitation to **self-examination**. Cain's anger is misdirected. He is angry at Abel, angry at God, but he should be angry at **himself**—at his own pride, his own sin, his own rebellion.

### "Sin Is Crouching at the Door"

This is one of the most vivid images of sin in Scripture. Sin is personified as a **predatory beast** (רֹבֵץ, *robets*—crouching, lying in wait) at the door of Cain's heart. It is ready to pounce. It waits for an opportunity. It desires (תְּשׁוּקָה, *teshuqah*) to devour Cain—the same word used in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 16|Genesis 3:16]] for the woman's "desire" for her husband.

The imagery is clear: **Sin is aggressive, dangerous, predatory**. It does not passively wait to be indulged. It actively seeks to master and destroy.

### "You Must Rule Over It"

God commands Cain to "rule over" (מָשַׁל, *mashal*) sin. This echoes the **dominion mandate** given to Adam ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26, 28]]—"have dominion"). Just as Adam was to subdue the earth, Cain must subdue sin.

This is the new reality after the Fall: Humanity must not only subdue **external chaos** (wilderness, creation) but also **internal chaos** (sin, temptation, the fallen nature).

**The tragedy**: Cain **can** rule over sin. God says he "must" (imperative). The ability is there. The responsibility is clear. Cain is not a helpless victim. He has a choice.

But Cain **refuses**. He will not rule over sin. Instead, **sin will rule over him**. He will not master the beast. Instead, the beast will master him.

This is the essence of human rebellion: **We are called to rule, but we choose to be ruled**. We are meant to be kings, but we become slaves.

## Stage 4: The Murder—Genesis 3:15 Enacted

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 8|Genesis 4:8]]**: "Cain spoke to Abel his brother. And when they were in the field, Cain rose up against his brother Abel and killed him."

The Hebrew is stark and direct. "Cain spoke to Abel his brother"—the text does not record what he said. Some ancient versions (Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch) add "Let us go out to the field." Whether premeditated or spontaneous, the result is the same: **fratricide**.

### The First Murder

This is the **first act of violence** in human history. The first death by human hands. The first bloodshed in the Bible. The escalation from Adam's sin (eating forbidden fruit) to Cain's sin (murdering his brother) is staggering.

**Sin escalates**. It never stays static. It always grows worse. What begins with pride and rebellion culminates in violence and death. "Sin, when it is fully grown, brings forth death" ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 1#James 1 . 15|James 1:15]]).

### Fratricide: Brother Kills Brother

The text emphasizes the relationship: "Abel **his brother**" (אָחִיו, *achiv*) is repeated multiple times in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 8|Genesis 4:8-11]]. Cain does not kill a stranger or an enemy. He kills his **own brother**—his flesh and blood, the one with whom he shares parents, home, upbringing.

This is not merely murder. It is **fratricide**—the ultimate betrayal of family, the shattering of the most fundamental human bond. Cain destroys the one he should have loved and protected.

The **family**, which was meant to be the foundation of society and the locus of covenant faithfulness, is now fractured by violence. The pattern of broken relationships that began in Genesis 3 (Adam and Eve hiding, blame-shifting) reaches a new low in Genesis 4 (brother murdering brother).

This prefigures the relational chaos that will pervade fallen humanity: **families torn apart, brothers at war, violence within the covenant community** (Jacob and Esau, Joseph and his brothers, Absalom and Amnon, Israel's civil wars).

### Genesis 3:15 Enacted: The Serpent's Seed Strikes

The murder of Abel is the **first fulfillment** of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]: "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."

- **Cain** = seed of the serpent ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]]—"of the evil one")
- **Abel** = seed of the woman (righteous, faithful, accepted by God)

The serpent's seed **strikes** the woman's seed. Abel is murdered. The righteous suffer. The wicked prosper (temporarily). This is the pattern of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] throughout history.

But Genesis 3:15 also promises that the woman's seed will ultimately **crush the serpent's head**. Abel's death is not the end. God will raise up Seth to replace Abel ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]]). The seed line will continue. The ultimate Seed—Jesus Christ—will come and defeat the serpent once and for all.

Abel's death foreshadows **all the righteous martyrs** who will suffer at the hands of the wicked:

- **Prophets** killed by Israel ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 29|Matthew 23:29-37]])
- **John the Baptist** beheaded by Herod ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 14#Matthew 14 . 10|Matthew 14:10]])
- **Stephen** stoned by religious leaders ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 59|Acts 7:59-60]])
- **James** executed by Herod Agrippa ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 12#Acts 12 . 2|Acts 12:2]])

**Jesus** himself identifies Abel as the first in a long line of martyrs: "From the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah... I tell you, it will be required of this generation" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 11#Luke 11 . 51|Luke 11:50-51]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 35|Matthew 23:35]]).

Abel's blood cries out. The blood of all the righteous cries out. And God **hears**. He will vindicate. He will avenge. Justice will come.

## Stage 5: Cain Lies to God

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 9|Genesis 4:9]]**: "Then the LORD said to Cain, 'Where is Abel your brother?' He said, 'I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?'"

After the murder, God confronts Cain—just as he confronted Adam after the Fall ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 9|Genesis 3:9]]—"Where are you?"). God's question is not for information (he knows where Abel is) but for **confession**. God gives Cain an opportunity to repent, to acknowledge his sin, to seek mercy.

But Cain **lies**. "I do not know." This is a blatant falsehood. Cain knows exactly where Abel is—he just killed him.

### "Am I My Brother's Keeper?"

Cain's question is dripping with contempt. He not only denies knowledge but also denies **responsibility**. "Am I my brother's keeper?" The answer, of course, is **yes**.

Humanity is created for **community**. We are our brother's keeper. We are called to love, protect, and care for one another. "You shall love your neighbor as yourself" ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 18|Leviticus 19:18]]). "Bear one another's burdens" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 2|Galatians 6:2]]).

Cain's question reveals his **radical individualism**—the belief that he has no obligation to anyone, that he is autonomous, that he can do as he pleases without regard for others. This is the spirit of the serpent: **autonomy, self-rule, rejection of covenant obligations**.

### The Pattern: Sin → Cover-Up → Lie

Cain's lie follows the same pattern as Adam's blame-shifting ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 12|Genesis 3:12-13]]):

1. **Sin is committed**
2. **Guilt is felt**
3. **Cover-up is attempted**
4. **Lies are told**
5. **Blame is shifted**

This is the universal human response to sin. We hide, deny, rationalize, and deflect. We do everything except **confess and repent**.

Cain's refusal to confess seals his fate. Had he repented, God might have shown mercy (as he did later with David, who confessed his sin and was forgiven, though consequences remained). But Cain **hardens his heart**. He doubles down. He refuses grace.

## Theological Significance: The Escalation of Sin

### 1. Sin Escalates from Internal to External

Cain's sin begins in the **heart** (anger, jealousy, pride) and progresses to **action** (murder, lies, rebellion). This illustrates the biblical principle: **Sin is not merely external behavior but internal condition**.

**Jesus** teaches that sin begins in the heart: "Out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false witness, slander" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 15#Matthew 15 . 19|Matthew 15:19]]). The heart is "deceitful above all things, and desperately sick" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 17#Jeremiah 17 . 9|Jeremiah 17:9]]).

Cain's murder is not an isolated act. It is the **fruit** of a corrupt heart. The external sin reveals the internal rebellion.

### 2. Sin Escalates from Generation to Generation

Adam eats forbidden fruit. Cain murders his brother. By Genesis 6, the earth is "filled with violence" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 11|Genesis 6:11, 13]]). The downward spiral accelerates.

This demonstrates the **metastasizing nature of sin**. It does not stay contained. It spreads, infects, corrupts. What begins with one act of disobedience ends with global depravity.

Only the **flood** (judgment) and the **new covenant** (redemption) can break the cycle.

### 3. The Righteous Suffer, the Wicked Prosper (Temporarily)

Abel is righteous, yet he dies. Cain is wicked, yet he lives. This is the paradox of life under the curse. Justice is not immediate. The wicked often prosper. The righteous often suffer.

This pattern pervades the **Psalms** and **Wisdom Literature**:

- **Psalm 73**: "I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked" (cf. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 73#Psalm 73 . 3|Psalm 73:3-17]])
- **Job**: The righteous man suffers, while the wicked seem to escape judgment
- **Ecclesiastes**: "There are righteous people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked, and there are wicked people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous" ([[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 8#Ecclesiastes 8 . 14|Ecclesiastes 8:14]])

The resolution comes only in **final judgment**. God will set all things right. Abel's blood cries out, and God hears. Cain's sin will be punished. Justice is delayed but not denied.

### 4. The Two Seeds Are Now in Open Conflict

[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] prophesied enmity between the two seeds. Genesis 4 enacts it. The righteous line (Abel, later Seth) and the wicked line (Cain) are now at war. This conflict will continue throughout history until Christ crushes the serpent's head.

The church lives in this tension. We are the seed of the woman, hated by the world (the seed of the serpent). "Do not be surprised, brothers, that the world hates you. We know that we have passed out of death into life, because we love the brothers. Whoever does not love abides in death. Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 13|1 John 3:13-15]]).

Abel's death is the first, but not the last. The righteous will suffer until Christ returns.

---

## Summary: The Intrusion Fails—Sin Triumphs

The **Sin** phase of this intrusion marks the failure of the attempt to restore humanity through the Adamic commission. Cain and Abel were given the opportunity to succeed where Adam failed. Instead:

- **Cain's offering is rejected**—he approaches God in pride, not faith
- **Cain becomes angry**—he refuses to repent or examine his heart
- **God warns Cain**—he is given a chance, but he rejects it
- **Cain murders Abel**—the seed of the serpent strikes the seed of the woman
- **Cain lies to God**—he compounds sin with deception and defiance

The pattern of **sin → exile → deeper curse** continues. This intrusion does not break the cycle. It reinforces it. The need for a **sinless Adam**, a **perfect sacrifice**, a **conquering King** becomes more urgent.

Only Jesus Christ will finally succeed where Cain (and all subsequent Adam figures) failed. Christ will resist temptation, offer the perfect sacrifice, conquer sin and death, and restore humanity to God's presence.
