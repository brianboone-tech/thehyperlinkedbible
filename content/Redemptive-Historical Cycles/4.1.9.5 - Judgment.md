# 4.1.9.5 - Judgment

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.9 - Intrusion - Wilderness Wanderings (Spying to Jordan)|← Back to 4.1.9]]

## Overview: Deaths, Plagues, and the Bronze Serpent

The Judgment phase of the 38-year wilderness wandering reveals God's consistent pattern: **sin brings death**, yet even in judgment God provides **mercy** and **hope**. The major judgments include Korah's company swallowed by the earth, 14,700 dying in plague, Moses and Aaron forbidden to enter the land, many dying from fiery serpents (with healing through the bronze serpent), and 24,000 dying at Baal Peor.

Yet alongside these judgments come demonstrations of grace: the bronze serpent provides healing for those who look in faith, Phinehas' zeal stops the plague, military victories over Sihon and Og demonstrate God's favor on the new generation, and ultimately the second generation stands poised to enter the land their parents refused.

G.K. Beale notes that these judgments function as **intrusions of final judgment** into the Common Grace period—previews of the Day of the LORD when all unbelief and rebellion will be judged definitively. Yet they remain **partial** and **remedial**, intended to warn and purify rather than utterly destroy.

---

## 1. Korah's Company: Swallowed by the Earth

The judgment on **Korah's rebellion** is immediate and catastrophic:

"And as soon as he had finished speaking all these words, the ground under them split apart. And the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up, with their households and all the people who belonged to Korah and all their goods. So they and all that belonged to them went down alive into Sheol, and the earth closed over them, and they perished from the midst of the assembly" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 31|Numbers 16:31-33]]).

### De-Creation Judgment

The earth "opening its mouth" is **de-creation** language—the ordered creation returns to chaos. This echoes the flood ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 11|Genesis 7:11]]) where the "fountains of the great deep burst forth." God unmakes what He made to judge rebellion.

Jude uses this as a warning: "Woe to them! For they walked in the way of Cain and abandoned themselves for the sake of gain to Balaam's error and perished in Korah's rebellion" ([[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 11|Jude 1:11]]).

### Fire Consumes the 250 Incense Offerers

"And fire came out from the LORD and consumed the 250 men offering the incense" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 35|Numbers 16:35]]).

This parallels Nadab and Abihu's judgment ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 10#Leviticus 10 . 2|Leviticus 10:2]])—unauthorized worship brings consuming fire. God's holiness cannot be approached presumptuously.

### The Bronze Censers: Permanent Memorial

God commands the bronze censers to be hammered into a covering for the altar "to be a reminder to the people of Israel, so that no outsider, who is not of the descendants of Aaron, should draw near to burn incense before the LORD, lest he become like Korah and his company" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 40|Numbers 16:40]]).

Physical memorials throughout Scripture serve to **teach future generations** the consequences of sin.

---

## 2. The Congregation Grumbles: 14,700 Die in Plague

The day after Korah's judgment, the congregation incredibly **grumbles again**:

"But on the next day all the congregation of the people of Israel grumbled against Moses and against Aaron, saying, 'You have killed the people of the LORD'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 41|Numbers 16:41]]).

They accuse Moses and Aaron of murder, refusing to acknowledge that **God executed judgment**. This demonstrates the hardness of the human heart—even miraculous judgment does not automatically produce repentance.

### Plague Breaks Out

"And behold, the plague had already begun among the people. And he put on the incense and made atonement for the people. And he stood between the dead and the living, and the plague was stopped. Now those who died in the plague were 14,700, besides those who died in the affair of Korah" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 47|Numbers 16:47-49]]).

### Aaron's Intercession

Aaron **stands between the dead and the living**, making atonement with incense. This is priestly mediation in its most vivid form—the mediator literally stands in the gap, stopping death from advancing.

This prefigures Christ, who "always lives to make intercession" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]) and who stands between sinners and God's wrath.

---

## 3. Moses and Aaron Forbidden to Enter the Land

After Moses strikes the rock twice at Meribah, God pronounces judgment:

"And the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, 'Because you did not believe in me, to uphold me as holy in the eyes of the people of Israel, therefore you shall not bring this assembly into the land that I have given them'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 12|Numbers 20:12]]).

### The Severity of the Sentence

This seems **disproportionately harsh**—Moses has faithfully led Israel for forty years, interceded countless times, and endured constant rebellion. Yet for one moment of anger and disobedience, he is excluded from the land.

The severity demonstrates:
- **Greater responsibility brings greater accountability** ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 12#Luke 12 . 48|Luke 12:48]])
- **God's holiness** cannot be treated lightly, even by His closest servants
- **The law** cannot bring us into final rest (Moses represents the law; Joshua represents grace)

### Miriam and Aaron Die

Both of Moses' siblings die before entering the land:

**Miriam** dies at Kadesh ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 1|Numbers 20:1]]) with no recorded mourning period (unlike Aaron).

**Aaron** dies on Mount Hor after transferring his priestly garments to Eleazar ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 26|Numbers 20:26-28]]). "And when all the congregation saw that Aaron had perished, all the house of Israel wept for Aaron thirty days" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 20#Numbers 20 . 29|Numbers 20:29]]).

These deaths mark **generational transition**—the Exodus generation leadership passes away; new leadership emerges.

---

## 4. Fiery Serpents and the Bronze Serpent

When the people complain about manna, God sends judgment:

"Then the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, so that many people of Israel died" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 6|Numbers 21:6]]).

### The Serpent as Curse

The **serpent** recalls Eden's fall ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 1|Genesis 3:1-15]]). Sin brings the serpent's bite (death). This is poetic justice—they despised God's provision; death comes through serpents (agents of the curse).

### Repentance and God's Remedy

"And the people came to Moses and said, 'We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD and against you. Pray to the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.' So Moses prayed for the people" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 7|Numbers 21:7]]).

God provides a remedy: "And the LORD said to Moses, 'Make a fiery serpent and set it on a pole, and everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live.' So Moses made a bronze serpent and set it on a pole. And if a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 8|Numbers 21:8-9]]).

### The Bronze Serpent as Type of Christ

Jesus explicitly connects this to His crucifixion:

"And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14-16]]).

The typology is precise:
- **Bronze serpent** = Christ bearing the curse (serpent = curse; bronze = judgment by fire)
- **Lifted up on a pole** = Crucifixion
- **Look and live** = Faith brings salvation
- **Physical healing** = Type of eternal life

This is one of Scripture's clearest gospel pictures: **look to Christ crucified in faith and live**.

### Later Corruption

Ironically, the bronze serpent later becomes an **idol** that King Hezekiah must destroy: "He removed the high places and broke the pillars and cut down the Asherah. And he broke in pieces the bronze serpent that Moses had made, for until those days the people of Israel had made offerings to it (it was called Nehushtan)" ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 4|2 Kings 18:4]]).

This warns against **venerating the means** rather than trusting the God who provides them.

## 5. The Plague at Baal Peor: 24,000 Die

The climactic judgment of the wilderness period occurs at Baal Peor:

"And those who died by the plague were twenty-four thousand" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 25#Numbers 25 . 9|Numbers 25:9]]).

This is the **largest single-day judgment** besides the original 40-year sentence. It demonstrates the severity of **sexual immorality** and **idolatry**.

### Phinehas' Zeal Stops the Plague

"Phinehas the son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, saw it, he rose and left the congregation and took a spear in his hand and went after the man of Israel into the chamber and pierced both of them, the man of Israel and the woman through her belly. Thus the plague on the people of Israel was stopped" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 25#Numbers 25 . 7|Numbers 25:7-8]]).

### God's Commendation

"Phinehas the son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the priest, has turned back my wrath from the people of Israel, in that he was jealous with my jealousy among them, so that I did not consume the people of Israel in my jealousy. Therefore say, 'Behold, I give to him my covenant of peace, and it shall be to him and to his descendants after him the covenant of a perpetual priesthood, because he was jealous for his God and made atonement for the people of Israel'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 25#Numbers 25 . 11|Numbers 25:11-13]]).

**Zeal for God's holiness** is rewarded with a **perpetual covenant**. Phinehas prefigures Christ's zeal for the Father's house ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 17|John 2:17]]).

---

## 6. Military Victories: God's Favor on the New Generation

As the 40 years conclude, Israel experiences **military success**, demonstrating God's favor on the second generation:

### Defeat of Sihon, King of the Amorites

"Then Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, saying, 'Let me pass through your land...' But Sihon would not allow Israel to pass through his territory. He gathered all his people together and went out against Israel to the wilderness and came to Jahaz and fought against Israel. And Israel defeated him with the edge of the sword and took possession of his land" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 21|Numbers 21:21-24]]).

### Defeat of Og, King of Bashan

"So they defeated him and his sons and all his people, until he had no survivor left. And they possessed his land" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 35|Numbers 21:35]]).

### Theological Significance

These victories demonstrate:
- **God's presence** with the new generation
- **Obedience** brings blessing (unlike presumptuous invasion in [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 39|Numbers 14:39-45]])
- **Preparation** for conquest of Canaan
- **Fulfillment** of promises made to Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 18|Genesis 15:18-21]])

Deuteronomy recounts these victories as **encouragement**: "You have seen all that the LORD your God has done to these two kings. So will the LORD do to all the kingdoms into which you are crossing" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 3#Deuteronomy 3 . 21|Deuteronomy 3:21]]).

---

## 7. Moses Views the Land and Dies

The final judgment on Moses is exclusion from the land, yet God grants him a **gracious preview**:

"Go up to the top of Pisgah and lift up your eyes westward and northward and southward and eastward, and look at it with your eyes, for you shall not go over this Jordan" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 3#Deuteronomy 3 . 27|Deuteronomy 3:27]]).

### Moses' Death on Mount Nebo

"So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the LORD, and he buried him in the valley in the land of Moab opposite Beth-peor; but no one knows the place of his burial to this day" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 5|Deuteronomy 34:5-6]]).

**God Himself buries Moses**—a remarkable honor. The hidden burial prevents idolatry (no shrine to Moses).

"Moses was 120 years old when he died. His eye was not dim, nor his vigor abated" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 7|Deuteronomy 34:7]]). Moses dies in full strength, not from natural decay.

### Israel Mourns

"And the people of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days. Then the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 8|Deuteronomy 34:8]]).

### Moses' Unique Status

"And there has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face, none like him for all the signs and the wonders that the LORD sent him to do in the land of Egypt, to Pharaoh and to all his servants and to all his land, and for all the mighty power and all the great deeds of terror that Moses did in the sight of all Israel" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 10|Deuteronomy 34:10-12]]).

Yet this statement anticipates a **greater prophet**: "The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you, from your brothers—it is to him you shall listen" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 18#Deuteronomy 18 . 15|Deuteronomy 18:15]])—fulfilled in Christ ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 3#Acts 3 . 22|Acts 3:22-23]]).

## 8. The Second Census: Fulfillment of God's Word

God commands a new census as the 40 years conclude:

"These are those who were listed by Moses and Eleazar the priest, who listed the people of Israel in the plains of Moab by the Jordan at Jericho. But among these there was not one of those listed by Moses and Aaron the priest, who had listed the people of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. For the LORD had said of them, 'They shall die in the wilderness.' Not one of them was left, except Caleb the son of Jephunneh and Joshua the son of Nun" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 63|Numbers 26:63-65]]).

### God's Word Precisely Fulfilled

**Not one** of the condemned generation survived (except Joshua and Caleb). This demonstrates:
- **God's word never fails** ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 14|Joshua 23:14]])
- **Judgment is certain** when pronounced
- **New generation** stands ready to enter

The total number is similar to the first census ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 1#Numbers 1 . 46|Numbers 1:46]]: 603,550 vs. [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 26#Numbers 26 . 51|Numbers 26:51]]: 601,730), demonstrating God's faithfulness to multiply His people despite forty years of judgment.

## 9. Christological Fulfillment: Christ Bears the Ultimate Judgment

The judgments of the wilderness wandering point to Christ:

### Christ Bears the Curse

Where Israel died under God's curse in the wilderness, Christ becomes **cursed for us**:

"Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us—for it is written, 'Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree'" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]).

The bronze serpent typology makes this explicit: Christ becomes **sin** for us ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]), bearing the serpent's curse, so that by looking to Him in faith we receive life.

### Christ as the True Mediator

Where Aaron stood between the dead and living, Christ **permanently** mediates:

"Therefore he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, since a death has occurred that redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 15|Hebrews 9:15]]).

### Christ Enters Where Moses Could Not

Moses dies outside the land, demonstrating the **limitation of the law**. Christ, the true prophet like Moses, enters the **true promised land** (heaven itself) and brings many sons to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]).

---

## Conclusion: Judgment and Mercy Intertwined

The Judgment phase of the wilderness wandering demonstrates that God's justice is **precise**, His word is **certain**, and His holiness **cannot be trifled with**. Korah's rebellion, the plagues, the deaths of Moses' siblings, the fiery serpents, and Baal Peor all reveal that sin brings death.

Yet even in judgment, God provides **mercy**:
- Aaron stands in the gap, stopping the plague
- The bronze serpent heals those who look in faith
- Phinehas' zeal turns away wrath
- Military victories demonstrate favor on the new generation
- The second generation is prepared and ready to enter

These judgments are **intrusions of final judgment**—previews of the Day of the LORD—yet they remain partial and remedial, intended to warn and purify rather than utterly destroy. They demonstrate that while God's people may experience temporal judgment, His covenant purposes **never fail**.

Ultimately, all these judgments point to **Christ**, who bears the ultimate curse, stands as the perfect mediator between God and man, and leads His people into eternal rest. The bronze serpent becomes the gospel in miniature: look to the lifted-up Christ in faith and live. Those who look receive eternal life; those who refuse perish in the wilderness of this world.

The warning remains: "Therefore, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 1|Hebrews 4:1]]). Today is the day of salvation. Look to Christ crucified and enter the rest God has prepared.
