# 4.1.12.2 - Creation: The Judges Period and Ruth - Divine Intrusions of Grace and Deliverance

## Introduction: God Raises Deliverers

Despite Israel's repeated apostasy, covenant rebellion, and moral collapse during the Judges period, the narrative reveals a stunning theological reality: **Yahweh does not abandon His people**. Instead, He repeatedly intervenes in grace, raising up judges who deliver Israel from their oppressors. These deliverances are not earned—they are pure grace. Israel cries out in distress, and God responds with mercy.

The creation theme in Judges is expressed through:
1. **God raising judges**: Divine appointment of deliverers
2. **The Spirit empowering judges**: The Spirit of the LORD comes upon them
3. **Military victories**: God gives enemies into Israel's hand
4. **Periods of rest**: Temporary Sabbath-rest in the land
5. **Boaz's kinsman-redemption**: New creation through marriage and offspring

G.K. Beale's framework helps us see these deliverances as temporary restorations of the Adamic commission. Each judge is raised to function as a corporate Adam figure, subduing enemies and establishing order. However, their temporary success only highlights the need for the ultimate Adam who will permanently establish creation order.

Meredith Kline's intrusion eschatology explains the pattern: the judges are eschatological intrusions—foretastes of the coming age breaking into the present evil age. They provide temporary Sabbath-rest but cannot establish the permanent Sabbath. They point forward to the ultimate Deliverer who will bring the final rest.

The book of Ruth climaxes with Boaz's act of kinsman-redemption, which results in new life (the birth of Obed) and a renewed family line that leads to King David and ultimately to Jesus Christ. This is creation-redemption at its most personal and profound level.

## The Pattern: God Raises Judges

The theological thesis of the book is stated in [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 16|Judges 2:16-18]]:

> **"Then the LORD raised up judges, who saved them out of the hand of those who plundered them... Whenever the LORD raised up judges for them, the LORD was with the judge, and he saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge. For the LORD was moved to pity by their groaning because of those who afflicted and oppressed them."**

This passage establishes several critical truths:

**1. Divine Initiative**: The judges are not self-appointed or democratically elected—they are **raised by the LORD**. This is the language of divine creation and sovereignty. Just as God raised up Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 10|Exodus 3:10]]) and Joshua ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 31#Deuteronomy 31 . 23|Deuteronomy 31:23]]), He now raises judges to deliver His people.

**2. Salvation, Not Mere Military Victory**: The judges "saved" Israel—the language of redemption. This is not merely political or military deliverance but covenantal salvation from the consequences of sin.

**3. Divine Presence**: **"The LORD was with the judge"**—echoing God's presence with Joseph ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 39#Genesis 39 . 2|Genesis 39:2]]), Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 12|Exodus 3:12]]), and Joshua ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 5|Joshua 1:5]]). The judge does not succeed by his own strength but by Yahweh's presence and power.

**4. Divine Compassion**: God is **"moved to pity by their groaning."** This is covenant faithfulness rooted in divine compassion. Despite Israel's unfaithfulness, Yahweh remembers His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 2#Exodus 2 . 24|Exodus 2:24]]).

Beale observes that this pattern of God raising deliverers is a mini-Exodus repeated throughout Judges. Just as Yahweh heard Israel's groaning in Egypt and raised Moses to deliver them, so He hears their groaning under each oppressor and raises a judge to save them. This demonstrates that Yahweh's redemptive character is unchanging—He is the God who saves.

Kline's intrusion theology explains why these deliverances are temporary: they are eschatological intrusions that point forward to the final deliverance. Each judge is a type of the coming Messiah, but none of them is the reality. They provide foretastes of the Sabbath-rest, but only the ultimate Judge-King can establish the eternal rest.

## Othniel: The Model Judge and the Spirit's Empowerment

The first judge mentioned is **Othniel**, and his account serves as the paradigmatic example of the cyclical pattern:

> **"The people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD. They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asheroth. Therefore the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia. And the people of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years. But when the people of Israel cried out to the LORD, the LORD raised up a deliverer for the people of Israel, who saved them, Othniel the son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother. The Spirit of the LORD was upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand. And his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim. So the land had rest forty years. Then Othniel the son of Kenaz died."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 7|Judges 3:7-11]])

This passage establishes the full cycle and contains several key creation themes:

### 1. The Spirit of the LORD

**"The Spirit of the LORD was upon him"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 10|Judges 3:10]])—this is the first explicit mention of the Spirit empowering a judge. The Spirit's coming upon Othniel echoes:
- The Spirit hovering over the waters at creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]])
- The Spirit filling Bezalel for tabernacle construction ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 31#Exodus 31 . 3|Exodus 31:3]])
- The Spirit resting on the seventy elders ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 25|Numbers 11:25]])

Beale notes that the Spirit's empowerment signifies that Othniel is functioning as God's image-bearer and vice-regent, subduing chaos (represented by the foreign oppressor) and establishing order (rest in the land). This is an Adamic commission renewed.

The Spirit-empowerment also anticipates the coming of the Spirit-anointed Messiah ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 2|Isaiah 11:2]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 61#Isaiah 61 . 1|Isaiah 61:1]]), who will permanently judge in righteousness and establish the kingdom.

### 2. The LORD Gave the Enemy into His Hand

The phrase **"the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Mesopotamia into his hand"** emphasizes divine sovereignty in the victory. This is not Othniel's achievement—it is Yahweh's gift. The same language is used throughout Joshua (e.g., [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 2|Joshua 6:2]]; [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 1|Joshua 8:1]]).

Kline observes that this divine gift of victory is an intrusion of the eschatological Sabbath-warfare into the present age. The victory prefigures the final victory when all enemies will be put under Christ's feet ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 25|1 Corinthians 15:25]]).

### 3. The Land Had Rest

**"So the land had rest forty years"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 11|Judges 3:11]])—the language of Sabbath-rest. The land, which had been under the curse of oppression, now enters a period of rest. This echoes the original Sabbath-rest of creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 2|Genesis 2:2-3]]) and anticipates the ultimate rest promised in [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9]].

The number forty is significant—it is a generation. During Othniel's lifetime, the generation experiences the blessing of rest. However, **"Then Othniel the son of Kenaz died"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 11|Judges 3:11]]), and the cycle begins again. This mortality highlights the need for a Judge who will never die or who will die and rise again to reign forever.

## Ehud: Creation Through Unlikely Means

The second judge is **Ehud**, a left-handed Benjaminite who delivers Israel from Eglon, the king of Moab:

> **"But when the people of Israel cried out to the LORD, the LORD raised up for them a deliverer, Ehud, the son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 15|Judges 3:15]])

Ehud's story contains several creation themes:

### 1. God Raises an Unlikely Deliverer

Ehud is described as **"a left-handed man"**—in Hebrew, literally "restricted in his right hand." In ancient Near Eastern culture, left-handedness was often viewed as a disability or disadvantage. Yet God chooses Ehud precisely because of what others would consider a weakness. This echoes God's pattern throughout Scripture of using the weak to shame the strong ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 27|1 Corinthians 1:27]]).

Ehud uses his left-handedness to conceal a sword on his right thigh (where guards would not expect it) and assassinates Eglon ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 21|Judges 3:21-22]]). This is God creating deliverance through unexpected means.

### 2. The LORD Gave Moab into Their Hand

After Eglon's death, Ehud rallies Israel and defeats the Moabites: **"And the LORD gave them Moab into their hand"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 28|Judges 3:28]]). Once again, the emphasis is on divine agency. The victory is the LORD's gift.

### 3. The Land Had Rest Eighty Years

**"So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel. And the land had rest for eighty years"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 30|Judges 3:30]])—the longest period of rest recorded in Judges. This extended Sabbath-rest demonstrates God's abundant grace and the effectiveness of Ehud's leadership.

Beale notes that the language of subduing Moab echoes the Adamic commission to subdue the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]). Ehud, empowered by God, functions as a corporate Adam subduing the enemy and establishing rest.

## Deborah: Creation Through Prophetic Leadership

The third judge is **Deborah**, a prophetess who leads Israel during the oppression of Jabin, king of Canaan:

> **"Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time. She used to sit under the palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the people of Israel came up to her for judgment."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 4|Judges 4:4-5]])

Deborah's leadership is unique in several ways:

### 1. Female Leadership as Divine Provision

Deborah is the only female judge in the book. Her prominence reveals both God's willingness to use anyone to accomplish His purposes and the failure of male leadership in Israel. Barak, the military commander, refuses to go to battle unless Deborah accompanies him ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 8|Judges 4:8]]), revealing a crisis of male courage and leadership.

Deborah's leadership is a creation-act: God raises her to fill the vacuum left by faithless men. She functions as prophet, judge, and military strategist—a multi-faceted deliverer.

### 2. The LORD Gave Sisera into Your Hand

Deborah prophesies to Barak: **"Has not the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded you, 'Go, gather your men at Mount Tabor... And I will draw out Sisera, the general of Jabin's army, to meet you by the river Kishon with his chariots and his troops, and I will give him into your hand'?"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 6|Judges 4:6-7]])

The phrase **"I will give him into your hand"** is divine promise. The LORD is the one who will deliver Sisera. When the battle comes, **"the LORD routed Sisera and all his chariots and all his army"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 15|Judges 4:15]])—divine intervention brings victory.

### 3. Jael: Creation Through Unlikely Heroism

The climax of the story involves Jael, a Kenite woman, who kills Sisera by driving a tent peg through his temple while he sleeps ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 21|Judges 4:21]]). Deborah had prophesied that **"the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 9|Judges 4:9]]), and this is fulfilled through Jael.

This is a striking reversal of Genesis 3. In the garden, the woman (Eve) listened to the serpent and brought death to humanity. Here, a woman (Jael) crushes the head of the enemy and brings deliverance to Israel. This anticipates the ultimate fulfillment of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]], where the seed of the woman will crush the serpent's head.

### 4. Deborah's Song of Victory

Deborah and Barak celebrate the victory with a song ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 1|Judges 5:1-31]]), which is one of the oldest poetic texts in the Hebrew Bible. The song praises Yahweh as the Divine Warrior:

> **"LORD, when you went out from Seir, when you marched from the region of Edom, the earth trembled and the heavens dropped, yes, the clouds dropped water. The mountains quaked before the LORD, even Sinai before the LORD, the God of Israel."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 4|Judges 5:4-5]])

This is theophanic language—God Himself fights for Israel. The victory is not achieved by human strength but by divine intervention. The song concludes: **"So may all your enemies perish, O LORD! But your friends be like the sun as he rises in his might."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 31|Judges 5:31]])

The result: **"And the land had rest for forty years"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 31|Judges 5:31]])—another generation of Sabbath-rest.

## Gideon: Creation Through Divine Reduction

The fourth major judge is **Gideon**, raised to deliver Israel from the oppression of Midian:

> **"The people of Israel did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD gave them into the hand of Midian seven years... And the people of Israel cried out for help to the LORD."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 1|Judges 6:1, 6]])

Gideon's story is marked by God's dramatic reduction of Israel's army to ensure that the glory for the victory goes to God alone.

### 1. The Spirit of the LORD Clothed Gideon

After Gideon tears down his father's altar to Baal ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 25|Judges 6:25-32]]), the text says: **"But the Spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 34|Judges 6:34]], ESV margin). This is striking language—the Spirit envelops Gideon, empowering him for the task. This echoes:
- God clothing Adam and Eve after the fall ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 21|Genesis 3:21]])—covering their shame
- The priests being clothed for service in the tabernacle ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 2|Exodus 28:2]])—consecration for holy work
- Believers being clothed with Christ ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 27|Galatians 3:27]])—new creation identity

The Spirit's clothing of Gideon signifies that he is being equipped for holy warfare as God's representative.

### 2. God Reduces the Army: "Lest Israel Boast"

Gideon gathers 32,000 men to fight the Midianites. But God says: **"The people with you are too many for me to give the Midianites into their hand, lest Israel say, 'My own hand has saved me.'"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 2|Judges 7:2]])

God reduces the army twice:
- First reduction: **22,000 leave** (those who are fearful)
- Second reduction: By the water test, God selects only **300 men** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 7|Judges 7:7]])

The final ratio is absurd: **300 Israelites vs. 135,000 Midianites** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 10|Judges 8:10]])—a ratio of 1 to 450. This ensures that the victory will be unmistakably divine.

Beale observes that this is a new creation act. Just as God created the universe *ex nihilo* (out of nothing), He creates military victory out of impossibility. The 300 are not self-sufficient warriors—they are instruments through whom God demonstrates His power.

Kline's intrusion theology sees this as an eschatological foretaste: the final victory over evil will not be won by human armies but by divine intervention. The 300 prefigure the ultimate Warrior-King who will tread the winepress of God's wrath alone ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 3|Isaiah 63:3]]).

### 3. The Victory: Trumpets, Torches, and Confusion

Gideon's strategy is unconventional: **300 men surround the Midianite camp with trumpets, empty jars, and torches**. At Gideon's signal, they blow the trumpets, break the jars, and shout, **"A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 20|Judges 7:20]])

The result: **"When they blew the 300 trumpets, the LORD set every man's sword against his comrade and against all the army"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 22|Judges 7:22]]). The Midianites turn on each other in confusion and flee. Israel pursues and achieves total victory.

This is divine creation: God brings order (Israel's deliverance) out of chaos (Midianite confusion). The victory is the LORD's doing.

### 4. The Land Had Rest Forty Years

**"So Midian was subdued before the people of Israel, and they raised their heads no more. And the land had rest forty years in the days of Gideon."** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 28|Judges 8:28]])

Another generation of Sabbath-rest is established. However, Gideon's legacy is marred by his creation of a golden ephod ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]]), which becomes a snare. This foreshadows that even the best human judges cannot establish permanent righteousness.

## Jephthah: Creation Through a Rejected Outcast

The fifth major judge is **Jephthah**, described as **"a mighty warrior, but he was the son of a prostitute"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 1|Judges 11:1]]). He is driven from his family and becomes the leader of a band of "worthless fellows" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 3|Judges 11:3]]).

### 1. The Elders of Gilead Call Jephthah

When the Ammonites oppress Israel, the elders of Gilead go to Jephthah and ask him to be their commander ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 6|Judges 11:6]]). This is ironic—the man they rejected is now their only hope.

This mirrors Israel's situation with God: they have rejected Yahweh by serving other gods ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 10#Judges 10 . 6|Judges 10:6]]), yet they cry out to Him for deliverance. And God, in grace, responds.

Beale notes that Jephthah is a type of the rejected stone that becomes the cornerstone ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]]). This anticipates Jesus, who was rejected by His own but became the Savior of the world ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 11|John 1:11]]).

### 2. The Spirit of the LORD Was Upon Jephthah

**"Then the Spirit of the LORD was upon Jephthah"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|Judges 11:29]])—the divine empowerment that marks true judges. Despite Jephthah's questionable background, the Spirit comes upon him to accomplish God's purposes.

This demonstrates the sovereign freedom of God to use anyone He chooses. Creation is not limited by human qualifications or social status.

### 3. Jephthah's Victory

Jephthah defeats the Ammonites in a great slaughter: **"So the Ammonites were subdued before the people of Israel"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 33|Judges 11:33]]). The language of subduing again echoes the Adamic commission ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]).

However, Jephthah's triumph is tragically marred by his rash vow and the apparent sacrifice of his daughter ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 30|Judges 11:30-40]]). This reveals the growing moral compromise of the judges—even in their victories, sin intrudes.

## Samson: Creation and Destruction Intertwined

The sixth and final major judge is **Samson**, whose story occupies more space than any other judge (Judges 13-16). Samson is uniquely consecrated from birth:

> **"And the angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and said to her, 'Behold, you are barren and have not borne children, but you shall conceive and bear a son. Therefore be careful and drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean, for behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. No razor shall come upon his head, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines.'"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 3|Judges 13:3-5]])

Samson's story is marked by extraordinary creation-power and tragic self-destruction.

### 1. The Spirit of the LORD Rushed Upon Samson

The Spirit's empowerment of Samson is described with unusual intensity:
- **"The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him"** when he tore a lion apart with his bare hands ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 6|Judges 14:6]])
- **"The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him"** when he struck down thirty men of Ashkelon ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 19|Judges 14:19]])
- **"The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him"** when he broke free from ropes and killed 1,000 Philistines with a donkey's jawbone ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 15#Judges 15 . 14|Judges 15:14-15]])

The Spirit's empowerment gives Samson superhuman strength—a creation-gift that prefigures the resurrection power that will be given to believers through the Spirit ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 19|Ephesians 1:19-20]]).

### 2. Samson's Victories

Despite his moral failures, Samson achieves significant victories against the Philistines:
- He kills 1,000 Philistines with a donkey's jawbone ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 15#Judges 15 . 15|Judges 15:15]])
- He carries away the gates of Gaza ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 3|Judges 16:3]])
- In his final act, he pulls down the temple of Dagon, killing more Philistines in his death than in his life ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 30|Judges 16:30]])

These victories are acts of divine creation—God uses Samson to deliver Israel despite Samson's personal failures. The text says: **"And he judged Israel twenty years"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 15#Judges 15 . 20|Judges 15:20]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 31|Judges 16:31]]).

### 3. Samson as Failed Adam

Beale observes that Samson is a tragic Adam figure. He is set apart for holiness (Nazirite vow) but repeatedly defiles himself:
- He touches a lion's carcass ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 8|Judges 14:8-9]])—violating his vow
- He pursues Philistine women ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 14#Judges 14 . 1|Judges 14:1]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 1|Judges 16:1, 4]])—violating the prohibition against intermarriage
- He reveals the secret of his strength to Delilah ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 17|Judges 16:17]])—forfeiting his consecration

When Samson's hair is cut, **"the LORD had left him"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 20|Judges 16:20]]). This echoes Adam's expulsion from Eden—the loss of divine presence due to covenant violation.

Yet even in his final moments, Samson prays: **"O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes"** ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 28|Judges 16:28]]). And God, in grace, grants his request. Samson's death is both judgment (he dies for his sin) and deliverance (he kills the Philistine leaders).

Kline's intrusion theology sees Samson as a flawed intrusion of the coming Deliverer. He "begins to save Israel" ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 5|Judges 13:5]]) but cannot complete the task. He points forward to the ultimate Deliverer who will lay down His life to destroy the enemies of God's people.

## Ruth: New Creation Through Kinsman-Redemption

Against the backdrop of the judges' mixed successes and failures, the book of Ruth tells the story of genuine creation-redemption at the personal and familial level.

### 1. Naomi's Emptiness Filled

Naomi returns to Bethlehem bitter and empty: **"I went away full, and the LORD has brought me back empty"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 21|Ruth 1:21]]). She has lost her husband and two sons. She is barren of male heirs.

But God provides Ruth, a Moabite daughter-in-law who refuses to abandon Naomi: **"Where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 16|Ruth 1:16-17]]).

Ruth's covenant loyalty is itself a creation act—she creates a new covenant bond with Naomi and with Yahweh. Beale notes that Ruth reverses the pattern of Israel's apostasy: while Israel abandons Yahweh for foreign gods, Ruth abandons foreign gods for Yahweh.

### 2. Boaz: The Kinsman-Redeemer

The climax of the story is Boaz's role as kinsman-redeemer. When Ruth gleans in Boaz's field, he shows her extraordinary favor ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 8|Ruth 2:8-16]]). Naomi recognizes that Boaz is a close relative who could redeem Elimelech's property ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 20|Ruth 2:20]]).

Ruth, following Naomi's instructions, goes to the threshing floor and asks Boaz to spread his wings over her—a request for marriage and redemption ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 9|Ruth 3:9]]). Boaz responds with covenant faithfulness:

> **"May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have made this last kindness greater than the first in that you have not gone after young men, whether poor or rich. And now, my daughter, do not fear. I will do for you all that you ask, for all my fellow townsmen know that you are a worthy woman."** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 10|Ruth 3:10-11]])

Boaz redeems Ruth and Elimelech's property: **"So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. And he went in to her, and the LORD gave her conception, and she bore a son"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 13|Ruth 4:13]]).

### 3. New Life: Obed, Jesse, David

The birth of Obed is a creation-act: **"the LORD gave her conception"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 13|Ruth 4:13]]). This child, born to a Moabite woman through kinsman-redemption, becomes the grandfather of King David ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 17|Ruth 4:17-22]]).

The women of Bethlehem celebrate: **"Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you this day without a redeemer, and may his name be renowned in Israel! He shall be to you a restorer of life and a nourisher of your old age, for your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is more to you than seven sons, has given birth to him"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 14|Ruth 4:14-15]]).

Naomi, who was empty, is now full. The family line, which was cut off, is now restored. The covenant promises continue.

### 4. Typology of Christ the Redeemer

Boaz's kinsman-redemption is one of the clearest Old Testament types of Christ:

**Boaz as Type**:
- Near kinsman (related by blood) → Jesus takes on human flesh ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]])
- Willing to redeem → Jesus lays down His life willingly ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 18|John 10:18]])
- Able to pay the price → Jesus' blood is sufficient ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]])
- Redeems both land and person → Jesus redeems creation and humanity ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 21|Romans 8:21-23]])
- Raises up seed for the dead → Jesus brings resurrection life ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 20|1 Corinthians 15:20-22]])
- Marriage union → Jesus the Bridegroom and the Church ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 25|Ephesians 5:25-27]])

Beale notes that Ruth's inclusion in the Messianic line anticipates the inclusion of Gentiles in the people of God. The book ends with the genealogy: **"Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David"** ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 22|Ruth 4:22]]). This same genealogy appears in [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5-6]], explicitly including Ruth the Moabitess in the line leading to Jesus Christ.

## Theological Synthesis: Temporary Intrusions Pointing to Permanent Creation

The creation theme in the Judges period is characterized by divine intrusions of grace into a collapsing covenant order. God repeatedly raises judges, empowers them by His Spirit, gives enemies into their hand, and establishes temporary periods of rest.

### Beale's Corporate Adam Typology

The judges function as corporate Adams, subduing enemies and establishing order. However, their temporary success and personal failures demonstrate the need for the Last Adam:
- **Othniel**: Model judge, but he dies
- **Ehud**: Effective deliverer, but he dies
- **Deborah**: Prophetic judge, but she dies
- **Gideon**: Miraculous victory, but ends in idolatry and dies
- **Jephthah**: Rejects turned deliverer, but makes rash vow and dies
- **Samson**: Strongest judge, but morally weakest and dies tragically

Each judge demonstrates some aspect of the Adamic commission—subduing enemies, establishing rest—but none can permanently fulfill it. Only Jesus, the Last Adam, succeeds where all others failed ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45-49]]).

### Kline's Intrusion Eschatology

The judges are eschatological intrusions—temporary foretastes of the coming age breaking into the present evil age:
- They provide **Sabbath-rest** (40 years, 80 years) but not eternal rest
- They subdue **enemies** but do not eliminate evil
- They are empowered by the **Spirit** but are mortal

These intrusions point forward to the ultimate Judge who will:
- Establish **eternal Sabbath-rest** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9]])
- Definitively defeat **all enemies** ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 25|1 Corinthians 15:25-26]])
- Pour out the **Spirit without measure** on all flesh ([[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 2#Joel 2 . 28|Joel 2:28]]; [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 17|Acts 2:17]])

### Ruth: Paradigm of Redemptive Creation

Ruth's story provides a paradigm of how God creates new life out of death:
- **Emptiness → Fullness**: Naomi's bitterness transformed to joy
- **Barrenness → Fruitfulness**: Ruth bears a son
- **Death → Life**: Dead husband's name raised up
- **Exclusion → Inclusion**: Moabite woman grafted into Israel
- **Curse → Blessing**: Exile to Moab reversed to inheritance in Bethlehem

This paradigm anticipates the gospel: God creates new life out of death, includes outsiders, and raises up seed through the ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer.

## Christological Fulfillment: Jesus, the Ultimate Creator-Deliverer

The creation theme in Judges points forward to Jesus Christ in multiple ways:

### 1. Jesus as the Spirit-Anointed Judge

The judges were temporarily empowered by the Spirit. Jesus is permanently anointed by the Spirit:
- At His baptism: **"The Spirit of the Lord is upon me"** ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18]])
- In His ministry: **"God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power"** ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]])
- In His resurrection: **"Raised from the dead by the glory of the Father"** ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 4|Romans 6:4]])

Jesus does not lose the Spirit (as Samson did) because He perfectly obeys the Father.

### 2. Jesus as the Victorious Deliverer

The judges won temporary victories. Jesus wins the ultimate victory:
- Over sin: **"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree"** ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]])
- Over death: **"Death is swallowed up in victory"** ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 54|1 Corinthians 15:54]])
- Over Satan: **"That through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death"** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]])

### 3. Jesus as the Eternal Sabbath-Bringer

The judges provided temporary rest. Jesus provides eternal rest:
- **"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest"** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28]])
- **"There remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God"** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9]])

Jesus is the Lord of the Sabbath ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 8|Matthew 12:8]]), the one who fulfills all Sabbath typology.

### 4. Jesus as the Ultimate Kinsman-Redeemer

Boaz redeemed Ruth and raised up seed. Jesus redeems His people and raises us to new life:
- **Near kinsman**: Jesus shares our humanity ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]])
- **Willing redeemer**: Jesus lays down His life for the sheep ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 15|John 10:15]])
- **Able redeemer**: His blood is sufficient for all ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 2#1 John 2 . 2|1 John 2:2]])
- **Bridegroom**: Jesus marries His Church ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 7|Revelation 19:7-9]])
- **Life-giver**: Jesus raises the dead ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 25|John 11:25-26]])

---

## Conclusion: Grace Creating Deliverance Despite Failure

The creation theme in the Judges period demonstrates that God's grace is greater than human failure. Despite Israel's repeated apostasy, God raises deliverers, empowers them by His Spirit, gives enemies into their hand, and establishes periods of rest. These are pure acts of grace—unearned, undeserved, and rooted solely in God's covenant faithfulness.

The judges provide temporary relief, but they cannot establish permanent order. They point forward to the ultimate Judge-King who will break the cycle of sin and death. Boaz redeems Ruth and raises up seed, anticipating the greater Kinsman-Redeemer who will redeem all creation and raise the dead to eternal life.

The creation acts in Judges are intrusions of the age to come into the present evil age. They are foretastes of the final deliverance, signs of the eternal Sabbath-rest, and glimpses of the resurrection life. They all point forward to Jesus Christ, who is the ultimate Creator-Deliverer, the eternal Sabbath-Bringer, and the perfect Kinsman-Redeemer.
