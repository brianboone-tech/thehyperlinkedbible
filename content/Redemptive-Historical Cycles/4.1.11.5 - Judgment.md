## The Dual Nature of Judgment in Joshua

The judgment phase in the conquest of Canaan operates on two distinct but related levels: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">judgment on the Canaanites for their wickedness, and judgment on Israel for their covenant violations</span>. Both forms of judgment reveal God's holiness, justice, and covenant faithfulness. Together they demonstrate that God shows no partiality—He judges wickedness wherever it appears, whether among pagan nations or His own covenant people.

From the perspective of G.K. Beale's <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">temple theology</span>, the judgments in Joshua represent the cleansing necessary before sacred space can be established. Just as the tabernacle could not be inhabited by God's presence until it was ritually purified, so the land of Canaan must be purged of defilement through judgment before it can become the dwelling place of God's covenant community. The judgments are not arbitrary acts of divine wrath but necessary acts of sanctification.

From Meredith Kline's <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">intrusion framework</span>, the judgments in Joshua represent eschatological judgment breaking into history. The conquest is holy war—a temporary intrusion of the final judgment that properly belongs to the last day. The Canaanites experience temporal-physical judgment that foreshadows the eternal-spiritual judgment awaiting all who persist in rebellion against God. Israel's judgment for covenant violation demonstrates that even during this intrusion of blessing, God's holiness remains absolute and His standards unyielding.

The judgment phase in Joshua thus serves multiple theological purposes: it vindicates God's righteousness, purges wickedness from the land, disciplines God's people for covenant violations, demonstrates the seriousness of sin, and points forward to the ultimate judgment when Christ returns to finally and comprehensively judge all evil.

## Judgment on Achan: Purging Corporate Guilt

The first judgment in the conquest narrative falls not on the Canaanites but on <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">an Israelite who violated the *herem*</span>. Achan son of Carmi took devoted things from Jericho—a beautiful cloak from Shinar, 200 shekels of silver, and a bar of gold weighing 50 shekels ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 21|Joshua 7:21]]). This violation brought God's wrath upon the entire nation, resulting in defeat at Ai and the death of thirty-six Israelite soldiers ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 5|Joshua 7:5]]).

The judgment process unfolds in stages. First, God reveals to Joshua that <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Israel has sinned; they have transgressed my covenant"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 11|Joshua 7:11]]). The corporate language is significant—though only one man sinned, the entire nation bears responsibility. This reflects the principle of <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">covenant solidarity</span>: Israel is not merely a collection of individuals but a unified body in covenant relationship with Yahweh. When one member sins, the whole body is defiled.

Second, God commands that the guilty party be identified through casting lots: <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"In the morning therefore you shall be brought near by your tribes. And the tribe that the LORD takes by lot shall come near by clans. And the clan that the LORD takes shall come near by households. And the household that the LORD takes shall come near man by man"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 14|Joshua 7:14]]). The lot-casting process narrows systematically from tribe to clan to household to individual, publicly demonstrating that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">God knows the hidden sin and will expose it</span>.

Third, when Achan is identified, he confesses: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"Truly I have sinned against the LORD God of Israel"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 20|Joshua 7:20]]). The confession acknowledges that his sin was not merely against Israel's social order but against Yahweh Himself. All sin is ultimately sin against God ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 4|Psalm 51:4]]).

Fourth, the stolen items are retrieved from Achan's tent and displayed before the LORD ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 23|Joshua 7:23]]). The physical evidence confirms Achan's guilt. The items that he coveted and hid are now exposed in the presence of God and the community—a dramatic reversal of his attempt at concealment.

Fifth, the judgment is executed: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"And Joshua and all Israel with him took Achan the son of Zerah, and the silver and the cloak and the bar of gold, and his sons and daughters and his oxen and donkeys and sheep and his tent and all that he had. And they brought them up to the Valley of Achor. And Joshua said, 'Why did you bring trouble on us? The LORD brings trouble on you today.' And all Israel stoned him with stones. They burned them with fire and stoned them with stones"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 24|Joshua 7:24-25]]).

The comprehensiveness of the judgment is striking. Not only Achan but also <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">his family, his livestock, and all his possessions</span> are destroyed. Several theological factors explain this severity:

First, this is an <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">intrusion of eschatological judgment</span>. In the final judgment, sin will be punished comprehensively. The temporal judgment on Achan's household pictures that ultimate reality. Those who persist in covenant rebellion will experience not merely personal destruction but the destruction of everything they valued and clung to.

Second, the judgment completes the *herem* that was violated. Achan took devoted things for himself; now he and his household become devoted things. The <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">contagion of sin is purged through complete destruction</span>. The same fate that should have befallen the plunder of Jericho now befalls Achan's household.

Third, the public nature of the judgment serves as a <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">deterrent for the entire community</span>. "All Israel" participates in the stoning ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 25|Joshua 7:25]]), ensuring that every member witnesses the consequences of covenant violation. The object lesson is unmistakable: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">hidden sin will be exposed, and covenant violation will be punished severely</span>.

Fourth, the judgment restores covenant relationship. After Achan's execution, "the LORD turned from his burning anger" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 7#Joshua 7 . 26|Joshua 7:26]]). The breach in covenant relationship caused by Achan's sin is healed through judgment. God's presence returns to Israel, and they are ready to resume the conquest. The purging of sin precedes the reception of blessing.

The place is named <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">the Valley of Achor</span>—"Achor" meaning "trouble." Joshua's words explain the wordplay: "Why did you bring trouble on us? The LORD brings trouble on you today." The name serves as a perpetual reminder that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">sin brings trouble, both to the sinner and to the community</span>. But prophetically, even this place of judgment will become "a door of hope" ([[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 2#Hosea 2 . 15|Hosea 2:15]]), anticipating the gospel transformation where judgment gives way to grace through Christ's substitutionary death.

## Judgment on Jericho: The Firstfruits Offering

The conquest of Jericho is more than a military victory; it is an act of <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">divine judgment executed through holy war</span>. Jericho is the first Canaanite city conquered, making it the firstfruits offering of the entire conquest. As firstfruits belong entirely to God, so Jericho is devoted entirely to destruction—no plunder taken, no survivors spared (except Rahab's household), the city burned as a whole burnt offering.

The judgment on Jericho unfolds through miraculous divine intervention rather than conventional military tactics. God commands Israel to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">march around the city once a day for six days, then seven times on the seventh day</span>, with priests carrying the ark and blowing trumpets ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 3|Joshua 6:3-5]]). This liturgical procession emphasizes that the conquest is fundamentally <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Yahweh's campaign, not Israel's</span>. The ark—representing God's throne—leads the procession, and the fall of the walls will be divine work, not human achievement.

On the seventh day, after the seventh circuit, <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"the wall fell down flat, so that the people went up into the city, every man straight before him, and they captured the city"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 20|Joshua 6:20]]). The collapse of the walls is miraculous, requiring no siege equipment or battering rams. God Himself breaches the defenses, allowing His people to enter and execute judgment.

The judgment is comprehensive: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Then they devoted all in the city to destruction, both men and women, young and old, oxen, sheep, and donkeys, with the edge of the sword"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 21|Joshua 6:21]]). Nothing is spared except Rahab and her family, who are preserved because of her faith and the oath sworn to her by the spies ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 25|Joshua 6:25]]).

After the human inhabitants are killed, <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">the city itself is burned</span>: "And they burned the city with fire, and everything in it. Only the silver and gold, and the vessels of bronze and of iron, they put into the treasury of the house of the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 24|Joshua 6:24]]). The burning of the city symbolizes complete destruction—Jericho ceases to exist as a functioning city. The only items preserved are precious metals dedicated to the tabernacle treasury, signifying that even the material wealth of the city belongs to God.

Joshua pronounces a curse on anyone who attempts to rebuild Jericho: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"Cursed before the LORD be the man who rises up and rebuilds this city, Jericho. At the cost of his firstborn shall he lay its foundation, and at the cost of his youngest son shall he set up its gates"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 26|Joshua 6:26]]). This curse is fulfilled centuries later when Hiel of Bethel rebuilds Jericho and loses his sons ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 16#1 Kings 16 . 34|1 Kings 16:34]]), demonstrating the enduring power of divinely pronounced judgment.

The judgment on Jericho serves multiple theological purposes. First, it demonstrates <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">God's sovereign power over fortified cities</span>. What was humanly impregnable is divinely vulnerable. The walls that symbolized Jericho's strength collapse at God's command, showing that no human defense can withstand divine judgment.

Second, the judgment establishes a pattern for the entire conquest. <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Jericho is the paradigm—subsequent conquests will follow this model of divine intervention and comprehensive destruction</span>. The firstfruits offering sets the standard for what is to follow.

Third, the judgment vindicates God's righteousness. The Canaanites are not destroyed arbitrarily but because <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">their iniquity has reached its fullness</span> ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 16|Genesis 15:16]]). God had waited four hundred years, providing opportunity for repentance. Now judgment falls, demonstrating that God's patience, while extensive, is not infinite.

Fourth, the preservation of Rahab demonstrates that <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">judgment is not ethnic but spiritual</span>. Ethnic Canaanites who turn to Yahweh in faith are spared (Rahab). Ethnic Israelites who violate covenant are destroyed (Achan). The criterion is covenant faithfulness, not biological descent.

The judgment on Jericho thus becomes a microcosm of the entire conquest and, typologically, of the final judgment. Just as Jericho's walls collapsed under divine judgment, so all human defenses against God will ultimately fail. Just as the city was devoted to destruction for its wickedness, so the entire cosmos corrupted by sin will be purged by divine fire before the new creation emerges.

## Judgment on Ai: Restored After Repentance

The conquest of Ai follows a different pattern than Jericho because it is preceded by Israel's sin and subsequent judgment. After the defeat caused by Achan's violation, God commands Joshua: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Do not fear and do not be dismayed. Take all the fighting men with you, and arise, go up to Ai. See, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, and his people, his city, and his land"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 1|Joshua 8:1]]).

The renewed commission demonstrates that <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">covenant blessing is restored after sin is purged</span>. With Achan judged and removed, God's anger turns away, and His presence returns to fight for Israel. The promise "I have given into your hand" is virtually identical to previous promises, indicating that the covenant relationship has been fully restored.

The conquest strategy for Ai involves military tactics rather than liturgical procession. Joshua sets an ambush behind the city, lures the defenders out with a feigned retreat, then attacks from both front and rear ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 3|Joshua 8:3-22]]). This demonstrates that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">God works through means</span>—He does not always employ miracles but often uses human wisdom and tactics, while still ensuring the outcome.

The judgment on Ai is comprehensive: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"All who fell that day, both men and women, were 12,000, all the people of Ai"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 25|Joshua 8:25]]). The entire population is destroyed, and the king of Ai is hanged on a tree until evening, then buried under a heap of stones ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 29|Joshua 8:29]]). The public display of the king's body serves as a warning to other Canaanite cities of the fate awaiting those who resist Israel's divinely mandated conquest.

Unlike Jericho, Israel is permitted to take the livestock and spoil of Ai as plunder ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 27|Joshua 8:27]]). This distinction indicates that not every conquest requires the same level of *herem*. Jericho, as firstfruits, was entirely devoted to God. Subsequent cities allow for limited plunder, providing economic benefit to Israel while still executing judgment on the inhabitants.

After conquering Ai, Joshua builds an altar on Mount Ebal and conducts a covenant renewal ceremony ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 8#Joshua 8 . 30|Joshua 8:30-35]]). The entire Law is read aloud—both blessings and curses—with the people standing on opposite sides of the ark, half facing Mount Gerizim (blessings) and half facing Mount Ebal (curses). This dramatic ceremony reinforces that <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">Israel's success in the conquest depends on covenant faithfulness</span>. The judgment just executed on Ai serves as vivid reminder of what awaits covenant violators.

The conquest of Ai demonstrates several theological principles. First, <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">defeat can be turned to victory when sin is judged and covenant relationship restored</span>. Israel lost the first battle against Ai because of Achan's sin. After purging the sin, they easily conquered the city. The pattern teaches that God's blessing flows through covenant obedience.

Second, <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">God works through human means while ensuring divine ends</span>. The ambush strategy was human wisdom, but the victory was divine gift. God does not bypass human agency but works through it to accomplish His purposes.

Third, <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">judgment on enemies provides opportunity for covenant renewal among God's people</span>. The conquest of Ai is immediately followed by covenant ceremony at Mounts Ebal and Gerizim. The execution of judgment on the wicked creates context for recommitment among the righteous.

## Judgment on the Southern Coalition: Five Kings Defeated

When the southern Canaanite kings hear of Jericho and Ai's fall, and especially when they learn that Gibeon has made peace with Israel, five Amorite kings form a coalition to attack Gibeon ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 5|Joshua 10:5]]). This creates a crisis: Israel has sworn an oath to protect the Gibeonites, even though the treaty was obtained through deception. Israel must now defend those who should have been destroyed.

God commands Joshua: <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"Do not fear them, for I have given them into your hands. Not a man of them shall stand before you"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 8|Joshua 10:8]]). Despite the irregular circumstances, God fights for Israel against the coalition. This demonstrates that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">God works even through human failures to accomplish His purposes</span>. The Gibeonite treaty was disobedience, yet God uses it to draw out multiple Canaanite armies for comprehensive judgment.

The judgment on the southern coalition involves multiple miraculous interventions. First, God throws the coalition armies into panic as Israel attacks ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 10|Joshua 10:10]]). Second, <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">God hurls large hailstones from heaven</span>, killing more Amorites than Israel killed with the sword ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 11|Joshua 10:11]]). Third, and most spectacularly, <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">the sun stands still</span> at Joshua's command, allowing Israel to complete the destruction before nightfall ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 12|Joshua 10:12-13]]).

The sun-standing-still miracle is one of the most debated passages in Scripture. The text says: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"The sun stopped in the midst of heaven and did not hurry to set for about a whole day. There has been no day like it before or since, when the LORD heeded the voice of a man, for the LORD fought for Israel"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 13|Joshua 10:13-14]]). Whether this involves the sun literally stopping (geocentric perspective), the earth's rotation ceasing (heliocentric perspective), or some form of refracted light extending daylight, the miracle demonstrates <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">God's absolute control over the cosmos in service of His redemptive purposes</span>.

The five coalition kings flee and hide in a cave at Makkedah. Joshua orders the cave entrance sealed until the battle is finished, then has the kings brought out for public judgment. He commands the Israelite commanders to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"put your feet on the necks of these kings"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 24|Joshua 10:24]])—a symbolic act of total victory. Joshua then declares: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Do not be afraid or dismayed; be strong and courageous. For thus the LORD will do to all your enemies against whom you fight"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 25|Joshua 10:25]]).

The kings are struck down and hanged on five trees until evening, then taken down and thrown into the cave where they had hidden, with large stones sealing the entrance as a permanent memorial ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 26|Joshua 10:26-27]]). The public execution and burial serve as visible testimony to <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">the futility of resisting God's purposes</span>. Those who oppose Yahweh's people oppose Yahweh Himself and will inevitably be judged.

Following the defeat of the coalition army, Joshua systematically conquers the southern cities: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Then Joshua and all Israel with him passed on from Makkedah to Libnah and fought against Libnah. And the LORD gave it also and its king into the hand of Israel. And he struck it with the edge of the sword, and every person in it; he left none remaining in it. And he did to its king as he had done to the king of Jericho"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 29|Joshua 10:29-30]]).

The same formula is repeated for Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, and Debir ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 31|Joshua 10:31-39]]). The repetition emphasizes the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">systematic and comprehensive nature of the judgment</span>. This is not random violence but ordered execution of divine sentence. The judgment is also complete—"he left none remaining" is the recurring refrain, indicating that the *herem* is fully executed.

The summary statement is sweeping: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"So Joshua struck the whole land, the hill country and the Negeb and the lowland and the slopes, and all their kings. He left none remaining, but devoted to destruction all that breathed, just as the LORD God of Israel commanded"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 10#Joshua 10 . 40|Joshua 10:40]]). The theological justification is explicit: this was done "just as the LORD God of Israel commanded"—the judgment is not Joshua's initiative but divine mandate.

The southern campaign demonstrates that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">God fights for His people against overwhelming odds</span>. Five kings with their combined armies could not stand against Israel because Yahweh fought for them. The miraculous interventions—panic, hailstones, extended daylight—all emphasize that the victory is divine work. The judgment on the southern coalition is not merely military conquest but <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">holy war in which God Himself wages battle against His enemies</span>.

## Judgment on the Northern Coalition: Hazor Burned

The northern coalition forms in response to Israel's victories in the south. Jabin king of Hazor assembles a massive army: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"And they came out, with all their troops, a great horde, in number like the sand that is on the seashore, with very many horses and chariots"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 4|Joshua 11:4]]). The reference to horses and chariots is significant—these represent advanced military technology that Israel lacks. From a human perspective, the northern coalition is invincible.

But God promises Joshua: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Do not be afraid of them, for tomorrow at this time I will give over all of them, slain, to Israel. You shall hamstring their horses and burn their chariots with fire"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 6|Joshua 11:6]]). The promise of victory is unconditional—"I will give over all of them." The command to hamstring horses and burn chariots is theologically significant: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Israel must not rely on military technology but on God alone</span>. The very weapons that made the coalition formidable must be destroyed, ensuring that Israel attributes victory to Yahweh, not to captured equipment.

Joshua launches a surprise attack at the waters of Merom, and <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">"the LORD gave them into the hand of Israel, who struck them and chased them"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 8|Joshua 11:8]]). The rout is complete: "And they struck them until he left none remaining." The horses are hamstrung and the chariots burned as commanded, removing the temptation to trust in military power.

Joshua then turns back to capture Hazor, the head of the coalition kingdoms. The judgment on Hazor is particularly severe: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"And Joshua turned back at that time and captured Hazor and struck its king with the sword, for Hazor formerly was the head of all those kingdoms. And they struck with the sword all who were in it, devoting them to destruction; there was none left that breathed. And he burned Hazor with fire"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 10|Joshua 11:10-11]]).

Hazor alone among the northern cities is burned—the other cities are captured but left standing ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 13|Joshua 11:13]]). This selective burning indicates that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Hazor, as the head of the coalition, receives special judgment</span>. The destruction of Hazor serves as a warning to other cities of what awaits those who lead resistance against God's purposes.

The northern campaign concludes with a summary emphasizing the comprehensiveness of the conquest: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"So Joshua took all that land, the hill country and all the Negeb and all the land of Goshen and the lowland and the Arabah and the hill country of Israel and its lowland from Mount Halak, which rises toward Seir, as far as Baal-gad in the Valley of Lebanon below Mount Hermon. And he captured all their kings and struck them and put them to death"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 16|Joshua 11:16-17]]).

The text explicitly states that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Joshua made war a long time with all those kings"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 18|Joshua 11:18]])—indicating that the conquest was not accomplished through a few quick miracles but through sustained military campaigns requiring perseverance and faith over extended time. Yet the victory is attributed to divine intervention: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"For it was the LORD's doing to harden their hearts that they should come against Israel in battle, in order that they should be devoted to destruction and should receive no mercy but be destroyed, just as the LORD commanded Moses"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 20|Joshua 11:20]]).

This verse raises the difficult question of divine hardening. God "hardened their hearts" so that they would fight rather than make peace, ensuring their destruction. This echoes Pharaoh's hardening in Exodus ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 21|Exodus 4:21]]). The hardening demonstrates that <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">God is sovereign even over human hearts, directing them toward judgment</span> when the measure of iniquity is full. The Canaanites' refusal to repent is both their responsibility and God's judicial hardening—two truths held in tension throughout Scripture.

The northern campaign also includes the destruction of the Anakim—the giant descendants of the Nephilim who had terrified the ten faithless spies forty years earlier: <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"And Joshua came at that time and cut off the Anakim from the hill country, from Hebron, from Debir, from Anab, and from all the hill country of Judah, and from all the hill country of Israel. Joshua devoted them to destruction with their cities. There was none of the Anakim left in the land of the people of Israel. Only in Gaza, in Gath, and in Ashdod did some remain"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 21|Joshua 11:21-22]]).

The defeat of the Anakim is symbolically crucial. These were the giants who caused Israel to tremble in unbelief at Kadesh Barnea. Now they are destroyed, demonstrating that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">no enemy is too formidable when God fights for His people</span>. The note that some Anakim remained in Philistine territory (Gaza, Gath, Ashdod) foreshadows the incomplete nature of the conquest and sets the stage for later conflicts—including David's confrontation with Goliath of Gath ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 17#1 Samuel 17 . 4|1 Samuel 17:4]]).

The judgment on the northern coalition completes the major military campaigns of the conquest. The summary is triumphant: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"So Joshua took the whole land, according to all that the LORD had spoken to Moses. And Joshua gave it for an inheritance to Israel according to their tribal allotments. And the land had rest from war"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 23|Joshua 11:23]]).

## The List of Defeated Kings: Comprehensive Victory

Joshua 12 provides a comprehensive list of thirty-one kings defeated by Israel—two east of the Jordan under Moses (Sihon and Og), and twenty-nine west of the Jordan under Joshua ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 12#Joshua 12 . 24|Joshua 12:24]]). The list serves multiple purposes:

First, it demonstrates the <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">comprehensive scope of the judgment</span>. The conquest was not limited to a few major cities but extended throughout the land. Every Canaanite kingdom encountered was defeated and its king killed.

Second, it validates <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">God's promises to the patriarchs</span>. The land promised to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has been conquered. God's faithfulness across multiple generations is vindicated.

Third, it provides a <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">memorial for future generations</span>. The list preserves the names of defeated kings as testimony to Yahweh's power. When future Israelites read this list, they are reminded of what God accomplished through Joshua.

Fourth, it sets up the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">tension between already and not-yet</span>. While the list is impressive, the subsequent chapters will reveal that much land remains unpossessed ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 13#Joshua 13 . 1|Joshua 13:1]]). The victory is real but incomplete—a pattern throughout redemptive history pointing toward ultimate fulfillment in Christ.

The list of defeated kings functions like the victory stela common in ancient Near Eastern conquest accounts, except that Israel's stela is textual rather than monumental. It proclaims: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Yahweh has triumphed gloriously; horse and rider he has thrown into the sea"</span> ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 1|Exodus 15:1]]). The God who defeated Pharaoh has now defeated thirty-one Canaanite kings.

## Joshua's Farewell Warnings: Future Judgment Prophesied

As Joshua nears the end of his life, he gathers Israel to deliver farewell addresses that are both retrospective and prospective. He reviews what God has accomplished, but he also warns of <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">judgment to come if Israel forsakes the covenant</span>.

Joshua reminds Israel: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"You have seen all that the LORD your God has done to all these nations for your sake, for it is the LORD your God who has fought for you"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 3|Joshua 23:3]]). The victories were divine work, not human achievement. This establishes the foundation for the warnings that follow.

Joshua then warns against compromise with the remaining nations: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"Know for certain that the LORD your God will no longer drive out these nations before you, but they shall be a snare and a trap for you, a whip on your sides and thorns in your eyes, until you perish from off this good land that the LORD your God has given you"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 13|Joshua 23:13]]).

The metaphors are vivid: <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">snare, trap, whip, thorns</span>. The nations that Israel failed to destroy will become instruments of divine judgment. God will use Israel's enemies to discipline His people when they stray from covenant faithfulness. This principle will dominate the book of Judges, where Israel's incomplete obedience leads to oppression by the very nations they should have destroyed.

Joshua continues with even more severe warnings: <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">"If you transgress the covenant of the LORD your God, which he commanded you, and go and serve other gods and bow down to them, then the anger of the LORD will be kindled against you, and you shall perish quickly from off the good land that he has given to you"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 16|Joshua 23:16]]).

The warning prophetically anticipates Israel's future apostasy and exile. The land is conditional—<span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">covenant faithfulness secures tenure, covenant violation results in expulsion</span>. The same God who judged the Canaanites and drove them from the land will judge Israel and drive them out if they follow Canaanite practices.

In his final address at Shechem, Joshua issues a dramatic challenge: <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">"Now therefore fear the LORD and serve him in sincerity and in faithfulness. Put away the gods that your fathers served beyond the River and in Egypt, and serve the LORD. And if it is evil in your eyes to serve the LORD, choose this day whom you will serve, whether the gods your fathers served in the region beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you dwell. But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 14|Joshua 24:14-15]]).

Joshua's "choose this day" challenge forces Israel to declare their allegiance. The people respond: <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">"Far be it from us that we should forsake the LORD to serve other gods, for it is the LORD our God who brought us and our fathers up from the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery, and who did those great signs in our sight and preserved us in all the way that we went, and among all the peoples through whom we passed"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 16|Joshua 24:16-17]]).

But Joshua is skeptical of their profession: <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">"You are not able to serve the LORD, for he is a holy God. He is a jealous God; he will not forgive your transgressions or your sins. If you forsake the LORD and serve foreign gods, then he will turn and do you harm and consume you, after having done you good"</span> ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 24#Joshua 24 . 19|Joshua 24:19-20]]).

This is one of the most remarkable statements in Scripture. Joshua, the covenant mediator, tells the people they are <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">unable to serve Yahweh</span>. This is not defeatism but realism. Joshua recognizes that human nature, even when blessed with covenant relationship and divine presence, remains prone to rebellion. The people's professed commitment, however sincere in the moment, will not endure when tested by temptation.

Joshua's warning proves prophetically accurate. The book of Judges will demonstrate Israel's repeated failure to remain faithful. The warnings of judgment pronounced by Joshua will be fulfilled as Israel experiences oppression, exile, and ultimately the destruction of the northern kingdom and Babylonian captivity of the southern kingdom.

The farewell addresses demonstrate that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">judgment is not merely past but future</span>. The Canaanites have been judged, but Israel too will face judgment if they violate covenant. The same holy God who executed judgment on pagan nations will execute judgment on His own people if they adopt pagan practices. <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Covenant relationship does not exempt from judgment; it heightens accountability</span>.

## Theological Analysis: Beale's Escalation and Incompleteness

From G.K. Beale's perspective, the judgments in Joshua represent an <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">escalation in redemptive history toward ultimate fulfillment</span>, while simultaneously revealing incompleteness that points beyond Joshua to a greater deliverer.

The judgment on the Canaanites is more comprehensive than previous judgments in Genesis. The Flood judged the whole world except Noah's family. Sodom and Gomorrah were destroyed, but Abraham interceded and Lot was saved. In Joshua, entire regions are systematically judged—southern and northern coalitions defeated, thirty-one kings killed, populations devoted to destruction.

Yet even this comprehensive judgment is <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">incomplete</span>. Much land remains unpossessed ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 13#Joshua 13 . 1|Joshua 13:1]]). Many Canaanites remain in the land ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 15#Joshua 15 . 63|Joshua 15:63]]; [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 16#Joshua 16 . 10|Joshua 16:10]]; [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 17#Joshua 17 . 12|Joshua 17:12-13]]). The incomplete conquest necessitates further judgment in the period of Judges and beyond.

This pattern of escalation-yet-incompleteness demonstrates that <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">each stage of redemptive history advances toward the goal while revealing the need for further advancement</span>. The conquest under Joshua is more comprehensive than the victory over Egypt, yet less comprehensive than what is needed. This points forward to Christ, who will accomplish the ultimate conquest by destroying sin, death, and Satan—the root enemies that lie beneath all historical enemies.

Moreover, the judgment on Israel for covenant violation (Achan) alongside judgment on Canaanites demonstrates that <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">covenant privilege does not exempt from judgment</span>. This pattern escalates throughout biblical history: Israel is judged through exile, Jerusalem is destroyed in AD 70, and ultimately all humanity will face final judgment. The pattern reveals that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">only those who are in Christ, the perfectly obedient covenant keeper, will escape eschatological judgment</span>.

## Theological Analysis: Kline's Intrusion of Final Judgment

Meredith Kline's framework illuminates why the judgments in Joshua are so severe and why they are not normative for all times. The conquest represents an <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">intrusion of eschatological judgment into the common grace era</span>. Normally, God exercises patience with sinners, allowing time for repentance and restraining full judgment ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]]). But in certain pivotal moments, God brings forward the judgment that properly belongs to the last day.

The judgments in Joshua are <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">temporal-physical pictures of eternal-spiritual realities</span>. The Canaanites experience in time and space what all unrepentant sinners will experience at the final judgment: comprehensive destruction under divine wrath. The conquest is therefore a preview, a breaking through of the age to come into the present age.

This explains why <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">New Testament ethics do not include commands to execute physical judgment on unbelievers</span>. The church age is a common grace period when believers live among unbelievers, proclaim the gospel, and patiently endure persecution. Final judgment awaits Christ's return. The intrusion that occurred in Joshua's time was unique to that redemptive-historical moment and is not replicated in the church age.

But the theological principles remain relevant. God's holiness, justice, and intolerance of sin are unchanging. The judgment that fell on Canaanites temporally will fall on all who reject Christ eternally. The severity of judgment in Joshua testifies to the severity of judgment to come—<span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">either borne by Christ on behalf of believers or borne personally by unbelievers</span>.

Kline's framework also illuminates the two-register structure of the conquest. On the earthly-historical register, Israel fights physical battles against Canaanite armies. On the heavenly-eschatological register, Yahweh wages cosmic war against the spiritual powers behind Canaanite idolatry. <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">The physical conquest is the visible manifestation of the invisible spiritual conflict</span>. The commander of the LORD's army ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 5#Joshua 5 . 14|Joshua 5:14]]) represents the heavenly dimension of the war being fought simultaneously on earth.

This two-register understanding prevents both triumphalism and despair. Triumphalism would claim that believers today should execute physical judgment as Israel did. Despair would conclude that judgment is incompatible with God's love. Kline's framework shows that <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">judgment is eschatological reality that intruded temporarily in Joshua's time and will intrude permanently at Christ's return</span>. In between, the church proclaims the gospel that offers escape from coming judgment through faith in Christ.

## The Christological Fulfillment: Christ the Judge

Every dimension of judgment in Joshua finds ultimate fulfillment in <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Jesus Christ, the righteous Judge</span>. Just as Joshua executed judgment on Canaanite nations, so Jesus will execute final judgment on all who persist in rebellion against God.

The judgment on Achan typologically points to <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">Christ bearing the judgment for covenant violation</span>. Achan was stoned for taking devoted things; Christ was crucified for taking upon Himself the sin of the world ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]). The judgment that fell on Achan in the Valley of Achor was transferred to Christ on Golgotha, making Achor truly "a door of hope" ([[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 2#Hosea 2 . 15|Hosea 2:15]]) for all who trust in Christ's substitutionary death.

The judgment on Jericho's walls foreshadows <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Christ's destruction of the dividing wall between Jew and Gentile</span> ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14]]) and ultimately His destruction of all barriers to the new creation. Just as Jericho's walls fell at God's command, so all opposition to God's kingdom will collapse at Christ's word.

The judgment on the five southern kings, with Joshua's commanders placing their feet on the kings' necks, typologically points to <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Christ's victory over all His enemies</span>. Paul writes: "For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 25|1 Corinthians 15:25]]), echoing both Joshua 10 and Psalm 110. The foot-on-neck imagery becomes the pattern for Christ's comprehensive victory over sin, death, Satan, and all hostile powers.

The list of thirty-one defeated kings anticipates <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">Revelation's vision of Christ conquering the kings of the earth</span> ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 19|Revelation 19:19-21]]). Just as Joshua systematically defeated every king who opposed Israel, so Christ will systematically judge every power that opposes His kingdom.

Most comprehensively, Joshua's warnings about future judgment on Israel for covenant violation point to the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">necessity of Christ's perfect obedience</span>. Israel could not maintain covenant faithfulness and therefore came under judgment. Christ maintained perfect faithfulness and therefore can transfer His righteousness to those who trust in Him ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 19|Romans 5:19]]).

The incomplete conquest under Joshua points to the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">complete conquest under Christ</span>. Joshua conquered Canaan partially; Christ conquers the cosmos completely. Joshua destroyed physical enemies; Christ destroys spiritual enemies. Joshua brought temporary rest; Christ brings eternal rest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8-11]]).

The judgment phase in Joshua thus serves as both warning and promise. It warns that <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">God's holiness demands judgment on sin</span>—either borne by Christ or borne personally. It promises that <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">God will finally and fully judge all evil</span>, vindicating His righteousness and establishing His kingdom forever. The judgments Joshua executed temporally anticipate the judgment Christ will execute eternally, when every knee bows and every tongue confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 10|Philippians 2:10-11]]).
