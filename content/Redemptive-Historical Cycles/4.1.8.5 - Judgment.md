# 4.1.8.5 - Judgment

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1.8 - Intrusion - Wilderness Wanderings (Sinai to Spying)|← Back to 4.1.8]]

## Overview: God's Measured Response to Covenant Treason

The Judgment phase ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 11|Numbers 14:11-45]]) reveals God's response to Israel's catastrophic refusal to enter the Promised Land. When the people reject God's promise, threaten to stone Joshua and Caleb, and propose returning to Egypt, God threatens **total destruction** of the nation. Moses intercedes with one of Scripture's greatest prayers, and God relents from complete annihilation—but not from judgment.

The sentence is precisely measured: the entire wilderness generation will **die over 40 years**, one year for each day the spies reconnoitered the land ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 34|Numbers 14:34]]). The ten unfaithful spies die immediately by plague ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 36|Numbers 14:36-38]]). And when the people presumptuously attempt to enter the land without God's blessing, they are defeated by the Amalekites and Canaanites ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 39|Numbers 14:39-45]]).

This judgment establishes a redemptive-historical **pattern** that will recur throughout Scripture: rebellion brings exile, yet God preserves a remnant for the next generation. The wilderness becomes a **crucible of judgment and preparation**—the old generation must die so the new generation can enter. This foreshadows the ultimate judgment on unbelief and points to Christ, who enters God's rest on behalf of all who trust Him.

---

## 1. God's Initial Response: Threat of Total Destruction

When the glory of the LORD appears at the tent of meeting ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 10|Numbers 14:10]]), God speaks to Moses:

"And the LORD said to Moses, 'How long will this people despise me? And how long will they not believe in me, in spite of all the signs that I have done among them? I will strike them with the pestilence and disinherit them, and I will make of you a nation greater and mightier than they'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 11|Numbers 14:11-12]]).

### The Charges Against Israel

God's indictment contains two fundamental charges:

**1. Despising God**: "How long will this people despise me?" The Hebrew verb *na'ats* means to scorn, treat with contempt, blaspheme. Israel's refusal to enter is not merely fear but **contempt** for God's promise and character.

**2. Unbelief**: "How long will they not believe in me?" Despite witnessing the ten plagues, the Red Sea crossing, manna from heaven, water from the rock, the glory cloud, and Mount Sinai's theophany—they still refuse to trust God's word.

The phrase "in spite of all the signs" (*be'otot*) is devastating. God has given overwhelming evidence of His power and faithfulness, yet Israel treats His promise as unreliable.

### The Threatened Judgment

God proposes to "strike them with the pestilence"—a comprehensive plague that would annihilate the entire nation. This is not hyperbole; God genuinely threatens to **disinherit** Israel, removing them as His covenant people, and start over with Moses: "I will make of you a nation greater and mightier than they."

This echoes God's threat after the golden calf ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 10|Exodus 32:10]]), where Moses also interceded successfully. The repetition shows the **persistence of Israel's rebellion** despite God's patience.

### Theological Significance: God's Holiness and Justice

God's threat reveals His **absolute holiness**. Covenant rebellion cannot be tolerated indefinitely. The psalmist asks, "If you, O LORD, should mark iniquities, O Lord, who could stand?" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 130#Psalm 130 . 3|Psalm 130:3]]). The answer is: no one.

Yet God's threat also serves a **pedagogical purpose**—it demonstrates the seriousness of sin and creates the context for Moses' intercession. God invites Moses to plead for the people, thereby revealing His own **covenant faithfulness** through the mediator.

---

## 2. Moses' Intercession: Appeal to God's Reputation and Character

Moses responds with one of Scripture's most profound intercessory prayers ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 13|Numbers 14:13-19]]):

"Then the Egyptians will hear of it, for you brought up this people in your might from among them, and they will tell the inhabitants of this land... Now if you kill this people as one man, then the nations who have heard your fame will say, 'It is because the LORD was not able to bring this people into the land that he swore to give to them that he has killed them in the wilderness'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 13|Numbers 14:13-16]]).

### First Argument: God's Reputation Among the Nations

Moses appeals to God's **glory among the nations**. If God destroys Israel now, the Egyptians and Canaanites will conclude that Yahweh **lacked power** to fulfill His promise—"the LORD was not able to bring this people into the land."

This argument assumes that God's ultimate purpose is His own **glory**—the display of His character and power to all nations. Israel exists not for its own sake but as the vehicle through which God will reveal Himself to the world ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 43#Isaiah 43 . 7|Isaiah 43:7]]—"everyone who is called by my name, whom I created for my glory").

Moses is essentially saying: "Your covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob is at stake. Your reputation is tied to this people. If You destroy them, the nations will blaspheme Your name."

### Second Argument: God's Revealed Character

Moses then appeals to God's **self-revelation** at Sinai:

"And now, please let the power of the Lord be great as you have promised, saying, 'The LORD is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love, forgiving iniquity and transgression, but he will by no means clear the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children, to the third and the fourth generation.' Please pardon the iniquity of this people, according to the greatness of your steadfast love, just as you have forgiven this people, from Egypt until now" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 17|Numbers 14:17-19]]).

Moses quotes nearly verbatim God's self-disclosure after the golden calf ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 6|Exodus 34:6-7]]). He is holding God to **God's own revealed character**:

- **Slow to anger**: Don't act immediately on Your just wrath
- **Abounding in steadfast love**: Your *hesed* (covenant loyalty) is greater than Your wrath
- **Forgiving iniquity**: You have the power and willingness to pardon
- **But not clearing the guilty**: Justice will be satisfied, but through Your means

Moses' final appeal is to God's **past faithfulness**: "just as you have forgiven this people, from Egypt until now." You've already forgiven the golden calf, the grumbling at Taberah and Kibroth-hattaavah, Miriam's rebellion—continue Your pattern of mercy.

### Theological Significance: The Power of Intercessory Prayer

Moses demonstrates the **efficacy of intercessory prayer**. He does not deny Israel's guilt or minimize their sin. Instead, he appeals to:
- God's glory (His reputation)
- God's character (His self-revelation)
- God's past actions (His track record)

This is the model for all Christian intercession: grounding our prayers in **God's own promises and character** rather than in the merit of those for whom we pray.

Moses prefigures Christ, the ultimate Mediator, whose intercession is grounded not merely in God's character but in His own **finished work** on the cross ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]—"he always lives to make intercession").

## 3. God's Response: Pardon and Judgment Combined

God's response reveals the complex nature of divine justice and mercy:

"Then the LORD said, 'I have pardoned, according to your word. But truly, as I live, and as all the earth shall be filled with the glory of the LORD, none of the men who have seen my glory and my signs that I did in Egypt and in the wilderness, and yet have put me to the test these ten times and have not obeyed my voice, shall see the land that I swore to give to their fathers. And none of those who despised me shall see it'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 20|Numbers 14:20-23]]).

### Pardon: God Relents from Total Destruction

"I have pardoned" (*salach*)—God forgives the sin in the sense that He will not destroy the entire nation. Moses' intercession succeeds; Israel as a people will continue to exist.

Yet this is **corporate pardon**, not individual absolution. The nation survives, but the generation that sinned will still face consequences.

### Judgment: The Wilderness Generation Will Die

Despite the pardon, judgment falls: "none of the men who have seen my glory... shall see the land." The adults who witnessed God's redemptive acts but still refused to trust will **die in the wilderness** without entering rest.

God specifies the charges:
- **Seen my glory and signs**: They had overwhelming evidence
- **Put me to the test ten times**: Repeated pattern of unbelief
- **Have not obeyed my voice**: Covenant disobedience
- **Despised me**: Treated God with contempt

The "ten times" likely refers to:
1. Complaining at the Red Sea ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 14#Exodus 14 . 11|Exodus 14:11-12]])
2. Waters of Marah ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 24|Exodus 15:24]])
3. Wilderness of Sin—no food ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 2|Exodus 16:2-3]])
4. Sabbath-breaking with manna ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 16#Exodus 16 . 20|Exodus 16:20, 27]])
5. Rephidim—no water ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 17#Exodus 17 . 2|Exodus 17:2-3]])
6. Golden calf ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 1|Exodus 32:1-6]])
7. Taberah ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 1|Numbers 11:1-3]])
8. Kibroth-hattaavah ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 4|Numbers 11:4-34]])
9. Miriam and Aaron's rebellion ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 12#Numbers 12 . 1|Numbers 12:1-2]])
10. Refusal to enter at Kadesh ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 1|Numbers 14:1-4]])

This demonstrates the **patience of God**—He endures not one or two rebellions but ten before pronouncing final judgment on this generation.

### The Exception: Joshua and Caleb

"But my servant Caleb, because he has a different spirit and has followed me fully, I will bring into the land into which he went, and his descendants shall possess it" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 24|Numbers 14:24]]).

Joshua and Caleb are specifically exempted from judgment because they "followed the LORD fully" (*male' acharai*—literally "filled up after me"). They will enter the land, demonstrating that **faith leads to rest**, even when the majority perishes in unbelief.

## 4. The Forty-Year Sentence: Precise and Proportional

God pronounces the specific terms of judgment:

"According to the number of the days in which you spied out the land, forty days, a year for each day, you shall bear your iniquity forty years, and you shall know my displeasure" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 34|Numbers 14:34]]).

### The Measure of Judgment

The **forty-year wandering** corresponds exactly to the **forty days** of reconnaissance. This demonstrates God's **precise justice**—the punishment fits the crime. For forty days they trusted human observation over divine promise; for forty years they will wander.

The number forty becomes significant throughout Scripture:
- **Flood**: Forty days and nights ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 12|Genesis 7:12]])
- **Moses on Sinai**: Forty days ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 18|Exodus 24:18]])
- **Elijah's journey**: Forty days to Horeb ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 19#1 Kings 19 . 8|1 Kings 19:8]])
- **Jesus' temptation**: Forty days in wilderness ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 2|Matthew 4:2]])
- **Resurrection appearances**: Forty days ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 3|Acts 1:3]])

Forty becomes the biblical number for **testing, judgment, and preparation**.

### "You Shall Know My Displeasure"

The Hebrew phrase *ten'u'ati* (literally "my opposition" or "my frustration") is rare. God is declaring that they will **experience** His opposition—not in sudden destruction but in prolonged exile. The wilderness becomes a living judgment.

### Mercy in Judgment: The Next Generation

"And your little ones, who you said would become a prey, I will bring in, and they shall know the land that you have rejected. But as for you, your dead bodies shall fall in this wilderness" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 31|Numbers 14:31-32]]).

Even in judgment, God shows **mercy to the next generation**. The children whom the people claimed would become "prey" to the Canaanites ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 3|Numbers 14:3]]) will instead **inherit the promise**. God's covenant purposes will not fail; they are simply delayed one generation.

This establishes the pattern of **generational consequences**—parents' unbelief affects their children, yet God preserves a remnant for future blessing.

## 5. Immediate Judgment: The Ten Spies Die by Plague

"And the men whom Moses sent to spy out the land, who returned and made all the congregation grumble against him by bringing up a bad report about the land—the men who brought up a bad report of the land—died by plague before the LORD. Of those men who went to spy out the land, only Joshua the son of Nun and Caleb the son of Jephunneh remained alive" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 36|Numbers 14:36-38]]).

### Swift Justice for the Ringleaders

The ten unfaithful spies die **immediately** by plague. They do not get forty years; their judgment is instant. This demonstrates the principle that **greater responsibility brings greater accountability**.

The spies were leaders, representatives of the tribes, entrusted with a critical mission. Their bad report led the entire congregation into sin. Therefore, their judgment is swift and severe.

This foreshadows Jesus' teaching: "Everyone to whom much was given, of him much will be required" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 12#Luke 12 . 48|Luke 12:48]]), and James' warning: "Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers, for you know that we who teach will be judged with greater strictness" ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 3#James 3 . 1|James 3:1]]).

### Joshua and Caleb Alone Survive

Only Joshua and Caleb "remained alive" (*chayah*)—they alone of the twelve spies will enter the land. Their **faith** is vindicated, their **witness** preserved. They become living testimonies that trusting God's word leads to life.

## 6. Presumptuous Invasion: Works Without Faith

After hearing God's judgment, the people exhibit the opposite error—**presumption**:

"And they rose early in the morning and went up to the heights of the hill country, saying, 'Here we are. We will go up to the place that the LORD has promised, for we have sinned.' But Moses said, 'Why now are you transgressing the command of the LORD, when that will not succeed? Do not go up, for the LORD is not among you, lest you be struck down before your enemies'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 40|Numbers 14:40-42]]).

### The Nature of Presumption

Yesterday, the people **refused to go** when God commanded them to enter. Today, they **insist on going** when God has commanded them to turn back. Both are forms of unbelief:

- **Unbelief** refuses to trust God's promise
- **Presumption** refuses to accept God's judgment

The people claim "we have sinned"—but this is not genuine repentance. True repentance submits to God's discipline; presumption tries to **undo the consequences** through human effort.

### Moses' Warning

Moses warns them three times:
1. "Why now are you transgressing the command of the LORD?"
2. "That will not succeed"
3. "The LORD is not among you"

The critical issue is God's **presence**. Without the ark, without the cloud, without God's blessing, military action is futile. This is the lesson Israel must learn: **God's presence, not human strength, determines victory**.

### The Defeat

"But they presumed to go up to the heights of the hill country, although neither the ark of the covenant of the LORD nor Moses departed out of the camp. Then the Amalekites and the Canaanites who lived in that hill country came down and defeated them and pursued them, even to Hormah" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 44|Numbers 14:44-45]]).

The result is military disaster. The Amalekites (who attacked Israel earlier, [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 17#Exodus 17 . 8|Exodus 17:8-16]]) and Canaanites rout the Israelites, pursuing them "even to Hormah" (meaning "destruction").

### Theological Significance: Faith vs. Works

This incident illustrates the difference between **faith** and **works**:

- **Faith**: Trusting God's promise and obeying His command at the right time
- **Works**: Attempting to earn blessing or undo judgment through human effort

Paul will later contrast these: "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]]).

The Israelites tried to **work their way into the land** after refusing to **trust their way in**. Both approaches fail. Only **faith in God's timing and power** leads to rest.

## 7. Theological Themes: Justice, Mercy, and the Pattern of Judgment

### Measured Judgment: Not Too Little, Not Too Much

God's judgment on the wilderness generation is **precisely calibrated**:

- **Not total destruction**: The nation survives; the covenant continues
- **Not immediate annihilation**: A forty-year period allows for transition
- **Not arbitrary**: The sentence corresponds to the offense (40 days = 40 years)
- **Not universal**: The next generation will enter; Joshua and Caleb are exempted

This demonstrates God's **perfect justice**—punishment is neither lenient nor excessive but **perfectly fitted** to the crime.

### Generational Consequences

The forty-year judgment establishes the principle of **generational consequences**: "visiting the iniquity of the fathers on the children, to the third and the fourth generation" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 7|Exodus 34:7]]).

Yet this is not **fatalism**. The next generation is not condemned for their parents' sin; rather, they **inherit the consequences** (delayed entry) while still having the opportunity to **trust God themselves**.

Ezekiel will later clarify: "The soul who sins shall die. The son shall not suffer for the iniquity of the father, nor the father suffer for the iniquity of the son" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 18#Ezekiel 18 . 20|Ezekiel 18:20]]). Each generation is responsible for its own response to God.

### The Wilderness as Crucible

The forty years in the wilderness serve multiple purposes:

1. **Judgment**: The unbelieving generation dies off
2. **Preparation**: The next generation is raised and trained
3. **Testing**: Will the new generation trust God where their parents failed?
4. **Revelation**: God continues to reveal Himself (laws in Numbers 15-19, etc.)
5. **Pattern**: This becomes the paradigm for exile and restoration

The wilderness is not merely **punitive** but **formative**—it shapes the people who will finally enter the land.

## 8. Kline's Framework: Delaying the Consummation

Meredith Kline's two-register framework illuminates this judgment:

### The Promised Consummation Is Delayed

The **upper register** (God's eschatological rest) was ready to invade the **lower register** (Common Grace period). The land was there, visible, abundant. But Israel's unbelief prevented the consummation.

Now, the **Common Grace period is extended** for forty years. The wilderness wandering is not the final age, nor is it the Sabbath rest—it's a prolonged period of preparation and judgment.

### Judgment as Preview of Final Judgment

The death of the wilderness generation is an **intrusion of final judgment** into the Common Grace period. They experience individually what all unbelievers will experience corporately on the Last Day—**exclusion from God's rest**.

Hebrews applies this typologically: "They were unable to enter because of unbelief. Therefore, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 19|Hebrews 3:19]]-[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 1|4:1]]).

### The Next Generation: New Creation

The rising generation represents **new creation** emerging from the death of the old. This pattern—death of the old, life in the new—becomes foundational to biblical theology:

- **Noah**: Old world destroyed; new world through the flood
- **Exodus**: Egyptian oppressors destroyed; Israel delivered
- **Exile**: Judah judged; remnant returns
- **Cross**: Old Adam dies; new Adam rises

Paul applies this to believers: "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]).

## 9. Christological Fulfillment: The Greater Moses

The Judgment phase points to Christ in multiple ways:

### Jesus as the Perfect Intercessor

Moses intercedes for Israel, and God relents from total destruction. But Moses cannot bear the penalty of sin—he can only plead for mercy.

Jesus, by contrast, is the **perfect Mediator** who not only intercedes but **satisfies justice**: "But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 5|Isaiah 53:5]]).

Christ's intercession is grounded in His finished work: "Therefore he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]).

### Jesus Bears the Forty-Year Judgment

Where the wilderness generation wandered forty years as judgment, Jesus endured **forty days** in the wilderness, facing the same temptations Israel faced—and succeeding perfectly ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 1|Matthew 4:1-11]]).

Jesus **condenses** the forty-year test into forty days and **passes** where Israel failed. He is the true Israel who trusts God's word, refuses to test God, and worships God alone.

### Jesus Enters the Rest We Refused

The wilderness generation refused to enter the rest God offered. Jesus, the **pioneer of our salvation** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]), enters the true rest on our behalf:

"For Christ has entered, not into holy places made with hands, which are copies of the true things, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]]).

Through union with Christ, believers **enter the rest** that Israel refused: "So then, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God, for whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]]).

### Warning Against Unbelief

The wilderness judgment becomes Scripture's premier warning against unbelief. The writer of Hebrews repeatedly uses it to exhort the church:

"Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 12|Hebrews 3:12]]).

"Therefore, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 1|Hebrews 4:1]]).

"Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 11|Hebrews 4:11]]).

## Conclusion: Judgment That Preserves the Promise

The Judgment phase demonstrates God's perfect **balance of justice and mercy**. The sin is too great to overlook—the entire generation that despised God will die in the wilderness. Yet the nation survives, the covenant continues, and the next generation will enter the land.

This pattern of **judgment that preserves a remnant** runs throughout redemptive history. God judges the wicked but always maintains a faithful line through whom His promises will be fulfilled. Even when the majority perishes in unbelief, God ensures that Joshua, Caleb, and the next generation will carry forward His redemptive purposes.

The forty-year sentence is both **punitive and redemptive**. It punishes the guilty generation's unbelief while preparing a new generation for faith. The wilderness becomes a crucible where God shapes His people through discipline, testing, and revelation.

Ultimately, this judgment points to the **final Day of the Lord** when all unbelief will be judged and all who trust in Christ will enter eternal rest. The question Hebrews poses to every generation remains urgent: "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 15|Hebrews 3:15]]).

Moses' intercession succeeded in preserving the nation, but he could not reverse the consequences of sin. Only Christ, the perfect Mediator, can both intercede and atone, both plead for mercy and satisfy justice. In Him, we find rest from the wilderness of this fallen world and enter the eternal Sabbath prepared for God's people.
