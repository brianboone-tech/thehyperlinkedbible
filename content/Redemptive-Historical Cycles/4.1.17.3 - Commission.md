# 4.1.17.3 - Commission (Prophets After the Exile)

## Overview: The Calling to Faithfulness in Incomplete Restoration

The commission given to the post-exilic community differs significantly from previous biblical commissions. Unlike Adam's charge to fill and subdue the earth, Abraham's call to be a blessing to the nations, or Israel's mandate to be a kingdom of priests, the post-exilic commission is more modest in scope yet no less demanding in faithfulness. The returning exiles are called not to conquer and expand but to rebuild and maintain, not to exercise dominion over nations but to remain faithful under foreign rule, not to manifest glory but to trust that glory will come.

The commission has five interlocking elements: **rebuild God's house and prioritize His worship**, **maintain covenant distinctiveness through separation from pagan practices**, **practice justice and righteousness in community life**, **bring the full tithe and honor God with firstfruits**, and **prepare for the coming Messenger through faithfulness and expectant hope**. Each element addresses specific failures in the community while pointing toward the greater fulfillment that awaits.

Significantly, the post-exilic commission includes explicit warning that failure carries consequences—economic hardship for misplaced priorities ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 1#Haggai 1 . 6|Haggai 1:6]]), covenant curse for withholding tithes ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 9|Malachi 3:9]]), divine displeasure for defiled worship ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 1#Malachi 1 . 10|Malachi 1:10]]). But it also includes extraordinary promises—blessing overflowing if tithes are brought ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 10|Malachi 3:10]]), greater glory coming to the temple ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 2#Haggai 2 . 9|Haggai 2:9]]), nations streaming to Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 8#Zechariah 8 . 22|Zechariah 8:22-23]]).

Beale emphasizes that the post-exilic commission is fundamentally preparatory. The community is called to maintain covenant faithfulness and distinct identity so that when Messiah comes, there will be a people prepared to receive Him. The commission is not to accomplish the Adamic task (they cannot) but to sustain faithfulness in the interim, to keep the lamp burning until the true Light arrives, to preserve the line through which the Seed will come.

Kline adds that the post-exilic commission reflects the "already but not yet" tension. The kingdom has intruded (temple rebuilt, people returned), but consummation awaits. The commission is to live faithfully in this tension—maintaining worship, keeping covenant, practicing justice—while looking forward to the greater intrusion when the Lord will suddenly come to His temple. The commission is to be faithful witnesses to covenant reality in a time when covenant promises seem delayed.

## Commission 1: Rebuild and Prioritize God's House

### Haggai's Call to Action

The first and most urgent commission is to complete the temple rebuilding. Haggai delivers God's rebuke: "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?" ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 1#Haggai 1 . 4|Haggai 1:4]]). The people have prioritized personal comfort over God's glory, building their own homes while God's house lies unfinished.

God commands direct action: "Thus says the LORD of hosts: Consider your ways. Go up to the hills and bring wood and build the house, that I may take pleasure in it and that I may be glorified, says the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 1#Haggai 1 . 7|Haggai 1:7-8]]). The commission is clear: go, bring, build. Stop making excuses, cease prioritizing self, begin the work God has called you to complete.

The reason is theological: "that I may take pleasure in it and that I may be glorified." God's glory and pleasure are at stake. The temple is not merely a building but the visible sign that God dwells among His people, the place where heaven and earth meet, the center from which blessing flows to the world. To leave it in ruins is to deny God's centrality and to forfeit the blessing that comes from right priorities.

The commission includes promise and threat. The threat: "You looked for much, and behold, it came to little. And when you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? declares the LORD of hosts. Because of my house that lies in ruins, while each of you busies himself with his own house. Therefore the heavens above you have withheld the dew, and the earth has withheld its produce. And I have called for a drought on the land and the hills, on the grain, the new wine, the oil, on what the ground brings forth, on man and beast, and on all their labors" ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 1#Haggai 1 . 9|Haggai 1:9-11]]).

The economic hardship they experience is not random misfortune but divine discipline. God actively frustrates their efforts ("I blew it away"), withholds agricultural blessing, and calls for drought—all to turn their hearts back to proper priorities.

The promise comes once they obey: On the day they resume construction, God declares: "From this day on I will bless you" ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 2#Haggai 2 . 19|Haggai 2:19]]). Obedience unlocks blessing; prioritizing God's house restores covenant favor.

### Zechariah's Complementary Exhortation

Zechariah reinforces Haggai's message with divine assurance: "Thus says the LORD of hosts: Let your hands be strong, you who in these days have been hearing these words from the mouth of the prophets who were present on the day that the foundation of the house of the LORD of hosts was laid, that the temple might be built" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 8#Zechariah 8 . 9|Zechariah 8:9]]).

He contrasts their previous state with what God will do: "For before those days there was no wage for man or any wage for beast, neither was there any safety from the foe for him who went out or came in, for I set every man against his neighbor. But now I will not deal with the remnant of this people as in the former days, declares the LORD of hosts. For there shall be a sowing of peace. The vine shall give its fruit, and the ground shall give its produce, and the heavens shall give their dew. And I will cause the remnant of this people to possess all these things" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 8#Zechariah 8 . 10|Zechariah 8:10-12]]).

The commission to build is coupled with the promise that God will bless the work, transform hardship into prosperity, and establish peace. The people are not merely completing a construction project; they are partnering with God's redemptive purposes.

### The Greater Temple Anticipated

Implicit in the commission to rebuild the physical temple is the greater commission to be the spiritual temple. The Second Temple, for all its significance, is provisional. It points beyond itself to the ultimate dwelling of God with humanity.

Peter later identifies believers as "living stones being built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 5|1 Peter 2:5]]). Paul declares that "you are... God's building" and "Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you?" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 9|1 Corinthians 3:9, 16]]).

The commission to rebuild the temple in 520 BC carries forward to the New Testament commission to build the church—the community in which God's Spirit dwells, through which His glory is manifested, and from which blessing flows to the nations.

## Commission 2: Maintain Covenant Distinctiveness

### Ezra's Call to Separation from Pagan Practices

When Ezra discovers that the people have intermarried with surrounding nations, he tears his garment, pulls out hair from his head and beard, and sits appalled until the evening sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 3|Ezra 9:3]]). His public grief catalyzes conviction in the community.

The people gather, weeping, and Shecaniah proposes: "We have broken faith with our God and have married foreign women from the peoples of the land, but even now there is hope for Israel in spite of this. Therefore let us make a covenant with our God to put away all these wives and their children, according to the counsel of my lord and of those who tremble at the commandment of our God, and let it be done according to the Law" ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 10#Ezra 10 . 2|Ezra 10:2-3]]).

This leads to systematic separation: "Then all the men of Judah and Benjamin assembled at Jerusalem... And all the people sat in the open square before the house of God, trembling because of this matter and because of the heavy rain. And Ezra the priest stood up and said to them, 'You have broken faith and married foreign women, and so increased the guilt of Israel. Now then make confession to the LORD, the God of your fathers, and do his will. Separate yourselves from the peoples of the land and from the foreign wives'" ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 10#Ezra 10 . 9|Ezra 10:9-11]]).

The people respond: "It is so; we must do as you have said" ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 10#Ezra 10 . 12|Ezra 10:12]]). The commission is radical separation from covenant-breaking practices, even at great personal cost.

This is not ethnic prejudice but covenant faithfulness. God's concern is theological: "For they have taken some of their daughters to be wives for themselves and for their sons, so that the holy race has mixed itself with the peoples of the lands" ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 2|Ezra 9:2]]). The "holy race" is set apart for redemptive purposes—through this people, the Messiah will come. If they assimilate into surrounding nations, covenant identity is lost and redemptive purposes are thwarted.

Ezra's prayer articulates the commission: "O our God, what shall we say after this? For we have forsaken your commandments, which you commanded by your servants the prophets, saying, 'The land that you are entering, to take possession of it, is a land impure with the impurity of the peoples of the lands, with their abominations that have filled it from end to end with their uncleanness. Therefore do not give your daughters to their sons, neither take their daughters for your sons, and never seek their peace or prosperity, that you may be strong and eat the good of the land and leave it for an inheritance to your children forever'" ([[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 10|Ezra 9:10-12]]).

The commission is to remain distinct, maintain holy separation, reject assimilation, and preserve covenant identity for the sake of future generations and ultimate redemptive purposes.

### Nehemiah's Enforcement of Covenant Boundaries

When Nehemiah returns for a second term as governor (after being away in Persia), he discovers renewed covenant violations. His response is immediate and forceful.

Regarding Sabbath desecration: "In those days I saw in Judah people treading winepresses on the Sabbath, and bringing in heaps of grain and loading them on donkeys, and also wine, grapes, figs, and all kinds of loads, which they brought into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day. And I warned them on the day when they sold food" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 15|Nehemiah 13:15]]).

Nehemiah confronts the nobles: "What is this evil thing that you are doing, profaning the Sabbath day? Did not your fathers act in this way, and did not our God bring all this disaster on us and on this city? Now you are bringing more wrath on Israel by profaning the Sabbath" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 17|Nehemiah 13:17-18]]).

He takes action: "As soon as it began to grow dark at the gates of Jerusalem before the Sabbath, I commanded that the doors should be shut and gave orders that they should not be opened until after the Sabbath. And I stationed some of my servants at the gates, that no load might be brought in on the Sabbath day" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 19|Nehemiah 13:19]]).

When merchants camp outside the walls hoping to trade, Nehemiah warns them: "Why do you lodge outside the wall? If you do so again, I will lay hands on you" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 21|Nehemiah 13:21]]).

Regarding renewed intermarriage: "In those days also I saw the Jews who had married women of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab. And half of their children spoke the language of Ashdod, and they could not speak the language of Judah, but only the language of each people" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 23|Nehemiah 13:23-24]]).

The children speaking foreign languages rather than Hebrew indicates cultural and religious assimilation. Covenant identity is being lost. Nehemiah's response is passionate: "And I confronted them and cursed them and beat some of them and pulled out their hair. And I made them take an oath in the name of God, saying, 'You shall not give your daughters to their sons, or take their daughters for your sons or for yourselves. Did not Solomon king of Israel sin on account of such women?'" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 25|Nehemiah 13:25-26]]).

He invokes Solomon's apostasy as warning: "Among the many nations there was no king like him, and he was beloved by his God, and God made him king over all Israel. Nevertheless, foreign women made even him to sin" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 26|Nehemiah 13:26]]).

The commission is uncompromising: maintain Sabbath observance, refuse intermarriage with pagans, preserve distinct covenant identity, remember the lessons of history, and refuse to repeat the sins that brought exile.

### The Theological Foundation: Holy People for Holy Purposes

The commission to maintain distinctiveness rests on Israel's calling as holy people: "For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. The LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession, out of all the peoples who are on the face of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 6|Deuteronomy 7:6]]).

Holiness means separation—set apart for God's purposes. Israel's distinctiveness is not ethnic superiority but covenantal calling. Through this people, God will bless all nations. The Seed promised to Abraham will come through Israel. The Messiah will be born in this community. To assimilate is to forfeit the calling and thwart redemptive purposes.

Peter applies this commission to the church: "But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).

The commission to maintain distinctiveness continues: believers are called to be "in the world but not of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 14|John 17:14-16]]), to "not be conformed to this world" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 2|Romans 12:2]]), and to be "blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 15|Philippians 2:15]]).

## Commission 3: Practice Justice and Righteousness

### Zechariah's Moral Exhortation

Beyond structural rebuilding and covenant separation, the post-exilic community is commissioned to practice justice and righteousness in their daily life together. Zechariah delivers God's word: "Thus says the LORD of hosts, Render true judgments, show kindness and mercy to one another, do not oppress the widow, the fatherless, the sojourner, or the poor, and let none of you devise evil against another in your heart" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 7#Zechariah 7 . 9|Zechariah 7:9-10]]).

This commission echoes the pre-exilic prophets. Micah summarized covenant faithfulness: "He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?" ([[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 6#Micah 6 . 8|Micah 6:8]]).

Zechariah reminds them that failure in this area brought exile: "But they refused to pay attention and turned a stubborn shoulder and stopped their ears that they might not hear. They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear the law and the words that the LORD of hosts had sent by his Spirit through the former prophets. Therefore great anger came from the LORD of hosts. 'As I called, and they would not hear, so they called, and I would not hear,' says the LORD of hosts, 'and I scattered them with a whirlwind among all the nations that they had not known'" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 7#Zechariah 7 . 11|Zechariah 7:11-14]]).

The commission is to learn from history, avoid repeating the sins that brought judgment, and demonstrate covenant faithfulness through justice, mercy, and integrity in community life.

### Malachi's Indictment of Injustice

By Malachi's time, injustice has again crept into the community. God announces: "Then I will draw near to you for judgment. I will be a swift witness against the sorcerers, against the adulterers, against those who swear falsely, against those who oppress the hired worker in his wages, the widow and the fatherless, against those who thrust aside the sojourner, and do not fear me, says the LORD of hosts" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 5|Malachi 3:5]]).

The list is comprehensive: sorcery (consulting other spiritual powers), adultery (violating marriage covenant), false oaths (lying while invoking God's name), oppressing workers (withholding wages), mistreating widows and orphans (failing to protect the vulnerable), and thrusting aside sojourners (refusing hospitality to foreigners).

Each violation demonstrates failure to fear God. The commission is to maintain justice in economic relationships, protect the vulnerable, honor marriage covenants, tell truth, and treat foreigners with hospitality—all as expressions of fearing God.

### Nehemiah's Economic Reforms

Nehemiah confronts economic oppression when the people cry out: "We are forcing our sons and our daughters to be slaves, and some of our daughters have already been enslaved, but it is not in our power to help it, for other men have our fields and our vineyards" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 5#Nehemiah 5 . 5|Nehemiah 5:5]]).

Nehemiah's response is righteous anger: "I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these words. I took counsel with myself, and I brought charges against the nobles and the officials. I said to them, 'You are exacting interest, each from his brother.' And I held a great assembly against them" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 5#Nehemiah 5 . 6|Nehemiah 5:6-7]]).

He appeals to covenant identity: "The thing that you are doing is not good. Ought you not to walk in the fear of our God to prevent the taunts of the nations our enemies? Moreover, I and my brothers and my servants are lending them money and grain. Let us abandon this exacting of interest" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 5#Nehemiah 5 . 9|Nehemiah 5:9-10]]).

The nobles and officials agree: "We will restore these and require nothing from them. We will do as you say" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 5#Nehemiah 5 . 12|Nehemiah 5:12]]). Nehemiah makes them take an oath, demonstrating that the commission to justice requires public commitment and accountability.

The commission is to structure economic relationships according to covenant principles: cancel debts, restore property, refuse usury against brothers, protect the poor from exploitation. Covenant community is to model justice and mercy in tangible economic structures.

## Commission 4: Honor God with Tithes and Offerings

### Malachi's Challenge: "Test Me in This"

The most explicit commission regarding material support of worship comes through Malachi: "Will man rob God? Yet you are robbing me. But you say, 'How have we robbed you?' In your tithes and contributions. You are cursed with a curse, for you are robbing me, the whole nation of you" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 8|Malachi 3:8-9]]).

The language is shocking: systematic withholding of tithes is robbing God. The people are under covenant curse as a direct result of this robbery. The commission is immediate and clear: "Bring the full tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in my house" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 10|Malachi 3:10]]).

The purpose of the tithe is practical: "that there may be food in my house." The Levites and priests depend on tithes for sustenance. Withholding tithes means the temple servants go hungry, worship is hindered, and God's house is neglected.

But God adds an extraordinary promise: "And thereby put me to the test, says the LORD of hosts, if I will not open the windows of heaven for you and pour down for you a blessing until there is no more need. I will rebuke the devourer for you, so that it will not destroy the fruits of your soil, and your vine in the field shall not fail to bear, says the LORD of hosts. Then all nations will call you blessed, for you will be a land of delight, says the LORD of hosts" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 10|Malachi 3:10-12]]).

This is the only place in Scripture where God invites His people to "test" Him. The challenge is: bring the full tithe and see if I don't pour out overwhelming blessing—agricultural abundance, protection from crop destruction, fruitful vineyards, and international recognition of blessing.

The commission is to trust God's provision by giving Him the first and best, to prioritize His house over personal accumulation, and to prove His faithfulness through obedient generosity.

### Nehemiah's Restoration of Tithe System

Nehemiah discovers that the tithe system has collapsed: "I also found out that the portions of the Levites had not been given to them, so that the Levites and the singers, who did the work, had fled each to his field" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 10|Nehemiah 13:10]]).

Without tithe support, the Levites abandoned temple service to work their own fields for survival. Worship suffers because God's servants are not sustained.

Nehemiah confronts the officials: "Why is the house of God forsaken?" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 11|Nehemiah 13:11]]). He gathers the Levites and singers back to their posts and reinstates the tithe: "And I appointed treasurers over the storehouses, Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, and Pedaiah of the Levites, and as their assistant Hanan the son of Zaccur, son of Mattaniah, for they were considered reliable, and their duty was to distribute to their brothers" ([[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 13|Nehemiah 13:13]]).

The commission involves both giving (bringing the tithe) and administering (faithful stewardship of what is given). Reliable leaders must distribute resources equitably, ensuring that all who serve in God's house are sustained.

### The Greater Principle: Honoring God with Firstfruits

The tithe commission reflects the broader principle of honoring God with firstfruits: "Honor the LORD with your wealth and with the firstfruits of all your produce; then your barns will be filled with plenty, and your vats will be bursting with wine" ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 3#Proverbs 3 . 9|Proverbs 3:9-10]]).

Giving God the first and best demonstrates that He, not wealth, is our security. It acknowledges that all we have comes from Him. It prioritizes His kingdom over our accumulation. It demonstrates faith that He will provide.

Jesus applies this principle: "But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 6#Matthew 6 . 33|Matthew 6:33]]). Paul echoes it: "The point is this: whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows bountifully will also reap bountifully. Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 9#2 Corinthians 9 . 6|2 Corinthians 9:6-7]]).

The commission is to honor God with material resources, demonstrating faith through generosity, supporting those who minister in His house, and trusting His promise to bless those who prioritize His kingdom.

## Commission 5: Prepare for the Coming Messenger

### Malachi's Climactic Announcement

The final and most significant commission is to prepare for the coming Messenger. Malachi announces: "Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me. And the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple; the messenger of the covenant in whom you delight, behold, he is coming, says the LORD of hosts" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 1|Malachi 3:1]]).

This prophecy has dual fulfillment:
1. **John the Baptist** is the messenger who prepares the way ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 10|Matthew 11:10]]; [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 1#Mark 1 . 2|Mark 1:2-3]])
2. **Jesus Christ** is "the Lord whom you seek" who comes suddenly to His temple

The commission is to watch expectantly, maintain faithfulness, and be ready when the Lord appears. But Malachi warns that His coming will be a time of purification: "But who can endure the day of his coming, and who can stand when he appears? For he is like a refiner's fire and like fullers' soap. He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, and he will purify the sons of Levi and refine them like gold and silver, and they will bring offerings in righteousness to the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 2|Malachi 3:2-3]]).

The Lord's coming will bring judgment and purification. The corrupt priesthood will be refined. Offerings will finally be made in righteousness. The commission is not merely to wait passively but to prepare through repentance, purification, and covenant faithfulness.

### The Promise of Elijah's Return

Malachi's final oracle announces: "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the LORD comes. And he will turn the hearts of fathers to their children and the hearts of children to their fathers, lest I come and strike the land with a decree of utter destruction" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 4#Malachi 4 . 5|Malachi 4:5-6]]).

This is the Old Testament's final promise: before the Day of the Lord, Elijah will come to prepare hearts, restore families, and prevent total destruction.

Jesus identifies John the Baptist as the fulfillment: "For all the Prophets and the Law prophesied until John, and if you are willing to accept it, he is Elijah who is to come" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 13|Matthew 11:13-14]]). The angel announces John's birth in Elijah's terms: "And he will turn many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God, and he will go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just, to make ready for the Lord a people prepared" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 16|Luke 1:16-17]]).

The commission is to recognize the messenger when he appears, respond to his call for repentance, and prepare for the Lord who follows. John's ministry will be to "make ready for the Lord a people prepared"—a people whose hearts are turned, whose sins are confessed, whose lives are reoriented toward God.

### Expectant Waiting with Faithful Action

The commission to prepare involves both waiting and working. It is not passive but active—maintaining faithfulness while watching expectantly. Those who fear the Lord gather to encourage one another: "Then those who feared the LORD spoke with one another. The LORD paid attention and heard them, and a book of remembrance was written before him of those who feared the LORD and esteemed his name. 'They shall be mine, says the LORD of hosts, in the day when I make up my treasured possession, and I will spare them as a man spares his son who serves him. Then once more you shall see the distinction between the righteous and the wicked, between one who serves God and one who does not serve him'" ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 3#Malachi 3 . 16|Malachi 3:16-18]]).

A remnant maintains faithfulness. They speak together, encouraging one another in covenant loyalty. God takes note—a "book of remembrance" is written. On the day of judgment, they will be God's "treasured possession," spared as a father spares an obedient son.

The commission is to be part of this faithful remnant, to encourage one another in covenant loyalty, to distinguish oneself through service to God, and to await confidently the coming vindication when righteousness will be rewarded and wickedness judged.

## Theological Summary: Commission as Preparation for Christ

The post-exilic commission differs from previous biblical commissions in being primarily preparatory rather than inauguratory. Adam was commissioned to fill and subdue the earth—an expansive, creative mandate. Abraham was commissioned to be a blessing to all nations—a redemptive, universal mandate. Moses was commissioned to lead Israel out of bondage—a liberating, formative mandate.

The post-exilic community receives a more modest commission: rebuild and maintain what was lost, preserve covenant distinctiveness, practice justice and mercy, honor God with tithes, and prepare for the coming Messenger. It is a commission of faithfulness in the interim, of maintaining covenant identity while awaiting ultimate fulfillment.

Yet this commission is no less crucial. The survival of the Jewish community through the post-exilic period ensures that when the Messiah comes, there is a people to receive Him, a covenant identity preserved, a Scripture maintained, a worship structure functioning, and a prophetic expectation alive.

Beale emphasizes that the post-exilic commission is to maintain the lampstand burning until the true Light arrives. The community cannot accomplish the Adamic task (fill the earth, expand God's presence, bring the nations to worship), but they can remain faithful, preserve the line through which Messiah will come, and sustain hope through Scripture, worship, and covenant life. The commission is not to be the solution but to prepare for the One who will be the solution.

Kline adds that the post-exilic commission reflects life in the "already but not yet" tension. The kingdom has intruded (temple rebuilt, worship restored), but consummation awaits. The commission is to live faithfully in this tension—maintaining worship, keeping covenant, practicing justice—while looking forward to the greater intrusion when the Lord will suddenly come to His temple. The commission creates and sustains expectant hope.

The post-exilic commission culminates in John the Baptist's ministry. He embodies all five elements: he calls people to prepare (rebuild hearts, not just buildings), to repent (maintain distinctiveness from sin), to bear fruit in keeping with repentance (practice justice), to share with those in need (honor God with resources), and to prepare for the Coming One (watch expectantly). His message: "Prepare the way of the Lord; make his paths straight" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 3|Matthew 3:3]]).

The post-exilic community's faithfulness to their commission ensures that when John appears in the wilderness, when Jesus walks in the Second Temple, when the Messiah rides into Jerusalem on a donkey, there is a people prepared—however imperfectly—to receive Him. The commission is fulfilled not in the post-exilic period itself but in creating the conditions for Christ's coming and the recognition of His identity by at least a faithful remnant.
