# 4.1.3 - Intrusion - The Flood and Tower of Babel

[[Redemptive-Historical Cycles/4.1 - The Delay - The Age of Preparation|← Back to 4.1]]

---

## Overview

**Biblical Context**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5]] through [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 9|Genesis 11:9]]

The third intrusion in redemptive history spans from the announcement of the Flood judgment to the scattering at Babel. This intrusion is unique because it contains **two major movements**: the Flood narrative (Genesis 6-9) and the Babel rebellion ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 1|Genesis 11:1-9]]), with the Table of Nations (Genesis 10) serving as a transition. Together, these narratives demonstrate humanity's persistent rebellion even after experiencing God's most catastrophic judgment to date.

**Central Themes**:
- Noah as new Adam, receiving the full Adamic commission
- The Flood as de-creation and re-creation
- God's covenant faithfulness despite human failure
- The Tower of Babel as the climax of post-Flood rebellion
- The scattering of nations as judgment that becomes the framework for redemption

### The Context: After Total Corruption

The previous intrusion ended with humanity in complete moral collapse. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5]] provides the devastating diagnosis: "The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." This is **total depravity** on full display.

The "sons of God" (Sethite line) had intermarried with the "daughters of men" (Cainite line), breaking down the holy separation between the covenant community and the world ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 1|Genesis 6:1-2]]). The earth was "filled with violence" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 11|Genesis 6:11-13]]). All flesh had corrupted their way. The chaos of sin had reached cosmic proportions.

Into this chaos, God announces judgment: "I will blot out man whom I have created from the face of the land" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 7|Genesis 6:7]]). But immediately, grace appears: "But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 8|Genesis 6:8]]). This is the pattern—judgment and grace, wrath and mercy, always intertwined.

### Noah as New Adam: The Corporate Head

**G. K. Beale** emphasizes that Noah functions as a **second Adam**, a corporate representative who receives the same commission Adam was given in Eden. The parallels are striking:

**Original Creation (Genesis 1-2)**:
- God creates the heavens and earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]])
- God separates waters from dry land ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 9|Genesis 1:9-10]])
- God fills creation with life ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 20|Genesis 1:20-25]])
- God commissions humanity to be fruitful and multiply ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])
- Adam placed in garden-temple to work and keep it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]])

**New Creation After Flood (Genesis 8-9)**:
- God makes wind blow over the waters ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1]]—echoing [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]])
- Waters recede and dry land appears ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 13|Genesis 8:13-14]]—echoing [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 9|Genesis 1:9-10]])
- Animals leave the ark to multiply ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 17|Genesis 8:17-19]])
- Noah commanded to be fruitful and multiply ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 1|Genesis 9:1, 7]]—echoing [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])
- Noah builds altar, offering sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 20|Genesis 8:20-21]]—priestly function)

The language is deliberately parallel. God is giving humanity a **second chance**. Through Noah, the human race will start over. Will Noah and his descendants succeed where Adam failed?

### The Flood as De-Creation and Re-Creation

**Meredith Kline** describes the Flood as a **de-creation event**—God unmakes the ordered cosmos of Genesis 1, returning the earth to the formless, watery chaos of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]]. This is not merely punishment but **cosmic judgment**:

**De-Creation (Genesis 7)**:
- The boundaries separating waters are dissolved ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 11|Genesis 7:11]]—"fountains of the deep burst forth, windows of heavens opened")
- Waters cover the earth completely ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 19|Genesis 7:19-20]]—"all the high mountains under heaven were covered")
- All flesh dies ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 21|Genesis 7:21-23]]—systematic reversal of Genesis 1:20-25)
- Only chaos remains, with the ark floating above ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 17|Genesis 7:17-18]])

**Re-Creation (Genesis 8)**:
- God "remembers" Noah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1]]—actively intervenes)
- The Spirit/wind moves over the waters ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1]]—רוּחַ, *ruach*, same word as [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 2|Genesis 1:2]])
- Waters recede, dry land appears ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 3|Genesis 8:3-5]])
- Life emerges from the ark ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 15|Genesis 8:15-19]])
- God establishes covenant ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 8|Genesis 9:8-17]])

This pattern—**chaos → Spirit hovering → dry land → life → commission**—is the same as Genesis 1. The Flood is both judgment on the old creation and the birth pangs of new creation. It demonstrates that God's redemptive purposes cannot be thwarted. Even when sin brings cosmic chaos, God creates anew.

### The Noahic Covenant: Grace and Restraint

After the Flood, God establishes a **covenant with Noah** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 8|Genesis 9:8-17]]). This covenant has several critical features:

1. **Universal Scope**: Made with "all flesh" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 15|Genesis 9:15-16]])—animals and humans, covenant people and nations
2. **Unconditional Promise**: God pledges never again to destroy the earth by flood ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 11|Genesis 9:11]])
3. **Sign of the Rainbow**: Visible reminder of God's covenant faithfulness ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 12|Genesis 9:12-17]])
4. **Preservation of Creation Order**: Seasons will continue ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 22|Genesis 8:22]])—natural law remains stable
5. **Restraint of Violence**: Capital punishment instituted to curb violence ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 5|Genesis 9:5-6]])

**Kline's Insight**: This is the covenant of **Common Grace**. God commits to preserving the natural order despite human sin. The world will not be destroyed again until the final judgment ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 7|2 Peter 3:7]]—by fire, not water). This creates the stable platform on which redemptive history unfolds.

The rainbow is particularly significant. In ancient Near Eastern literature, the bow is a weapon of divine warfare. God "hangs up His bow," as it were, pointing it away from earth toward the heavens—a sign that His war against humanity has been stayed. Yet the bow remains, a reminder that judgment is postponed, not cancelled.

### The Fivefold Pattern in the Flood Narrative

This intrusion follows the redemptive-historical cycle:

1. **Chaos** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 17|Genesis 7:17-24]]): The Flood—waters cover the earth, all flesh dies, de-creation complete
2. **Creation** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1-5]]): God remembers Noah, wind blows, waters recede, dry land appears
3. **Commission** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 1|Genesis 9:1-7]]): Fivefold Adamic mandate restated—be fruitful, multiply, fill earth, subdue, have dominion
4. **Sin** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 20|Genesis 9:20-27]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 1|Genesis 11:1-4]]): Noah's drunkenness and Ham's sin, followed by Babel rebellion
5. **Judgment** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 7|Genesis 11:7-9]]): Confusion of languages, scattering of nations

The pattern is clear: Even after the most catastrophic judgment, even after a complete reset, **sin returns**. Noah himself falls into drunkenness ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 21|Genesis 9:21]]). Ham dishonors his father ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 22|Genesis 9:22]]). Canaan is cursed ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 25|Genesis 9:25-27]]). And within just a few generations, humanity is united in rebellion at Babel.

### The Tower of Babel: Corporate Rebellion

The Babel narrative ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 1|Genesis 11:1-9]]) is the climax of post-Flood rebellion. After receiving the commission to "fill the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 1|Genesis 9:1]]), humanity instead gathers in one place and declares: "Come, let us build ourselves a city and a tower with its top in the heavens, and let us make a name for ourselves, lest we be dispersed over the face of the whole earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 4|Genesis 11:4]]).

**Key Elements of the Rebellion**:

1. **Defiance of God's Command**: God said "fill the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 1|Genesis 9:1]]); humanity says "lest we be dispersed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 4|Genesis 11:4]])
2. **Self-Glorification**: "Let us make a name for ourselves"—humanity seeks autonomy and fame apart from God
3. **Usurping God's Authority**: "A tower with its top in the heavens"—attempt to ascend to divine status (echoing the serpent's lie in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 5|Genesis 3:5]]—"you will be like God")
4. **Unity in Rebellion**: One language, one purpose, collective idolatry

**Beale's Framework**: The tower represents an **anti-temple**. Instead of expanding God's presence from the garden-temple outward (as Adam was commissioned), Babel centralizes human power in defiance of God. It is an attempt to build heaven on earth through human achievement rather than divine grace.

The irony is thick: Humanity builds a tower to reach heaven, but God must "come down" to see it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 5|Genesis 11:5]]). Their greatest achievement is barely visible from God's perspective. This mocks the pretensions of human autonomy.

### The Scattering: Judgment that Becomes Redemption

God's response to Babel is swift: "Come, let us go down and there confuse their language, so that they may not understand one another's speech" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 7|Genesis 11:7]]). The result: "So the LORD dispersed them from there over the face of all the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 8|Genesis 11:8]]).

This judgment is **both punitive and redemptive**:

**Punitive**: Humanity wanted unity in rebellion; God gives them division. They wanted one name; God scatters them into many nations with many languages. They refused to fill the earth; God forces the issue.

**Redemptive**: The scattering creates the **framework for redemption**. The very next narrative is the call of Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1-3]]), through whom "all the families of the earth shall be blessed." The nations scattered at Babel are the nations Abraham's seed will bless.

The pattern continues throughout Scripture:
- Israel scattered among nations but promised restoration ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 1|Deuteronomy 30:1-5]])
- Gospel goes to the nations ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 8|Acts 1:8]])
- Pentecost reverses Babel ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 4|Acts 2:4-11]]—many languages, one message)
- New creation gathers all nations ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]])

### Why This Intrusion Fails

Despite the fresh start, this intrusion **fails to break the cycle of sin**:

- **Noah falls into drunkenness** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 21|Genesis 9:21]])—the new Adam proves sinful
- **Ham dishonors his father** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 22|Genesis 9:22]])—family brokenness continues
- **Humanity rebels at Babel** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 4|Genesis 11:4]])—corporate sin escalates again
- **Nations scattered in judgment** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 8|Genesis 11:8]])—exile pattern repeats

The Flood did not change the human heart. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 21|Genesis 8:21]] makes this explicit: "The intention of man's heart is evil from his youth." Even after de-creation and re-creation, humanity remains fallen. The problem is not environmental (Eden vs. post-Flood world) but **internal**—the human heart is corrupt.

This points forward to the need for:
- **Heart transformation** (not just external reset): [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]]—"A new heart I will give you"
- **Perfect obedience** (not just common grace): [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 19|Romans 5:19]]—Christ's active obedience
- **Death of the old Adam** (not just preservation): [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 6|Romans 6:6]]—"Our old self was crucified with him"
- **New creation in Christ** (not just re-creation): [[Readable Bible/46 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]—"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation"

### Theological Significance

#### The Remnant Pattern Intensifies

From Abel to Seth to Noah, the **remnant theme** grows stronger. Each intrusion shows God preserving a faithful few through whom the Seed promise continues. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 23|Genesis 7:23]] makes it explicit: "Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark."

This establishes the pattern that will define redemptive history:
- **Eight persons** saved through the Flood ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 20|1 Peter 3:20]])
- **Abraham alone** called from among the nations ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 1|Genesis 12:1]])
- **Remnant of Israel** preserved after exile ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 10#Isaiah 10 . 20|Isaiah 10:20-22]])
- **Christ alone** survives God's wrath, becomes source of new humanity ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45-49]])

#### Common Grace and Eschatological Patience

The Noahic covenant establishes the **common grace period**—the time between the Fall and the final judgment during which God restrains His wrath. [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]] explains the purpose: "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance."

The rainbow is a **visible reminder** of God's patience. Every storm that does not destroy the world is a testimony to God's covenant faithfulness. Common grace provides the stable platform for redemptive grace to work. The natural order continues—seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 22|Genesis 8:22]])—so that the gospel can go forth to the nations.

#### The Problem is Internal, Not External

The Flood demonstrates that **changing circumstances does not change hearts**. Humanity was given:
- A fresh start (new creation after Flood)
- Personal knowledge of God's judgment (they witnessed the Flood's devastation)
- Direct revelation from Noah (a righteous preacher, [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 2#2 Peter 2 . 5|2 Peter 2:5]])
- Covenant promises (rainbow, seasons, divine protection)

Yet within a few generations, rebellion returns. This proves that the solution to the human problem must be **internal transformation**, not external reformation. The prophets will later proclaim this:
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]—"I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts"
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26]]—"I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you"
- [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 3|John 3:3]]—"Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God"

Only the **new birth** through the Spirit can solve the problem displayed at Babel.

### Christological Fulfillment

#### Christ as the True Noah

Where Noah succeeded temporarily but ultimately failed, **Christ succeeds perfectly**:

**Noah's Righteousness**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 9|Genesis 6:9]]—"Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generation. Noah walked with God."

**Christ's Perfect Righteousness**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]—"Holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners"

**Noah Falls**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 21|Genesis 9:21]]—Noah becomes drunk, lies naked

**Christ Never Falls**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]—"Tempted as we are, yet without sin"

**Noah's Ark Saves Eight**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 7|Genesis 7:7]]

**Christ Saves Multitudes**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]—"A great multitude that no one could number, from every nation"

#### Christ as the Ark of Salvation

[[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 20|1 Peter 3:20-21]] explicitly connects the ark to baptism and Christ's resurrection: "God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water. Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you... through the resurrection of Jesus Christ."

**Typology**:
- The ark had **one door** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 16|Genesis 6:16]]) → Christ is the **only way** ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]])
- God **shut Noah in** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 16|Genesis 7:16]]) → No one can snatch us from Christ ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 28|John 10:28-29]])
- The ark **saves through water** → Christ saves through death and resurrection ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 3|Romans 6:3-4]])
- **Only those in the ark survive** → Only those in Christ escape wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]])

#### Pentecost Reverses Babel

At Babel, God confused languages and scattered humanity ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 7|Genesis 11:7-9]]). At Pentecost, the Spirit enables the apostles to speak in multiple languages, and people from every nation hear the gospel in their own tongue ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 4|Acts 2:4-11]]).

**Babel**: One language → Many languages (judgment, scattering)

**Pentecost**: Many languages → One gospel (grace, gathering)

**New Creation**: Many nations → One people ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]—"from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages")

The scattering at Babel creates the **nations that will be gathered** at Pentecost and consummated in the new creation. Judgment becomes the framework for redemption.

### Connection to Abraham

The next intrusion (4.1.4—Call of Abraham) emerges directly from the chaos of Babel. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 9|Genesis 11:9]] ends with scattering: "Therefore its name was called Babel, because there the LORD confused the language of all the earth. And from there the LORD dispersed them over the face of all the earth."

The very next narrative introduces Abram's family in Ur of the Chaldeans ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 31|Genesis 11:31]]), a city descended from the Babel dispersion. God calls Abraham **out of the scattered nations** to become the source of blessing for **all the families of the earth** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]).

The pattern:
1. **Babel scatters humanity** (judgment)
2. **Abraham called from the nations** (grace)
3. **Abraham's seed blesses the nations** (redemption)

This establishes the trajectory: The nations scattered in judgment will be gathered through the seed of Abraham, who is ultimately Christ.

## Summary: The Pattern Intensifies

The Flood and Babel narrative reveals several critical truths:

- **Human nature is unchanged by external circumstances**—even a complete reset fails to produce lasting righteousness
- **God's covenant faithfulness persists despite human failure**—the rainbow testifies to divine patience
- **Judgment and grace are intertwined**—the Flood destroys but preserves a remnant; Babel scatters but creates the framework for redemption
- **Corporate Adam figures fail**—Noah, like Adam, falls into sin, pointing to the need for a perfect Adam
- **De-creation precedes re-creation**—God must unmake the broken world before making all things new
- **The remnant theme intensifies**—from many, God saves few; from few, He will raise many

This intrusion, like those before it, **fails to break the cycle** of sin and judgment. But it establishes crucial patterns:
- The ark as place of salvation (fulfilled in Christ)
- The covenant of common grace (the platform for redemptive history)
- The scattering of nations (the mission field for the gospel)
- The remnant preserved through water (baptism into Christ)

Only Jesus Christ, the ultimate Noah and Seed of Abraham, will succeed where all previous corporate Adams have failed. He will pass through the waters of judgment, emerge victorious, and establish an eternal kingdom that cannot be shaken.
