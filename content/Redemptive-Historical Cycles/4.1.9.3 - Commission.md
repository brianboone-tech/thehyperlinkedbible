## Overview: Preparation for Conquest—Laws for Life in the Land

The Commission phase of the 38-year wilderness wandering demonstrates God's commitment to prepare the second generation for their corporate Adamic calling. Even as the condemned first generation dies, God gives extensive **laws for life in the land**, commissions **Joshua as the new leader**, and instructs Israel in their responsibility to **possess, subdue, and sanctify** Canaan.

This phase reveals that God's commission to Israel as corporate Adam continues despite their parents' failure. The fivefold Adamic mandate—be fruitful, multiply, fill, subdue, have dominion—is restated and refined throughout Leviticus, Numbers, and especially Deuteronomy. The new generation is being prepared to do what their parents refused: **enter God's rest** and expand His kingdom-temple presence throughout the land.

G.K. Beale notes that the laws given during this period are **Eden-restoration** laws—they envision Israel living in Canaan as a new garden-sanctuary, with God's presence dwelling among them, His law structuring their life, and His blessing flowing through them to the nations. This is the commission that the church will ultimately fulfill in Christ.

---

## 1. Laws for When You Enter the Land

Immediately after pronouncing judgment, God begins giving laws that assume future fulfillment:

"Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, When you come into the land you are to inhabit, which I am giving you, and you offer to the LORD from the herd or from the flock a food offering or a burnt offering or a sacrifice..." ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 15#Numbers 15 . 2|Numbers 15:2-3]]).

### Forward-Looking Grace

This is remarkable—God has just sentenced the generation to die in the wilderness, yet He immediately affirms: "When you come into the land." Not **if** but **when**. The promise is certain. This demonstrates:

- **God's faithfulness** despite human failure
- **Covenant continuity** across generations
- **Hope** for the next generation

### Categories of Laws

Throughout Numbers 15-36 and Deuteronomy, God gives laws in multiple categories:

#### Worship and Sacrifice

Laws for offerings ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 15#Numbers 15 . 3|Numbers 15:3-16]]), including grain offerings, drink offerings, and regulations for both Israelites and sojourners. These assume **settled agricultural life** in the land, with crops to harvest and flocks to tend.

The **Day of Atonement** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 1|Leviticus 16]]) envisions annual cleansing of the tabernacle/temple. The **Sabbath year** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 25#Leviticus 25 . 1|Leviticus 25:1-7]]) and **Jubilee** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 25#Leviticus 25 . 8|Leviticus 25:8-17]]) regulate land rest and redemption.

#### Inheritance and Property

Laws for daughters inheriting when there are no sons ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 1|Numbers 27:1-11]]), tribal land retention ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 36#Numbers 36 . 1|Numbers 36:1-13]]), and cities for Levites ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 35#Numbers 35 . 1|Numbers 35:1-8]]) demonstrate God's commitment to **just distribution** of the land.

Each tribe receives an **inheritance** (*nachalah*)—permanent possession tied to God's covenant promise. The land is holy, belonging to God, and Israel holds it in trust.

#### Justice and Cities of Refuge

The establishment of **cities of refuge** ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 35#Numbers 35 . 9|Numbers 35:9-34]]) provides asylum for those who commit unintentional manslaughter, demonstrating God's concern for **justice tempered with mercy**.

Laws for witnesses ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 19#Deuteronomy 19 . 15|Deuteronomy 19:15]]), false testimony, and judicial procedures ensure **righteous judgment**.

#### Warfare and Conquest

God gives specific instructions for **conquest** ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 1|Deuteronomy 7:1-5]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 20#Deuteronomy 20 . 1|20:1-20]]): destroy the Canaanites, avoid intermarriage, tear down idols. This is not ethnic cleansing but **herem** (devoted destruction)—judgment on nations whose sin has reached full measure ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 16|Genesis 15:16]]).

Israel is commissioned as God's instrument of judgment while simultaneously being warned not to adopt Canaanite practices.

#### Holiness and Separation

Extensive laws regulate **cleanness and uncleanness** ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 11#Leviticus 11 . 1|Leviticus 11-15]]), dietary restrictions, sexual ethics ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 18#Leviticus 18 . 1|Leviticus 18]]), and social justice ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 1|Leviticus 19]]).

The repeated refrain: "You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy" ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 19#Leviticus 19 . 2|Leviticus 19:2]]). Israel's commission is to be **distinct** from the nations, reflecting God's character.

## 2. Deuteronomy: Moses' Final Commission to the New Generation

The entire book of Deuteronomy functions as Moses' **commissioning address** to the second generation on the plains of Moab. Its structure follows ancient Near Eastern covenant/treaty form:

### Historical Prologue (Deuteronomy 1-4)

Moses recounts Israel's journey from Egypt to Moab, emphasizing:
- God's faithfulness in delivering from Egypt ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 30|Deuteronomy 1:30]])
- The catastrophic failure at Kadesh ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 26|Deuteronomy 1:26-40]])
- God's sustaining grace through forty years ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 2#Deuteronomy 2 . 7|Deuteronomy 2:7]])
- Recent victories over Sihon and Og ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 2#Deuteronomy 2 . 31|Deuteronomy 2:31-3:11]])

This historical review demonstrates **precedent**: God has been faithful; trust Him now.

### Covenant Stipulations (Deuteronomy 5-26)

Moses restates the **Ten Commandments** ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 5#Deuteronomy 5 . 1|Deuteronomy 5]]) and expounds the law extensively, emphasizing:

**Love as Foundation**: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 4|Deuteronomy 6:4-5]]). This **Shema** becomes Israel's central confession.

**Teaching the Next Generation**: "And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 6|Deuteronomy 6:6-7]]). Generational transmission of covenant faith is essential.

**Warning Against Idolatry**: Repeated warnings not to worship other gods ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 14|Deuteronomy 6:14]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 25|7:25-26]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 2|12:2-3]]), since idolatry is **covenant treason**.

**Justice and Compassion**: Laws protecting the widow, orphan, and sojourner ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 24#Deuteronomy 24 . 17|Deuteronomy 24:17-22]]); honest weights and measures ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 25#Deuteronomy 25 . 13|Deuteronomy 25:13-16]]); just treatment of workers ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 24#Deuteronomy 24 . 14|Deuteronomy 24:14-15]]).

### Blessings and Curses (Deuteronomy 27-28)

Moses outlines the **consequences** of obedience and disobedience:

**Blessings for Obedience** ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 1|Deuteronomy 28:1-14]]): Fertility, prosperity, victory, abundance—Eden blessings restored.

**Curses for Disobedience** ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 15|Deuteronomy 28:15-68]]): Drought, defeat, disease, exile—return to chaos and ultimately exile from the land ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 64|Deuteronomy 28:64]]).

This prophetically anticipates the Babylonian exile, demonstrating that covenant **requires** faithfulness.

### Covenant Renewal (Deuteronomy 29-30)

Moses calls the new generation to **renew the covenant** in Moab ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 29#Deuteronomy 29 . 1|Deuteronomy 29:1]]). This is a **second covenant** alongside Sinai, binding the new generation who were children at Sinai.

The choice is stark: "I call heaven and earth to witness against you today, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and curse. Therefore choose life, that you and your offspring may live" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 19|Deuteronomy 30:19]]).

## 3. Commissioning Joshua: Leadership Transition

A central element of the commission is the **transfer of leadership** from Moses to Joshua:

### Public Commissioning

"So Moses continued to speak these words to all Israel. And he said to them, 'I am 120 years old today. I am no longer able to go out and come in. The LORD has said to me, "You shall not go over this Jordan." The LORD your God himself will go over before you... Joshua will go over at your head, as the LORD has spoken'" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 31#Deuteronomy 31 . 1|Deuteronomy 31:1-3]]).

Moses publicly transfers authority, emphasizing:
- **God goes before** you—victory is certain
- **Joshua will lead**—human leadership continues
- **Do not fear**—God's presence removes terror

### Divine Commissioning

God Himself commissions Joshua directly:

"And the LORD commissioned Joshua the son of Nun and said, 'Be strong and courageous, for you shall bring the people of Israel into the land that I swore to give them. I will be with you'" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 31#Deuteronomy 31 . 23|Deuteronomy 31:23]]).

The promise "I will be with you" is foundational. Joshua's qualification is not personal ability but **divine presence**. This promise is reiterated in Joshua 1:

"Just as I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not leave you or forsake you. Be strong and courageous, for you shall cause this people to inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 5|Joshua 1:5-6]]).

### The Commission to Be Courageous

Three times God commands Joshua: "Be strong and courageous" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 6|Joshua 1:6]], [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 7|7]], [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 9|9]]). This is not **self-confidence** but **God-confidence**, trusting that Yahweh will fulfill His promise.

Joshua must be courageous to:
- Lead military conquest against fortified cities
- Distribute the land justly
- Maintain covenant faithfulness among the people
- Complete what Moses could not

## 4. The Corporate Adamic Commission Renewed

The laws and Moses' addresses renew Israel's **corporate Adamic calling**. The five elements of the Genesis mandate are reaffirmed:

### 1. Be Fruitful

"The LORD will make you abound in prosperity, in the fruit of your womb and in the fruit of your livestock and in the fruit of your ground, within the land that the LORD swore to your fathers to give you" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 28#Deuteronomy 28 . 11|Deuteronomy 28:11]]).

Population growth in the land is a covenant blessing.

### 2. Multiply

"And the LORD your God will make you abundantly prosperous... if you obey the voice of the LORD your God" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 9|Deuteronomy 30:9]]).

Israel is to become numerous, fulfilling God's promise to Abraham ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 5|Genesis 15:5]]).

### 3. Fill the Land

"When the LORD your God brings you into the land that he swore to your fathers... to give you—with great and good cities that you did not build, and houses full of all good things that you did not fill... then take care lest you forget the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 10|Deuteronomy 6:10-12]]).

Israel is to **inhabit** the land fully, settling every region.

### 4. Subdue

"You shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you and destroy all their figured stones and destroy all their metal images and demolish all their high places. And you shall take possession of the land and settle in it" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 33#Numbers 33 . 52|Numbers 33:52-53]]).

Israel must **conquer** the Canaanites, removing idolatry and establishing God's rule.

### 5. Have Dominion / Exercise Rule

Israel's dominion is to be **theocratic**—God ruling through them. The king, when appointed, must write a copy of the law and read it daily ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 17#Deuteronomy 17 . 18|Deuteronomy 17:18-19]]), demonstrating that even human kingship is **under God's law**.

The ultimate purpose: Israel as a **kingdom of priests** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), mediating God's presence to the nations.

## 5. The Land as New Eden

The laws envision Canaan as **Eden restored**:

### Sabbath Rest

The land itself must rest every seventh year ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 25#Leviticus 25 . 4|Leviticus 25:4]]), reflecting the Sabbath principle from creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 2|Genesis 2:2-3]]).

### Sanctuary Presence

The tabernacle (later temple) functions as the **holy of holies**, God's dwelling among His people. The land radiates outward in concentric circles of holiness: Most Holy Place → Holy Place → Tabernacle Court → Camp → Land → Nations.

### Abundance and Blessing

If Israel obeys, God promises:

"If you walk in my statutes and observe my commandments and do them, then I will give you your rains in their season, and the land shall yield its increase, and the trees of the field shall yield their fruit. Your threshing shall last to the time of the grape harvest, and the grape harvest shall last to the time for sowing. And you shall eat your bread to the full and dwell in your land securely" ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 26#Leviticus 26 . 3|Leviticus 26:3-5]]).

This is **Eden-level abundance**—perpetual harvest, safety, satisfaction.

## 6. Christological Fulfillment

The Commission phase points to Christ and the church:

### Christ as the True Joshua

Jesus (Greek form of "Joshua"—"Yahweh saves") is the ultimate leader who brings God's people into rest. Where Joshua led into Canaan, Jesus leads into **eternal rest** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8-11]]).

### The Church's Great Commission

Jesus renews the Adamic mandate for the church:

"All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]]).

Elements parallel Israel's commission:
- **Authority**: Christ has dominion
- **Go/Fill**: Make disciples of all nations
- **Subdue**: Through gospel proclamation, not military conquest
- **Teach**: Pass on God's commands
- **Presence**: "I am with you always"

### The Law Fulfilled in Christ

The laws given to Israel find their fulfillment in Christ:

"Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 17|Matthew 5:17]]).

Christ **perfectly obeys** the law ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]), **fulfills** its typology, and establishes the **new covenant** ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 8|Hebrews 8:8-13]]).

---

## Conclusion

The Commission phase demonstrates that God's purposes for His people continue despite their rebellion. The laws for the land, Moses' Deuteronomic addresses, and Joshua's commissioning all affirm that the second generation **will** enter, **will** possess, and **will** live as God's people in the land of promise.

This commission renews the corporate Adamic mandate: be fruitful, multiply, fill, subdue, have dominion—but now with extensive legal and covenantal structure. Israel is to be Eden restored, a kingdom of priests, a light to the nations.

Ultimately, this points to Christ, the true Israel who fulfills the law perfectly, and to the church, commissioned to make disciples of all nations. The rest that Israel glimpsed in Canaan finds its ultimate fulfillment in the eternal rest Christ provides. The commission to subdue and have dominion is renewed as the gospel advances to the ends of the earth, bringing all nations under the lordship of Christ.
