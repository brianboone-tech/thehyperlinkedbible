# 4.1.8.3 - Commission

## Overview: Command to Spy Out and Possess the Land

The Commission phase ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 1|Numbers 13:1-25]]) presents God's command to send spies into Canaan—not to determine **whether** they should enter, but to see **how** they will conquer it. This is Israel's **Adamic commission** renewed: to enter God's rest-land, subdue enemies, and expand God's temple-presence throughout Canaan.

G.K. Beale emphasizes that the spying mission is part of Israel's corporate Adam role. Just as Adam was to subdue the earth ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]]), Israel is to subdue Canaan. Just as Adam was to guard the garden from the serpent, Israel is to drive out the Canaanites and their idolatry.

The land itself is presented as **new Eden**—flowing with milk and honey, abundant fruit, God's promised inheritance. The commission is clear: "Go up and take possession, as the LORD, the God of your fathers, has told you. Do not fear or be dismayed" ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 21|Deuteronomy 1:21]]).

---

## 1. God's Command to Spy Out the Land

"The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 'Send men to spy out the land of Canaan, which I am giving to the people of Israel. From each tribe of their fathers you shall send a man, every one a chief among them'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 1|Numbers 13:1-2]]).

**Key phrase**: "which I am **giving**"—the land is God's gift, already determined. The spies are sent to **scout**, not to **decide**.

Twelve leaders are selected, one from each tribe ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 3|Numbers 13:3-16]]), including Caleb (Judah) and Hoshea/Joshua (Ephraim).

---

## 2. Moses' Instructions: Assess for Conquest

"Moses sent them to spy out the land of Canaan and said to them, 'Go up into the Negeb and go up into the hill country, and see what the land is, and whether the people who dwell in it are strong or weak, whether they are few or many, and whether the land that they dwell in is good or bad, and whether the cities that they dwell in are camps or strongholds, and whether the land is rich or poor, and whether there are trees in it or not. Be of good courage and bring some of the fruit of the land'" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 17|Numbers 13:17-20]]).

Moses asks for **intelligence gathering**:
- Terrain: hill country vs. plains
- Population: strong/weak, few/many
- Fortifications: camps vs. strongholds
- Land quality: good/bad, rich/poor, forested
- Evidence: bring back fruit

This is **strategic reconnaissance** for a military campaign God has already ordained to succeed.

---

## 3. The Spies' Reconnaissance: 40 Days

"So they went up and spied out the land from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob, near Lebo-hamath... And they came to the Valley of Eshcol and cut down from there a branch with a single cluster of grapes, and they carried it on a pole between two of them; they also brought some pomegranates and figs" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 21|Numbers 13:21-23]]).

The **40 days** of spying will later become 40 years of wandering—one year for each day ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 34|Numbers 14:34]]).

The **fruit** they bring back—grapes so large two men must carry them, pomegranates, figs—demonstrates the land's Edenic abundance. This is creation as God intended, a land blessed beyond measure.

---

## 4. The Adamic Commission Renewed

Israel's commission to possess Canaan fulfills all five elements of Adam's original mandate:

**Be fruitful and multiply**: Israel has grown from 70 ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 46#Genesis 46 . 27|Genesis 46:27]]) to over 600,000 men ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 1#Numbers 1 . 46|Numbers 1:46]])

**Subdue and rule**: "Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you" ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 1#Joshua 1 . 3|Joshua 1:3]])

**Serve and guard**: Israel is to worship Yahweh alone, guarding against Canaanite idolatry

**Expand the garden-temple**: The tabernacle will be established in Canaan, God's presence filling the land

**Reflect God's image**: As a holy nation ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]), Israel displays God's character to the nations

The land is presented as **new Eden**: abundant fruit, flowing with milk and honey, guarded by formidable enemies (like cherubim guarding Eden's entrance).

---

## 5. The Land as New Eden

The description emphasizes Edenic parallels:

**Abundance**: "It flows with milk and honey, and this is its fruit" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 27|Numbers 13:27]])

**Fruit imagery**: Grapes, pomegranates, figs—the same fruits associated with blessing in Eden and throughout Scripture

**God's promise**: "a land flowing with milk and honey" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 8|Exodus 3:8]])—language of paradise restored

**Guardians**: The Anakim and fortified cities function like the cherubim with flaming sword—obstacles that require **faith in God** to overcome

Beale notes that Canaan is an **expansion of Eden**, the next stage in God's plan to fill the earth with His glory through His image-bearing people.

---

## 6. The Test of Faith

The commission tests whether Israel will trust God's promise over human calculation. God has **already promised victory**:

- "I will give it to you to possess" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 2|Numbers 13:2]])
- "I am with you" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 12|Exodus 3:12]])
- "I will drive out the Canaanites" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 2|Exodus 33:2]])

The question is not **Can we conquer?** but **Will we trust God?**

This parallels Adam and Eve's test in Eden—will they trust God's word about the tree, or will they trust the serpent's lie? Israel faces the same choice: trust God's promise about the land, or trust the spies' fearful assessment.

---

## 7. Theological Themes: Commission and Trust

### God's Sovereign Gift

The land is given by divine decree: "which I am **giving**" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 13#Numbers 13 . 2|Numbers 13:2]]). Israel's task is to **receive** what God gives, not to **achieve** what seems impossible.

This establishes the principle that salvation is gift, not achievement. Paul: "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]]).

### Corporate Responsibility

All twelve tribes send representatives—the entire nation is implicated in the mission. When ten spies bring a bad report, the whole community suffers.

This demonstrates corporate solidarity in Adam (and in Christ). One man's sin affects all; one man's obedience saves all ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12-21]]).

### Faith as Prerequisite for Rest

Entering the land requires **faith**. Physical ability, military strategy, and numerical superiority are irrelevant—what matters is trust in God's promise.

Hebrews applies this directly: "And to whom did he swear that they would not enter his rest, but to those who were disobedient? So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 18|Hebrews 3:18-19]]).

---

## 8. Kline's Framework: Commission to Consummate the Age

The commission to possess Canaan is a commission to **transition from Common Grace to Consummation**—from wilderness wandering to Sabbath rest.

The land represents **eschatological rest**, the place where God's people dwell with Him in peace, subduing enemies, expanding His kingdom. Entering the land would have been an **upper-register breakthrough**, a foretaste of the final rest.

Israel's failure demonstrates that Common Grace persists when faith fails. The opportunity for consummation is offered but not forced—human response determines whether intrusion leads to blessing or judgment.

---

## 9. Christological Fulfillment: The Greater Joshua

Jesus is the **true Israel** who obeys the commission perfectly:

**Jesus enters rest**: Where Israel refused to enter Canaan, Jesus enters the true Promised Land—heaven itself ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]])

**Jesus conquers enemies**: Through the cross, Jesus defeats sin, death, and Satan ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]])

**Jesus leads God's people**: As the "founder and perfecter of our faith" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]]), Jesus brings many sons to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]])

The **Great Commission** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]]) is the church's Adamic mandate: go into all the world, subdue nations for Christ, expand God's kingdom, reflect His image.

---

## Conclusion: Commission Offered, Commission Refused

The Commission phase presents Israel with the climactic opportunity of the Exodus—to enter God's rest, possess the land, and become the new Eden people. God's promise is clear, the land is ready, the commission is given.

But commission requires faith. In the next phase, Israel will fail the test spectacularly, choosing fear over faith, human assessment over divine promise. The commission will be refused, and a generation will die in the wilderness.

Yet God's purposes cannot be thwarted. A second generation will enter under Joshua. And ultimately, the true Joshua—Jesus—will lead God's people into eternal rest, fulfilling the commission perfectly and inviting all who believe to enter with Him.

The call to the church: "Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 11|Hebrews 4:11]]).
