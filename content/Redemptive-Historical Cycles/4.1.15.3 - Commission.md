##### 4.1.15.3 - Commission

**Wisdom's Call: Fear the LORD, Pursue Wisdom, Worship Rightly**

Having established creation's order despite chaos, the Wisdom Literature issues its commission: humanity is called to fear the LORD, pursue wisdom, keep God's commandments, and worship rightly. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 7|Proverbs 1:7]] declares the foundational principle: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; fools despise wisdom and instruction." This fear is not terror but reverent awe, covenant loyalty, and submission to divine authority. The Psalter summons Israel to worship, to ascend God's holy hill with clean hands and pure heart ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 24#Psalm 24 . 1|Psalm 24]]). Even [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 1#Ecclesiastes 1 . 1|Ecclesiastes]], after surveying life's vanity, concludes with a clear commission: "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man" ([[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 13|Ecclesiastes 12:13]]).

**Wisdom's Public Invitation**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 20|Proverbs 1:20-33]] personifies Wisdom crying aloud in the streets, raising her voice in the public squares, calling from the city gates. This is a universal invitation, not whispered to an elite few but proclaimed to all. Wisdom offers her counsel ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 23|verse 23]]), promising safety and freedom from fear to those who listen ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 33|verse 33]]).

The invitation intensifies in [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 1|Proverbs 8-9]]. After declaring her presence at creation ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 22|8:22-31]]), Wisdom summons humanity: "And now, O sons, listen to me: blessed are those who keep my ways" ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 32|8:32]]). [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 35|Proverbs 8:35-36]] presents the stakes clearly: "For whoever finds me finds life and obtains favor from the LORD, but he who fails to find me injures himself; all who hate me love death."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 1|Proverbs 9:1-6]] depicts Wisdom building her house, preparing her banquet, and sending out her young women to call from the highest places: "Whoever is simple, let him turn in here!... Come, eat of my bread and drink of the wine I have mixed. Leave your simple ways, and live, and walk in the way of insight." This is covenant table fellowship, life-giving communion with Wisdom.

Beale notes the Christological fulfillment: Jesus is Wisdom incarnate who came preaching publicly ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28-30]]), inviting all who are weary to come to Him. He offers living water ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 14|John 4:14]]), bread of life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 35|John 6:35]]), and promises that those who come to Him will never hunger or thirst ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 35|verse 35]]). The Last Supper ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 26|Matthew 26:26-28]]) is the ultimate Wisdom banquet, giving His body and blood as covenant meal.

**The Fear of the LORD**

The phrase "fear of the LORD" appears throughout Wisdom Literature as the governing principle. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 1#Proverbs 1 . 7|Proverbs 1:7]] makes it the "beginning" (*reshit*) of knowledge—not merely a starting point to be surpassed, but the foundational principle that governs all true understanding. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 9#Proverbs 9 . 10|Proverbs 9:10]] repeats: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is insight."

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 2#Proverbs 2 . 1|Proverbs 2:1-5]] describes pursuing wisdom like searching for hidden treasure; the result is understanding the fear of the LORD and finding the knowledge of God. This fear is personal relationship, not abstract philosophy. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 3#Proverbs 3 . 7|Proverbs 3:7]] commands: "Be not wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD, and turn away from evil." Fearing God means rejecting autonomous human reason and submitting to divine revelation.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 26|Proverbs 14:26-27]] presents the fear of the LORD as strong confidence, a secure fortress, and a fountain of life. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 15#Proverbs 15 . 33|Proverbs 15:33]] declares that "the fear of the LORD is instruction in wisdom, and humility comes before honor." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 19#Proverbs 19 . 23|Proverbs 19:23]] promises: "The fear of the LORD leads to life, and whoever has it rests satisfied; he will not be visited by harm."

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 111#Psalm 111 . 10|Psalm 111:10]] echoes Proverbs: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; all those who practice it have a good understanding." [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 25#Psalm 25 . 12|Psalm 25:12-14]] connects fear of the LORD with receiving divine instruction, covenant blessing, and intimate friendship: "The friendship of the LORD is for those who fear him, and he makes known to them his covenant."

[[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 28#Job 28 . 28|Job 28:28]], in the great wisdom poem searching for understanding's location, concludes: "Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom, and to turn away from evil is understanding." True wisdom is not merely intellectual attainment but moral and spiritual orientation toward God.

Kline emphasizes that fear of the LORD is the covenant response appropriate to Common Grace revelation. Creation order points to the Creator; proper response is worship and obedience. This is the universal human obligation, though Special Grace provides fuller revelation and redemptive relationship through Christ.

**The Commission to Seek Wisdom**

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 2#Proverbs 2 . 1|Proverbs 2:1-4]] commissions intense pursuit: "My son, if you receive my words and treasure up my commandments with you, making your ear attentive to wisdom and inclining your heart to understanding; yes, if you call out for insight and raise your voice for understanding, if you seek it like silver and search for it as for hidden treasures..." This is active, diligent, costly pursuit—not passive absorption but intentional quest.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 5|Proverbs 4:5-7]] commands repeatedly: "Get wisdom; get insight... Do not forsake her, and she will keep you; love her, and she will guard you. The beginning of wisdom is this: Get wisdom, and whatever you get, get insight." The imperative urgency reflects wisdom's supreme value.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 13|Proverbs 4:13]] urges: "Keep hold of instruction; do not let go; guard her, for she is your life." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 23|Proverbs 4:23]] gives the famous charge: "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life." Internal orientation determines external conduct; wisdom begins in the heart.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 23#Proverbs 23 . 23|Proverbs 23:23]] commands: "Buy truth, and do not sell it; buy wisdom, instruction, and understanding." This commercial metaphor emphasizes wisdom's incomparable value—worth any price, never to be traded away. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 16#Proverbs 16 . 16|Proverbs 16:16]] declares: "How much better to get wisdom than gold! To get understanding is to be chosen rather than silver."

**The Commission to Keep God's Commandments**

Wisdom is not abstract philosophy but covenant obedience. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 3#Proverbs 3 . 1|Proverbs 3:1-2]] charges: "My son, do not forget my teaching, but let your heart keep my commandments, for length of days and years of life and peace they will add to you." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 20|Proverbs 6:20-23]] commands: "My son, keep your father's commandment, and forsake not your mother's teaching. Bind them on your heart always; tie them around your neck. When you walk, they will lead you; when you lie down, they will watch over you; and when you awake, they will talk with you."

This language echoes [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 6|Deuteronomy 6:6-9]]'s Shema command to bind God's words on hands, foreheads, doorposts. Wisdom is Torah-shaped, not separate from covenant law but its interior appropriation.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 1|Proverbs 7:1-3]] intensifies the commission: "My son, keep my words and treasure up my commandments with you; keep my commandments and live; keep my teaching as the apple of your eye; bind them on your fingers; write them on the tablet of your heart." This anticipates [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]'s new covenant promise of law written on hearts.

[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 19#Proverbs 19 . 16|Proverbs 19:16]] states bluntly: "Whoever keeps the commandment keeps his life; he who despises his ways will die." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28#Proverbs 28 . 7|Proverbs 28:7]] declares: "The one who keeps the law is a son with understanding, but a companion of gluttons shames his father."

**Ecclesiastes' Commission**

After surveying all of life's vanities, [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 13|Ecclesiastes 12:13-14]] presents the conclusion: "The end of the matter; all has been heard. Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man. For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every secret thing, whether good or evil."

This is the commission that transcends life "under the sun": orient vertically toward God in fear, obey His commandments, live in light of coming judgment. [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 5#Ecclesiastes 5 . 1|Ecclesiastes 5:1-7]] earlier warned about proper worship: approach God's house to listen, not offer foolish sacrifices; be quick to hear, slow to speak; fear God. Even in recognizing vanity, the Preacher maintains the commission to covenant faithfulness.

[[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 11#Ecclesiastes 11 . 9|Ecclesiastes 11:9]] acknowledges youthful joy but frames it with accountability: "Rejoice, O young man, in your youth... walk in the ways of your heart... But know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment." Freedom exists within divine oversight, not autonomous independence.

**The Psalter's Worship Commission**

The Psalms commission Israel to worship, often using imperative verbs. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 1|Psalm 95:1-2]] summons: "Oh come, let us sing to the LORD; let us make a joyful noise to the rock of our salvation! Let us come into his presence with thanksgiving; let us make a joyful noise to him with songs of praise!"

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 6|Psalm 95:6-7]] continues the commission: "Oh come, let us worship and bow down; let us kneel before the LORD, our Maker! For he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hand." This grounds worship in creation (our Maker) and covenant (His people).

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 100#Psalm 100 . 1|Psalm 100:1-2]] issues fourfold imperatives: "Make a joyful noise to the LORD, all the earth! Serve the LORD with gladness! Come into his presence with singing!" [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 100#Psalm 100 . 4|Psalm 100:4]] adds: "Enter his gates with thanksgiving, and his courts with praise! Give thanks to him; bless his name!"

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 29#Psalm 29 . 1|Psalm 29:1-2]] commissions heavenly beings: "Ascribe to the LORD, O heavenly beings, ascribe to the LORD glory and strength. Ascribe to the LORD the glory due his name; worship the LORD in the splendor of holiness." If angels must worship, how much more humans?

The final psalms (146-150) form a crescendo of worship commission. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 150#Psalm 150 . 1|Psalm 150]] commands "Praise the LORD" thirteen times, summoning praise with every instrument, concluding: "Let everything that has breath praise the LORD! Praise the LORD!" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 150#Psalm 150 . 6|verse 6]]). This is the universal commission: all creatures exist to worship their Creator.

**Psalm 15 and 24: Qualifications for Worship**

Not all worship is acceptable; God requires moral integrity. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 15#Psalm 15 . 1|Psalm 15:1]] asks: "O LORD, who shall sojourn in your tent? Who shall dwell on your holy hill?" The answer ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 15#Psalm 15 . 2|verse 2-5]]) lists ethical requirements: walking blamelessly, speaking truth, not slandering, honoring those who fear the LORD, keeping oaths even when costly.

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 24#Psalm 24 . 3|Psalm 24:3-4]] asks similar questions: "Who shall ascend the hill of the LORD? And who shall stand in his holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart, who does not lift up his soul to what is false and does not swear deceitfully." [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 24#Psalm 24 . 5|Psalm 24:5]] promises blessing and righteousness from God for such a person.

Beale notes these entrance liturgies reflect Eden's pattern: Adam was placed in the garden-sanctuary to "work and keep" it ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]]), language later used for priestly service. Access to God's presence requires righteousness—perfectly fulfilled in Christ who qualified by His sinlessness and now brings believers into God's presence ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-22]]).

**Commission to Teach the Next Generation**

Wisdom Literature emphasizes intergenerational transmission. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 4#Proverbs 4 . 1|Proverbs 4:1-4]] presents the pattern: "Hear, O sons, a father's instruction... When I was a son with my father, tender, the only one in the sight of my mother, he taught me and said to me, 'Let your heart hold fast my words; keep my commandments, and live.'"

[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 78#Psalm 78 . 1|Psalm 78:1-8]] commissions Israel to transmit God's works to the next generation so they would set their hope in God, not forget His works, and keep His commandments. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 78#Psalm 78 . 4|Psalm 78:4]] states the commission clearly: "We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD, and his might, and the wonders that he has done."

[[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 7|Deuteronomy 6:7]]'s command to teach children diligently underlies Proverbs' "my son" address throughout. Parents function as wisdom teachers, training children in the way they should go ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 22#Proverbs 22 . 6|Proverbs 22:6]]).

**Specific Moral Commissions**

Proverbs gives detailed moral commissions across life spheres:

**Sexual Purity**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 5#Proverbs 5 . 15|Proverbs 5:15-20]] commands faithfulness to one's spouse, rejoicing in the wife of youth. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 20|Proverbs 6:20-35]] warns extensively against adultery. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 7#Proverbs 7 . 1|Proverbs 7]] uses extended narrative warning to commission young men to avoid the adulteress.

**Economic Justice**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 1|Proverbs 11:1]] commands: "A false balance is an abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is his delight." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 14#Proverbs 14 . 31|Proverbs 14:31]] commissions care for the poor as honoring God. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 22#Proverbs 22 . 22|Proverbs 22:22-23]] warns: "Do not rob the poor, because he is poor, or crush the afflicted at the gate, for the LORD will plead their cause."

**Speech Ethics**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 19|Proverbs 10:19]] warns: "When words are many, transgression is not lacking, but whoever restrains his lips is prudent." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 12#Proverbs 12 . 18|Proverbs 12:18]] contrasts: "There is one whose rash words are like sword thrusts, but the tongue of the wise brings healing." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 15#Proverbs 15 . 1|Proverbs 15:1]] commissions: "A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."

**Diligence**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 6#Proverbs 6 . 6|Proverbs 6:6-11]] commissions the sluggard to consider the ant's wisdom, working without supervision. [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 10#Proverbs 10 . 4|Proverbs 10:4-5]] contrasts: "A slack hand causes poverty, but the hand of the diligent makes rich. He who gathers in summer is a prudent son, but he who sleeps in harvest is a son who brings shame."

**Humility**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 3#Proverbs 3 . 5|Proverbs 3:5-6]] gives the famous commission: "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 11#Proverbs 11 . 2|Proverbs 11:2]] warns: "When pride comes, then comes disgrace, but with the humble is wisdom."

**Friendship**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 13#Proverbs 13 . 20|Proverbs 13:20]] commissions: "Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise, but the companion of fools will suffer harm." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 17#Proverbs 17 . 17|Proverbs 17:17]] defines true friendship: "A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity." [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 27#Proverbs 27 . 6|Proverbs 27:6]] distinguishes: "Faithful are the wounds of a friend; profuse are the kisses of an enemy."

**Job's Integrity Commission**

Though suffering, Job maintains his commission to integrity. [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 1#Job 1 . 1|Job 1:1]] describes him as "blameless and upright, one who feared God and turned away from evil." [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 1|Job 31]] presents Job's oath of innocence, listing his commitments: eyes covenant not to lust ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 1|verse 1]]), feet not walking with falsehood ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 5|verse 5]]), heart not enticed by woman ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 9|verse 9]]), servants treated justly ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 13|verse 13-15]]), poor and needy not neglected ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 16|verse 16-22]]), no trust in wealth ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 24|verse 24-25]]), enemies not rejoiced over ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 29|verse 29-30]]), strangers welcomed ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 32|verse 32]]), sins not hidden ([[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 31#Job 31 . 33|verse 33-34]]).

This is comprehensive moral commission covering sexuality, economics, justice, hospitality, honesty, and humility. Job modeled the fear of the LORD in concrete life spheres.

**Song of Solomon's Covenant Faithfulness**

[[Readable Bible/22 - Song of Solomon/Song of Solomon 8#Song of Solomon 8 . 6|Song of Solomon 8:6-7]] commissions exclusive covenant love: "Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy is fierce as the grave. Its flashes are flashes of fire, the very flame of the LORD. Many waters cannot quench love, neither can floods drown it."

This is marital commission to unwavering faithfulness, reflecting God's covenant love for Israel and Christ's love for the church. The "seal" imagery recalls covenant ratification; the fire and water language emphasizes love's power and permanence.

**Theological Framework: Beale on Commission to Eden-Restoration**

Beale emphasizes that Wisdom Literature's commission continues the Adamic mandate to extend Eden. The call to fear God, pursue wisdom, and obey commandments is the call to image-bearing priesthood—representing God to creation and bringing creation's worship to God.

The Psalter's worship commission reflects temple liturgy, which represented Eden and anticipated new creation. Worshiping rightly means participating proleptically in the eternal worship of [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 4#Revelation 4 . 1|Revelation 4-5]], joining the heavenly chorus around God's throne.

The moral commissions in Proverbs shape humans toward Christ-likeness. The pure, just, humble, diligent, wise person described in Proverbs finds ultimate embodiment in Jesus, who perfectly feared God and obeyed His commandments. Believers pursue these virtues not to earn salvation but as fruit of the Spirit ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 22|Galatians 5:22-23]]), growing toward maturity in Christ.

**Theological Framework: Kline on Common Grace Obedience**

Kline's framework clarifies that Wisdom's commission applies at both Common Grace and Special Grace levels. The moral patterns in Proverbs—honesty, diligence, justice—are universally binding because rooted in creation order. All humans should live according to these principles because they reflect God's design.

However, the "fear of the LORD" as covenant relationship requires Special Grace—redemptive faith in God's promises. One can follow Proverbs' practical wisdom and still perish without saving faith. Conversely, believers filled with faith may still need to grow in practical wisdom, learning creation's patterns.

The commission is thus dual: externally conform to creation's moral order (Common Grace responsibility), and internally fear the LORD through covenant relationship (Special Grace gift). Ecclesiastes emphasizes this: fear God and keep commandments while recognizing all under-the-sun pursuits are vanity without this transcendent reference point.

**Christological Fulfillment: Jesus the Obedient Son**

Jesus perfectly fulfilled Wisdom Literature's commission. He feared God ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 7|Hebrews 5:7]]), learned obedience through suffering ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 8|verse 8]]), and became perfect obedience for us ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 9|verse 9]]). He kept all God's commandments without fail ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]), fulfilling the law's righteous requirements ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 17|Matthew 5:17]]).

[[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 15|Matthew 3:15]] shows Jesus insisting on baptism "to fulfill all righteousness." His entire life was commission-obedience, from boyhood in the temple ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 49|Luke 2:49]]) to Gethsemane's submission ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 39|Matthew 26:39]]) to the cross ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 30|John 19:30]]—"It is finished."

Jesus embodied Proverbs' wise person: speaking truth ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]]), caring for the poor ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18]]), exercising justice ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 24|John 7:24]]), maintaining sexual purity, honoring parents ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 15#Matthew 15 . 4|Matthew 15:4-6]]), working diligently ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 17|John 5:17]]), speaking wisely ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 7#Matthew 7 . 28|Matthew 7:28-29]]), and remaining humble ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 8|Philippians 2:8]]).

He fulfilled the worship commission, attending synagogue ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 16|Luke 4:16]]), observing festivals ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 10|John 7:10]]), cleansing the temple ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 16|John 2:16]]), and declaring His Father's house a house of prayer ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 13|Matthew 21:13]]). In resurrection, He leads eternal worship as great High Priest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]).

**The Great Commission as Wisdom's Expansion**

Jesus' Great Commission ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]]) continues Wisdom's call. "Make disciples of all nations" extends Wisdom's public invitation to every people group. "Teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you" transmits wisdom intergenerationally. "I am with you always" promises divine presence like Wisdom dwelling with those who find her ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 35|Proverbs 8:35]]).

The church's commission is to proclaim Christ, "whom we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 28|Colossians 1:28]]). This continues Wisdom Literature's pedagogical mission, now centered in Jesus.

**Application for God's People**

Believers today receive Wisdom Literature's commission personally. The call to fear God, pursue wisdom, keep commandments, and worship rightly remains binding. Christians should diligently study Scripture ([[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 2#2 Timothy 2 . 15|2 Timothy 2:15]]), growing in knowledge and discernment ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 1#Philippians 1 . 9|Philippians 1:9-10]]), pursuing holiness ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 14|Hebrews 12:14]]).

The moral commissions of Proverbs guide daily life: work diligently, speak truthfully, act justly, love faithfully, remain humble, choose companions wisely. These are not legalistic burdens but Spirit-empowered fruits ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 22|Galatians 5:22-23]]), wisdom from above that is pure, peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits ([[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 3#James 3 . 17|James 3:17]]).

The worship commission summons believers to gather for corporate praise, maintaining the pattern of [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 25|Hebrews 10:25]]: "not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another." Every Lord's Day is a rehearsal for eternal worship, an anticipation of the new Jerusalem where God dwells with His people ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]).

The commission to teach the next generation continues through parental discipleship and church catechesis, passing on "the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints" ([[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 3|Jude 3]]).
