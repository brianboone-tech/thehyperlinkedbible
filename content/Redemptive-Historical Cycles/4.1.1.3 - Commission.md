# 4.1.1.3 - Commission - Cain and Abel (Genesis 4:2-4)

## Text

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 2|Genesis 4:2]]**: "Now Abel was a keeper of sheep, and Cain a worker of the ground."

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-4]]**: "In the course of time Cain brought to the LORD an offering of the fruit of the ground, and Abel also brought of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions. And the LORD had regard for Abel and his offering."

---

## The Adamic Commission Continues

Though the commission is not explicitly restated to Cain and Abel, the narrative makes clear that they **inherit** the mandate given to Adam. Despite the Fall, despite the exile, despite the curse, humanity's fundamental calling persists:

- **Be fruitful and multiply** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])
- **Subdue the earth** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 28|Genesis 1:28]])
- **Have dominion over creation** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-28]])
- **Work and keep the garden** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]])
- **Image God** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-27]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 1|Genesis 5:1-3]])

The commission is not nullified by sin. Humanity remains God's image-bearers, called to represent God's rule, cultivate creation, and extend his kingdom. The difference is that the commission is now carried out **under the curse**, in a world marked by thorns, toil, and mortality.

Cain and Abel's vocations demonstrate their participation in this ongoing mandate. They work, they subdue, they exercise dominion. The question is: **Will they do so faithfully? Will they succeed where Adam failed?**

---

## Work: Subduing Creation Under the Curse

### Cain Works the Ground

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 2|Genesis 4:2]]**: "Cain [was] a worker of the ground."

Cain's vocation directly echoes Adam's original commission: "The LORD God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to work it (*abad*) and keep it" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 15|Genesis 2:15]]). The same verb (*abad*—to work, serve, cultivate) is used of Cain's labor.

But the ground Cain works is **cursed** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17-19]]). It resists him. It produces thorns and thistles. It demands sweat and toil. What was once joyful cultivation is now burdensome labor.

Cain's work is thus a **double-edged reality**:

- **Positive**: He fulfills the Adamic mandate to subdue the earth, cultivate creation, bring order out of chaos.
- **Negative**: His work is marked by futility, frustration, and the constant reminder of sin's consequences.

Cain's vocation foreshadows the broader biblical theme of **work under the curse**. Humanity must labor, but the labor is painful. Creation must be subdued, but it resists. The mandate persists, but its execution is marred by sin.

Only in Christ will the curse on the ground be fully lifted ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 3|Revelation 22:3]]—"No longer will there be anything accursed"). Until then, work remains both gift and burden, calling and curse.

### Abel Keeps Sheep

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 2|Genesis 4:2]]**: "Abel was a keeper of sheep."

Abel's vocation reflects the **dominion mandate**: "Let them have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over the livestock and over all the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26]]). Abel exercises authority over animals—specifically, sheep and goats—fulfilling humanity's calling to rule creation on God's behalf.

Shepherding becomes a theologically rich image throughout Scripture:

- **David** is called from tending sheep to shepherd Israel ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 11|1 Samuel 16:11]]; [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 78#Psalm 78 . 70|Psalm 78:70-72]])
- **Moses** shepherds flocks before leading Israel ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 1|Exodus 3:1]])
- **God** is Israel's shepherd ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 23#Psalm 23 . 1|Psalm 23:1]]; [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 34#Ezekiel 34 . 11|Ezekiel 34:11-16]])
- **Jesus** is the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11-18]])

Abel's vocation thus carries **typological significance**. As the first shepherd, he prefigures the shepherds of Israel and ultimately Christ himself—the shepherd who offers his life as a sacrifice for the flock.

Furthermore, Abel's work with animals prepares him to offer **blood sacrifices**. He raises lambs and goats—creatures that will become central to the sacrificial system. His vocation and his worship are intertwined. His daily work points toward the altar.

## Worship: Offering to the LORD

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-4]]**: "In the course of time Cain brought to the LORD an offering of the fruit of the ground, and Abel also brought of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions. And the LORD had regard for Abel and his offering."

The commission is not merely **vocational** (work the ground, tend flocks) but also **liturgical** (worship the LORD). Both brothers recognize the need to bring offerings to God. This suggests that Adam, though exiled from the garden-temple, continued to teach his sons about worship, sacrifice, and approaching God.

### The Nature of the Offerings

**Cain's offering**:
- "Fruit of the ground" (פְּרִי הָאֲדָמָה, *peri ha'adamah*)
- No indication that it was the **firstfruits** or the **best** of his produce
- Bloodless, grain offering

**Abel's offering**:
- "Firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions" (מִבְּכֹרוֹת צֹאנוֹ וּמֵחֶלְבֵהֶן, *mibbekhoroth tsono umechelbehem*)
- Explicitly the **firstborn** and the **fat portions**—the best, the choicest
- Blood sacrifice

The contrast is deliberate. Abel gives his **best**; Cain gives generically. Abel offers **blood**; Cain offers produce. Abel's offering reflects **faith** ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]]); Cain's offering reflects **duty** or perhaps **presumption**.

### Why Was Abel's Offering Accepted and Cain's Rejected?

The text does not explicitly state why God accepted Abel's offering but not Cain's, but several factors are evident:

#### 1. Faith (Hebrews 11:4)

**[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]]**: "By faith Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain, through which he was commended as righteous, God commending him by accepting his gifts."

The key difference is **faith**. Abel approached God trusting in God's mercy and grace. Cain approached God on his own terms, without true faith, perhaps with a sense of entitlement or self-righteousness.

This establishes the biblical principle: **Worship is not accepted based on external form but on the heart's posture**. "The LORD sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart" ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 7|1 Samuel 16:7]]).

#### 2. The Best vs. the Generic

**Abel** brought "the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions"—the **best** he had. The firstborn were sacred to God ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 2|Exodus 13:2, 12]]; [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 22#Exodus 22 . 29|Exodus 22:29-30]]). The fat portions were the choicest parts, reserved for God in later sacrificial regulations ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 3#Leviticus 3 . 16|Leviticus 3:16]]—"All fat is the LORD's").

**Cain** brought "the fruit of the ground"—generic produce, no indication of firstfruits or special quality. This suggests a **halfhearted** or **perfunctory** approach to worship. He gives to God, but not his best. He fulfills the duty, but without devotion.

The principle: **God deserves the first and the best, not leftovers**. "Honor the LORD with your wealth and with the firstfruits of all your produce" ([[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 3#Proverbs 3 . 9|Proverbs 3:9]]).

#### 3. Blood Sacrifice vs. Bloodless Offering

**Abel's blood sacrifice** anticipates the entire sacrificial system of the Mosaic Law and ultimately points to Christ's atoning death. "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 22|Hebrews 9:22]]).

Does this mean grain offerings are unacceptable? No—the Mosaic Law prescribes grain offerings ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 2#Leviticus 2 . 1|Leviticus 2]]). But grain offerings **accompany** blood sacrifices; they do not replace them. The foundational offering is **atonement**—blood shed for sin.

Cain's offering, while perhaps acceptable as a **thanksgiving** offering, is insufficient as an approach to God by a sinner. He needs **atonement**. He needs blood. He needs to acknowledge his guilt and God's mercy. Instead, he approaches God on his own terms, presuming acceptance without acknowledging need.

Abel, by offering a blood sacrifice, implicitly acknowledges: **"I am a sinner. I deserve death. This animal dies in my place. I come to God not on my own merit but through substitutionary atonement."** This is the gospel in seed form.

#### 4. Attitude of Heart

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 5|Genesis 4:5-7]]**: Cain's response to God's rejection reveals his heart. He becomes **angry**. His face falls. God confronts him: "Why are you angry? Why has your face fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted?"

God's question implies that Cain **knows** what is required. He is not ignorant. He has been taught. He understands the standard. Yet he refuses to meet it. His anger reveals a **proud, unrepentant heart**—one that demands acceptance without submission, blessing without obedience.

Abel, by contrast, is "righteous" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 35|Matthew 23:35]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]])—not sinless, but justified by faith, accepted by grace, approaching God with humility and trust.

The principle: **Worship must be according to God's revealed will, not human preference**. Later, Nadab and Abihu offer "unauthorized fire" and are consumed ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 10#Leviticus 10 . 1|Leviticus 10:1-3]]). Saul presumes to offer sacrifice and loses the kingdom ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 13#1 Samuel 13 . 8|1 Samuel 13:8-14]]). God is not indifferent to how he is worshiped. True worship requires **faith, obedience, and humility**.

## The Commission to Image God

Beyond work and worship, Cain and Abel are called to **image God**. This is the foundational mandate: "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26]]).

To **image God** means to:

- **Represent God's rule** over creation (dominion)
- **Reflect God's character** (holiness, justice, love)
- **Relate to God** in covenant fellowship (worship, obedience)
- **Reproduce image-bearers** (multiply, teach the next generation)

Abel succeeds, at least partially, in imaging God. He offers acceptable worship, lives righteously, and becomes a type of Christ—the ultimate image of God ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15|Colossians 1:15]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]).

Cain fails utterly. He images not God but the serpent. He murders, lies, rebels, and establishes a godless civilization ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 17|Genesis 4:17-24]]). His descendants boast in violence ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]), pursue cultural achievement apart from God, and embody the spirit of Babel—human autonomy and self-glorification.

The commission to image God, therefore, is not merely about **function** (work, subdue, multiply) but about **character** and **relationship**. Will humanity reflect God's holiness? Will they worship him in faith? Will they obey his commands?

The answer, in Cain's case, is no. The commission is given, but it is not fulfilled. The intrusion fails.

## Beale's Framework: Expanding the Temple

**G. K. Beale** argues that the Adamic commission was fundamentally about **expanding the garden-temple**. Adam was to extend God's presence, God's rule, and God's order to fill the whole earth. The garden was the **starting point**, not the end goal. Adam was to multiply image-bearers who would subdue the earth and transform it into a global sanctuary.

Adam failed. He was expelled. The temple was closed.

But the commission remains. Cain and Abel, as Adam's sons, inherit this mandate. Will they succeed? Will they expand the temple? Will they restore access to God's presence?

The answer is **partial**. Abel offers acceptable worship—a small intrusion of grace, a momentary restoration of right relationship with God. His altar becomes a provisional meeting place, an anticipation of the tabernacle and temple to come.

But Abel dies. The temple is not expanded. Access to God's presence is not restored. Cain is exiled further ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 16|Genesis 4:16]]—"from the presence of the LORD"). The commission remains unfulfilled.

This establishes the pattern: **Every Old Testament intrusion is an attempt to fulfill the Adamic commission, and every one falls short**. Noah, Abraham, Moses, David—all are Adam figures, all are called to expand the temple, and all ultimately fail. Only Jesus Christ, the true and greater Adam, will finally succeed.

---

## Kline's Framework: Intrusion Ethics and the Great Commission

**Meredith Kline** connects the Adamic commission to the **Great Commission** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18-20]]). Both involve:

- **Going** into all the earth
- **Subduing** (discipling nations)
- **Multiplying** image-bearers (baptizing, teaching)
- **Extending God's rule** (kingdom of heaven)

The difference is that the Adamic commission was given before the Fall, in a context of **creation ordinance**. The Great Commission is given after the Fall, in a context of **redemption**. The Great Commission does not merely **restore** the original mandate; it **redeems and transforms** it.

Cain and Abel's commission falls between these two poles. They live under the **Common Grace** period, where the creation mandate persists but is marred by sin. They experience **intrusions of special grace** (God's acceptance of Abel's sacrifice) that point forward to the ultimate redemption, but they do not themselves bring final deliverance.

The commission given to Cain and Abel is thus a **test**: Can fallen humanity fulfill the Adamic mandate? Can they image God, subdue creation, and expand the temple?

The answer is no. Not without **regeneration**. Not without the **new covenant**. Not without Christ. The commission is given, but it cannot be fulfilled until the greater Adam comes.

## God's Warning to Cain: The Opportunity to Obey

**[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 6|Genesis 4:6-7]]**: "The LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry, and why has your face fallen? If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it.'"

God does not reject Cain permanently. He gives him an **opportunity**. "If you do well, will you not be accepted?" The path to reconciliation is open. Cain can repent, offer the right sacrifice, humble himself, and be restored.

But God also warns: "Sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is contrary to you, but you must rule over it."

This language echoes the creation mandate. Adam was called to "have dominion" (רָדָה, *radah*) and to "rule" over creation ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26, 28]]). Now Cain is called to "rule over" (מָשַׁל, *mashal*) sin.

The commission is expanded: Not only must Cain subdue **creation**, he must also subdue **sin**. The enemy is no longer merely external (chaos, wilderness) but **internal** (the sin nature, the crouching beast).

This is the new reality after the Fall. The commission remains, but it is complicated by **indwelling sin**. Humanity must now fight on two fronts: subduing creation **and** resisting temptation.

Cain fails on both counts. He does not rule over sin. Instead, sin rules over him. He murders his brother, lies to God, and is exiled further. The commission is given, but Cain refuses it.

---

## Summary: Commission Given, Commission Failed

Cain and Abel inherit the Adamic commission:

1. **Work and subdue creation**—Cain cultivates the ground, Abel tends flocks
2. **Worship God**—Both bring offerings to the LORD
3. **Image God**—Reflect God's character, extend God's rule
4. **Rule over sin**—Resist temptation, obey God's commands

Abel partially succeeds. He offers acceptable worship. He lives righteously. He images God. But he dies, and the commission remains unfulfilled.

Cain utterly fails. He offers unacceptable worship. He murders his brother. He rejects God's warning. He is exiled further from God's presence.

The intrusion of grace—the acceptance of Abel's sacrifice, the opportunity given to Cain—does not succeed in restoring humanity to its original calling. The commission persists, but fallen humanity cannot fulfill it.

This establishes the pattern of the Old Testament: **Commission → Failure → Judgment → New Opportunity**. The cycle will repeat with Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, and the prophets. Each will be given the commission. Each will fall short. The need for a **sinless Adam**, a **perfect image-bearer**, a **greater High Priest** becomes increasingly urgent.

That Adam is Jesus Christ, who perfectly fulfills the commission, conquers sin and death, and finally expands the temple to fill the earth ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]—"Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man").
