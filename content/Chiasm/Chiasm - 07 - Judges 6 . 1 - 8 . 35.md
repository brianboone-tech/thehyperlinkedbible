- A. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 1|1]] Again the Israelites <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">did evil in the sight of the LORD</span>; so He delivered them into the hand of Midian for seven years,

    - B. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 1|1-6]] and the hand of Midian prevailed against Israel. Because of the Midianites, the Israelites prepared shelters for themselves in the mountains, caves, and strongholds. Whenever the Israelites would plant their crops, the <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Midianites</span>, Amalekites, and other people of the east would come up and invade them, encamping against them as far as Gaza and destroying the produce of the land. They left Israel with no sustenance, neither sheep nor oxen nor donkeys. For the <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Midianites</span> came with their livestock and their tents like a great swarm of locusts. They and their camels were innumerable, and they entered the land to ravage it. Israel was greatly impoverished by <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Midian</span>, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.

        - C. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 7|7-10]] Now when the Israelites cried out to the LORD because of Midian, He sent them a prophet, who told them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I brought you up out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery. I delivered you out of the hands of Egypt and all your oppressors. I drove them out before you and gave you their land. And I said to you: 'I am the LORD your God. You must not fear the <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">gods of the Amorites</span>, in whose land you dwell.' But you did not obey Me."

            - D. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 11|11-14]] Then the angel of the LORD came and sat down under the oak in Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, where his son <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Gideon</span> was threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianites. And the angel of the LORD appeared to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Gideon</span> and said, "The LORD is with you, O mighty man of valor." "Please, my Lord," <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Gideon</span> replied, "if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? And where are all His wonders of which our fathers told us, saying, 'Has not the LORD brought us up out of Egypt?' But now the LORD has forsaken us and delivered us into the hand of Midian." The LORD turned to him and said, "Go in the strength you have and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Am I not sending you?"

                - E. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 15|15-16]] "Please, my Lord," Gideon replied, "how can I save Israel? Indeed, my clan is the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">weakest</span> in Manasseh, and I am the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">youngest</span> in my father's house." "Surely I will be with you," the LORD replied, "and you will <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">strike down</span> all the Midianites as one man."

                    - F. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 17|17-24]] Gideon answered, "If I have found favor in Your sight, give me a sign that it is You speaking with me. Please do not depart from this place until I return to You. Let me bring my offering and set it before You." And the LORD said, "I will stay until you return." So Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span> and an ephah of flour. He placed the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot and brought them out to present to Him under the oak. And the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">angel</span> of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span>, lay them on this rock, and pour out the broth." And Gideon did so. Then the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">angel of the LORD</span> extended the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span>. And fire flared from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span>. Then the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">angel of the LORD</span> vanished from his sight. When Gideon realized that it was the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">angel of the LORD</span>, he said, "Oh no, Lord GOD! I have seen the <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">angel of the LORD</span> face to face!" But the LORD said to him, "Peace be with you. Do not be afraid, for you will not die." So Gideon built an altar to the LORD there and called it The LORD Is Peace. To this day it stands in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

                        - G. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 25|25-32]] On that very night the LORD said to Gideon, "Take your father's young bull and a second bull seven years old, tear down your father's altar to Baal, and cut down the Asherah pole beside it. Then build a proper altar to the LORD your God on the top of this stronghold. And with the wood of the Asherah pole you cut down, take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering." So Gideon took ten of his servants and did as the LORD had told him. But because he was too afraid of his father's household and the men of the city, he did it by night rather than in the daytime. When the men of the city got up in the morning, there was Baal's altar torn down, with the Asherah pole cut down beside it and the second bull offered up on the newly built altar. "Who did this?" they said to one another. And after they had investigated thoroughly, they were told, "Gideon son of Joash did it." Then the men of the city said to Joash, "Bring out your son. He must die, because he has torn down Baal's altar and cut down the Asherah pole beside it." But <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Joash</span> said to all who stood against him, "Are you contending for Baal? Are you trying to save him? Whoever pleads his case will be put to death by morning! If Baal is a god, let him contend for himself with the one who has torn down his altar." So on that day Gideon was called Jerubbaal, that is to say, "<span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Let Baal contend with him</span>," because he had torn down Baal's altar.

                            - H. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 33|33-35]] Then all the Midianites, Amalekites, and other people of the east <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">gathered together</span>, crossed over the Jordan, and camped in the Valley of Jezreel. So the Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon, who blew the <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">ram's horn</span> and rallied the Abiezrites behind him. Calling them to arms, Gideon sent messengers throughout Manasseh, as well as Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, so that they came up to meet him.

                                - I. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 36|36-40]] Then Gideon said to God, "If You are going to save Israel by my hand, as You have said, then behold, I will place a <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">fleece</span> of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew only on the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">fleece</span> and all the ground is dry, then I will know that You are going to save Israel by my hand, as You have said." And that is what happened. When Gideon arose the next morning, he squeezed the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">fleece</span> and wrung out the dew—a bowlful of water. Then Gideon said to God, "Do not be angry with me; let me speak one more time. Please allow me one more test with the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">fleece</span>. This time let it be dry, and the ground covered with dew." And that night God did so. Only the <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">fleece</span> was dry, and dew covered the ground.

                                    - J. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 1|1-8]] Early in the morning Jerubbaal (that is, Gideon) and all the men with him camped beside the spring of Harod. And the camp of Midian was north of them in the valley near the hill of Moreh. Then the LORD said to Gideon, "You have too many people for Me to deliver Midian into their hands, lest Israel glorify themselves over Me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.' Now, therefore, proclaim in the hearing of the people: 'Whoever is fearful and trembling may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.'" So twenty-two thousand of them turned back, but ten thousand remained. Then the LORD said to Gideon, "There are still too many people. Take them down to the water, and I will sift them for you there. If I say to you, 'This one shall go with you,' he shall go. But if I say, 'This one shall not go with you,' he shall not go." So Gideon brought the people down to the water, and the LORD said to him, "Separate those who lap the water with their tongues like a dog from those who kneel to drink." And the number of those who lapped the water with their hands to their mouths was <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">three hundred men</span>; all the others knelt to drink. Then the LORD said to Gideon, "With the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">three hundred men</span> who lapped the water I will save you and deliver the Midianites into your hand. But all the others are to go home." So Gideon sent the rest of the Israelites to their tents but kept the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">three hundred men</span>, who took charge of the provisions and rams' horns of the others. And the camp of Midian lay below him in the valley.

                                - I'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 9|9-14]] That night the LORD said to Gideon, "Get up and go down against the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand. But if you are afraid to do so, then go down to the camp with your servant Purah and listen to what they are saying. Then your hands will be strengthened to attack the camp." So he went with Purah his servant to the outposts where armed men were guarding the camp. Now the Midianites, Amalekites, and all the other people of the east had settled in the valley like a swarm of locusts, and their camels were as countless as the sand on the seashore. And as Gideon arrived, a man was telling his friend about a <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">dream</span>. "Behold, I had a <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">dream</span>," he said, "and I saw a loaf of barley bread come tumbling into the Midianite camp. It struck the tent so hard that the tent overturned and collapsed." His friend replied: "This is nothing less than the sword of Gideon son of Joash, the Israelite. God has delivered Midian and the whole camp into his hand."

                            - H'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 15|15-23]] When Gideon heard the dream and its interpretation, he bowed in worship. He returned to the camp of Israel and said, "Get up, for the LORD has delivered the camp of Midian into your hand." And he divided the three hundred men into three companies and gave each man a <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">ram's horn</span> in one hand and a large jar in the other, containing a torch. "Watch me and do as I do," Gideon said. "When I come to the outskirts of the camp, do exactly as I do. When I and all who are with me blow our <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">horns</span>, then you are also to blow your <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">horns</span> from all around the camp and shout, 'For the LORD and for Gideon!'" Gideon and the hundred men with him reached the outskirts of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, just after the changing of the guard. They blew their <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">horns</span> and broke the jars that were in their hands. The three companies blew their <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">horns</span> and shattered their jars. Holding the torches in their left hands and the <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">horns</span> in their right hands, they shouted, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!" Each Israelite took his position around the camp, and the entire Midianite army fled, crying out as they ran. And when the three hundred <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">rams' horns</span> sounded, the LORD set all the men in the camp against one another with their swords. The army <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">fled</span> to Beth-shittah toward Zererah as far as the border of Abel-meholah near Tabbath. Then the men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh, and they pursued the Midianites.

                        - G'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 24|24]]-[[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 2|8:2]] Gideon sent messengers throughout the hill country of Ephraim to say, "Come down against the Midianites and seize the waters of the Jordan ahead of them as far as Beth-barah." So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they captured the waters of the Jordan as far as Beth-barah. They also captured Oreb and Zeeb, the two princes of Midian; and they killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb and Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb. So they pursued the Midianites and brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side of the Jordan. Then the men of Ephraim said to Gideon, "Why have you done this to us? Why did you fail to call us when you went to fight against Midian?" And they contended with him violently. But Gideon answered them, "<span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Now what have I accomplished compared to you? Are not the gleanings of Ephraim better than the grape harvest of Abiezer?</span> God has delivered Oreb and Zeeb, the two princes of Midian, into your hand. What was I able to do compared to you?" When he had said this, their anger against him subsided.

                    - F'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 4|4-17]] Then Gideon and his three hundred men came to the Jordan and crossed it, exhausted yet still in pursuit. So Gideon said to the men of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span>, "Please give my troops some <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span>, for they are exhausted, and I am still pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian." But the leaders of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span> asked, "Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your possession, that we should give <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span> to your army?" "Very well," Gideon replied, "when the LORD has delivered Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, I will tear your flesh with the thorns and briers of the wilderness!" From there he went up to Penuel and asked the same from them, but the men of Penuel gave the same response as the men of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span>. So Gideon told the men of Penuel, "When I return in triumph, I will tear down this tower!" Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with their army of about fifteen thousand men—all that were left of the armies of the people of the east. A hundred and twenty thousand swordsmen had already fallen. And Gideon went up by way of the caravan route east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and he attacked their army, taking them by surprise. When Zebah and Zalmunna fled, Gideon pursued and captured these two kings of Midian, routing their entire army. After this, Gideon son of Joash returned from the battle along the Ascent of Heres. There he captured a young man of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span> and interrogated him. The young man wrote down for him the names of the seventy-seven leaders and elders of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span>. And Gideon went to the men of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span> and said, "Here are Zebah and Zalmunna, about whom you taunted me, saying, 'Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your possession, that we should give <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">bread</span> to your weary men?'" Then he took the elders of the city, and using the thorns and briers of the wilderness, he disciplined the men of <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">Succoth</span>. He also pulled down the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city.

                - E'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 18|18-21]] Next, Gideon asked Zebah and Zalmunna, "What kind of men did you kill at Tabor?" "Men like you," they answered, "each one resembling the son of a king." "They were my brothers," Gideon replied, "the sons of my mother! As surely as the LORD lives, if you had let them live, I would not kill you." So he said to Jether, his <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">firstborn</span>, "Get up and kill them." But the <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">young man</span> did not draw his sword; he was fearful because he was still a <span style="color: #d68910; font-weight: bold;">youth</span>. Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, "Get up and kill us yourself, for as the man is, so is his strength." So Gideon got up and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and he took the crescent ornaments from the necks of their camels.

            - D'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 22|22-23]] Then the Israelites said to <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Gideon</span>, "Rule over us—you and your son and grandson—for you have saved us from the hand of Midian." But <span style="color: #8e44ad; font-weight: bold;">Gideon</span> replied, "I will not rule over you, nor will my son. The LORD shall rule over you."

        - C'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 24|24-27]] Then he added, "Let me make a request of you, that each of you give me an earring from his plunder." (For the enemies had gold earrings because they were Ishmaelites.) "We will give them gladly," they replied. So they spread out a garment, and each man threw an earring from his plunder onto it. The weight of the gold earrings he had requested was 1,700 shekels, in addition to the crescent ornaments, the pendants, the purple garments of the kings of Midian, and the chains from the necks of their camels. From all this Gideon made an <span style="color: #16a085; font-weight: bold;">ephod</span>, which he placed in Ophrah, his hometown. But soon all Israel prostituted themselves by worshiping it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and his household.

    - B'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 28|28-32]] In this way <span style="color: #1f618d; font-weight: bold;">Midian</span> was subdued before the Israelites and did not raise its head again. So the land had rest for forty years in the days of Gideon, and he—Jerubbaal son of Joash—returned home and settled down. Gideon had seventy sons of his own, since he had many wives. His concubine, who dwelt in Shechem, also bore him a son, and he named him Abimelech. Later, Gideon son of Joash died at a ripe old age and was buried in the tomb of his father Joash in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

- A'. [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 33|33-35]] And as soon as Gideon was dead, the Israelites turned and prostituted themselves with the Baals, and they set up Baal-berith as their god. The Israelites <span style="color: #c0392b; font-weight: bold;">failed to remember the LORD</span> their God who had delivered them from the hands of all their enemies on every side. They did not show kindness to the house of Jerubbaal (that is, Gideon) for all the good things he had done for Israel.
