Ruth functions as a **providential type of the Gentile bride** who is brought into covenant relationship with God through faith. A Moabitess—from a nation explicitly excluded from Israel's assembly (Deut 23:3)—Ruth confesses YHWH as her God and clings to His people. Through her faithfulness and the kinsman-redeemer's grace, she becomes an ancestress of David and ultimately of Christ. Her story anticipates the mystery revealed in the NT: that Gentiles would be "fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel" (Eph 3:6). Where Boaz typifies the Redeemer, Ruth typifies those redeemed—the Bride who leaves her people and gods to follow the true God.

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged narrative); Forward-Looking (anticipates Gentile inclusion in the Messiah's people); Indirect Type (Ruth herself is not messianic but represents those Christ redeems).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must confess what Ruth confessed: "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God." You must leave your old identities, allegiances, and gods behind to cling to the God of Israel who is also the God of the Gentiles. You must take refuge under His wings.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You keep trying to earn your inclusion. You think your spiritual resume, your faithful service, your religious heritage qualifies you. Or you try to sneak in by eliminating the standards altogether. But neither approach works. You were outside the covenant, excluded by sin far more absolutely than Ruth was excluded by ethnicity. No amount of effort or tolerance cancels that debt.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ became your Kinsman-Redeemer. He took on flesh (became kinsman), possessed infinite resources (divine worth), and was willing to pay the price (the cross). He bought you with His blood, acquiring you as His bride. Where Boaz said, "I have bought Ruth to be my wife," Christ says, "I have bought you with a price—you are mine." The excluded become included; the foreigner becomes family.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

"You who were far off have been brought near." You are no longer a stranger but a fellow citizen. You are Ruth—the Moabitess written into the Messiah's genealogy. The great multitude from every nation standing before the Lamb includes you. Now you can stop trying to earn your place and start resting in it. And you can extend the same radical welcome to other "outsiders" that Christ extended to you—because your belonging is secure in Him.

## Lexicon Findings

The Ruth trajectory reveals precise Hebrew-to-Greek lexical continuity tracing the foreigner-to-family transformation. In Ruth 2:10-12, the Hebrew **נׇכְרִי** (*nokrîy*, [[Lexicon/H5201-5300#H5237|H5237]]) marks Ruth as "foreign, alien, stranger"—an outsider to Israel's covenant community. This term establishes the fundamental tension: ethnic exclusion versus faith-based inclusion. The LXX renders *nokrîy* with **ἀλλότριος** (*allotrios*, [[Lexicon/G201-300#G245|G245]]) and **ξένος** (*xenos*, [[Lexicon/G3501-3600#G3581|G3581]]), both meaning "foreign, strange, belonging to another." Paul employs these identical Greek terms in Ephesians 2:12-19 to describe Gentiles who were **ἀλλοτριόω** (*allótrioō*, "alienated") and **ξένος** ("strangers"), but are now **fellow citizens** through Christ. The contrast pivots on **עַם** (*am*, [[Lexicon/H5901-6000#H5971|H5971]])—"people, nation"—which Ruth embraced ("your people shall be my people"). The NT fulfillment uses **ἔθνος** (*ethnos*, [[Lexicon/G1401-1500#G1484|G1484]], "Gentiles, nations") and **λαός** (*laos*, [[Lexicon/G2901-3000#G2992|G2992]], "people") to show the once-foreign nations becoming God's covenant people. The kinsman-redeemer vocabulary connects through **גָּאַל** (*ga'al*, [[Lexicon/H1301-1400#H1350|H1350]], "to redeem, act as kinsman-redeemer"), translated in LXX as **λυτρόω** (*lytroō*, [[Lexicon/G3001-3100#G3084|G3084]], "to redeem, ransom"), the same root Paul uses for Christ's redemptive work bringing outsiders into the family.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: נׇכְרִי (*nokrîy*) - appears in Ruth 2:10, 12; Deuteronomy 23:3-6 context
- **LXX**: ἀλλότριος (*allotrios*) / ξένος (*xenos*) - standard translations of "foreigner"
- **NT**: ἀλλότριος / ξένος (*allotrios* / *xenos*) - NT continuation in Ephesians 2:12, 19
