Samson's narrative (Judges 13-16) presents the most sustained account of Spirit-empowerment in the book of Judges. The Spirit of the LORD (רוּחַ יְהוָה) is explicitly mentioned in relation to Samson five times: the Spirit "began to stir him" (13:25), "came powerfully upon him" (14:6), "rushed upon him" (14:19), "came mightily upon him" (15:14), and implicitly returned when God answered his final prayer (16:28-30). This repeated Spirit-empowerment for mighty acts of deliverance makes Samson a providential type of the Spirit-anointed Messiah. Yet Samson's ministry was marked by contradiction: consecrated as a Nazirite from birth but repeatedly compromising his vows; Spirit-empowered for supernatural feats yet enslaved by fleshly desires; destined to "begin deliverance" (13:5) but unable to complete it. His story is one of wasted potential—yet Hebrews 11:32-34 commends him among faith heroes who "through faith...gained strength from weakness, became mighty in battle." The trajectory reveals that God accomplishes His purposes even through flawed instruments, preparing for the perfect Spirit-anointed Deliverer. Most strikingly, Samson's greatest victory came through his death: "he killed more when he died than while he lived" (16:30). This victory-through-death pattern foreshadows Christ's cross, where apparent defeat became cosmic victory. Samson, Spirit-empowered yet flawed, shadows Jesus, Spirit-anointed without measure, who accomplished not partial but complete deliverance.

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must stop believing that your failures have disqualified you or that your wasted potential is beyond redemption. You must turn, as Samson did in his final moment, and call upon the Lord: "Remember me. Strengthen me." You must trust that the God who uses flawed instruments can still use you.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You can't forgive yourself for what you've wasted. The memories of squandered opportunities haunt you. You compare what you are to what you might have been and despair of the difference. Your past failures feel like permanent disqualification. Like Samson after Delilah, you try to "shake yourself free" as before, not realizing that your own strength is gone. You keep trying to recover in your own power what can only be restored by grace.

### 3. How He Did It

Jesus Christ, the perfect Nazirite, maintained complete consecration where Samson failed. He never compromised His calling, never squandered His potential, never lost the Spirit's presence. Yet He chose to enter the place of the squanderer—blind to the Father's face on the cross ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"), bound by the sins of His people, brought low in apparent defeat. And through His death—not despite it—He accomplished salvation. "He killed more when he died than while he lived." The pattern Samson foreshadowed, Christ fulfilled perfectly. What looked like ultimate waste was eternal redemption.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

United to Christ, your wasted years are covered by His faithful years. Your squandered potential is absorbed by His fulfilled potential. Your failures are included in the "it is finished" He proclaimed. Now you can stop calculating loss and start receiving grace. Like Samson, you can pray from your blindness and bondage, and discover that God answers. The Spirit who departs from the compromised is the same Spirit who returns to the repentant. Your greatest ministry may yet be ahead—not because you've recovered your potential, but because Christ's victory through death is sufficient for your failures. The temple of your accusers can still collapse. The Deliverer who won through dying can still deliver through you.

## Lexicon Findings

The Samson trajectory reveals profound lexical continuity from Hebrew to Greek, centering on Spirit-empowerment for deliverance. The foundational term **רוּחַ יְהוָה** (*ruach YHWH*, "Spirit of the LORD") appears five times in Judges 13-16, denoting divine breath/wind/spirit (H7307) that energizes God's chosen deliverer. The Spirit's action is described by **צָלַח** (*tsalach*, H6743), "to rush upon, come powerfully"—sudden, overwhelming enablement for victory. Samson was consecrated as **נָזִיר** (*nazir*, H5139), one separated/devoted to God from birth. His mission: **יָחֵל לְהוֹשִׁיעַ** (*yachel lehoshia*, "to begin to deliver," H2490 + H3467)—initiating but not completing salvation. This Hebrew vocabulary flows into the NT through πνεῦμα (*pneuma*, G4151), the Greek equivalent for "spirit/Spirit," used in Luke 4:14-21 and Acts 10:38 to describe Jesus' Spirit-anointing. The concept of strength-from-weakness appears in Hebrews 11:34: **ἐδυναμώθησαν ἀπὸ ἀσθενείας** (*edynamothesan apo astheneias*, "they gained strength from weakness," G1743 + G769). Christ as Χριστός (*Christos*, G5547, "Anointed One") fulfills what Samson's episodic Spirit-empowerment foreshadowed—permanent δύναμις (*dynamis*, G1411, "power") accomplishing complete deliverance.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: רוּחַ (*ruach*) - appears throughout Judges 13-16
- **Hebrew**: נָזִיר (*nazir*) - consecrated one, Nazirite
