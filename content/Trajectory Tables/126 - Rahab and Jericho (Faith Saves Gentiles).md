## RAHAB AND JERICHO (FAITH SAVES GENTILES) TRAJECTORY TABLE

The account of Rahab the Canaanite prostitute (Joshua 2, 6) stands as one of Scripture's most remarkable demonstrations that salvation comes through faith alone, transcending ethnicity, morality, and social status. Rahab lived in Jericho, a Canaanite city devoted to destruction (חֵרֶם, ḥērem). She was a זוֹנָה (zônâ, "prostitute"), a Gentile outsider with no covenant claim on Israel's God. Yet when Joshua's spies came to Jericho, Rahab risked her life to hide them, declaring her faith: "The LORD your God, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath" (Joshua 2:11). She requested salvation for herself and her family in exchange for her protection of the spies. The spies agreed, instructing her to bind a scarlet cord in her window and gather her family inside—on the day Jericho fell, all inside Rahab's house would be spared (Joshua 2:18-21). When Israel marched around Jericho for seven days and the walls collapsed, "they devoted all in the city to destruction, both men and women, young and old...But Rahab the prostitute and her father's household and all who belonged to her, Joshua saved alive" (Joshua 6:21, 25). This trajectory traces the scandalous grace of God that saves undeserving sinners by faith alone—from Rahab's rescue through Israel's prophetic inclusion of Gentiles, to Christ welcoming tax collectors and sinners, to the gospel going to all nations, culminating in the great multitude from every tribe and tongue. Rahab's story demonstrates that faith, not ethnicity or merit, is the basis of salvation; that God's grace reaches the most unlikely recipients; and that the scarlet cord prefigures Christ's blood as the sign of salvation.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged historical narrative) and **Forward-Looking** (Hebrews 11:31 commends Rahab's faith and James 2:25 cites her justification as an example of living faith).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must be saved from coming judgment. Like Jericho's inhabitants, you stand under divine wrath. You need rescue—not improvement, not qualification, but rescue.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You cannot qualify yourself. If the criterion is ethnicity, you're the wrong people. If the criterion is morality, you fail. If the criterion is religious performance, it's never enough. You cannot earn your way into God's family. Every attempt to establish your own righteousness falls short. You are an outsider by nature, a sinner by practice, condemned by justice.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ welcomed sinners. Tax collectors and prostitutes entered the kingdom before the religious elite (Matthew 21:31). He broke down "the dividing wall of hostility" between Jew and Gentile (Ephesians 2:14). The scarlet cord in Rahab's window anticipated His blood—the mark of salvation that rescues from judgment all who take shelter under it. He died for the ungodly (Romans 5:6), for sinners (Romans 5:8), for enemies (Romans 5:10). His blood has "brought near" those who were "far off" (Ephesians 2:13).

### 4. How Through Him You Can

"The righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction" (Romans 3:22). You're included not by your qualifications but by faith in Christ. The scarlet cord is hung. The blood has been shed. The door is open to whoever believes—regardless of ethnicity, regardless of moral history, regardless of religious pedigree. Rahab the Canaanite prostitute is in Christ's genealogy. You can be in His family. Not by earning a place, but by trusting the One who makes a place for sinners.

## Lexicon Findings

The Rahab trajectory reveals a stunning lexical thread connecting devotion to destruction with redemption through faith. Hebrew חֵרֶם (*ḥērem*, [[Lexicon/H2701-2800#H2764|H2764]]) marks Jericho as "devoted to destruction," designating the entire city for complete annihilation under holy war ban, yet Rahab the זוֹנָה (*zônâ*, [[Lexicon/H2101-2200#H2181|H2181]], "prostitute") escapes through faith marked by תִּקְוַת חוּט הַשָּׁנִי (*tiqwaṯ ḥûṭ haššānî*, "line of scarlet thread," [[Lexicon/H8101-8200#H8144|H8144]]). This crimson dye, extracted from the dried body of the female *coccus ilicis* insect, becomes typologically significant when the NT identifies Rahab as πόρνη (*pórnē*, [[Lexicon/G4201-4300#G4204|G4204]], "harlot") saved διὰ πίστεως (*dia pisteōs*, "through faith," [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4102|G4102]]) in Hebrews 11:31 and δικαιωθεῖσα (*dikaiōtheisa*, "having been justified," [[Lexicon/G1301-1400#G1344|G1344]]) in James 2:25. The LXX translators consistently rendered זוֹנָה as πόρνη, establishing verbal continuity between Testaments. The scarlet thread finds NT fulfillment in αἷμα (*haima*, [[Lexicon/G101-200#G129|G129]], "blood") of Christ, the crimson sign marking believers for deliverance from eschatological judgment. Remarkably, Ephesians 2:13-19 declares ἔθνη (*ethnē*, [[Lexicon/G1401-1500#G1484|G1484]], "Gentiles") once "far off" now "brought near by the blood of Christ," dismantling ethnic barriers erected by Torah. The lexical arc traces *ḥērem* (destruction) → *šānî* (scarlet sign of deliverance) → *pistis* (saving faith) → *dikaioō* (forensic justification) → *haima* (atoning blood) → *ethnē* (all nations saved), demonstrating that faith alone—not ethnicity, moral pedigree, or religious performance—saves, typified by Rahab's scarlet cord prefiguring Christ's crimson sacrifice for guilty sinners.
