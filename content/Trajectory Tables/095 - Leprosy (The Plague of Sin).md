## LEPROSY (THE PLAGUE OF SIN) TRAJECTORY TABLE


Leprosy (צָרַעַת, ṣāraʿaṯ) in Scripture serves as a vivid picture of sin's defiling, spreading, isolating, and deadly nature. The detailed diagnostic procedures in Leviticus 13, the isolation of lepers outside the camp, their torn clothes and cry of 'Unclean! Unclean!', and the elaborate cleansing ritual in Leviticus 14 all dramatize sin's severity and the miracle of cleansing. Only God could heal leprosy (2 Kings 5:7), making cleansing a leper equivalent to raising the dead—a messianic sign (Matthew 11:5). The trajectory moves from physical leprosy requiring priestly diagnosis and divine healing to spiritual leprosy (sin) requiring Christ's cleansing touch and complete restoration. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted ceremonial law) and **Forward-Looking** (Jesus explicitly identifies cleansing lepers as messianic evidence in Matthew 11:5, fulfilling Isaiah's prophecies of the eschatological age).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must acknowledge your uncleanness and come to Christ for cleansing. You cannot clean yourself up. You cannot quarantine sin effectively. You must cry "Unclean!" and approach the only One who can heal you.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

Pride refuses to admit the depth of defilement. You want to present a manageable version of your condition—not leprous, just a little rough around the edges. You keep trying home remedies, spiritual self-help, moral improvement programs. But leprosy spreads. The longer you delay, the worse it gets. And part of you knows that admitting you're a leper means admitting you can't fix yourself—and that's terrifying.

### 3. How He Did It

Jesus stretched out His hand and touched the untouchable. He who was perfectly clean entered our unclean world, took on human flesh, bore our griefs and carried our sorrows. He was "esteemed stricken, smitten by God"—treated like a leper, driven outside the camp (Hebrews 13:12), so that we could be brought inside. His holiness was stronger than our defilement. His purity was contagious. He became what we were so we could become what He is.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Come to Him as a leper, not as someone who has cleaned up nicely. Say, "Lord, if you will, you can make me clean." And hear His response: "I will; be clean." The cleansing is His work, not yours. Your role is to confess, to come, to receive. Then live as a cleansed leper—one who knows the depth of defilement you were rescued from, who returns like the Samaritan to give thanks, who tells others that the Physician who heals lepers is still practicing. The one who once cried "Unclean!" now cries "Cleansed!"—not by your effort but by His touch.

## Lexicon Findings

The leprosy trajectory reveals profound lexical continuity across Hebrew and Greek. The foundation term **צָרַעַת** (*ṣāraʿaṯ*, H6883) describes the malignant skin disease requiring priestly diagnosis in Leviticus 13-14, derived from the verb **צָרַע** (*ṣāraʿ*, H6879, "to be stricken with leprosy"). The LXX translates this with **λεπρός** (*leprós*, G3015, "scaly, leprous"), establishing direct linguistic continuity into the NT. The clean/unclean binary structures the entire trajectory: **טָמֵא** (*ṭāmēʼ*, H2930, "to be ceremonially unclean") versus **טָהֵר** (*ṭāhēr*, H2891, "to be clean, purify"). These Hebrew roots find their Greek equivalents in **καθαρίζω** (*katharízō*, G2511, "to cleanse, especially a leper") and **καθαρός** (*katharós*, G2513, "clean, pure"). The NT maintains this ceremonial vocabulary while expanding it spiritually—Matthew 8:3's "be clean" uses the same cleansing verb employed for Levitical purification. First John 1:9's promise of cleansing from sin through **ὁμολογέω** (*homologéō*, G3670, "to confess") transforms the leper's cry "Unclean!" into redemptive confession accessing Christ's purifying blood. The trajectory demonstrates Hebrew ceremonial language becoming Greek soteriological vocabulary while preserving core meanings.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: צָרַעַת (*ṣāraʿaṯ*) - leprosy disease (Lev 13-14, 2 Kgs 5)
- **Hebrew**: טָמֵא/טָהֵר (*ṭāmēʼ/ṭāhēr*) - unclean/clean binary throughout Leviticus
- **LXX/NT**: λεπρός (*leprós*) - standard translation for צָרַעַת
- **NT**: καθαρίζω (*katharízō*) - cleanse (Matt 8:3, Mark 1:41, Luke 17:14, 1 John 1:9)
- **NT**: ὁμολογέω (*homologéō*) - confess (1 John 1:9, James 5:16)
