## VINE AND VINEYARD (TRUE ISRAEL) TRAJECTORY TABLE

The vine and vineyard imagery traces God's intent for Israel as His covenant people to bear fruit for His glory. From its foundation in patriarchal promises through prophetic indictments of fruitlessness to Christ's claim to be the "True Vine," this trajectory demonstrates how Jesus fulfills what Israel failed to accomplish. Where national Israel proved to be a degenerate vine producing sour grapes, Christ embodies the ideal Israel—the genuine vine who bears perfect fruit and enables His people to participate in His fruitfulness through union with Him. Isaiah's Song of the Vineyard (Isaiah 5:1-7) establishes the pattern: God lavished care on His vineyard expecting good grapes, but it produced wild grapes, resulting in judgment. Jesus retells this parable (Matthew 21:33-44) with a crucial shift—focusing on wicked tenants who reject the owner's son. Then in John 15, Jesus declares "I am the true vine" (ἐγώ εἰμι ἡ ἄμπελος ἡ ἀληθινή), replacing failed national Israel with Himself as the authentic source of spiritual fruitfulness. Believers become branches drawing life from Him.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged imagery) and **Forward-Looking** (prophetic texts anticipate restoration after judgment).

## Four-Step Application

**Step 1 - What You Must Do**: "Bear fruit in keeping with repentance" (Matthew 3:8). "By this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit and so prove to be my disciples" (John 15:8). Scripture commands fruit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control (Galatians 5:22-23); works of righteousness; evangelistic harvest; transformed character. The Vinedresser expects grapes, not wild grapes. Fruitlessness is not acceptable.

**Step 2 - Why You Cannot Do It**: But you are the problem, not the solution. Israel had every advantage: transplanted from Egypt, planted in fertile hill, cleared of stones, supplied with watchtower and winepress, tended by the perfect Vinedresser. Still they produced wild grapes. The vine itself was degenerate, not the care given to it. You cannot make yourself fruitful by trying harder. Jeremiah's diagnosis haunts you: "I planted you a choice vine, wholly of pure seed. How then have you turned degenerate?" The corruption is internal. Vine wood without fruit is worthless; your attempts to manufacture fruit are self-deceiving.

**Step 3 - How He Did It**: Jesus declares: "I am the true vine" (John 15:1). He IS what Israel failed to be. He produced perfect fruit: perfect love, perfect obedience, perfect righteousness. He lived the fruitful life the vineyard could not produce. And then He offers His fruitfulness to you: "Abide in me, and I in you... whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit" (John 15:4-5). The True Vine does not just model fruitfulness; He shares His fruitful life with all who are grafted into Him. You become a branch in the True Vine, drawing life from Him, bearing His fruit.

**Step 4 - How Through Him You Can**: Because you are united to the True Vine, fruit flows through you. The branch does not strain to produce grapes; it receives life from the vine and fruit appears. "Apart from me you can do nothing" means that with Him you can do everything (Philippians 4:13). Abiding is not working harder but remaining connected: staying in His Word, in prayer, in fellowship, in dependence. The Vinedresser prunes fruitful branches to make them more fruitful (John 15:2); even the painful pruning is evidence of life and promise of greater harvest. You are freed from producing fruit through self-effort and freed for receiving fruit through connection. The wild grapes of the old vine give way to the good grapes of the True Vine. And Isaiah's promise finds fulfillment: "Israel shall blossom and put forth shoots and fill the whole world with fruit" (Isaiah 27:6), as those grafted into Christ bear fruit in every nation.

## Lexicon Findings

The vine/vineyard trajectory exhibits remarkable lexical continuity across both Testaments. Hebrew גֶּפֶן (*gephen*, [[Lexicon/H1601-1700#H1612|H1612]]) denotes "vine" as a twining, bending plant—used literally and figuratively of Israel (Genesis 49:11, Psalm 80:8, Jeremiah 2:21). The specialized term שֹׂרֵק (*soreq*, [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8321|H8321]]) designates "choice vine," the richest purple-grape variety (Genesis 49:11, Jeremiah 2:21). For "vineyard," Hebrew employs כֶּרֶם (*kerem*, [[Lexicon/H3701-3800#H3754|H3754]]), appearing in Isaiah's Song (5:1-7) and the Pleasant Vineyard oracle (27:2-6). The LXX translates גֶּפֶן with ἄμπελος (*ampelos*, [[Lexicon/G201-300#G288|G288]]), a word denoting a coiling vine supported by a stake—the exact term Jesus appropriates in John 15:1. The LXX renders כֶּרֶם with ἀμπελών (*ampelōn*, [[Lexicon/G201-300#G290|G290]]), "vineyard," preserving agricultural imagery in Matthew 21:33. Jesus' claim to be ἡ ἄμπελος ἡ ἀληθινή (*hē ampelos hē alēthinē*)—"the TRUE vine" (John 15:1)—employs ἀληθινός (*alēthinos*, [[Lexicon/G201-300#G228|G228]]), meaning "genuine, real, not counterfeit," contrasting Christ's authentic fruitfulness with Israel's degenerate failure. Supporting vocabulary includes κλῆμα (*klēma*, [[Lexicon/G2801-2900#G2814|G2814]]), "branch/vine-shoot"; καρπός (*karpos*, [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2590|G2590]]), "fruit"; and μένω (*menō*, [[Lexicon/G3301-3400#G3306|G3306]]), "abide/remain," central to union with Christ.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: גֶּפֶן (*gephen*) - vine (Genesis 49:11, Psalm 80:8, Jeremiah 2:21); שֹׂרֵק (*soreq*) - choice vine (Genesis 49:11, Jeremiah 2:21); כֶּרֶם (*kerem*) - vineyard (Isaiah 5:1-7, 27:2-6)
- **LXX**: ἄμπελος (*ampelos*) - standard translation of גֶּפֶן, establishing continuity to NT usage; ἀμπελών (*ampelōn*) - vineyard
- **NT**: ἄμπελος (*ampelos*) - vine (Matthew 21:33, John 15:1, Revelation 14:17-20); ἀληθινός (*alēthinos*) - true/genuine (John 15:1), distinguishing Christ from failed Israel; κλῆμα (*klēma*) - branch (John 15:4-5); καρπός (*karpos*) - fruit (John 15:2-8); μένω (*menō*) - abide (John 15:4-10)
