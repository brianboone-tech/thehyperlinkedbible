## NAZIRITE VOW (SEPARATION UNTO GOD) TRAJECTORY TABLE

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/cULGeI3nH2E" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

▶️ [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/cULGeI3nH2E)

The Nazirite vow (נָזִיר, nāzîr, "separated, consecrated") stands as one of Scripture's most striking expressions of voluntary dedication to God—a time-bound commitment to holiness marked by three specific abstentions: no wine or strong drink, no cutting of hair, and no contact with dead bodies (Numbers 6:1-21). The term נָזִיר derives from the root נָזַר (nāzar, "to dedicate, consecrate, separate"), emphasizing the fundamental purpose: separation unto the LORD. Unlike the priesthood (which was hereditary and permanent), the Nazirite vow was voluntary and typically temporary—anyone, male or female, could take it for a specified period. The vow created a state of heightened holiness, bringing the individual into a priest-like status of consecration. The three prohibitions symbolized complete devotion: abstaining from wine (joy, celebration) meant finding joy in God alone; uncut hair (glory, strength) grew as a visible sign of the vow; avoiding corpses (death, defilement) demonstrated life-oriented purity. When the vow concluded, the Nazirite shaved their hair and offered it as a sacrifice—the culmination and consecration of the entire period (Numbers 6:18). This trajectory reveals God's desire for His people's wholehearted devotion and anticipates Christ, who was "separate from sinners" (Hebrews 7:26), perfectly consecrated, and who calls believers to present themselves as "living sacrifices, holy and acceptable to God" (Romans 12:1). The principle: voluntary separation from the world for dedication to God, temporary obedience pointing to eternal consecration. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted vow) and **Forward-Looking** (Hebrews 7:26 describes Christ as "separated from sinners," and believers are called to "be separate" in 2 Corinthians 6:17).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must be holy. "You shall be holy, for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16). You must be separated from sin, separated unto God. You must not be "conformed to this world" (Romans 12:2) but transformed. You must "cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God" (2 Corinthians 7:1). God calls for total consecration--not a partial, compromised dedication but wholehearted separation unto Him.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You cannot produce holiness through self-discipline. Samson was set apart from birth, empowered by the Spirit, and still broke every element of his vow. Your best efforts at separation will fail. You will touch unclean things--metaphorically and literally. You will seek the world's pleasures. You will compromise your consecration. Even if you could maintain external separation, your heart would remain impure: "Out of the heart come evil thoughts" (Matthew 15:19). The Nazirite vow was temporary because no one could maintain it permanently. The holiness God requires is not partial or temporary but complete and eternal--and you cannot achieve it.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ is "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens" (Hebrews 7:26). His separation was not external avoidance but internal purity. He ate with sinners without becoming sinful. He touched lepers and the dead without becoming defiled--instead, His holiness flowed outward to cleanse them. He "committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" (1 Peter 2:22). He "consecrated himself" (John 17:19) for our sake--setting Himself apart for the mission of redemption. And He declared, "For their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth" (John 17:19). His perfect separation sanctifies us.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Through Christ, you are set apart by His holiness, not your own. "You were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Corinthians 6:11). Now you can pursue holiness not as achievement but as response--"present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God" (Romans 12:1). The motivation is "by the mercies of God," not by the merit of self-discipline. The Spirit empowers what the flesh could never sustain. You "go out from among them and be separate" (2 Corinthians 6:17), but not to earn acceptance--you are already accepted. You separate from sin because you belong to Christ, not to prove that you do. What the Nazirite vow required temporarily, Christ accomplishes permanently. What Samson failed to maintain, Christ fulfilled perfectly. And what Samuel and John could only point toward, you now possess in Christ: true consecration, genuine holiness, and the Spirit who empowers you to live as one truly "separated unto God."

## Lexicon Findings

The Nazirite vow trajectory exhibits remarkable lexical continuity from Hebrew through the Septuagint to the Greek New Testament. The Hebrew root נָזַר (nāzar, H5144, "to dedicate, consecrate, separate") forms the foundation, generating the noun נָזִיר (nāzîr, H5139, "separated one, Nazirite") and נֶזֶר (nēzer, H5145, "consecration, crown, separation"). This semantic field emphasizes voluntary dedication to Yahweh through ritual separation from common life. In Numbers 6, the LXX consistently renders נָזִיר with Greek ἁγιάζω (hagiazō, G37, "to sanctify, set apart") and the cognate noun ἁγιασμός (hagiasmos, "sanctification"), along with related holiness terminology: ἅγιος (hagios, G40, "holy") and ἁγνίζω (hagnizō, "to purify"). The LXX translates the vow's purpose as "to purify oneself in purity" (Numbers 6:2), establishing that separation functions as sanctification, not mere isolation. Significantly, when Hebrews 7:26 describes Christ as κεχωρισμένος (kechōrismenos, perfect passive participle of χωρίζω, G5563, "separated"), it applies spatial separation vocabulary to moral-ethical purity—Christ is "separated from sinners" not geographically but ontologically. John 17:19's ἁγιάζω ("I consecrate myself") directly parallels the Nazirite's self-dedication language. Paul's exhortation to "be separate" (2 Corinthians 6:17, ἀφορίζω) continues this lexical thread. The trajectory demonstrates how voluntary separation (נָזַר/ἁγιάζω) progresses from external ritual observance to internal spiritual reality, finding ultimate fulfillment in Christ's perfect holiness and believers' positional sanctification.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: נָזַר (nāzar) - appears in Numbers 6:2-21, Judges 13:5, Amos 2:11-12
- **LXX**: ἁγιάζω (hagiazō) - standard translation for consecration terminology
- **NT**: ἁγιάζω (hagiazō), ἅγιος (hagios), κεχωρισμένος (kechōrismenos) - NT continuation
