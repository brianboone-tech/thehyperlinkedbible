## REMNANT (FAITHFUL FEW PRESERVED) TRAJECTORY TABLE

The remnant theme runs throughout Scripture as God's consistent pattern of preserving a faithful few through judgment. Even in the midst of widespread apostasy and divine judgment, God always ensures the survival of a believing remnant through whom His purposes continue. This trajectory reveals both God's holiness (judgment on sin is certain) and God's faithfulness (His covenant promises never fail completely). The remnant concept operates on multiple levels: (1) preservation through judgment—Noah's family through the flood, Lot from Sodom, the 7,000 who had not bowed to Baal; (2) continuation of covenant promises through the faithful few; (3) refinement and purification as judgment removes the unfaithful; (4) eschatological hope as the remnant becomes the nucleus of the restored people. The escalation is remarkable: Israel's remnant was physical and national; the church is a spiritual remnant from all nations. Israel's remnant survived earthly judgment; believers survive eternal judgment. The remnant principle culminates in the redeemed from every tribe and tongue (Revelation 7:9).

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged historical pattern) and **Forward-Looking** (prophetic remnant texts explicitly anticipate fulfillment).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must acknowledge that salvation belongs to God's preserving grace, not your achieving effort. You must stop taking credit for your faithfulness and recognize that remnant membership is pure gift. And you must hope in the God who preserves a people through every judgment.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You desperately want credit. When you look at those who have fallen away, you instinctively think, "I'm different. I'm stronger. I believed more genuinely." When you compare yourself to the "unfaithful," you feel superior. The idol of spiritual achievement runs deep in the religious heart.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ is the true Remnant—the one Israelite who perfectly kept covenant, who never bowed to Baal, who remained faithful when all others failed. He is the "holy seed" that survived every judgment. And all who are united to Him by faith share His preserved status. You are remnant not because of your faithfulness but because of His. You are preserved not by your grip on God but by His grip on you.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Because Christ is the faithful One, you can rest in Him. Because He kept you from bowing to Baal (even when you were inclined to bow), you can give Him thanks rather than taking credit. And because the remnant grows—Noah's eight becoming a multitude no one can number—you can have confidence that God's purposes never fail. The faithful few becomes the innumerable many. The stump produces a forest. And you, preserved by grace, get to be part of it—not because you deserved it but because God is faithful to His promises.

## Lexicon Findings

The remnant trajectory reveals profound lexical continuity across both Testaments. Hebrew שָׁאַר (sha'ar, H7604) forms the verbal root meaning "to remain, be left over," appearing throughout Old Testament remnant theology. Its derivative שְׁאֵרִית (she'erith, H7611) specifically denotes "remainder, remnant" used by Isaiah and Jeremiah for the preserved faithful. Another crucial Hebrew term, שָׂרִיד (sarid, H8300), emphasizes "survivor, that which remains" in Isaiah's Sodom comparison. Complementing these, פְּלֵיטָה (peleitah, H6413) denotes "escaped remnant, deliverance" used in Ezra's post-exilic confession. Isaiah's signature phrase זֶרַע קֹדֶשׁ (zera qodesh, H2233 + H6944) combines "seed" with "holy" to describe the preserved kernel carrying covenant identity forward.

The Septuagint translates these Hebrew remnant terms into Greek κατάλειμμα (kataleimma, G2640) and κατάλοιπος (kataloipos, G2645), both emphasizing what is "left remaining." Paul employs these LXX renderings when quoting Isaiah in Romans 9:27-29, using ὑπόλειμμα (hypoleimma, related to G5275) and σπέρμα (sperma, G4690—notably defined as "a remnant, as if kept for planting"). Romans 11:5 introduces λεῖμμα (leimma, G3005) for the present Jewish remnant. Remarkably, the grace foundation appears in both testaments: Hebrew חֵן (chen, H2580) meaning "favor, grace" parallels Greek χάρις (charis, G5485), establishing that remnant preservation operates "according to the election of grace" across all redemptive history.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: שָׁאַר/שְׁאֵרִית (*sha'ar/she'erith*) - appears in Genesis 7:23, 1 Kings 19:18, Isaiah 1:9, 6:13, 10:20-22, Jeremiah 23:3
