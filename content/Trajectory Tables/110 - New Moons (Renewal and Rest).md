## NEW MOONS (RENEWAL AND REST) TRAJECTORY TABLE

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/boRgUJOGdF4" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

▶️ [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/boRgUJOGdF4)

The observance of new moons marked the beginning of each month with special sacrifices, trumpet blasts, and rest from labor. These monthly celebrations reminded Israel of God's sovereignty over time and creation's rhythms. Mather writes: "The new moons were shadows pointing to that continual renewal which believers experience in Christ, who makes all things new. What the monthly cycle symbolized in creation's order, Christ accomplishes in redemption—perpetual renewal, fresh mercies, and the new creation inaugurated at His resurrection." Paul identifies new moons as 'shadows' pointing to Christ (Colossians 2:16-17). The trajectory moves from monthly new moon celebrations to Christ who is the reality these shadows prefigured, providing continual renewal and rest for His people. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted calendar observance) and **Forward-Looking** (Colossians 2:16-17 explicitly identifies new moons as "shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ").

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You need genuine renewal—not just better behavior but actual transformation. Not just modified externals but a new heart, a renewed mind, a fresh spirit. You need what the new moons symbolized: real fresh starts that don't fade, genuine newness that doesn't decay back into oldness.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

Self-renewal is a contradiction. The self that needs renewing is the self attempting the renewal. It's like trying to perform surgery on your own hands. Every resolution is made by the same old heart. Every fresh start carries the same old patterns. The new moon would rise, and by month's end, Israel was back to the same old sins. That's why the sacrifices had to be repeated monthly. That's why the resolutions keep failing. You cannot make yourself new.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ entered creation's rhythms—born under the law, observing new moons and Sabbaths and festivals. But He didn't just keep the calendar; He fulfilled what the calendar taught. He died and rose—the ultimate fresh start, the definitive new beginning. His resurrection inaugurated the new creation. Now He's seated on the throne declaring: "Behold, I am making all things new" (Revelation 21:5). Not "I made" (past only) or "I will make" (future only) but "I am making"—present, ongoing, continuous. The renewal began at His resurrection and continues until the consummation.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

If you are in Christ, you are already a new creation. That's your status—settled, definitive, irreversible. And you are being renewed—progressively, daily, by the Spirit. "Be transformed by the renewal of your mind" (Romans 12:2). "Put on the new self, created after the likeness of God" (Ephesians 4:24). You're not generating renewal; you're living out an identity already given. You don't need monthly resolutions that fail monthly. You have daily mercies that are "new every morning." You don't exhaust yourself in self-improvement; you rest in Christ while the Spirit transforms you from glory to glory. The shadow has given way to the substance. Stop staring at the moon and worship the Son.

## Lexicon Findings

The New Moons trajectory demonstrates remarkable lexical continuity across testaments through the Hebrew root חדשׁ (*chadash*) and its Greek counterparts. The foundational Hebrew term חֹדֶשׁ (*chodesh*, [[Lexicon/H2301-2400#H2320|H2320]]) designates both "new moon" and "month," derived from the primitive root חָדַשׁ (*chadash*, [[Lexicon/H2301-2400#H2318|H2318]]) meaning "to be new, renew, repair." This root also generates the adjective חָדָשׁ (*chadash*, [[Lexicon/H2301-2400#H2319|H2319]]), meaning "new, fresh," which appears in Isaiah 65:17's "new heavens and new earth" and Lamentations 3:23's mercies "new every morning" (חֲדָשִׁים לַבְּקָרִים, *chadashim labboqarim*). The LXX translates חֹדֶשׁ with νουμηνία (*noumēnia*, [[Lexicon/G3501-3600#G3561|G3561]]), a compound of νέος (*neos*, "new") and μήν (*mēn*, "moon"). Paul's critical text in Colossians 2:16-17 employs this precise term (νεομηνίας), explicitly identifying new moons as shadows fulfilled in Christ. The NT renewal language utilizes καινός (*kainos*, [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2537|G2537]]), denoting qualitative newness and freshness, in phrases like "new creation" (καινὴ κτίσις, 2 Corinthians 5:17) and renewal verbs ἀνακαινίζω (*anakainizō*, [[Lexicon/G301-400#G340|G340]]) and ἀνακαινόω (*anakainoō*, [[Lexicon/G301-400#G341|G341]]) in Romans 12:2 and Ephesians 4:23-24. This lexical thread—from monthly lunar renewal (*chodesh*) through prophetic new creation promises (*chadash*) to Christological perpetual newness (*kainos*)—traces the trajectory from shadow to substance.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: חֹדֶשׁ (*chodesh*) - new moon, month; appears in Numbers 28:11-15, Psalm 81:3
- **Hebrew**: חָדָשׁ (*chadash* adj.) - new, fresh; appears in Isaiah 65:17, Lamentations 3:23
- **Hebrew**: חָדַשׁ (*chadash* verb) - to renew, make new; foundational renewal concept
- **Hebrew**: חֶסֶד (*chesed*) - steadfast love, covenant faithfulness; appears in Lamentations 3:22 with renewal theme
- **LXX**: νουμηνία (*noumēnia*) - new moon festival; standard translation of חֹדֶשׁ
- **NT**: νουμηνία (*noumēnia*) - appears in Colossians 2:16 as explicit shadow-substance indicator
- **NT**: καινός (*kainos*) - new, fresh, qualitatively novel; appears in 2 Corinthians 5:17, Revelation 21:5
- **NT**: ἀνακαινόω (*anakainoō*) - to renew; appears in Romans 12:2, 2 Corinthians 4:16, Ephesians 4:23
