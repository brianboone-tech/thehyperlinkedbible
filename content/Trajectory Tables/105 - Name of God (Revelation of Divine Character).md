The name of God in Scripture is far more than a label—it is the revelation of His character, presence, and covenant commitment. In ancient Near Eastern thought, a name embodied the essence and authority of the person; to know someone's name was to have access to them, and to invoke their name was to call upon their power and character. When God revealed His name YHWH (יְהוָה, the Tetragrammaton) to Moses—"I AM WHO I AM" (אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה, ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh)—He was not merely providing identification but disclosing His self-existent, covenant-keeping nature: "This is my name forever, and thus I am to be remembered throughout all generations" (Exodus 3:14-15). Throughout the OT, God progressively revealed His character through compound names: YHWH-Jireh ("The LORD will provide"), YHWH-Nissi ("The LORD is my banner"), YHWH-Shalom ("The LORD is peace"), YHWH-Rohi ("The LORD is my shepherd"), each unveiling another facet of His nature. The name was so sacred that the high priest pronounced it only once annually on the Day of Atonement in the Holy of Holies. God caused His name to "dwell" in the tabernacle and temple (Deuteronomy 12:11; 1 Kings 8:29), signifying His covenantal presence with Israel. The trajectory reveals that Jesus is the ultimate revelation of God's name—He came "in His Father's name" (John 5:43), revealed the Father's name to His disciples (John 17:6), and received "the name that is above every name" (Philippians 2:9). What was hidden, restricted, and mediated in the OT becomes fully disclosed in Christ: "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). To know Christ is to know God's name, character, and very essence. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted self-revelation) and **Forward-Looking** (John 17:6, 26 records Jesus explicitly claiming to have revealed the Father's name, and Philippians 2:9-11 declares Jesus has received "the name that is above every name").

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You need to know God's name—not as a theological concept to master but as a personal revelation to receive. The name hidden for millennia has been fully disclosed in Jesus Christ. Your access to God depends not on religious sophistication but on receiving the One who reveals the Father.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You keep trying to approach God through knowledge acquisition. You study to feel secure. You master doctrines to have leverage. You accumulate theological information as if it were currency. But God cannot be mastered. His name is not a formula that gives you control—it's a Person who demands surrender. All your theological knowledge, however accurate, cannot pierce the veil. You need a mediator, a revealer, someone who has seen the Father's face.

### 3. How He Did It

Jesus "made known" the Father's name. The verb in John 1:18 is exegeomai—He "exegeted" God, explained Him, made Him accessible. What was hidden in the Holy of Holies is now revealed in a human face. The name spoken once yearly by one priest is now spoken over all who trust Christ. Jesus didn't just tell us information about God; He is the revelation of God. To see Him is to see the Father (John 14:9).

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Because Christ has revealed the Father's name, you can now approach God directly. You pray "in Jesus' name"—not as a magical formula but as a claim on His revealing mediation. Baptism is "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit"—marking you as one who belongs to the revealed God. And in the new creation, "his name will be on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4)—what was hidden becomes your permanent identity. The name once veiled behind curtains is now inscribed on you forever. You don't just know about God's name; you bear it.

## Lexicon Findings

The trajectory reveals profound lexical continuity connecting Hebrew revelation to NT fulfillment. The divine name YHWH (H3068, *Yəhôvâh*) derives from the Hebrew verb H1961 (*hāyâh*, "to be"), establishing God's self-existence and eternal presence. At the burning bush, God declared "I AM WHO I AM" (*ʾehyeh ʾăšer ʾehyeh*), linking His name eternally to the verb "to be." The Hebrew term for "name," H8034 (*šēm*), signified not mere identification but character, authority, and accessible presence—God's name "dwelt" in the tabernacle and temple. The Septuagint translators rendered YHWH as G2962 (*kyrios*, "Lord"), and "name" as G3686 (*onoma*), preserving the theological weight. Remarkably, Jesus adopted the "I AM" formula using G1473 (*egō*, "I") + G1510 (*eimi*, "am") in declarations like John 6:20, 8:28, and 18:5-6, explicitly identifying Himself with the God who revealed His name to Moses. The lexical thread runs: Hebrew H1961 → YHWH (H3068) → LXX G2962 (*kyrios*) → NT application to Jesus as *kyrios*, demonstrating that the name once restricted to Israel's sanctuary now resides fully in Christ. Where Moses heard the divine name from fire, believers receive the name above every name (Philippians 2:9-11), written eternally on redeemed foreheads (Revelation 22:4).
