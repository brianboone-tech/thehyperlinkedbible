The Passover stands as one of Scripture's most explicit and detailed types, commemorating Israel's deliverance from Egypt through the blood of the lamb. On the night when God's judgment struck down every firstborn in Egypt, the Israelites were spared by the blood of a lamb applied to their doorposts—the Lord "passed over" houses marked with blood, sparing those within from the destroyer. The lamb had to be without blemish (prefiguring Christ's sinlessness), slain at twilight on the 14th of Nisan (the very day and hour Christ died), its blood providing protection from judgment, its flesh providing nourishment for the journey, and not a bone of it was to be broken (fulfilled literally at Christ's crucifixion, John 19:36). Paul provides the definitive interpretation: "Christ our Passover lamb has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7). The trajectory moves from Israel's physical deliverance from Egypt through blood to the spiritual deliverance from sin and death through Christ's blood. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely commanded institution) and **Forward-Looking** (embedded expectational force through annual commemoration and prophetic lamb imagery).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must take shelter under the blood of the Lamb. You must stop trusting in your own merit and trust entirely in Christ's substitutionary death.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You keep wanting to contribute something—your sincerity, your effort, your growth, your faith (as if faith itself saves rather than the One in whom you place faith). You're terrified of grace-alone because it strips you of all control, all credit, all bargaining position with God.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ became the unblemished Lamb—sinless, perfect, spotless. He died at Passover, at the hour of lamb-slaughter, fulfilling details preserved for 1,400 years. His blood was shed not for national deliverance from Egypt but for cosmic deliverance from sin, death, and hell. Not a bone broken. The slain Lamb standing in heaven's throne room.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

"Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." It's done. The blood has been applied. God sees the blood and passes over you—not because of your performance but because of Christ's. Now "let us celebrate the festival... with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Cor 5:8)—not to earn salvation but because salvation has been accomplished. Holy living flows from security, not anxiety.

## Lexicon Findings

The Passover trajectory displays remarkable lexical continuity from Hebrew Exodus vocabulary through LXX translation to NT fulfillment. The core Hebrew term פֶּסַח (*pesach*, H6453) denotes "passing over" and transliterates directly into Greek as πάσχα (*pascha*, G3957), preserving both phonetic and semantic content across testaments. The lamb imagery centers on שֶׂה (*seh*, H7716) meaning "sheep/goat of the flock," translated in the LXX and rendered in NT Greek through ἀμνός (*amnos*, G286, "lamb") in John 1:29 and ἀρνίον (*arnion*, G721, "little lamb") in Revelation 5. Blood theology connects דָּם (*dam*, H1818) with αἷμα (*haima*, G129), both denoting sacrificial blood securing atonement. The covenant formula τὸ αἷμά μου τῆς διαθήκης (*"blood of my covenant"*, G129/G1242) in Mark 14:24 directly echoes Exodus 24:8 LXX vocabulary. The unbroken bones motif links עֶצֶם (*etsem*, H6106, "bone") with ὀστέον (*osteon*, G3747), creating a precise typological marker from Exodus 12:46 to John 19:36. Isaiah's guilt offering אָשָׁם (*asham*, H817) integrates Levitical sacrifice with Passover lamb, anticipating Christ's comprehensive atoning work. This lexical web demonstrates intentional verbal continuity establishing Christ as the antitype fulfilling all Passover symbolism.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: פֶּסַח (*pesach*) - appears in Exodus 12:11, 21, 27, 43; Numbers 9:2-14
- **LXX**: πάσχα (*pascha*) - standard translation maintaining Hebrew phonetics
- **NT**: πάσχα (*pascha*) - continues in 1 Corinthians 5:7, John 19:14
