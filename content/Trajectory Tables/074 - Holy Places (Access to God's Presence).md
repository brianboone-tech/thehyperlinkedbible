## HOLY PLACES (ACCESS TO GOD'S PRESENCE) TRAJECTORY TABLE

God's command that Israel worship only at the place He chooses (Hebrew: קֹדֶשׁ, qōḏeš and קֹדֶשׁ הַקֳּדָשִׁים, qōḏeš haqqŏḏāšîm) established the principle of divinely appointed access to His presence. This restricted access taught that fallen humanity cannot approach God on their own terms or in their own way. The trajectory from local altars to the central tabernacle to Solomon's temple to Christ's body reveals God's plan: He designates the place and means of worship, ultimately providing His Son as the true meeting place between God and man. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely commanded central sanctuary) and **Forward-Looking** (John 2:19-21; Hebrews 9:11-12).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must approach God through Jesus. Not around Him. Not beside Him. Not through any other mediator or method. He is the way, the truth, and the life. Just as God designated one place for Israel's worship, He designates one Person as the means of access to Himself. The exclusivity of holy places points to the exclusivity of Christ.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

Your heart keeps reverting to the old system—religious rituals, spiritual techniques, holy feelings as prerequisites for access. You think you need to get cleaned up before approaching, when the whole point is that Jesus' blood cleanses you as you approach. You're still trying to earn what can only be received. You want to climb the ladder of holy places rather than enter through the torn veil.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ became the true temple—"the Word became flesh and dwelt (tabernacled) among us" (John 1:14). He didn't just visit the holy place; He became it. And then He became the sacrifice, the high priest, and the torn veil all at once. His body was broken—the curtain of His flesh torn—opening "a new and living way" into God's presence. He entered "the greater and more perfect tent not made with hands" (Hebrews 9:11) through His own blood. He didn't improve the access system; He replaced it entirely. He sat down at God's right hand because access is permanently secured.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Now enter with confidence. "We have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus" (Hebrews 10:19). Stop trying to earn access and simply use the access Christ secured. Moreover, you are now God's temple—His Spirit dwells in you (1 Corinthians 3:16). You don't visit God's dwelling; you are God's dwelling. The trajectory ends in Revelation 21:22: no temple in the New Jerusalem, "for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb." The entire city is holy place. All access barriers finally, permanently, gloriously removed. What holy places promised, Christ accomplished, and the new creation consummates: God with us, unbarred and eternal.

## Lexicon Findings

This trajectory traces a tight lexical network showing how divine presence moved from geographic location to incarnate Person to indwelt community. The Hebrew foundation rests on three core terms: **māqôm** (H4725, "place") designates God's chosen dwelling location, **bayt** (H1004, "house") denotes the temple structure, and **qōḏeš** (H6944, "holiness/sanctuary") marks set-apart sacred space. Deuteronomy's repeated phrase "the **place** (*māqôm*) where the LORD will **choose** (*bāḥar*, H977) to make His **name** (*šēm*, H8034) **dwell** (*šākan*, H7931)" establishes covenant vocabulary linking geographic centralization to divine self-revelation. The LXX translates *qōḏeš* as **hágios** (G40, "holy") and *bayt* as **oîkos** (G3624, "house"), creating lexical continuity. The NT radically reapplies this vocabulary: **naós** (G3485, "temple sanctuary") shifts from Jerusalem's building to Christ's **sōma** (G4983, "body") in John 2:19-21, then to believers corporately (1 Cor 3:16) and individually (1 Cor 6:19). Revelation 21:22 completes the trajectory—*ouk eîdon naón* ("I saw no temple")—because God and the Lamb themselves become the sanctuary. The lexical progression maps redemptive-historical movement: designated location → embodied Person → Spirit-indwelt people → eternal presence without mediation.

**Key Lexical Threads:**

**Hebrew:**
- **māqôm** (H4725) - "place, location" - appears throughout Deuteronomy 12, 1 Kings 8, designating God's chosen worship site
- **bayt** (H1004) - "house, temple" - Davidic promise (2 Sam 7:13), temple dedication (1 Kgs 8:29), Christological fulfillment (John 2:19)
- **qōḏeš** (H6944) - "holiness, sanctuary, sacred place" - foundational term for set-apart space where God dwells
- **bāḥar** (H977) - "to choose, elect" - divine selection of worship location (Deut 12:5; 1 Kgs 8:16)
- **šēm** (H8034) - "name" - God's reputation/presence dwelling at chosen place (Deut 12:5; 1 Kgs 8:29)
- **šākan** (H7931) - "to dwell, tabernacle" - God residing at designated sanctuary (Deut 12:11; related to *mishkan*)

**LXX Translation:**
- **hágios** (G40) - standard LXX rendering of *qōḏeš* - "holy, sacred, set apart"
- **oîkos** (G3624) - LXX translation of *bayt* - "house, dwelling, temple"

**NT Greek:**
- **naós** (G3485) - "temple sanctuary" (inner shrine, Holy Place/Most Holy) - applied to Christ's body (John 2:19-21), believers (1 Cor 3:16; 6:19), absent in new creation (Rev 21:22)
- **sōma** (G4983) - "body" - Christ's resurrection body becomes true temple (John 2:21), church as body-temple (1 Cor 6:19)
- **oîkos** (G3624) - "house, household" - Ephesians 2:19 ("household of God"), 1 Peter 2:5 ("spiritual house")
