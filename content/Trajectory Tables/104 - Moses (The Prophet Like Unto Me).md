## MOSES (THE PROPHET LIKE UNTO ME) TRAJECTORY TABLE

▶️ [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/8tR-X6NSlC8)

Moses towers as perhaps the greatest individual type of Christ in the Old Testament. God Himself declared, 'I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee' (Deuteronomy 18:18), a prophecy definitively applied to Christ by Peter (Acts 3:22-23). The parallels are extensive: both faced death as infants through a tyrant's decree yet were miraculously preserved; both emerged as deliverers of God's people from bondage; both served as mediators of covenant, lawgivers, and intercessors; both performed mighty signs and wonders. Yet the superiority of Christ over Moses is equally emphasized: 'Moses was faithful as a servant... but Christ as a son over his own house' (Hebrews 3:5-6). Where Moses brought Israel out of Egypt and gave the law written on stone, Christ delivers from spiritual bondage and writes the law on hearts. Where Moses could not enter Canaan due to one sin, Christ's perfect obedience leads His people into eternal rest. This is a **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged person) and **Forward-Looking** (Deuteronomy 18:15-19 explicitly prophesies a coming Prophet).

## Canonical Intertextuality Pairs

### OT to OT


**02 - Exodus**

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19 to Deuteronomy 4.5-5\|Exodus 19 to Deuteronomy 4.5-5]] - Exodus 19 describes Sinai theophany where God revealed law through Moses; Deuteronomy 4:5 retrospectively affirms "I have taught you statutes and rules, as the LORD my God commanded me." This pair establishes Moses as mediator between God and Israel, receiving divine words and transmitting them—core to Deuteronomy 18:18's "I will put my words in his mouth." HIGH relevance: demonstrates Moses' mediatorial prophetic function, foundational to prophet-like-Moses typology, pointing to Christ who speaks only Father's words (John 12:49).

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19 to Deuteronomy 4.5\|Exodus 19 to Deuteronomy 4.5]] - Same connection as previous, narrower Deuteronomy reference. Establishes Moses receiving and teaching divine law as paradigm for prophetic office. The trajectory from Moses-as-lawgiver to Christ-as-final-revealer (Hebrews 1:1-2) is central to this typology. HIGH relevance: directly demonstrates vocabulary of divine words placed in prophet's mouth, mediatorial function between God and people.

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20.19 to Deuteronomy 18.15-18\|Exodus 20.19 to Deuteronomy 18.15-18]] - **CRITICAL:** HIGH CONNECTION. Exodus 20:19 records Israel's fear-driven request at Sinai: "You speak to us, and we will listen; but do not let God speak to us, lest we die." Deuteronomy 18:15-18 explicitly references this Horeb event as basis for prophet-like-Moses promise. The people's request for mediator (Exodus 20:19) receives divine approval and promissory expansion (Deuteronomy 18:16-18). This pair is foundational to entire trajectory: Israel's need for mediator → God's promise of ultimate Prophet → NT fulfillment in Christ. Uses core vocabulary: prophet (נָבִיא), hearing (שָׁמַע), mediatorial function.

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20.19 to Deuteronomy 18.15\|Exodus 20.19 to Deuteronomy 18.15]] - Same critical connection, narrower Deuteronomy reference. Establishes mediatorial necessity and prophetic promise as direct OT-to-OT development. HIGH relevance: uses exact vocabulary (נָבִיא, "like me"), directly cited in Acts 3:22-23, 7:37.

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32.10 to Numbers 14.12\|Exodus 32.10 to Numbers 14.12]] - Both texts record God's threat to destroy Israel and make Moses into great nation; both occasions show Moses' intercession. This pair demonstrates Moses' intercessory pattern—refusing personal exaltation, pleading for people's preservation. HIGH relevance: Moses' intercessory mediation prefigures Christ's superior intercession (Hebrews 7:25). While lacking explicit נָבִיא vocabulary, intercession is core to mediatorial prophetic office, and Christ fulfills as ultimate Mediator (1 Timothy 2:5).

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32.10-13 to Numbers 14.12-19\|Exodus 32.10-13 to Numbers 14.12-19]] - Expanded version of previous pair, including full intercession texts. Moses' pattern of mediating God's wrath through intercession based on covenant promises and God's reputation among nations demonstrates prophetic-mediatorial function. HIGH relevance: establishes intercessory template Christ fulfills supremely, though lacks explicit Deuteronomy 18 vocabulary.

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33.11 to Deuteronomy 34.10-12\|Exodus 33.11 to Deuteronomy 34.10-12]] - **CRITICAL:** HIGH CONNECTION. Exodus 33:11 states "the LORD spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend"; Deuteronomy 34:10-12 concludes Torah with "there has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face." This pair establishes Moses' unique prophetic intimacy with God (פָּנִים אֶל־פָּנִים, panim el-panim), unmatched in OT, creating expectation for Deuteronomy 18:15's promised Prophet. The "face to face" vocabulary is foundational to Moses-Christ typology: Moses knew God face-to-face but couldn't see His face (Exodus 33:20); Christ dwells eternally "in the Father's bosom" (John 1:18). Uses core vocabulary: prophet (נָבִיא), know (יָדַע), face (פָּנִים).

- [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33.11 to Deuteronomy 34.10\|Exodus 33.11 to Deuteronomy 34.10]] - Same critical connection, narrower Deuteronomy reference. Establishes Moses' unparalleled OT prophetic status, creating eschatological expectation for greater Prophet. HIGH vocabulary match: נָבִיא, יָדַע, פָּנִים.

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must listen to Jesus as the Prophet like Moses but greater than Moses. His words are God's words placed in His mouth (John 12:49, fulfilling Deuteronomy 18:18). You cannot selectively listen—"every soul who does not listen to that prophet shall be destroyed" (Acts 3:23).

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You keep wanting religious leaders you can control—prophets who confirm what you already believe, authorities who validate your existing commitments. True listening requires submission, and submission feels like death to your autonomous self. You'd rather be your own prophet.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ came as the Prophet like Moses but infinitely greater. Where Moses mediated God's word externally on tablets of stone, Christ writes God's law on human hearts through the Spirit (2 Corinthians 3:3). Where Moses' glory faded, Christ's glory increases. Where Moses could not enter rest due to one sin, Christ's perfect obedience secured eternal rest for all who trust Him. He is the Word incarnate (John 1:14)—not merely speaking God's words but being God's Word.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Because Christ is the greater Prophet, your relationship with God doesn't depend on your perfect listening but on His perfect speaking. When you fail to hear, He intercedes (Hebrews 7:25). When you disobey, His obedience covers you. Your posture shifts from anxious striving ("Am I listening well enough?") to restful attention ("He will make me hear"). Moses pointed forward; Christ points back to His finished work and forward to your final rest. Listen to Him—not because your listening earns salvation, but because the One who speaks creates what He commands in those who trust Him.

## Lexicon Findings

The Moses-Christ typology rests on a precise lexical foundation spanning Hebrew, Septuagint, and Greek. The prophetic promise uses נָבִיא (*nabi*, [[Lexicon/H5001-5100#H5030|H5030]]) meaning "prophet/spokesman," rendered προφήτης (*prophetes*, [[Lexicon/G4301-4400#G4396|G4396]]) in both LXX and NT quotations. God's promise to "raise up" (קוּם, *qum*, [[Lexicon/H6901-7000#H6965|H6965]]) becomes ἀναστήσω (*anasteso*, [[Lexicon/G401-500#G450|G450]]) in Acts 3:22 and 7:37, connecting divine commissioning with resurrection terminology. The command to "hear/obey" (שָׁמַע, *shama*, [[Lexicon/H8001-8100#H8085|H8085]]) translates to ἀκούω (*akouo*, [[Lexicon/G101-200#G191|G191]]), maintaining the dual sense of auditory perception and covenantal obedience. The "word/words" placed in the prophet's mouth (דָּבָר, *davar*, [[Lexicon/H1601-1700#H1697|H1697]]) becomes λόγος (*logos*, [[Lexicon/G3001-3100#G3056|G3056]]) in John 12:49, culminating in Christ as incarnate Word (John 1:14). Hebrews establishes servant-son distinction through πιστός (*pistos*, [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4103|G4103]], "faithful"), θεράπων (*therapon*, [[Lexicon/G2301-2400#G2324|G2324]], "servant"), and υἱός (*huios*, [[Lexicon/G5201-5300#G5207|G5207]], "son"). Paul's Moses-Christ glory comparison employs δόξα (*doxa*, [[Lexicon/G1301-1400#G1391|G1391]]) to contrast fading Mosaic radiance with permanent new covenant transformation.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: נָבִיא (*nabi*) prophet - Deuteronomy 18:15,18; קוּם (*qum*) raise up - Deuteronomy 18:15,18; שָׁמַע (*shama*) hear/obey - Exodus 20:19, Deuteronomy 18:15; דָּבָר (*davar*) word - Deuteronomy 18:18
- **LXX**: προφήτης (*prophetes*) standard translation of נָבִיא; ἀναστήσω (*anasteso*) for קוּם; ἀκούσετε (*akousete*) for שָׁמַע
- **NT**: προφήτης (*prophetes*) - Acts 3:22, 7:37, John 1:21, 6:14; ἀναστήσει (*anastesei*) - Acts 3:22, 7:37; ἀκούω (*akouo*) - Acts 3:22-23; λόγος (*logos*) - John 12:49; πιστός (*pistos*), θεράπων (*therapon*), υἱός (*huios*) - Hebrews 3:2-6; δόξα (*doxa*) - 2 Corinthians 3:7-18
