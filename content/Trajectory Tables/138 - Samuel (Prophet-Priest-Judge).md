Samuel (שְׁמוּאֵל, šəmûʾēl, "heard by God" or "name of God") stands as one of Scripture's most significant transitional figures—the last judge, the first prophet after Moses, and a priest who combined all three offices in anticipation of Christ. Born miraculously to barren Hannah in answer to prayer (1 Samuel 1), Samuel was dedicated to the LORD from birth and raised in the tabernacle under Eli the priest. God called him as a boy (1 Samuel 3:1-21), establishing him as a prophet whose words never failed (3:19). As judge, Samuel led Israel for decades, delivered them from Philistine oppression, and administered justice throughout the land (7:15-17). As priest, he offered sacrifices and interceded for the nation (7:9; 12:23). As prophet, he anointed Israel's first two kings—Saul and David—transitioning Israel from the period of judges to the monarchy (1 Samuel 10; 16). Samuel's unique combination of offices was unprecedented and unrepeated until Christ, who is the ultimate Prophet (revealing God perfectly), Priest (offering the final sacrifice and interceding eternally), and King (ruling God's kingdom forever). The trajectory demonstrates that the three offices were always meant to converge in one person—the Messiah—and that partial combinations in the OT (Samuel, Moses, David) pointed forward to perfect union in Christ. Samuel also typifies the pattern of miraculous birth to barren women (Isaac, Samson, John the Baptist, Jesus), demonstrating that God's redemptive work begins with divine intervention, not human ability. This is a **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged prophet-priest-judge) and **Forward-Looking** (Acts 3:24 identifies Samuel as first in the line of prophets who "proclaimed these days" of the Messiah, and Hebrews 7:24-25 shows Christ's permanent priesthood surpassing all human priestly ministry).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must receive Christ in all three of His offices—as Prophet who speaks God's final word, as Priest who bears your sins and intercedes for you eternally, as King who has all authority. You cannot accept one while rejecting or ignoring another.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You keep trying to separate the offices. You want a Christ who gives you good advice (prophet) but don't want Him exposing your sin. You want a Christ who forgives (priest) but don't want to deal with the gravity of what required His blood. You want a Christ who blesses and protects (king) but don't want to submit to His lordship over your choices. You want a Savior you can manage, a Mediator whose mediation is partial and optional.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ converged all three offices in Himself—perfectly and eternally. "Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son"—Prophet. "He holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever"—Priest. "On his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and Lord of lords"—King. Samuel held the offices together temporarily and imperfectly; Christ holds them together forever and completely. He offered the final sacrifice, speaks the final word, and reigns on the final throne.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

United to Christ, you now share in His offices. You are a prophet who proclaims God's word. You are a priest who offers spiritual sacrifices and intercedes for others. You are royalty who will reign with Him. Samuel's unique ministry—combining offices no one else could—finds its fulfillment in Christ and its extension in you. You don't need to find separate mediators for truth, forgiveness, and security. You have one Mediator, and through Him you have all three—now and forever.

## Lexicon Findings

Samuel's threefold office trajectory reveals deep lexical continuity from Hebrew Scripture through the Septuagint to New Testament Greek, demonstrating divinely orchestrated typological correspondence. The Hebrew name שְׁמוּאֵל (*šəmûʾēl*, H8050) derives from שָׁמַע (*shāmaʿ*, H8085, "heard") and אֵל (*ʾēl*, H410, "God"), establishing Samuel's identity as "heard by God"—the prophet who both hears God and is heard by God in intercession. This hearing motif connects directly to the prophetic office. The LXX renders his name as Σαμουήλ (*Samouēl*, G4545), maintaining the phonetic connection and theological significance. Samuel's prophetic role (נָבִיא, *nābîʾ*, H5030) finds NT continuation in προφήτης (*prophētēs*, G4396), with Acts 3:24 explicitly linking Samuel to the prophetic line culminating in Christ. The priestly dimension emerges through כֹּהֵן (*kōhēn*, H3548), transliterated as ἱερεύς (*hiereús*, G2409) in the LXX and applied to Christ as ἀρχιερεύς (*archiereús*, G749, "high priest") in Hebrews 7:24-25. Samuel's intercessory prayer (פָּלַל, *pālal*, H6419, "intercede") anticipates Christ's eternal mediation (ἐντυγχάνω, *entynchanō*, G1793; μεσίτης, *mesitēs*, G3316, "mediator"). The anointing theme connects Hebrew מָשַׁח (*māšaḥ*, H4886) to Greek χρίω (*chriō*, G5548), uniting Samuel's anointing of kings with God's anointing of Jesus (Acts 10:38), the ultimate Χριστός (*Christos*, "Anointed One"). This lexical thread—from hearing/anointing in Hebrew to fulfillment/mediation in Greek—demonstrates Scripture's organic unity pointing to Christ's convergent offices.
