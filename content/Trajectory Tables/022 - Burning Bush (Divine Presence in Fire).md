## BURNING BUSH (DIVINE PRESENCE IN FIRE) TRAJECTORY TABLE


The burning bush (סְנֶה, seneh) is one of Scripture's most evocative occasional types—a bush ablaze with fire yet unconsumed. As Samuel Mather observed, this was "a most lively Emblem of the Churches subsisting in the Fire of Persecution through the presence and good will of him that dwelt in the burning Bush." The paradox is profound: fire typically destroys, yet this bush was not consumed because God was in it. This typifies the Church throughout history—afflicted but not destroyed, persecuted but not abandoned, burning but not consumed. The Angel of the LORD (a theophany) appeared in the flame, revealing that divine presence transforms fire from destroyer to preserver. Israel in the "iron furnace" of Egypt, the three youths in Nebuchadnezzar's furnace, the early Church under Roman persecution, believers throughout history—all are bushes burning but not consumed because God dwells within them. The trajectory culminates in the consuming fire that does not harm those who dwell in God (Hebrews 12:29).

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged vision) and **Forward-Looking** (the pattern is explicitly applied to the Church's experience of persecution with divine preservation).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must have God's presence with you--not just divine ideas about you, not merely religious feelings within you, but the actual presence of the living God. You must survive encounters with holiness. You must pass through fire--the trials and persecutions and refining processes of life--without being consumed. You need what the three men had in the furnace: a fourth figure walking with them, so that "the fire had no power over their bodies" (Daniel 3:27). You must be a bush that burns but is not consumed.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You cannot generate divine fire. The bush did not set itself ablaze--God appeared within it. You cannot make yourself survive what should consume you--the furnace was heated seven times hotter, yet Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego emerged without even the smell of smoke. Your religious passion, however fervent, cannot create divine presence. Your spiritual intensity, however sustained, cannot make you fireproof. "Our God is a consuming fire" (Hebrews 12:29)--and you are combustible. Moses hid his face, Isaiah cried "Woe is me!", John fell at Christ's feet "as though dead." Sinful humanity cannot survive unmediated holiness. The fire is real, and by yourself, you would be consumed.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ is the true burning bush--"in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" (Colossians 2:9). Divine fire in human nature, yet humanity is not destroyed. He is the Angel of the LORD who appeared in the flame--the pre-incarnate Son, now incarnate, dwelling among sinful people without consuming them. At Pentecost, tongues of fire rested on the disciples (Acts 2:3), and they were filled with the Spirit--not destroyed but empowered. The fire that should consume now indwells. Christ's presence transforms the nature of the fire for those who are His: what would destroy becomes what preserves, what would consume becomes what refines, what would judge becomes what saves.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Through Christ, you become a burning bush--the location of divine fire, burning but not consumed. "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon you" (1 Peter 4:14). The fiery trial that comes upon you (1 Peter 4:12) does not destroy because God dwells within you. The church throughout history has been the burning bush: "All along under the New Testament this Bush hath been burning in several Fires, and yet not consumed" (Mather). Persecution, affliction, trial--the fires have raged, but the bush remains. "We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed... persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed" (2 Corinthians 4:8-9). And the trajectory ends when there are no more fires: "He will wipe away every tear... neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore" (Revelation 21:4). The bush that burned unconsumed now rests eternally in God's presence--no more trial, no more refining fire, only the consuming fire transformed into eternal light and warmth and presence without threat.

## Lexicon Findings

The burning bush trajectory traces a profound lexical continuity from Hebrew to Greek, demonstrating how divine presence transforms fire's destructive nature into preserving power. In Exodus 3:2, the Hebrew seneh (סְנֶה, H5572, "thornbush") becomes LXX batos (βάτος, G942, "bramble bush")—both emphasizing the lowly, combustible nature of the bush that paradoxically survives divine fire. The core tension appears in the Hebrew phrase "not being consumed" (אֵינֶנּוּ אֻכָּל, from 'akal, H398, "to eat/consume"), rendered in LXX as ou katakaio (οὐ κατακαίω, G2618, "not burn down/incinerate")—the intensified form emphasizing complete destruction that did not occur. The Hebrew 'esh (אֵשׁ, H784, "fire") translates consistently to Greek pyr (πῦρ, G4442), the root appearing in Peter's pyrosis (πύρωσις, G4451, "fiery trial/burning"). The LXX uses kaio (καίω, G2545, "to burn/kindle") for active burning, but the negated kata-kaio emphasizes what did not happen—total consumption. This lexical thread continues in NT usage: Stephen quotes the bush narrative (Acts 7:30) using batos, while Peter applies pyrosis to believers' trials (1 Pet 4:12), and Hebrews declares God a "consuming fire" (pyr katanaliskon, 12:29) that refines rather than destroys those in covenant relationship. The trajectory shows fire's dual nature—consuming for enemies, preserving for God's people—consistently expressed from Exodus through Revelation.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: seneh (סְנֶה) "thornbush" - appears Exod 3:2-4; Deut 33:16
- **LXX**: batos (βάτος) "bramble bush" - standard translation
- **NT**: batos (Acts 7:30-35, Mark 12:26, Luke 20:37) - NT continuation of bush imagery

- **Hebrew**: 'esh (אֵשׁ) "fire" - appears throughout OT (Exod 3:2, 13:21, 19:18, Dan 3:25)
- **LXX**: pyr (πῦρ) "fire" - standard translation
- **NT**: pyr (1 Pet 1:7, 4:12; Heb 12:29; Rev 21:4) - continued fire imagery

- **Hebrew**: 'akal (אָכַל) "to consume/eat" - negated in Exod 3:2
- **LXX**: katakaio (κατακαίω) "to burn down/consume completely" - negated (ou katakaio)
- **NT**: pyrosis (πύρωσις) from pyr + -osis - "burning/fiery trial" (1 Pet 4:12)
