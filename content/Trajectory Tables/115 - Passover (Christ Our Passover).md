## PASSOVER (CHRIST OUR PASSOVER) TRAJECTORY TABLE


The Passover stands as one of the most explicit and detailed types in Scripture, commemorating Israel's deliverance from Egypt through the blood of the lamb. On the night when God's judgment fell on Egypt, striking down every firstborn, the Israelites were spared by the blood of a lamb applied to their doorposts. The Lord 'passed over' houses marked with blood, sparing those within from the destroyer. This foundational event, celebrated annually, pointed forward to 'Christ our Passover lamb' who 'has been sacrificed' for us (1 Corinthians 5:7). The lamb had to be without blemish, prefiguring Christ's sinlessness; it was slain at twilight on the 14th of Nisan, the very day and hour Christ died; its blood provided protection from judgment; its flesh provided nourishment for the journey; and not a bone of it was to be broken, fulfilled literally at Christ's crucifixion (John 19:36). The trajectory moves from Israel's physical deliverance from Egypt through blood to the spiritual deliverance from sin and death through Christ's blood. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted sacrificial feast) and **Forward-Looking** (1 Corinthians 5:7 explicitly declares "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed," and John 19:36 identifies the unbroken bones requirement as fulfilled in Christ).

## Lexicon Findings

The Passover trajectory exhibits remarkable lexical continuity across the Hebrew Bible, Septuagint, and New Testament, creating a unified typological thread centered on sacrificial deliverance. The Hebrew **פֶּסַח** (*pesach*, H6453) denotes both the festival and the sacrificial victim, derived from H6452 meaning "to pass over," establishing exemption from judgment through blood. This term translates directly into Greek as **πάσχα** (*pascha*, G3957), maintaining semantic identity across testaments and explicitly linking Christ's crucifixion to the original Passover lamb (1 Corinthians 5:7). The lamb terminology develops from Hebrew **שֶׂה** (*seh*, H7716) and **כֶּבֶשׂ** (*kebes*, H3532), both denoting young sheep or goats, which the LXX renders with Greek **ἀμνός** (*amnos*, G286). John's Gospel employs this precise term when the Baptist declares Jesus "the Lamb of God" (John 1:29), while Revelation's apocalyptic vision uses the diminutive **ἀρνίον** (*arnion*, G721) for the enthroned Lamb. The covenant-blood connection centers on **דָּם** (*dam*, H1818) and **בְּרִית** (*berith*, H1285), corresponding to Greek **αἷμα** (*haima*, G129) and **διαθήκη** (*diatheke*, G1242), linking Exodus 24:8's covenant inauguration to Jesus' Last Supper words (Mark 14:24) and Hebrews' exposition of Christ's superior sacrifice.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: פֶּסַח (*pesach*) - appears in Exodus 12:1-14, Numbers 9:12, throughout Pentateuch
- **LXX**: πάσχα (*pascha*) - standard translation maintaining typological continuity
- **NT**: πάσχα (*pascha*) - NT continuation in John 18:28, 1 Corinthians 5:7

- **Hebrew**: שֶׂה (*seh*), כֶּבֶשׂ (*kebes*) - lamb/sheep terminology in Exodus 12:3-5, Isaiah 53:7
- **LXX**: ἀμνός (*amnos*) - lamb rendering
- **NT**: ἀμνός (*amnos*) - John 1:29, 1 Peter 1:19; ἀρνίον (*arnion*) - Revelation 5:6

- **Hebrew**: דָּם (*dam*) - blood in Exodus 12:7, 24:8; בְּרִית (*berith*) - covenant in Exodus 24:8
- **LXX**: αἷμα (*haima*) - blood; διαθήκη (*diatheke*) - covenant
- **NT**: αἷμα (*haima*) - blood in Mark 14:24, 1 Peter 1:18-19; διαθήκη (*diatheke*) - covenant
