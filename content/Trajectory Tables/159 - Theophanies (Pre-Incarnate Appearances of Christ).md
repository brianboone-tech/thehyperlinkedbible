## THEOPHANIES (PRE-INCARNATE APPEARANCES OF CHRIST) TRAJECTORY TABLE


Before the Incarnation, the eternal Son appeared in various forms to the patriarchs and prophets. These theophanies—visible manifestations of the invisible God—were pre-incarnate appearances of the Second Person of the Trinity. John Owen observed that these "representations that were made of him and his glory under the Old Testament, before his actual incarnation" prepared God's people for the full revelation in the Word made flesh. John 12:41 explicitly identifies Isaiah's throne vision as seeing Christ's glory. These appearances took multiple forms: the Angel of the LORD (mal'ak YHWH) who bore the divine Name and received worship; glory visions of the divine throne with a human-like figure upon it; and mysterious visitors who spoke with divine authority. The trajectory moves from early personal encounters (Abraham, Jacob, Moses), through prophetic throne visions (Isaiah, Ezekiel, Daniel), to the Incarnation where "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14), culminating in the glorified Christ of Revelation. These were not merely symbolic visions but genuine pre-incarnate manifestations of the Son, foreshadowing His permanent assumption of human nature in the fullness of time. As Fairbairn noted, these appearances demonstrated the Son's unique role as mediator between God and man even before His incarnation.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged divine appearances) and **Forward-Looking** (pointed explicitly to the Incarnation and the greater glory to be revealed).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must see God. Not merely know about God, not merely believe correct propositions about God, but actually behold Him--"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God" (Matthew 5:8). You must encounter the living God, not a philosophical concept or religious system. You need what Abraham, Jacob, Moses, Isaiah, and Ezekiel experienced--divine manifestation that transforms, commissions, and sends. You must have an encounter with God that leaves you both terrified by His holiness and healed by His grace.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You cannot generate a theophany. Abraham did not summon the three visitors at Mamre; they appeared. Jacob did not arrange the wrestling match at Peniel; he was confronted. Moses did not seek the burning bush; it confronted him. Isaiah was in the temple doing religious duty when suddenly the heavenly throne room broke through. The theophanies were divine initiatives, not human achievements. You cannot manufacture an encounter with the living God through religious technique, mystical practice, or spiritual discipline. The transcendent God must condescend; the invisible must make Himself visible; the one who dwells in unapproachable light must accommodate Himself to human sight. And even when He does--like Moses, you cannot see His face and live (Exodus 33:20).

### 3. How He Did It

The Son who appeared temporarily in the theophanies came permanently in the Incarnation. "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14). What the patriarchs and prophets glimpsed in terrifying moments, the disciples saw day after day--the eternal Son in human flesh. John 12:41 confirms: Isaiah's throne vision was a vision of Christ's glory. The One who walked through Eden, appeared to Abraham, wrestled with Jacob, spoke from the bush, confronted Joshua, sat enthroned before Isaiah and Ezekiel--this One "became flesh." The temporary manifestations pointed to permanent presence. And Christ's incarnation opened what theophanies only hinted at: not just momentary encounter but abiding relationship. "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18).

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Through Christ, you see God--not in terrifying theophany but in gracious incarnation. "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). The theophanies' terror is transformed: John fell at the glorified Christ's feet "as though dead," but Christ laid His hand on him saying "Fear not" (Revelation 1:17). The same divine presence that made Moses hide his face now says "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden" (Matthew 11:28). The trajectory moves from glimpse to fullness to consummation: "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4). What was forbidden to Moses ("you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live"--Exodus 33:20) becomes the eternal privilege of the redeemed. The pre-incarnate appearances, the incarnate life, the post-resurrection glory, and the eschatological face-to-face vision--all are stages of the same divine initiative: God coming toward humanity, seeking us out, making Himself known, until at last we behold Him fully and forever.

## Lexicon Findings

The theophanic trajectory reveals precise lexical continuity across covenantal epochs. The divine Name **יְהוָה** (*Yᵉhôvâh*, H3068—"the self-Existent One") appears consistently, often paired with **אֲדֹנָי** (*Adonai*, H136—"Lord"), identifying the pre-incarnate Christ as YHWH manifest. The paradox of divine visibility emerges through **מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה** (*mal'ak YHWH*, H4397—"Angel of the LORD"), a figure bearing God's Name, receiving worship, yet appearing in human form as **אִישׁ** (*ish*, H376—"man"). Ezekiel's throne vision employs **דְּמוּת** (*demut*, H1823—"likeness") and **מַרְאֶה** (*mareh*, H4758—"appearance") to describe the human-like figure upon the throne, anticipating incarnation. Daniel's **כְּבַר אֱנָשׁ** (*kebar enash*—"one like a son of man") becomes Jesus' self-designation: **ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου** (*ho huios tou anthrōpou*, G5207 + G444). John's Gospel consummates this vocabulary: **ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο** (*ho logos sarx egeneto*, G3056 + G4561—"the Word became flesh") and **ἐσκήνωσεν** (*eskēnōsen*, G4637—"tabernacled") among us, revealing **δόξα** (*doxa*, G1391—the **כָּבוֹד** / *kabod*, H3519 glory) previously seen only in theophanies. The lexical network demonstrates typological progression: temporary manifestations of YHWH in human-like form point toward permanent assumption of human nature in Christ.
