## VEIL (ACCESS THROUGH CHRIST'S FLESH) TRAJECTORY TABLE


The veil (Hebrew: פָּרֹכֶת, pārōḵeṯ) separating the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place embodied the barrier between holy God and sinful humanity. Only the high priest could pass through, only once per year, only with blood. Its beauty (blue, purple, scarlet, cherubim) spoke of heaven's glory; its thickness spoke of sin's barrier; its restriction spoke of the law's inability to bring anyone into God's presence. The trajectory moves from the veil that excluded to the veil torn at Christ's death, opening access for all believers to enter God's presence through 'the new and living way' of Christ's flesh. This is a **Direct Type** (divinely instituted barrier) and **Forward-Looking** (Matthew 27:51; Hebrews 10:19-20).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must enter God's presence--not stand at a respectful distance, not merely approach the outer courts, but enter the Most Holy Place where God dwells. You must have access to the throne of grace to receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need (Hebrews 4:16). You must live in God's presence continually, not annually like Aaron. You must see His face, know Him directly, commune with Him without barrier.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

You cannot enter on your own. The veil barred the way absolutely. Its cherubim recalled Eden's guardians with flaming sword--you cannot return to paradise by your own effort. Even Aaron, chosen by God, consecrated by elaborate ritual, wearing sacred garments, could enter only once yearly, only with blood, only with incense cloud lest he see God and die. Your sin is the barrier, and you cannot remove it. Your best moral effort cannot tear the veil. Your most sincere religious approach cannot pass through it. Death awaits unauthorized entry, and you have no authorization.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ entered as both high priest and sacrifice. He did not need to bring another's blood; He brought His own. He did not need to enter once yearly; He entered once for all. He did not need incense cloud to hide from God's holiness; He is the radiance of God's glory. At the moment of His death, God Himself tore the veil--"from top to bottom" (Matthew 27:51)--not human effort reaching up but divine action reaching down. Hebrews identifies Christ's flesh with the veil (Hebrews 10:20): His body broken, His flesh torn, opens the way. The cherubim who barred Eden now attend the empty tomb (John 20:12), standing aside because the curse is lifted, death is defeated, and the way to life is open.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

Through Christ's torn flesh, you can "enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus" (Hebrews 10:19). The access once restricted to one man, one day, one year, is now yours continually, freely, confidently. You can "draw near to the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16)--not the throne of judgment but the throne of grace, because Christ's blood has changed everything. You can have "full assurance of faith" (Hebrews 10:22)--not because of your worthiness but because of His sacrifice. Where Aaron approached with fear and elaborate protection, you approach with confidence. Where Aaron entered briefly and retreated, you are invited to dwell. And in the end, you will "see his face" (Revelation 22:4)--no veil, no barrier, no restriction, no mediation needed except the Lamb who opens the way. "For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face" (1 Corinthians 13:12). The torn veil was always pointing to this: unhindered, eternal, face-to-face communion with God through Christ.

## Lexicon Findings

The veil trajectory establishes a profound lexical continuity from Hebrew tabernacle to Greek fulfillment. The Hebrew **פֹּרֶכֶת** (*pōreketh*, H6532) meaning "separatrix" or screen appears in Exodus 26:31 and Leviticus 16:2, denoting the barrier dividing Holy Place from Most Holy Place. The LXX consistently translates this with **καταπέτασμα** (*katapetasma*, G2665), meaning "something spread thoroughly" or curtain—the exact term Matthew and Mark use for the temple veil torn at Christ's death (Matthew 27:51; Mark 15:38). Hebrews 10:20 identifies Christ's **σάρξ** (*sarx*, G4561—flesh, body) as the veil itself, creating a typological equation: old veil excluded, Christ's flesh-veil includes. The mercy seat (**כַּפֹּרֶת**, *kapporeth*, H3727) behind the veil, where blood was sprinkled, connects to **ἱλασμός** (*hilasmos*, G2434—propitiation) in 1 John 2:2, showing Christ as both sacrifice and mercy seat. Hebrews emphasizes **παρρησία** (*parrēsia*, G3954—bold confidence, free speech) for believers entering God's presence (Hebrews 4:16; 10:19)—the polar opposite of Aaron's fearful, restricted annual entry. The lexical thread traces exclusion (Hebrew veil) → LXX translation → NT torn curtain → Christ's flesh → confident access.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew**: פֹּרֶכֶת (*pōreketh*) - appears in Exodus 26:31-33, Leviticus 16:2, 12, 15
- **LXX**: καταπέτασμα (*katapetasma*) - standard translation of פֹּרֶכֶת
- **NT**: καταπέτασμα (*katapetasma*) - continued in Matthew 27:51, Mark 15:38, Hebrews 6:19, 9:3, 10:20
- **Hebrew**: כַּפֹּרֶת (*kapporeth*) - mercy seat, from root כפר (to cover/atone)
- **Greek**: ἱλασμός (*hilasmos*) - propitiation, echoes mercy seat function (1 John 2:2)
- **Greek**: σάρξ (*sarx*) - Christ's flesh identified as the veil (Hebrews 10:20)
- **Greek**: παρρησία (*parrēsia*) - bold confidence for access (Hebrews 4:16, 10:19)
