When fiery serpents plagued Israel in the wilderness as judgment for their sin, God commanded Moses to craft a bronze serpent (נְחַשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת, neḥaš neḥōšeṯ, "bronze serpent") and lift it up on a pole. Anyone bitten who looked upon the bronze serpent would live. This strange remedy—looking at the image of that which brought death—powerfully prefigures the gospel. Jesus Himself applies this type directly to His crucifixion: 'As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life' (John 3:14-15). The bronze serpent bore the likeness of the serpent but contained no venom; Christ was 'made to be sin' (2 Corinthians 5:21) yet knew no sin. The serpent was lifted up on a pole; Christ was lifted up on the cross. Looking in faith to the bronze serpent brought physical healing; looking in faith to Christ crucified brings eternal life.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged event) and **Forward-Looking** (John 3:14-15 explicitly applies this typology to Christ's crucifixion).

## Four-Step Application

### 1. What You Must Do

You must look to Jesus "lifted up" on the cross. Hebrews 12:2 commands "looking to Jesus"—present, continuous, ongoing. Not occasional glances but fixed gaze. Not one-time decision but lifelong beholding. Your eyes must stay on the crucified One.

### 2. Why You Can't Do It

Your eyes keep drifting to yourself—your spiritual progress, your faith quality, your obedience track record. You keep wanting to look at the looking, examine the examining, have faith in your faith. And every time you turn from Christ to yourself, the venom spreads. You can't maintain the gaze because you keep thinking the healing depends on how well you look rather than on what you look at.

### 3. How He Did It

Christ was lifted up on the cross bearing the form of the curse—"made to be sin," "became a curse for us." Like the bronze serpent, He bore sin's image without sin's poison. He became what was killing us while remaining the only One who could heal us. And from the cross, lifted up between heaven and earth, He draws all people to Himself (John 12:32). His lifting accomplishes the drawing.

### 4. How Through Him You Can

The bronze serpent's healing power didn't depend on the quality of Israel's looking—they could barely see through dying eyes—but on God's promise attached to the lifted image. Likewise, your salvation doesn't depend on the strength of your faith but on the strength of Christ's finished work. Even weak looking at a strong Savior saves. Even trembling faith in an unshakeable Redeemer heals. So keep looking—not because your looking generates anything, but because the lifted One generates everything. In the new creation, "death shall be no more" (Revelation 21:4)—the serpent's venom finally and completely eradicated by the One who bore its curse.

## Lexicon Findings

The Bronze Serpent trajectory traces three interwoven lexical threads across the OT and NT. First, the **serpent vocabulary** moves from Hebrew נָחָשׁ (*nachash*, [[Lexicon/H5101-5200#H5175|H5175]] - "serpent") modified by שָׂרָף (*saraph*, [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8314|H8314]] - "fiery serpent," poisonous from burning effect) in Numbers 21, to Greek ὄφις (*ophis*, [[Lexicon/G3701-3800#G3789|G3789]] - "serpent") in John 3:14. Second, the **lifting up language** creates the trajectory's theological spine: Hebrew רוּם (*rum*, [[Lexicon/H7301-7400#H7311|H7311]] - "to be lifted up, exalted") in Numbers 21:8-9 and Isaiah 52:13 becomes Greek ὑψόω (*hupsoo*, [[Lexicon/G5301-5400#G5312|G5312]] - "to lift up, exalt") in John 3:14, 8:28, and 12:32-33. Third, the **looking/believing correspondence** connects Hebrew רָאָה (*ra'ah*, [[Lexicon/H7101-7200#H7200|H7200]] - "to see, look at") in Numbers 21:9 to Greek πιστεύω (*pisteuo*, [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4100|G4100]] - "to believe, have faith") in John 3:14-15, establishing the typological transfer from physical sight to spiritual faith. The curse/sin vocabulary completes the pattern: Hebrew חַטָּאָה (*chatta'ah*, [[Lexicon/H2401-2500#H2403|H2403]] - "sin") and קְלָלָה (*qelalah*, [[Lexicon/H7001-7100#H7045|H7045]] - "curse") find fulfillment in Greek ἁμαρτία (*hamartia*, [[Lexicon/G201-300#G266|G266]] - "sin") and κατάρα (*katara*, [[Lexicon/G2601-2700#G2671|G2671]] - "curse") in 2 Corinthians 5:21 and Galatians 3:13.

**Key Lexical Threads:**
- **Hebrew Serpent**: נָחָשׁ נְחֹשֶׁת (*nachash nechosheth*) - bronze serpent (Numbers 21:9) / שָׂרָף (*saraph*) - fiery serpent (Numbers 21:6)
- **LXX Translation**: ὄφις (*ophis*) - standard Greek rendering of נָחָשׁ
- **NT Continuation**: ὄφις (*ophis*) in John 3:14; Revelation 20:2 (ancient serpent)
- **Hebrew Lifting**: רוּם (*rum*) - lifted up, exalted (Numbers 21:8-9; Isaiah 52:13)
- **NT Lifting**: ὑψόω (*hupsoo*) - lift up (John 3:14; 8:28; 12:32-33)
- **Hebrew Looking**: רָאָה (*ra'ah*) - to see, look (Numbers 21:9)
- **NT Believing**: πιστεύω (*pisteuo*) - to believe (John 3:14-15)
- **Hebrew Sin/Curse**: חַטָּאָה (*chatta'ah*) / קְלָלָה (*qelalah*)

- **NT Looking to Jesus**: ἀφοράω (*aphorao*, [[Lexicon/G801-900#G872|G872]] - "to fix eyes upon") in Hebrews 12:2 - continuous looking
