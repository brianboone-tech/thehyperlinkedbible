Chiasms:

The word chiasm is derived from the Greek letter chi (written X), which symbolizes the crossover pattern of words, phrases, clauses, or ideas that are repeated in reverse order.  The simplest type of chiasm is A B B’ A’ – a structure that comes full circle by highlighting key concepts in reverse order.  A chiastic pattern keeps the reader’s or hearer’s attention focused on the main concepts.

- What Chiasms Are and Do
	- **An Extension of Parallelism** - Parallelism is a well-known and widely recognized feature of Hebrew poetry, and a chiastic literary structure simply extends that parallelism to units of text of varying length.
	- **Providing Structure and Boundaries** - Chiastic structures operate like other literary forms that both structure presentations and set limits within which authors work.
	- **An Aid to Memory** - Chiastic structures function similarly to mnemonic devices, helping readers remember relationships between topics by providing a recognizable pattern
	- **Creating Synergy** - The synergy created by chiasms enhances the meaning of biblical texts, often centering important points within the structure, guiding readers to the core message.
	- **A Vehicle for Artistic Beauty** - Chiastic structures contribute to the aesthetic quality of biblical literature, balancing and harmonizing the text while also communicating deeper meanings.

Biblical authors seem to have employed chiastic structures, inclusios, and other devices that rely on the repetition of key words, phrases, or thematic concepts to structure their material.

There is evidence that early on the biblical texts were widely memorized, as well as evidence that they were regularly read aloud. I think it plausible that authors expected their audiences to recognize chiastic structures and inclusios formed by the repetition of key words, phrases, and concepts, and if they weren’t caught on first hearing (those accustomed to listening closely to texts being read aloud probably had more facility for hearing such things–I notice that my sons, who have heard us read aloud to them a lot, seem to catch more from a first reading than my wife and I sometimes do) they could be noticed in the memorization/meditation/recitation process.

Sometimes chiasms are more apparent if the texts are read in the original Greek, Hebrew, or Aramaic, though if you’re reading a more literal translation you might still pick it up if you’re paying close attention and thinking hard about how the text hangs together.
