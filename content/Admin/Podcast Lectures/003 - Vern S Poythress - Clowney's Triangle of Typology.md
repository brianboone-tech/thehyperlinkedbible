Vern S Poythress' Summary of "Clowney’s Triangle of Typology in Preaching and Biblical Theology"

**I. Overview of Clowney’s Triangle**
- Edmund P. Clowney developed a triangle diagram to illustrate the function of typology in the Old Testament, which has become a significant tool for biblical interpretation. This triangle, featured in Clowney's book Preaching and Biblical Theology, serves as a framework for understanding the relationship between symbols, their meanings, and their fulfillment in Christ ([1][2]).


**II. Components of Clowney’s Triangle**

![[Figure 1. Clowney's Triangle of Typology.png]]
- Symbol (S): Represents the original symbolic element, such as the tabernacle.
- Truth-1 (T1): The initial meaning of the symbol in its historical context, e.g., God's presence with His people.
- Truth-n (Tn): The ultimate truth or fulfillment of the symbol, which is found in Christ.
- Typical Reference: The relationship between the symbol and its fulfillment ([2][3]).

Example: The Tabernacle

![[Figure 2. The Tabernacle as a Type of Christ.png]]


- The tabernacle signifies God's desire to dwell among His people, representing T1.
- Tn is realized in Christ, who embodies the fullness of God's presence ([2]).

**III. Clarifications and Enhancements**

![[Figure 3. Clowney's Triangle with Relabeling for Clarification.png]]

Clowney's triangle has been refined to include clearer labels:

- "Symbol" instead of S
- "Truth in Anticipation" instead of T1
- "Truth in Fulfillment" instead of Tn
- "Typological Reference" for clarity on the nature of the relationship between the symbol and its fulfillment ([3][4]).

![[Figure 4. Clowney's Triangle with Application.png]]




**IV. The Value of Clowney’s Triangle**

Clowney’s triangle provides a structured approach to typological reasoning, helping to avoid arbitrary interpretations while recognizing genuine typological connections. It emphasizes the importance of grounding interpretations in the historical context of the symbols ([4][5]).


**V. Dangers in Typological Interpretation**

1. Arbitrary Typology: The risk of inventing types based on superficial connections can lead to fanciful interpretations, neglecting the intended meaning of the text. Clowney's first step encourages interpreters to respect the original context and meaning of symbols ([5][6]).
2. Minimizing Typology: Scholars may overlook deeper symbolic meanings by insisting on explicit New Testament references. Clowney’s second step reminds us that all truths in the Old Testament are interconnected and lead to their fulfillment in Christ ([6][7]).

**VI. Broader Significance**

Clowney’s triangle is not a definitive answer generator but serves as a guide for understanding the structure of redemptive history. It encourages deeper engagement with biblical texts, promoting a more profound comprehension of their symbolic meanings ([8]).
