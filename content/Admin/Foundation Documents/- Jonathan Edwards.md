## Overview

This document synthesizes the heart of Jonathan Edwards' doctrinal position as expressed in his magnum opus, "A History of the Work of Redemption" (1739). Edwards planned this work as "a body of divinity, in a new method, and in the form of a history," showing how all of Scripture and providence—from the fall to the end of the world—serves the single, unified purpose of accomplishing redemption through Jesus Christ.

The work reveals Edwards as a profoundly Reformed, redemptive-historical theologian who saw all of history, all of Scripture, and all of God's works converging on one grand design: the glorification of God through the redemption of His people in Jesus Christ.

---

## I. The Central Doctrine: Redemption as the Sum of All God's Works

### The Governing Thesis

**"The Work of Redemption is a Work That God Carries on From the Fall of Man to the End of the World"**

For Edwards, the work of redemption is not merely one divine work among many—it is **the supreme work to which all other works are subordinated**. This is the lens through which all of history, all of providence, and all of Scripture must be understood.

### Redemption Defined in Two Senses

Edwards distinguishes between redemption in a **narrow sense** and a **broad sense**:

1. **Narrow Sense** (The Purchase): The work Christ accomplished during His humiliation—from incarnation through death and resurrection. This was "begun and finished with Christ's humiliation" and was "virtually done and finished" at Christ's resurrection.

2. **Broad Sense** (The Whole Work): All that God works to accomplish this end, including:
   - **Preparatory works** before Christ's coming (from the fall to the incarnation)
   - **The purchase itself** (Christ's humiliation and work on earth)
   - **Application works** (from Christ's resurrection to the end of the world)

Edwards focuses on this **broad, redemptive-historical sense**, tracing God's unified plan across all ages.

### The Supremacy of Redemption

Edwards makes the staggering claim that **redemption is greater than creation itself**:

> "The work of redemption is the greatest of all God's works of which we have any notice, and it is the end of all his other works."

Why? Because:
- **All providence is greater than creation** (the use of a house is greater than its building)
- **Redemption is the sum of all God's works of providence** (all are subordinate to it)
- **The new creation excels the old** (as the temple exceeded the tabernacle, the new covenant the old)

Edwards boldly states:
> "God has used the creation which he has made, for no other purpose but to subserve the designs of this affair... God created the world to provide a spouse and a kingdom for his Son."

### The Three-Period Structure

Edwards divides redemptive history into three great periods:

**Period One: From the Fall to Christ's Incarnation** (~4,000 years)
- The time of preparation and promise
- God overturning the world repeatedly to prepare the way
- Prophets, types, promises, and providence all pointing forward

**Period Two: The Time of Christ's Humiliation** (~33 years)
- Christ's incarnation, life, suffering, death, and burial
- The **purchase** of redemption accomplished
- The price paid in full through Christ's blood

**Period Three: From Christ's Resurrection to the End of the World** (Church Age to Consummation)
- The **application** and **success** of the purchase
- Christ receiving His reward
- The gradual but certain triumph of the Gospel over Satan's kingdom

## II. Edwards' Redemptive-Historical Framework

### God's Sovereignty Over All History

For Edwards, history is not random or chaotic—it is **the unfolding of God's eternal purpose**. Every event, every revolution, every change in the world serves the single design of accomplishing redemption.

Key metaphors Edwards uses:

**The Great River**:
> "God's providence may not unfitly be compared to a large and long river, having innumerable branches, beginning in different regions, and at a great distance one from another, and all conspiring to one common issue... all at length discharge themselves at one mouth into the same ocean."

**The Great Wheel**:
> "Providence is like a mighty wheel, whose circumference is so high that it is dreadful, with the glory of the God of Israel above upon it... We have seen the revolution of this wheel, and how, as it was from God, so its return has been to God again."

**The Great Building**:
> "God builds up age after age, by a series of such great things which he brings to pass... a complete lofty structure, whose top reaches to the heaven of heavens, a building worthy of the great God, the King of kings."

### All Providence Reducible to Redemption

Edwards argues that **every work of providence—from the creation of the world to its final destruction—serves the work of redemption**:

- **Kings rise and fall** to prepare the way for Christ
- **Nations are overturned** to make room for the Gospel
- **Empires come and go** to serve God's redemptive purposes
- **Even Satan's works** are overruled to glorify Christ's triumph

Edwards writes:
> "All the revolutions in the world are to subserve this grand design. So that the work of redemption is, as it were, the sum of God's works of providence."

### The Unity and Coherence of Providence

Against those who see history as "jumbled and confused," Edwards demonstrates the **perfect harmony and order** of God's works:

> "The wheels of providence are not turned round by blind chance, but they are full of eyes round about... and they are guided by the Spirit of God... And all God's works of providence through all ages meet in one at last, as so many lines meeting in one centre."

Edwards compares God's providence to:
- **One building** with many stones, all fitly joined together with one foundation and one top stone
- **One work** with many parts, all united in a single scheme
- **One chain** with many links, the first from God and the last to Him

This unified vision shows that **Scripture alone** reveals what God is doing in history:
> "Nothing else but the Scriptures has any pretence for showing any manner of regular scheme or drift in those revolutions which God orders from age to age."

## III. The Christocentric Heart of Edwards' Theology

### Christ's Absolute Preeminence

For Edwards, **Christ has the preeminence in all things** because redemption is "all his work":

> "This great work of redemption is all his work. He is the great Redeemer, and therefore the work of redemption being as it were the sum of God's works of providence, this shows the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, as being above all, and through all, and in all."

Edwards demonstrates Christ's supremacy by showing:
- **All purposes of God are purposed in Christ** (Col. 1:15-18)
- **The world was created by Christ and for Christ** (Eph. 3:9-12)
- **All kingdoms fall, but Christ's kingdom stands forever**
- **All enemies become His footstool** in the end

### The Greatness of Christ's Work

Edwards magnifies the **magnitude** of what Christ accomplished through three considerations:

1. **The greatness of particular events**: The incarnation (God becoming man!), Christ's 33 years of labor and suffering, the crucifixion, the resurrection, the ascension—each is staggering

2. **The number of great events**: So many mighty acts that they fill 6,000+ years (compared to creation's 6 days)

3. **The glorious issue**: The perfect and eternal destruction of the wicked, the consummate glory of the righteous

Edwards marvels:
> "When Christ rose from the dead, and was exalted from that abasement to which he submitted for our salvation, the purchase of eternal life was completely made, so that there was no need of anything more to be done in order to it."

### The Incarnation as the Hinge

Edwards emphasizes that **God becoming man** was necessary to capacitate Christ for the work of redemption. This "wonderful thing" was accomplished "after such preparation" across four millennia, bringing about "the union of the divine nature with the human in one person."

## IV. Edwards' Covenant Theology

### God's Covenant Faithfulness

At the heart of Edwards' theology is **God's unwavering faithfulness to His covenant promises**. The text from Isaiah 51:8 grounds the entire work:

> "The moth shall eat them up like a garment, and the worm shall eat them like wool: but my righteousness shall be forever, and my salvation from generation to generation."

Edwards interprets "righteousness" as **God's covenant faithfulness**:
> "By God's righteousness here, is meant his faithfulness in fulfilling his covenant promises to his church... These benefits, though they are bestowed of free and sovereign grace, are altogether undeserved. Yet as God has been pleased, by the promises of the covenant of grace, to bind himself to bestow them, so they are bestowed in the exercise of God's righteousness or justice."

### The Eternal Covenant of Redemption

Edwards teaches that **all of God's decrees belong to the eternal covenant of redemption** between the Father and the Son:

> "All the decrees of God do some way or other belong to that eternal covenant of redemption which was between the Father and the Son before the foundation of the world. Every decree of God is some way or other reducible to that covenant."

This covenant is the foundation of:
- God's unchanging purpose across all ages
- The certainty of redemption's success
- The security of believers

### God's Mercy Endures Forever

Edwards repeatedly celebrates **the perpetuity of God's mercy** to His people:

> "We need not wonder that the Psalmist, in Psalm 136, so often repeats this, 'For his mercy endureth forever,' as if he were in an ecstasy at the consideration of the perpetuity of God's mercy to his church."

This mercy is demonstrated in:
- Preserving the church when it was "but as a little spark of fire, or as smoking flax"
- Never suffering enemies to quench it
- Bringing forth judgment unto victory in the end

## V. Edwards' Doctrine of God

### God as Alpha and Omega

Edwards emphasizes that **God is the beginning and end of all things**:

> "God is called in the beginning of this book of Revelation... 'I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty.'"

This means:
- **All things are from Him** (He initiated the work at the fall)
- **All things are through Him** (He sustains and governs all things)
- **All things are to Him** (All culminates in His glory)

Edwards exclaims:
> "O the depth of the riches both of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out!... For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever. Amen."

### God's Immutability and Eternality

In contrast to the **constant changes** in creation—kingdoms rising and falling, the world being overturned repeatedly—**God remains unchanged**:

> "We have seen what mighty changes there have been in the world but God is unchangeable, 'the same yesterday, today, and forever.'... He was before the beginning of this course of things, and he will be after the end of them."

All other "gods" perish:
- The ancient gods of the heathen are destroyed
- Antichrist who claims to be god on earth will fall
- Mahomet who claims religious honors will end
- Satan who set himself up as god of this world will be cast into the lake of fire

**But Jehovah remains**: "His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and of his dominions there is no end."

### God's Glorious Attributes Displayed

Edwards sees redemption as the supreme display of God's perfections:

**God's Power**:
> "The glorious power of God appears in thus conquering the devil, and bringing him under foot, time after time, after long time given him to strengthen himself to his utmost."

**God's Wisdom**:
> "How glorious doth the wisdom of God appear in that long course and series of great changes in the world, in bringing such order out of confusion, in so frustrating the devil, and so wonderfully turning all his most subtle machinations to his own glory!"

**God's Holiness, Grace, and Love**:
> "Here we have a most rational excellent account of this matter, worthy of God, and exceedingly showing forth the glory of his perfections, his majesty, his wisdom, his glorious holiness, and grace, and love, and his exaltation above all."

## VI. Edwards' Polemic Against Self-Righteousness

### The Arrogance of Self-Righteousness

Edwards devotes substantial space to **reproving self-righteousness**, calling it an act of supreme arrogance that:

1. **Takes upon oneself the work of the great Saviour**: "You take upon you to do the work of the great Saviour of the world... the very work that the only begotten Son of God did when upon earth."

2. **Makes all of God's 4,000-year preparation futile**: "If there be ground for you to trust in your own righteousness, then all that Christ did to purchase salvation... and all that God did from the first fall of man to that time to prepare the way for it, is in vain."

3. **Claims to do greater than creating a world**: "To take on yourself to work out redemption, is a greater thing than if you had taken it upon you to create a world."

4. **Charges Christ's sacrifice with folly**: "Their self-righteousness does in effect charge Christ's offering up himself in these sufferings, as the greatest instance of folly that ever men or angels saw, instead of being the most glorious display of the divine wisdom and grace that ever was seen."

### The Remedy: Come Empty-Handed to Christ

Edwards urges burdened souls:
> "Come naked as you are. Come as a poor condemned criminal. Come and cast yourself down at Christ's feet, as one justly condemned, and utterly helpless in yourself. Here is a complete salvation wrought out by Christ, and through him offered to you."

The ground of encouragement:
> "For Christ to reject one that thus comes to him, would be to frustrate all those great things which you have heard that God brought to pass from the fall of man to the incarnation of Christ."

## VII. Edwards' Eschatology

### The Certain Triumph of Christ's Kingdom

Edwards teaches a **postmillennial optimism** about the progress of redemption. Though Satan's kingdom appears strong, it will **melt away like wax before fire** when God appears:

> "Those mighty kingdoms of Antichrist and Mahomet, which have made such a figure for so many ages together, and have trampled the world under foot, when God comes to appear, will vanish away like a shadow, and will as it were disappear of themselves, and come to nothing, as the darkness in a room does, when the light is brought in."

### The Final Consummation

Edwards envisions the **glorious culmination** of all things:

> "At the consummation of all things, it appears in its greatest magnificence, as a complete lofty structure, whose top reaches to the heaven of heavens, a building worthy of the great God, the King of kings."

The final state:
- **Perfect and eternal destruction of the wicked**
- **Consummate glory of the righteous**
- **Christ's people tread on the lion and dragon**
- **All enemies become Christ's footstool**
- **The everlasting kingdom established**

### The Day of Judgment

Edwards sees the final judgment as the **ultimate manifestation** of redemption's purpose:

> "How rational, worthy, and excellent a revelation is this!... telling us what will become of things at last, how they will be finished off by a great day of judgment, and will issue in the subduing of God's enemies, and in the salvation and glory of his church, and setting up of the everlasting kingdom of his Son."

---

## VIII. The Church: A Happy and Blessed Society

### All Things for the Church

Edwards emphasizes that **this entire great work is for the church**:

> "For all this great work is for them. Christ undertook it for their sakes, and for their sakes he carries it on, from the fall of man to the end of the world. It is because he has loved them with an everlasting love."

The extent of God's care for His people:
- **He overturns states and kingdoms** for their sakes
- **He shakes heaven and earth** for their sakes
- **He gives men for them, and people for their life**
- **He first gives the blood of his own Son**, then the blood of all their enemies

Edwards exclaims:
> "He made the world, and for their sakes he will destroy it. For their sakes he built heaven, and for their sakes he makes his angels ministering spirits."

### The Blessedness of God's People

The church's happiness consists in:
- **Security**: God never forsakes His inheritance
- **Victory**: They shall finally prevail when all kingdoms end
- **Glory**: They arrive at consummate glory forever
- **Divine favor**: "All things are yours" (1 Cor. 3:21-22)

Edwards celebrates:
> "How blessed is this people who are redeemed from among men, and are the first fruits unto God, and to the Lamb, who have God in all ages for their protection and help!"

## IX. Edwards' Apologetic for Scripture

### Scripture Alone Reveals God's Design

Edwards argues that **the Bible's comprehensive explanation of redemptive history proves its divine origin**:

> "The Scriptures alone inform us what God is about, or what he aims at, in these works which he is doing in the world."

Only Scripture:
- Shows the **whole series** from beginning to end
- Reveals the **original of things** (how the world began)
- Provides an **orderly history** of God's government
- Gives **orderly prophecy** of future events
- Explains **what God seeks and intends** by all He does

### The Necessity of Divine Revelation

Edwards argues it is **fitting** that God should reveal His design because:

1. **Rational creatures are the principal concern**: "The thing that is God's great design in his works, is doubtless something concerning his reasonable creatures."

2. **God gave reason to see His glory in His works**: "God has given his rational creatures reason... for this end, that they may see God's glory in them."

3. **Mankind can actively cooperate**: "It is fit that mankind should be informed... because they are made capable of actively falling in with that design, and promoting of it, and acting herein as his friends and subjects."

Edwards concludes:
> "How rational, worthy, and excellent a revelation is this! and how excellent a book is the Bible... a book that the great Jehovah has given to mankind for their instruction, without which we should be left in miserable darkness and confusion."

## X. Edwards' Warnings to the Unconverted

### The Exceeding Misery of Those Without Christ

Edwards delivers **solemn warnings** to those who remain outside of Christ:

> "You that are in a Christless condition, you are those who shall have no part or lot in this matter. You are never the better for any of those things of which you have heard. Yea, your guilt is but so much the greater, and the misery you are exposed to so much the more dreadful."

The unconverted are:
- **Enemies against whom God exercises wrath**
- **Those liable to be made Christ's footstool**
- **Part of the serpent's seed** whose head will be bruised
- **Reserved for the triumph of Christ's power** in punishing them

### The Aggravation of Unbelief

Edwards shows how **unbelief is heinous** because it:

1. **Slights the glorious person** for whose coming God made such great preparation
2. **Despises the great Saviour** who accomplished the purchase through extreme suffering
3. **Makes light of all the glory of God's works** across 4,000 years
4. **Sets at naught the precious stone** God laid in Zion

He asks:
> "How great your sin, in thus rejecting Jesus Christ, appears to be from those things that have been said?"

### The Urgency of Seeking Christ

Edwards warns against **careless neglect**:

> "Have the angels been so engaged about this salvation which is by Christ, ever since the fall of man, though they are not immediately concerned in it, and will you who need it, and have it offered to you, be so careless about it!"

The logic is piercing:
> "Was it worth the while for Christ to labour so hard, and do and suffer so much to procure this salvation, and was it not worth the while for you to be at some labour in seeking it?"

Edwards concludes with the warning:
> "How shall we escape if we neglect so great salvation?" (Heb. 2:3)

## XI. Summary: The Heart of Edwards' Doctrinal Position

### Core Theological Convictions

1. **God's Absolute Sovereignty**: Every event in history serves God's eternal purpose to glorify Himself through redemption

2. **Christ's Centrality**: All of Scripture, all of history, and all of providence center on Jesus Christ and His redemptive work

3. **Covenant Faithfulness**: God's unchanging commitment to His covenant promises ensures the success of redemption

4. **Redemptive-Historical Unity**: History is not random but a unified unfolding of God's plan from fall to consummation

5. **The Supremacy of Redemption**: The work of redemption is greater than creation and is the end of all God's works

6. **Providence as Purposeful**: All revolutions, all changes, all events serve the work of redemption

7. **Scripture's Divine Origin**: Only the Bible explains the coherent design behind all of history

8. **Grace Alone**: Self-righteousness is the supreme arrogance; salvation comes only through Christ's finished work

9. **Certain Victory**: Despite opposition, Christ's kingdom will triumph and His people will be glorified

10. **Eternal Perspectives**: All things move toward a glorious consummation in the eternal kingdom of God

### Edwards' Distinctive Contributions

**Redemptive-Historical Method**: Edwards pioneered a "history of redemption" approach that views all of Scripture and providence through the single lens of God's redemptive purpose.

**Cosmic Scope**: Edwards thinks on the grandest scale—from creation to new creation, from fall to final judgment, encompassing all of history and all of providence.

**Christ-Centered Vision**: Everything—creation, providence, history, Scripture—exists for Christ and His glory in redemption.

**Theological Depth with Pastoral Heart**: Edwards combines rigorous Reformed theology with passionate appeals to the unconverted and encouragement to believers.

**Integration of Systematic and Biblical Theology**: Rather than organizing doctrine topically, Edwards traces the unfolding of redemption historically through Scripture.

### Why This Work Matters

Edwards' "History of Redemption" provides:

1. **A unifying framework** for understanding all of Scripture
2. **A theodicy** explaining God's purposes in history's trials
3. **A foundation for missions** showing redemption's cosmic scope
4. **An apologetic for Scripture** demonstrating its divine coherence
5. **A Christ-centered hermeneutic** for reading the whole Bible
6. **A refutation of self-righteousness** magnifying grace alone
7. **An eschatological hope** grounded in God's unchanging purpose
8. **A vision of God's glory** as the ultimate end of all things

## XII. Alignment with This Vault's Vision

Edwards' doctrinal framework profoundly aligns with the vision of this Obsidian vault:

### Shared Redemptive-Historical Approach

Both Edwards and this vault share:
- **Christ-centered interpretation** of all Scripture
- **Biblical theology method** (tracing themes through Scripture)
- **Redemptive-historical framework** (locating every text in God's unfolding plan)
- **Canonical approach** (Scripture interprets Scripture)

### Edwards Anticipates Greidanus's Seven Ways

Edwards' methodology foreshadows Sidney Greidanus's seven ways Christ appears in the OT:

1. **Redemptive-Historical Progression**: Edwards' entire framework is redemptive-historical, showing every OT passage pointing to Christ's coming

2. **Promise-Fulfillment**: Edwards traces explicit promises across millennia to their fulfillment in Christ

3. **Typology**: Edwards recognizes divinely-intended historical correspondences (Passover, Temple, Sacrifices)

4. **Analogy**: Edwards draws comparisons based on God's unchanging character

5. **Longitudinal Themes**: Edwards follows major themes (covenant, kingdom, sacrifice) as they culminate in Christ

6. **NT References**: Edwards examines how NT authors understood OT passages christologically

7. **Contrast**: Edwards recognizes discontinuities where Christ transcends OT patterns

### Why Edwards Matters for This Vault

1. **Historical precedent**: Edwards demonstrates that reading all Scripture as pointing to Christ is not novelty but historic Reformed orthodoxy

2. **Theological grounding**: Edwards provides robust theological foundations for the vault's hermeneutical approach

3. **Comprehensive vision**: Edwards shows how cross-references, types, and themes fit into the grand narrative

4. **Pastoral application**: Edwards models how to move from exegesis to proclamation to application

5. **Worship and doxology**: Edwards' work culminates in praise to the God who orchestrates all things for His glory

## Conclusion: Edwards' Enduring Legacy

Jonathan Edwards' "History of Redemption" stands as a monument to:
- **God's faithfulness** across all generations
- **Christ's supremacy** over all things
- **Scripture's coherence** as the unfolding of one divine plan
- **Grace's triumph** over sin and Satan
- **The glory of God** as the beginning and end of all things

His work teaches us to read Scripture not as isolated texts but as the **unified story of redemption**, to see history not as chaos but as **the unfolding of God's eternal purpose**, and to trust not in ourselves but in **the complete and finished work of Jesus Christ**.

As Edwards himself concludes:

> "Let us with like pleasure and joy celebrate the everlasting duration of God's mercy and faithfulness to his church and people, and let us be comforted by it under the present dark circumstances of the church of God, and all the uproar and confusions that are in the world, and all the threatenings of the church's enemies. And let us take encouragement earnestly to pray for those glorious things which God has promised to accomplish for his church."

*Soli Deo Gloria*
