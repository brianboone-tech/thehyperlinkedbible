# Geerhardus Vos

**Full Name:** Geerhardus Vos
**Role:** Professor of Biblical Theology
**Institutional Affiliation:** Princeton Theological Seminary (1894-1932)
**Academic Focus:** Biblical Theology, Redemptive-Historical Hermeneutics, Pauline Eschatology, Johannine Theology
**Key Works:** *Biblical Theology: Old and New Testaments*, *Pauline Eschatology*, *The Self-Disclosure of Jesus*, *The Teaching of Jesus Concerning the Kingdom of God and the Church*, *The Eschatology of the Old Testament*

---

## OVERVIEW

Geerhardus Vos (1862-1949) is universally recognized as **the father of Reformed Biblical Theology**. His pioneering work established biblical theology as a distinct discipline within theological studies, fundamentally reshaping how evangelicals understand Scripture's progressive revelation of redemption. Vos's central thesis is that **revelation is organically progressive**, unfolding in historical epochs as God's redemptive work advances from creation to consummation.

### Scholarly Contributions

**1. Founding Biblical Theology as a Discipline**
- Delivered landmark inaugural address "The Idea of Biblical Theology as a Science and as a Theological Discipline" (1894)
- Distinguished biblical theology from systematic theology: biblical theology traces the *historical* line of revelation, while systematic theology arranges truth in *logical* circles
- Established biblical theology as the study of God's self-revelation deposited in Scripture through redemptive history

**2. Redemptive-Historical Hermeneutics**
- Pioneered the redemptive-historical approach to Scripture interpretation
- Demonstrated that revelation is inseparable from redemption: God reveals Himself through His saving acts in history
- Showed that all of Scripture must be read as progressive disclosure of God's redemptive plan culminating in Christ

**3. Eschatological Framework**
- Articulated the "already/not yet" structure of biblical eschatology decades before it became commonplace
- Demonstrated that eschatology is not merely about the end times but permeates all of Scripture
- Showed how the resurrection of Christ inaugurates the age to come while we still live in this present age

**4. Pauline Studies**
- Produced magisterial work *Pauline Eschatology* showing Paul's theology is fundamentally eschatological
- Demonstrated that justification, sanctification, resurrection, and glorification are all eschatological realities
- Traced Paul's "two-age" framework: this present evil age vs. the age to come

## I. CORE THEOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK

### 1. Biblical Theology Defined: The Organic Progress of Revelation

**Central Definition:** <mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Biblical Theology is the exhibition of the organic progress of supernatural revelation in its historic continuity and multiformity.</mark>

**The Nature of Theology:**
- **Etymology:** Theology = knowledge concerning God (theos + logos)
- **Object:** God as He has revealed Himself (not God in Himself)
- **Foundation:** Theology *must* be based on revelation because God is a personal, spiritual Being whose inner nature can only be known through His voluntary self-disclosure

**Why Revelation is Necessary:**

**1. The Nature of God as Personal Spirit**
> "Only in so far as such a being chooses to open up itself can we come to know it. All spiritual life is by its very nature a hidden life, a life shut up in itself."

**2. The Infinite Distance Between God and Man**
> "Suppose it were possible for one human spirit to penetrate directly into another human spirit: it would still be impossible for the spirit of man to penetrate into the Spirit of God."

**3. The Abnormal State of Man Through Sin**
- Sin has deranged the original relation between God and man
- <mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">In his sinful condition, while retaining some knowledge of God, man for all pure and adequate information in divine things is absolutely dependent on that new self-disclosure of God which we call supernatural revelation.</mark>

**Biblical Theology's Unique Character:**

**Distinguished from Other Disciplines:**

| Discipline | Method | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Biblical History** | Traces redemptive *acts* | Sphere of *being* |
| **Biblical Theology** | Traces revelatory *words* | Sphere of *knowing* |
| **Systematic Theology** | Arranges truth *logically* | Creates a *circle* |
| **Biblical Theology** | Traces truth *historically* | Reproduces a *line* |

**The "Line" vs. "Circle" Analogy:**
- Systematic Theology endeavors to construct a **circle** (logical arrangement)
- Biblical Theology seeks to reproduce a **line** (historical sequence)
- The line is like a tree stem growing in rings—each successive ring grows from the preceding one
- From this stem springs a crown with branches, leaves, flowers, and fruit—that crown is Systematic Theology

### 2. The Four Main Features of Revelation

**Feature #1: Historic Progressiveness**

**Why Revelation is Progressive:**

**Reason 1: Human Limitation**
- Truth is so overwhelmingly great that it had to gradually be let in upon us, "ray after ray"
- God arranged the sequence so we might gradually grasp and truly know Him

**Reason 2 (Deeper Ground): The Nature of Redemption**
> "Revelation is not an isolated act of God, existing without connection with the other divine acts of supernatural character. It constitutes a part of that great process of the new creation through which the present universe as an organic whole shall be redeemed from the consequences of sin and restored to its ideal state."

**The Relationship of Revelation and Redemption:**
- Redemption is successive, unfolding in history
- Revelation is the *interpretation* of redemption
- Therefore, revelation must unfold in instalments as redemption does
- **Key Insight:** Revelation accompanies the gradual unfolding of objective, central redemption—not subjective, individual redemption

**Two Stages of Redemption:**

| Stage | Character | Scope | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Objective-Central** | Acts outside the person | Universal, affecting all | OT → NT (ends with apostles) |
| **Subjective-Individual** | Acts within the person | Individual application | Ongoing until consummation |

**Why Revelation Ceased After the Apostolic Age:**
> "It is as unreasonable to expect revelations after the close of the Apostolic age as it would be to think that the great saving facts of that period can be indefinitely increased and repeated."

**Feature #2: Actual Embodiment in History**

**Revelation Includes Both Words and Acts:**
- Word-revelation: God's spoken communication
- Act-revelation: God's mighty deeds that themselves reveal truth

**Examples of Act-Revelation:**
- Redemption from Egypt
- Crucifixion and resurrection of Christ
- These are not merely *illustrations* of truth—they *are* revelation

**The Interdependence of Word and Act:**
> "Word and act always accompany each other... Without God's acts the words would be empty, without His words the acts would be blind."

**Feature #3: Organic Nature of Historical Progress**

**Organic vs. Non-Organic Progress:**
- Not every progressive increase is organic
- Organic progress: from seed-form to full growth
- **Crucial:** The seed is qualitatively perfect even though small

**Implications:**

**1. Perfection at Every Stage**
> "The organic progress is from seed-form to the attainment of full growth; yet we do not say that in the qualitative sense the seed is less perfect than the tree."

**2. Soteric Sufficiency from the Beginning**
- In the seed-form, the minimum of indispensable knowledge was already present
- The Gospel of Paradise contains germinally what becomes explicit in Paul

**3. "Epochal" Character**
- Revelation does not proceed with uniform motion
- Where great epoch-making redemptive acts accumulate, revelation is correspondingly accelerated
- Critical epochs: Abraham, Moses, David, "especially the days of the Son of Man"

**4. Increasing Multiformity**
- More diversity in NT than OT
- More in prophets than in Moses
- Diversity is *not* contradiction—it reveals the "much-variegated wisdom of God"

**The Divine Shaping of Human Instruments:**
> "The truth having inherently many sides, and God having access to and control of all intended organs of revelation, shaped each one of these for the precise purpose to be served. The Gospel having a precise, doctrinal structure, the doctrinally-gifted Paul was the fit organ for expressing this, because his gifts had been conferred and cultivated in advance with a view to it."

**Feature #4: Practical Adaptability**

**Revelation is Not Primarily Intellectual:**
> "God's self-revelation to us was not made for a primarily intellectual purpose."

**The Semitic vs. Greek Concept of Knowledge:**
- **Greek (Hellenic):** To mirror the reality of a thing in one's consciousness
- **Semitic (Biblical):** To have the reality of something practically interwoven with one's life through closest intercourse

**Revelation in the Context of Covenant:**
> "God has not revealed Himself in a school, but in the covenant; and the covenant as a communion of life is all comprehensive, embracing all the conditions and interests of those contracting it."

### 3. Guiding Principles for Biblical Theology

**Principle #1: Infallibility of Revelation**

**Rooted in Theism:**
<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">If God be personal and conscious, then the inference is inevitable that in every mode of self-disclosure He will make a faultless expression of His nature and purpose. He will communicate His thought to the world with the stamp of divinity on it.</mark>

**The Alternative is Pantheism:**
- If revelation were fallible, God would be "tied up in the limitations and relativities of the world"
- This makes the medium of expression obstruct His intercourse with the world
- Such a view has a pantheistic, not theistic, background

**Principle #2: Objectivity of Revelation**

**Real Communications from God:**
- Revelation comes *ab extra* (from outside)
- This is not the "dictation view" (though dictation sometimes occurred)
- It's "unscientific" to dismiss this, for "the statements of the recipients of revelation show that such a process not seldom took place"

**Subjective Revelation Also Exists:**
- Some revelation is subjective: the Spirit's activity upon human subconsciousness causing God-intended thoughts to well up
- The Psalms offer examples
- **Critical:** Even subjective revelation has "absolute divine authority; otherwise it could not properly be called revelation"

**Warning Against Modern Subjectivizing:**
> "A favourite form is to confine revelation proper to the bare acts of self-disclosure performed by God, and then to derive the entire thought-content of the Bible from human reflection upon these acts. Such a theory, as a rule, is made a cover for involving the whole teaching of the Bible in the relativity of purely human reflection."

**Principle #3: Plenary Inspiration**

**Why Inspiration Matters for Biblical Theology:**
- If biblical theology studies only past beliefs (not guaranteed truth), it belongs to *Historical* Theology, not *Exegetical* Theology
- Our conception: revelation is from God, guaranteed by inspiration

**Plenary Inspiration is Required:**
- "Partial inspiration is a modern figment having no support in what the Bible teaches about its own make-up"
- Whenever NT speaks of OT inspiration, it's "always in the most absolute, comprehensive terms"
- "It is either 'plenary inspiration' or nothing at all"

**Three Reasons Plenary Inspiration is Essential:**

**1. Revelation Includes Historical Facts**
> "These facts moreover are not of a subordinate character: they constitute the central joints and ligaments of the entire body of redemptive revelation. From them the whole receives its significance and colouring."

**2. The Bible Gives Its Own Philosophy**
> "Paul, for instance, has his views in regard to the revelation structure of the Old Testament... If we believe that Paul was inspired in these matters, then it ought greatly to facilitate our task."

**3. Unity of Scripture's Teaching**
- The trustworthiness of revelations depends on the trustworthiness of their historical setting
- Unless historicity is vouched for "in a more reliable sense than can be done by mere historical research," revelation's value becomes doubtful

### 4. The Structure of Redemptive Revelation

**The Covenant Structure:**

**Pre-Redemptive Special Revelation:**
- Period: Creation to Fall
- Name: **Covenant of Works**
- Purpose: To subject man to an intensified, contracted probation to bring him to a state where he could no longer sin

**Redemptive Special Revelation:**
- Period: Fall to Consummation
- Name: **Covenant of Grace**
- Divisions: Old Covenant (Moses to Christ) and New Covenant (Christ to eternal state)

**The Meaning of "Covenant" (*Berith*):**

**Hebrew *Berith*:**
1. Never means "testament" (Hebrews had no concept of last will and testament)
2. Never means an "agreement" between equals
3. Means: A one-sided promise concluded by a special religious sanction
4. Outstanding characteristic: **unalterableness**
5. Can be broken by man, resulting in violation of its sacred religious sanction

**Greek *Diatheke*:**
1. Commonly means "testament"
2. Originally meant "a disposition that one made for himself"
3. LXX translators chose *diatheke* (not *syntheke* = agreement) to preserve the idea of divine sovereignty

**The Epochs of Old Testament Revelation:**

**1. Mosaic Epoch**
- The factual basis: Redemption from Egypt (type of deliverance from sin)
- The *berith* made at Sinai (Exodus 19, 24)
- The theocracy: God as direct King over Israel
- The Decalogue: Joining redemption (preamble) with ethics (commands)
- The ritual system: Symbolical and typical

**2. Prophetic Epoch**
- Guardians of the unfolding theocracy
- Restricted themselves to the *word* as their instrument
- Retrospectively: Linked to patriarchal and Mosaic past
- Prospectively: Anticipated the future through prediction
- Two main periods: Pre-writing prophets (Samuel to 8th century) and Writing prophets (8th century to close of OT)

**The Epochs of New Testament Revelation:**

**1. Revelation Connected with the Nativity**
- The vertical descent: From divine to human, from super-historical to historical
- The horizontal connection: Prophecy and fulfillment
- Mode of expression: OT language and forms
- Two ancient lines: Coming of Jehovah + Coming of Messiah (later shown to converge)

**2. Revelation Connected with John the Baptist**
- John as the culmination of OT prophecy
- Forerunner of entire OT with reference to Christ
- Baptism: Sacramental, preparatory, prospective to coming judgment
- Jesus' baptism: Identification with sinful Israel despite personal sinlessness

**3. Revelation in Jesus' Public Ministry**
- The Logos as comprehensive revealer: Nature, OT, Incarnation, Apostolic witness
- Kingdom of God teaching: Present (gradual, internal, spiritual) and Future (catastrophic, visible, perfect)
- Parables: Based on parallelism between natural and spiritual creation
- Attitude toward OT: Realistic, authoritative, goal-oriented (fulfilled in Himself)

**4. Apostolic Revelation**
- Interpretation of the finished work of Christ
- Final stage of objective-central revelation
- After this, no new revelation (only subjective appropriation)

## II. PAULINE ESCHATOLOGY

### 1. Eschatology as the Framework of Paul's Theology

**The Central Thesis:**
**Paul's entire theology is eschatological.** Justification, sanctification, the resurrection, the Spirit, the church—all are understood within an eschatological framework of two ages.

**The Two-Age Structure:**

| This Present Age | The Age to Come |
|---|---|
| Under sin, death, law | Under righteousness, life, grace |
| Flesh dominates | Spirit dominates |
| Corruption, mortality | Incorruption, immortality |
| Groaning, suffering | Glory, consummation |

**Key Insight: The Ages Overlap**
- The age to come has *already* broken into this present age through Christ's resurrection
- Believers live "between the times"—experiencing the firstfruits of the age to come while still in this present age
- This creates the "already/not yet" tension throughout Paul's theology

**Foundational Texts:**

**1 Corinthians 10:11**
> "...upon whom the ends of the ages have come."

**Galatians 1:4**
> "[Christ] gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil age."

**Ephesians 1:20-21**
> "...raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality, and power..."

---

### 2. The Resurrection as the Hinge of the Ages

**Resurrection = Inauguration of the Age to Come**

**Christ's Resurrection is Not Mere Resuscitation:**
- It is the beginning of the new creation
- His resurrection body is the firstfruits of the new cosmos
- The resurrection introduced eschatological realities into present history

**1 Corinthians 15:20-28: The Eschatological Harvest**

**The Structure:**
- **Firstfruits:** Christ's resurrection (the beginning of harvest)
- **Main Harvest:** Believers' resurrection at His coming
- **Final Act:** Destruction of death (the last enemy)
- **Consummation:** God all in all

**Key Verses:**

**v. 20:** "But now Christ has been raised from the dead, **the firstfruits** of those who are asleep."

**v. 23:** "But each in his own order: Christ **the firstfruits**, then at his coming **those who are Christ's**."

**v. 26:** "**The last enemy that will be abolished is death.**"

**Theological Implications:**

**1. Corporate Solidarity**
- Christ and believers form one organic unity
- What happened to the Head will happen to the body
- His resurrection guarantees ours

**2. The "Already" of Resurrection**
- Believers have *already* been raised with Christ (Ephesians 2:6; Colossians 3:1)
- This is not metaphorical but a spiritual reality
- Seated with Him in the heavenlies *now*

**3. The "Not Yet" of Resurrection**
- Physical resurrection still future (Romans 8:23)
- "Redemption of our bodies" at Christ's return
- Transformation from mortal to immortal (1 Corinthians 15:51-54)

---

### 3. The Spirit as the Guarantee of Eschatological Inheritance

**The Spirit's Eschatological Character:**

**The Spirit Belongs to the Age to Come**
- Outpouring of Spirit was an OT sign of the last days (Joel 2; Isaiah 32:15; Ezekiel 36:27)
- Paul sees Pentecost as fulfillment—the age to come has arrived
- Possession of the Spirit proves believers already inhabit eschatological reality

**Key Pauline Terms for the Spirit's Eschatological Function:**

**1. "Firstfruits" (*aparche*) — Romans 8:23**
> "...ourselves also, who have **the firstfruits of the Spirit**, groan within ourselves, waiting eagerly for our adoption as sons, the redemption of our body."

- The Spirit is the beginning of the eschatological harvest
- Where firstfruits exist, the full harvest is guaranteed
- We possess the age to come's beginning installment

**2. "Guarantee/Earnest" (*arrabon*) — 2 Corinthians 1:22; 5:5; Ephesians 1:14**
> "...who also sealed us and gave us **the Spirit in our hearts as a guarantee**" (2 Cor 1:22)

- Commercial term: down payment guaranteeing full payment
- The Spirit is the down payment of our full inheritance
- Possession of the Spirit legally obligates God to give the full inheritance

**3. "Seal" (*sphragis*) — Ephesians 1:13; 4:30**
> "...you were **sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise**" (Eph 1:13)

- Mark of ownership
- Authentication of genuineness
- Security until the day of redemption (Ephesians 4:30)

**The Spirit and the Two Ages:**

| Function | Relation to Ages |
|---|---|
| **Resurrection Agent** | Spirit raised Christ (Romans 8:11), will raise us |
| **Life-Giver** | Spirit gives life of the age to come (Romans 8:10-11) |
| **Sanctifier** | Spirit progressively transforms into Christ's image (2 Cor 3:18) |
| **Liberation** | Spirit frees from law of sin and death (Romans 8:2) |

---

### 4. Justification as Eschatological Verdict

**Justification is the Final Judgment Brought into the Present**

**The Eschatological Nature of Justification:**

**Traditional View:**
- Justification is a legal declaration of righteousness
- Based on Christ's imputed righteousness
- Received by faith

**Vos's Eschatological Insight:**
- Justification is the *eschatological verdict of the last day* pronounced *now*
- It anticipates the final judgment
- God declares in the present what He will declare on the last day

**Romans 8:33-34**
> "Who will bring a charge against God's elect? **God is the one who justifies**; who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died, yes, rather who was raised, who is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us."

**The Eschatological Courtroom:**
- Final judgment is an eschatological event
- In justification, God renders the verdict of that final judgment *now*
- No condemnation remains because the eschatological verdict is already settled

**The Basis: Christ's Resurrection**
- Romans 4:25: "...raised because of our justification"
- Christ's resurrection is God's declaration that sin has been atoned for
- The resurrection verdict is applied to believers in justification

**The "Already/Not Yet" of Justification:**

**Already:**
- Verdict rendered (Romans 8:1)
- No condemnation (Romans 8:33-34)
- Peace with God (Romans 5:1)
- Righteousness imputed (Romans 4:3-5)

**Not Yet:**
- Public vindication at last day (Romans 2:13—"doers of the law will be justified")
- Final declaration before assembled universe
- Resurrection of the body as visible proof of justification

---

### 5. Sanctification and the "Already/Not Yet"

**Sanctification is Life in the Overlap of the Ages**

**The Indicative and the Imperative:**

**Indicative (What God Has Done):**
- "You have died" (Colossians 3:3)
- "You were raised with Christ" (Colossians 3:1)
- "You are a new creation" (2 Corinthians 5:17)
- "Sin shall not have dominion over you" (Romans 6:14)

**Imperative (What We Must Do):**
- "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you" (Colossians 3:5)
- "Seek the things that are above" (Colossians 3:1)
- "Walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:4)
- "Do not let sin reign in your mortal body" (Romans 6:12)

**The Relationship:**
- The imperative *flows from* the indicative
- We are commanded to *become* what we *already are* eschatologically
- Sanctification is the process of manifesting in this present age what is already true in the age to come

**Romans 6: Death and Resurrection with Christ**

**The Eschatological Union:**

**vv. 3-4:** Baptism represents union with Christ's death and resurrection
> "...buried with him through baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, **so we too might walk in newness of life**."

**vv. 5-7:** United in His death and resurrection
> "For if we have become **united with him in the likeness of his death**, certainly we shall also be in the likeness of **his resurrection**."

**vv. 10-11:** Reckoning ourselves dead and alive
> "...even so **consider yourselves to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus**."

**The Logic:**
1. Christ died to sin's dominion (v. 10)
2. We died with Christ (vv. 3-4)
3. Therefore, we are dead to sin's dominion (v. 11)
4. Conclusion: Live accordingly (vv. 12-13)

**The Eschatological "Newness":**
- "Newness of life" (*kainotes zoes*) is resurrection life
- The age to come's life *now* operating in mortal bodies
- Not mere moral improvement—eschatological transformation

---

### 6. The Church as the New Creation Community

**The Church Exists in the Overlap of the Ages**

**Ephesians: The Church as Heavenly Reality**

**Ephesians 1:3**
> "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, **who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ**."

**Ephesians 2:6**
> "...and **raised us up with him, and seated us with him in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus**."

**The "Heavenly Places" (*epouraniois*):**
- Not merely future heaven
- The realm of the age to come
- Where Christ reigns (Ephesians 1:20)
- Where believers *already* are positioned (Ephesians 2:6)
- Where spiritual battle occurs (Ephesians 6:12)

**The Church's Dual Existence:**

| In This Age | In the Age to Come |
|---|---|
| On earth, suffering | In heavenly places, reigning |
| Mortal bodies | United to risen Christ |
| Spiritual warfare | Seated in victory |
| Groaning | Blessed with every blessing |

**Colossians 3:1-4: Hidden Life in the Age to Come**

**v. 1:** "Therefore if you have been raised up with Christ, **seek the things above**, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God."

**v. 3:** "For **you have died and your life is hidden with Christ in God**."

**v. 4:** "**When Christ, who is our life, is revealed**, then you also will be revealed with Him in glory."

**The Hiddenness:**
- Present reality: Life is "hidden with Christ in God"
- Not yet manifested to the world
- Future reality: Will be revealed at Christ's appearing
- Now we see through a glass darkly; then face to face

## III. THE SELF-DISCLOSURE OF JESUS

### 1. The Consciousness of Jesus Regarding His Own Person

**Vos's Approach:**
- Not to psychologize or speculate about Jesus' inner mental states
- To examine Jesus' *objective claims* and *self-testimony* in the Gospels
- To understand how Jesus presented Himself in relation to the kingdom and to God

**The Synoptic Presentation:**

**Jesus as the Bringer of the Kingdom:**
- The kingdom arrives *in* Jesus' person and ministry
- His miracles are signs that the kingdom has come (Matthew 12:28)
- He is not merely a herald but the very content of the kingdom message

**The "Messianic Secret" (Mark):**
- Jesus often commands silence about His identity
- Revealed privately to disciples, hidden from crowds
- Not because He was uncertain, but because of the timing of revelation
- Full disclosure awaits the cross and resurrection

**The Johannine Presentation:**

**Jesus as the Revealer of the Father:**
- The Father and Son language is pervasive
- Jesus is the One sent from the Father
- Mutual indwelling: "I in the Father and the Father in me" (John 14:10-11)
- No one comes to the Father except through Jesus (John 14:6)

**Jesus' "I Am" Statements (John's Gospel):**

| Statement | Reference | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| "I am the bread of life" | John 6:35 | Source of spiritual sustenance |
| "I am the light of the world" | John 8:12 | Revelation and salvation |
| "Before Abraham was, I am" | John 8:58 | Eternal pre-existence |
| "I am the resurrection and the life" | John 11:25 | Eschatological life-giver |
| "I am the way, the truth, and the life" | John 14:6 | Exclusive access to God |
| "I am the vine" | John 15:5 | Source of spiritual life |

**The Absolute "I AM" (John 8:58):**
> "Jesus said to them, 'Truly, truly, I say to you, **before Abraham was, I am**."

- Echoes Exodus 3:14 (God's self-identification to Moses)
- Claims eternal, divine existence
- Not "I was" but "I am"—the timeless present of deity

---

### 2. Jesus and the Kingdom of God

**The Kingdom's Two-Fold Character:**

**The Present Kingdom:**
- Comes gradually, internally, invisibly
- Contains imperfections (wheat and tares grow together)
- Spiritual reality before visible manifestation
- Operates through Word and Spirit

**The Future Kingdom:**
- Comes catastrophically, visibly, universally
- Perfectly realized, no imperfections
- Consummation at Christ's return
- Physical as well as spiritual

**Key Parables of the Kingdom:**

**Seed Parables (Mark 4; Matthew 13):**
- Organic growth from small beginnings
- Hidden development leading to visible harvest
- Present hiddenness, future manifestation

**Wheat and Tares (Matthew 13:24-30):**
- Mixed character of the present kingdom
- Patience required until the harvest (final judgment)
- Both grow together until the eschaton

**The Essence of the Kingdom:**

**Definition:**
> "The Kingdom of God means the actual exercise of the divine supremacy in the interest of the divine glory."

**Three Spheres of Divine Supremacy:**

**1. Sphere of Power**
- Correlate: Faith
- Faith recognizes God's omnipotence
- Miracle contexts emphasize this
- Faith is itself a divine work

**2. Sphere of Righteousness**
- Correlate: Repentance
- Righteousness = what agrees with and pleases God
- Repentance = turning from sin to alignment with God's will

**3. Sphere of Blessedness**
- The kingdom brings true happiness
- Beatitudes describe kingdom citizens
- Joy and peace in the Holy Spirit

---

### 3. Jesus' Attitude Toward the Old Testament

**The Fundamental Stance:**

**Jesus' Unique Relationship to the OT:**
- Not merely that He affirmed its truthfulness (every orthodox Jew did)
- **Unique factor:** The OT arrives at its goal in Himself
- Without Him, the OT would lose its significance
- He is the telos (goal/end) of the law (Romans 10:4)

**Jesus' Realistic View of OT Religion:**

**"A Religion of the Book":**
- His discourse is saturated with OT language
- The higher His teaching, the more frequent His quotations
- Used Scripture in His crises (temptation, cross)

**Scripture as Rule:**
- Treats Scripture as a rule of faith and practice
- Authenticates His Messiahship by pointing to OT prophecy fulfillment
- Opponents never tried to cast suspicion on His attitude toward Scripture

**The Fulfillment Theme:**

**Matthew 5:17**
> "Do not think that I came to abolish the Law or the Prophets; **I did not come to abolish but to fulfill**."

**Types of Fulfillment:**

**1. Prophetic Fulfillment**
- Direct predictions of Messiah (e.g., Isaiah 53; Psalm 22)
- Messianic kingship (2 Samuel 7; Psalm 110)

**2. Typological Fulfillment**
- OT persons, events, institutions foreshadow Christ
- Examples: Adam (Romans 5), Moses (Hebrews 3), Temple (John 2), Passover (1 Cor 5:7)

**3. Ethical Fulfillment**
- Sermon on the Mount: Jesus gives the true interpretation
- Not abolishing but bringing to full expression
- The OT pointed toward the higher righteousness Jesus embodies

**Jesus' Abrogation of OT Institutions:**

**The Principle:**
- Jesus did not criticize OT as *wrong for its time*
- He superseded it as *unsuitable for the incoming era*
- The OT was preparation; Christ is consummation
- The shadow gives way to the substance (Colossians 2:17)

---

## IV. HERMENEUTICAL PRINCIPLES FROM VOS

### 1. The Christocentric Reading of Scripture

**All Scripture Points to Christ:**
- Not merely predictive prophecy, but the whole organic structure
- OT is promise; NT is fulfillment
- Every epoch of revelation leads forward to Christ

**Luke 24:27, 44-47**
> "Then beginning with Moses and with all the prophets, He explained to them **the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures**... all things which are written about Me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms must be fulfilled."

**The Organic Connection:**
- Not isolated proof-texts pointing to Christ
- The entire redemptive-historical line climaxes in Him
- He is the Second Adam, True Israel, Davidic King, Suffering Servant, Eschatological Prophet, Final Temple

---

### 2. The Redemptive-Historical Method

**Interpreting Scripture in Its Redemptive Context:**

**Avoid Two Errors:**

**1. Flat Reading (Ignoring Historical Progress)**
- Treating all Scripture as if written at the same time
- Missing the organic development
- Failing to recognize fuller revelation in later stages

**2. Pure Historicism (Ignoring Organic Unity)**
- Treating each part as isolated historical artifact
- Missing the divine Author's unifying purpose
- Severing texts from redemptive trajectory

**The Correct Method:**
- Read each text in its own historical epoch
- Recognize its place in the line of unfolding revelation
- Trace connections backward and forward
- See how it prepares for or fulfills earlier revelation
- Understand it in light of Christ, the telos of revelation

**Example: Understanding the Law**

**In Its Historical Epoch:**
- Given to Israel after redemption from Egypt
- Regulates theocratic life
- Contains ceremonial, civil, moral elements

**In Redemptive History:**
- Points forward to need for atonement (sacrifices)
- Reveals God's holiness and man's sinfulness
- Acts as guardian until Christ (Galatians 3:24)

**In Light of Christ:**
- Fulfilled in Christ's perfect obedience
- Ceremonial law abolished, moral law upheld and deepened
- No longer covenant of works but instrument of sanctification under grace

---

### 3. Symbolism and Typology

**Vos's Definitions:**

**Symbol:**
> "Religiously, a symbol is something that portrays a certain fact or principle or relationship of a spiritual nature in visible form. The things it pictures are of present existence and application."

**Type:**
> "A type is prospective: it relates to what will become real or applicable in the future."

**The Relationship:**
> "A type can never be a type independent of its being first a symbol."

**The Rule for Typology:**
> "Only after having discovered what a thing symbolizes can we proceed to put the question of what it typifies, for the latter can never be anything else than the former lifted to a higher plane."

**Examples:**

**The Tabernacle:**
- **Symbolically:** God dwelling with His people (present reality in Moses' day)
- **Typically:** Christ tabernacling among us (John 1:14); Church as God's temple (1 Cor 3:16); New Jerusalem (Rev 21:3)

**The Passover:**
- **Symbolically:** Deliverance from Egypt through substitutionary death (present in Exodus)
- **Typically:** Christ our Passover sacrificed for us (1 Cor 5:7); Deliverance from sin and death

**The Sacrificial System:**
- **Symbolically:** Expiation and consecration in Israel's worship (present function)
- **Typically:** Christ's once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 9-10)

**Principles for Valid Typology:**

**1. Grounded in Redemptive History**
- Must be part of God's ordained redemptive acts
- Not fanciful allegorizing
- Based on actual historical correspondences

**2. Divinely Intended**
- God designed the type to foreshadow the antitype
- Not merely human discovery of parallels
- Scripture itself identifies the type-antitype relationship (or strongly implies it)

**3. Escalation Principle**
- The antitype is always greater than the type
- Antitype fulfills and transcends the type
- From shadow to substance, from copy to original

## V. PRACTICAL USES OF BIBLICAL THEOLOGY

### 1. Exhibits the Organic Structure of Scripture

**The Advantage:**
> "It shows to him that in the Bible there is an organization finer, more complicated, more exquisite than even the texture of muscles and nerves and brain in the human body; that its various parts are interwoven and correlated in the most subtle manner, each sensitive to the impressions received from all the others, perfect in itself, and yet dependent upon the rest."

**The Apologetic Value:**
- Such organic structure cannot be the product of chance or human invention
- Argues powerfully for divine authorship
- "The organic structure of the truth and the organic development of revelation as portrayed in the Bible bear exactly the same relation to Supernaturalism that the argument from design in nature bears to Theism."

---

### 2. Antidote to Destructive Criticism

**The Problem of Modern Criticism:**
> "These modern theories, however much may be asserted to the contrary, disorganize the Scriptures. Their chief danger lies, not in affirmations concerning matters of minor importance, concerning errors in historical details, but in the most radical claims upsetting the inner organization of the whole body of truth."

**The Solution:**
- Biblical theology demonstrates the organic unity
- Shows how one part stands or falls with all others
- Meets criticism positively by presenting revelation's structure
- Not merely defensive point-by-point refutation

---

### 3. Brings Freshness to Old Truth

**The Historical Vividness:**
> "It is certainly not without significance that God has embodied the contents of revelation, not in a dogmatic system, but in a book of history, the parallel to which in dramatic interest and simple eloquence is nowhere to be found."

**The Practical Benefit:**
- Shows truth in its original life-setting
- "The dew of the morning of revelation upon its opening leaves"
- Makes the divine truth "bone of our bone and flesh of our flesh"
- Enables more effective handling of God's Word in ministry

---

### 4. Supports Systematic Theology

**The Relationship:**
> "Dogmatic Theology is the crown which grows out of all the work that Biblical Theology can accomplish."

**How Biblical Theology Serves Systematics:**

**1. Demonstrates Objective Foundation**
- Shows that doctrines rest on historical revelation
- Not arbitrary interpretations of isolated proof-texts
- Makes doctrines "grow out organically before our eyes from the stem of revelation"

**2. Maintains Living Contact**
- Keeps systematic theology connected to "the soil of divine realities"
- Prevents abstraction from biblical moorings
- Provides strength for further development

**3. Proclaims the Necessity of Right Belief**
> "It will proclaim the fact, too often forgotten and denied in our days, that true religion cannot dispense with a solid basis of objective knowledge of the truth."

---

### 5. Grants a New Vision of God's Glory

**The Ultimate Purpose:**
> "As of all theology, so of Biblical Theology, the highest aim cannot lie in man, or in anything that serves the creature. Its most excellent practical use is surely this, that it grants us a new vision of the glory of Him who has made all things to the praise of His own wonderful name."

**The Eschatological Perspective:**
> "But since on our behalf and for our salvation He has condescended to work and speak in the form of time, and thus to make His works and His speech partake of that peculiar glory that attaches to all organic growth, let us also seek to know Him as the One that is, that was, and that is to come, in order that no note may be lacking in that psalm of praise to be sung by the Church into which all our Theology must issue."

---

## VI. VOS'S ENDURING LEGACY

### Influence on Reformed Theology

**Direct Students:**
- John Murray (systematician)
- Ned B. Stonehouse (NT scholar)
- Cornelius Van Til (apologist)
- Meredith G. Kline (biblical theologian)

**Indirect Influence:**
- G.K. Beale (NT use of OT, biblical theology)
- Richard B. Gaffin Jr. (Pauline theology, resurrection)
- Herman Ridderbos (redemptive-historical exegesis)
- Edmund Clowney (redemptive-historical preaching)

**Methodological Contributions:**

**1. Biblical Theology as Distinct Discipline**
- Established the field's autonomy and legitimacy
- Distinguished from both historical theology and systematic theology
- Defined its method (historical) and object (progressive revelation)

**2. Redemptive-Historical Hermeneutics**
- Provided framework for reading Scripture organically
- Avoided both rationalistic fragmentation and flat biblicism
- Showed how to trace redemptive history without losing doctrinal precision

**3. Inaugurated Eschatology**
- Articulated "already/not yet" before it became standard terminology
- Demonstrated eschatology pervades all of Scripture, not just apocalyptic texts
- Showed how NT writers understood OT promises as inaugurated in Christ

**4. Christocentric Interpretation**
- Demonstrated how all Scripture organically points to Christ
- Avoided allegorical excesses while maintaining typological validity
- Showed Christ as telos of redemptive history

## VII. KEY QUOTES

**On Biblical Theology's Definition:**
> "Biblical Theology, rightly defined, is nothing else than the exhibition of the organic progress of supernatural revelation in its historic continuity and multiformity."

**On Revelation's Necessity:**
> "Only in so far as such a being chooses to open up itself can we come to know it. All spiritual life is by its very nature a hidden life, a life shut up in itself."

**On Revelation and Redemption:**
> "Revelation is not an isolated act of God, existing without connection with the other divine acts of supernatural character. It constitutes a part of that great process of the new creation through which the present universe as an organic whole shall be redeemed from the consequences of sin."

**On Organic Progress:**
> "The organic progress is from seed-form to the attainment of full growth; yet we do not say that in the qualitative sense the seed is less perfect than the tree."

**On Word and Deed:**
> "Word and act always accompany each other, and in their interdependence strikingly illustrate our former statement... Without God's acts the words would be empty, without His words the acts would be blind."

**On Infallibility:**
> "If God be personal and conscious, then the inference is inevitable that in every mode of self-disclosure He will make a faultless expression of His nature and purpose. He will communicate His thought to the world with the stamp of divinity on it."

**On Divine Shaping of Revelation:**
> "The truth having inherently many sides, and God having access to and control of all intended organs of revelation, shaped each one of these for the precise purpose to be served."

**On Plenary Inspiration:**
> "Whenever the New Testament speaks about the inspiration of the Old, it is always in the most absolute, comprehensive terms... It is either 'plenary inspiration' or nothing at all."

**On Christ as Goal of OT:**
> "The unique fact in which Jesus regarded the OT was that it arrived at its goal in Himself, so that without Him the OT would lose its significance."

**On Biblical Theology's Apologetic Value:**
> "The organic structure of the truth and the organic development of revelation as portrayed in the Bible bear exactly the same relation to Supernaturalism that the argument from design in nature bears to Theism."

**On the Ultimate Goal:**
> "As of all theology, so of Biblical Theology, the highest aim cannot lie in man, or in anything that serves the creature. Its most excellent practical use is surely this, that it grants us a new vision of the glory of Him who has made all things to the praise of His own wonderful name."
