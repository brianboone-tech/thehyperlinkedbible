## Core Vision

### The Big Picture

The Hyperlinked Bible exists to help readers **trace the unified narrative of Scripture**—from Genesis 1-3 to its fulfillment in Jesus Christ—by making explicit the interconnected web of cross-references, literary structures, and thematic patterns that the biblical authors themselves embedded in the text.

**The foundational principle**: "And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself." (Luke 24:27)

The Hyperlinked Bible makes the "Luke 24:27 reading experience" accessible to anyone willing to explore Scripture as a unified Story, using the tools of digital hypertext to restore what was always there—the Bible as the first hyperlinked text.

### What Makes This Different

**Traditional Bible study approaches**:
- Commentary-driven: Relies on external voices to explain the text
- Fragmented: Reads verse-by-verse or book-by-book without seeing connections
- Surface-level: Focuses on immediate context while missing canonical context
- Scholar-dependent: Requires expertise to trace cross-references manually

**The Hyperlinked Bible's approach**:
- **Scripture-driven**: The Bible interprets itself through explicit cross-references
- **Christocentric without allegorizing**: Following Sidney Greidanus's seven ways, we trace authentic roads from OT texts to Christ based on the text's own message, redemptive-historical trajectory, and canonical connections—not by imposing foreign meanings onto details
- **Interconnected**: Makes visible the web of quotations, allusions, echoes, and typological patterns that the biblical authors themselves embedded
- **Multi-layered**: Balances immediate context (authorial intent for original hearers) with canonical context (progressive revelation culminating in Christ)
- **Redemptive-historically grounded**: Every text located within God's unfolding plan from creation to new creation, with Christ as the climax
- **Accessible**: Anyone can follow the links—scholarly rigor without requiring expertise

**The result**: Readers can experience what the apostles experienced—seeing Christ in all the Scriptures, tracing how God's redemptive plan unfolds from Genesis to Revelation, and understanding how every part connects to the whole Story.

### The Seven Ways Christ Appears in the Old Testament

Following Sidney Greidanus's methodology in *Preaching Christ from the Old Testament*, The Hyperlinked Bible recognizes that Scripture provides **seven distinct yet complementary pathways** from any OT text to Jesus Christ:

1. **Redemptive-Historical Progression**: The foundational way—locating every OT passage within God's dynamic history that progresses steadily to its climax in Christ
2. **Promise-Fulfillment**: Tracing explicit promises (messianic prophecies, covenantal pledges) to their progressive fulfillment in Christ
3. **Typology**: Discerning divinely-intended historical correspondences where OT persons, events, or institutions prefigure NT realities in Christ
4. **Analogy**: Drawing comparisons based on continuity in God's character and actions (what God did for Israel, He does in Christ)
5. **Longitudinal Themes**: Following major biblical themes (covenant, kingdom, sacrifice, temple) as they develop through Scripture and culminate in Christ
6. **New Testament References**: Examining how NT authors understood and applied OT passages christologically
7. **Contrast**: Recognizing discontinuities where Christ transcends, supersedes, or reverses OT patterns (shadow vs. reality, temporary vs. eternal)

**The method**: Not every text uses all seven ways. After establishing the text's original meaning for Israel, we ask: "Which of these seven ways provide authentic roads from this passage to Christ?" The goal is not merely to "draw lines to Christ" but to **preach Christ authentically** so people trust Him, love Him, and obey Him.

**Quality control**: These seven ways provide methodological discipline against allegorizing (imposing foreign meanings) while enabling genuine christocentric interpretation grounded in the text's own message and redemptive-historical trajectory.

Think of The Hyperlinked Bible as a **hub-and-spoke model** where **Readable Bible** is the center, and specialized resources branch out from it via **inline links** at the end of each verse. This design prevents cognitive overload while providing unlimited depth for those who want it.

```
                    Word Studies Branch
                   (Reference Bible → Lexicons → TOSK)
                            ↑
                            |
Intertextuality Pairs ← READABLE BIBLE → Reference Bible
       ↓                    ↓
  OT to OT              Inline Links (IP | C | TOSK | TT)
  NT to OT                  ↓
       ↓                Chiasms
  Trajectory Tables
```

### The Hub: Readable Bible

**Location**: `/Readable Bible/[##] - [Book Name]/[Book] [Chapter].md`

**Purpose**: Primary reading experience—full text of Scripture with inline cross-reference links

- Each verse has the text with strategic inline links at the end:
  - `| [[...|IP]] |` → Intertextuality Pairs (OT to OT or NT to OT)

#### Quality Standard: The Three-Stage Interpretive Process

*Following Greidanus's redemptive-historical christocentric method*:

**Stage 1: Historical Interpretation**
- What did this text mean to its original audience?
- What was the author's intended message for Israel?
- Literary context, historical setting, genre conventions

**Stage 2: Canonical-Redemptive-Historical Interpretation**
- How does this text function within the sweep of redemptive history from creation to new creation?
- Which of the seven ways provide authentic roads from this passage to Christ?
- How does later revelation (especially the NT) illuminate this passage?

**Stage 3: Contemporary Application**
- What does this text, understood in light of Christ, mean for God's people today?
- How does this passage call hearers to faith, trust, love, and obedience to Christ?

**Critical principle**: We do NOT skip Stage 1 to jump directly to Christ. The christological meaning emerges FROM the text's original meaning understood redemptive-historically, NOT imposed artificially onto details.

### Interpretive Safeguards: Avoiding Common Errors

*Following Greidanus's warnings from church history*:

**What we AVOID**:
- ❌ **Allegorizing**: Imposing meanings unrelated to the author's intention or the text's redemptive-historical function (e.g., "the scarlet cord = Christ's blood" when the text's message is about God's grace to Gentiles)
- ❌ **Typologizing**: Overextending typology to incidental details rather than central thrust (e.g., "Isaac carrying wood = Christ carrying cross" focuses on a detail, not the passage's theme: "The Lord provides a lamb")
- ❌ **Forced interpretation**: Twisting texts to reach Christ in ways that violate authorial intent or historical meaning
- ❌ **Biographical preaching**: Using OT characters merely as moral examples, missing their place in redemptive history
- ❌ **Drawing lines without preaching**: Merely noting "this points to Christ" without proclaiming Christ's person, work, or teaching persuasively

**What we PURSUE**:
- ✅ **Authorial intent FIRST**: Establish what the text meant to its original audience before moving to Christ
- ✅ **Redemptive-historical location**: Place every text within God's unfolding plan that climaxes in Christ
- ✅ **Multiple verification**: Check all seven ways; use combinations that align with the text's central theme
- ✅ **Canonical coherence**: Ensure our christological reading fits Scripture's own patterns (promise-fulfillment, typology as the NT identifies it)
- ✅ **Authentic proclamation**: Not just identifying connections but preaching Christ so people trust, love, and obey Him

**The balance**: We interpret the OT "in its own context AND in the context of the New Testament" (Greidanus). The OT is a **Christian book** (the Bible of Jesus and the apostles), but we respect its pre-incarnation horizon while recognizing it witnesses to Christ.

### Type Classification System

Following Schnittjer & Harmon's methodology, we classify types along two axes:

**Forward-Looking vs. Backward-Looking Types**:
| Type | Definition | Identification |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Forward-Looking (Expectational)** | Contains textual indicators within original context pointing forward | OT text itself anticipates fulfillment (Deut 18:15-19, Ps 110:4) |
| **Backward-Looking (Prefigurative)** | Recognized as typological only from NT vantage point | Connection real but only visible retrospectively (Ps 22 → crucifixion, Jonah → Matt 12:40) |

**Direct vs. Providential Types**:
| Type | Definition | Interpretive Approach |
|------|------------|----------------------|
| **Direct Type** | Divinely commanded institution (Passover, sacrifices, Day of Atonement) | Details often significant because God prescribed them |
| **Providential Type** | Sovereignly arranged person/event (Adam, Moses, David, Exodus) | Focus on broad pattern/role, not incidental details |

**Application**: Every trajectory table entry includes type classification to guide interpretation and prevent overextension of typological connections.

### Distinguishing Legitimate Typology from Allegorizing

*Key insight from Greidanus*: "Typology is grounded in redemptive history; allegory floats above it."

**Legitimate Typology**:
- ✅ Based on **historical correspondences** God wove into redemptive history
- ✅ **NT explicitly identifies** the type-antitype relationship (or the correspondence is undeniable from canonical perspective)
- ✅ Focuses on **central thrust** of the passage, not incidental details
- ✅ Shows **escalation** from type to antitype (shadow → reality, temporary → eternal)
- ✅ Respects the text's **function for Israel** while recognizing its **forward movement** to Christ

**Examples of legitimate types**:
- Passover lamb → Christ our Passover (1 Cor 5:7)
- Bronze serpent → Christ lifted up (John 3:14)
- Temple → Christ's body (John 2:19-21)
- Adam → Christ the last Adam (Rom 5:14; 1 Cor 15:45)

**Illegitimate Typologizing** (what we avoid):
- ❌ Isolating **incidental details**: "Isaac carrying wood = Christ carrying cross" (misses the passage's theme: "The Lord provides a lamb")
- ❌ **Random connections**: "Scarlet cord = Christ's blood" when the text's point is God's grace to a Gentile
- ❌ **No NT warrant**: Creating types the NT never identifies, without clear canonical basis
- ❌ **String of types**: Extended metaphor where every element "means" something (this is allegory, not typology)

**Test**: Does the proposed typology arise from (1) the text's central message, (2) its redemptive-historical function, and (3) NT identification or clear canonical pattern? If not, it's likely typologizing or allegorizing.

- OpenBible.info voting threshold ensures scholarly consensus

**2. Methodological Transparency** (*see [[- Hermeneutics.md]] for complete framework*)

**3. Integration with Standard Resources**
- Lexicons (Hebrew/Greek word studies)
- Treasury of Scripture Knowledge (comprehensive parallels)
- Reformed theological tradition (Westminster, Poythress, Beale, Clowney)
- Standard critical editions (Masoretic Text, Nestle-Aland Greek NT, LXX)

**4. Theological Grounding**
- Rooted in historic Christian orthodoxy (Nicene, Chalcedonian)
- Reformed hermeneutical tradition (Scripture interprets Scripture)
- Canonical approach (biblical theology, redemptive-historical reading)
- Christ-centered without allegorizing

**5. Methodological Discipline**
- Seven-way framework (Greidanus) prevents arbitrary interpretation
- Must establish original meaning BEFORE moving to christological interpretation
- Typology verified by NT identification or clear canonical pattern
- Safeguards against allegorizing: no imposed meanings on details disconnected from author's intent
- The constraint: Our christological reading must emerge FROM the text's message and redemptive-historical trajectory, not be imposed artificially ONTO it

- Don't need to understand hermeneutical debates to see the connections

## Methodological Framework Summary

The Hyperlinked Bible integrates multiple complementary hermeneutical approaches:

### Primary Frameworks

| Framework | Source | Key Contribution |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| **Seven Ways to Christ** | Sidney Greidanus | Methodological discipline for authentic christocentric interpretation |
| **Typological Principles** | Patrick Fairbairn | Five structural principles for tracing types through dispensations |
| **Temple Theology** | G.K. Beale | Eden-to-New Jerusalem trajectory of God's dwelling |
| **Seven Hermeneutical Choices** | Schnittjer & Harmon | Connected interpretation, vertical context, biblical priority |

### Schnittjer & Harmon's Seven Choices (2024)

1. **Sequestered vs. Connected**: Interpret biblical intertextuality as interconnected (not isolated)
2. **Adjusting Meaning vs. Advancement**: Later texts advance/develop earlier meanings (not change them)
3. **Detecting Allusions**: Distinguish allusions from coincidental similarities via verbal/contextual evidence
4. **Horizontal vs. Vertical Context**: Use both synchronic (same time) and diachronic (across time) context
5. **Biblical vs. Extrabiblical**: Prioritize biblical parallels over Second Temple sources
6. **Forward vs. Backward Typology**: Classify types as expectational (forward-looking) or prefigurative (backward-looking)
7. **Prosopological Exegesis**: Recognize divine speech patterns where NT identifies new speakers

### The Integration

```
OT Text → OT-to-OT Development → NT-to-OT Fulfillment → Christ
   ↓              ↓                      ↓                ↓
Greidanus    Schnittjer          Fairbairn's        All frameworks
Seven Ways   Vertical Context    Escalation         converge in Christ
```

**Key Insight**: These frameworks are complementary, not competing. Each addresses different aspects of how Scripture interprets itself, all pointing to Christ as the fulfillment of God's redemptive plan.
