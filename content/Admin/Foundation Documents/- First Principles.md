# First Principles of Jesus and the Apostles

**Navigation**: [[- Hermeneutics|Hermeneutics]] | [[- G.K. Beale|G.K. Beale]] | [[- Geerhardus Vos|Geerhardus Vos]] | [[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] | [[- How to Study the Bible's Use of the Bible|Schnittjer & Harmon]]

---

## Overview

These five "First Principles" represent the foundational hermeneutical presuppositions that Jesus and the apostles brought to their interpretation of the Hebrew Scriptures. These are not arbitrary frameworks imposed upon the text but arise organically from the OT itself and were demonstrated by Christ in His post-resurrection teaching.

Understanding these principles is essential because **the NT authors' method of interpreting the OT becomes normative for how we should interpret Scripture**. As [[- How to Study the Bible's Use of the Bible|Schnittjer & Harmon]] argue: "The authors of the New Testament did not invent a new hermeneutic. They followed the well-worn interpretive path used by prophets, psalmists, narrators, visionaries, and sages of Israel's scriptures."

These five presuppositions work together as an integrated whole. Remove any one, and the others lose their foundation.

## 1. The Christological Axiom

<span style="color: #1e90ff; font-weight: bold;">Jesus is the Messiah, and the Hebrew Scriptures testify to Him.</span>

### Key Texts

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 27|Luke 24:27]] - "And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 44|Luke 24:44-47]] - "Everything written about me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms must be fulfilled."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 39|John 5:39-46]] - "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me... For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me."

### The Most Fundamental Principle

This axiom is the **interpretive key that unlocks the whole of Scripture**. Without it, the other four principles have no center of gravity.

As [[- Geerhardus Vos|Geerhardus Vos]] articulates: "The unique fact in which Jesus regarded the OT was that it arrived at its goal in Himself, so that without Him the OT would lose its significance." Christ is not merely predicted in isolated proof-texts; He is the **telos** (goal/end) toward which the entire OT revelation organically moves.

### What the Christological Axiom Claims

**1. The OT Was Always Christ-Directed**

This is not an imposition on the text but a claim about what the text was always pointing toward. As Jesus Himself declared, Moses "wrote of me" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 46|John 5:46]]). The entire structure of OT revelation—its promises, types, institutions, and prophetic oracles—was designed by God to prepare for and point to Christ.

**2. Christ Provides the Hermeneutical Bridge**

[[- How to Study the Bible's Use of the Bible|Schnittjer & Harmon]] identify "Jesus as Hermeneutical Key" as foundational: "The life, death, resurrection, and teachings of Jesus Christ provide the interpretive bridge between the testaments. Jesus himself taught that all Scripture points to him."

**3. The OT Arrives at Its Goal in Christ**

[[- Geerhardus Vos|Vos]] explains the unique nature of Jesus' relationship to the OT:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"It is not merely that He affirmed its truthfulness (every orthodox Jew did). The unique factor is that the OT arrives at its goal in Himself. Without Him, the OT would lose its significance. He is the telos (goal/end) of the law."</span>

### How All Scripture Points to Christ

Following [[- Hermeneutics|The Hyperlinked Bible's hermeneutical framework]], Scripture points to Christ through multiple means:

| Mode | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Direct Prophecy** | Explicit predictions of the Messiah | [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 4\|Isaiah 53:4-6]] |
| **Typology** | Historical patterns designed to foreshadow Christ | Adam/Christ ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 14\|Romans 5:14]]) |
| **Thematic Development** | Themes find fulfillment in Christ | Temple, Sacrifice, Exodus, Kingship |
| **Narrative Trajectory** | The story moves toward Christ as goal | The Seed Promise ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15\|Genesis 3:15]]) |

### Practical Implications

  ▸ Every OT text must be read in light of its place in the redemptive storyline moving toward Christ

  ▸ Christ-centered interpretation is not allegory but recognition of divine authorial intent

  ▸ We must ask of every passage: "How does this prepare for, anticipate, or connect to Christ?"

**See also**: [[- Hermeneutics#6. Christocentric Reading|Hermeneutics: Christocentric Reading]]

## 2. Eschatological Location

<span style="color: #1e90ff; font-weight: bold;">The apostles understood themselves as living in the inaugurated "last days" when God's promises were reaching fulfillment.</span>

### Key Texts

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 16|Acts 2:16-17]] - "This is what was uttered through the prophet Joel: 'And in the last days it shall be, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh.'"

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 11|1 Corinthians 10:11]] - "These things happened to them as examples, and they were written down for our instruction, on whom the ends of the ages have come."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 1#Galatians 1 . 4|Galatians 1:4]] - "Who gave himself for our sins to deliver us from the present evil age."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 2|Hebrews 1:2]] - "In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 6#Hebrews 6 . 5|Hebrews 6:5]] - Those who have "tasted... the powers of the age to come."

### The Age of Fulfillment Has Dawned

The apostles did not see OT prophecy as describing merely distant future events. They believed the messianic age had **already begun** in Christ. As [[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] explains:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The 'last days' prophesied in the OT are **not future** from the NT perspective but have **already begun** with Christ's first coming."</span>

This is the doctrine of **inaugurated eschatology**: the age to come has broken into the present age through Christ's death and resurrection.

### The Already/Not Yet Pattern

[[- Geerhardus Vos|Vos]] pioneered the recognition that Paul's theology is fundamentally eschatological, structured around "two ages":

| This Present Age | The Age to Come |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Under sin, death, law | Under righteousness, life, grace |
| Flesh dominates | Spirit dominates |
| Corruption, mortality | Incorruption, immortality |
| Groaning, suffering | Glory, consummation |

**Key Insight**: These ages **overlap** in the present. The age to come has already broken into this present age through Christ's resurrection. Believers live "between the times"—experiencing the firstfruits of the age to come while still in this present age.

### Scriptural Evidence of Inaugurated Eschatology

[[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] provides this comprehensive chart:

| OT Expectation | NT Fulfillment (Already) | NT Consummation (Not Yet) |
|----------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Resurrection of dead | Christ raised ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 20\|1 Cor 15:20]]) | Believers raised ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 51\|1 Cor 15:51-54]]) |
| New creation | Spiritual new creation ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17\|2 Cor 5:17]]) | Physical new creation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1\|Rev 21:1-5]]) |
| Spirit poured out | Pentecost ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 17\|Acts 2:17-21]]) | Spirit's final work ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 17\|Rev 22:17]]) |
| Kingdom established | Christ reigns ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 28\|Matt 12:28]]) | Kingdom consummated ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 15\|Rev 11:15]]) |
| Temple rebuilt | Christ/Church as temple ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19\|John 2:19]]; [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16\|1 Cor 3:16]]) | Eternal temple ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22\|Rev 21:22]]) |
| Sabbath rest | Rest in Christ ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28\|Matt 11:28]]) | Eternal rest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9\|Heb 4:9-11]]) |

### Why Eschatological Location Matters for Interpretation

**1. Scripture Isn't Merely Describing Past Events**

OT prophecy anticipates **present realities** for NT believers. When we read of Israel's restoration, temple rebuilding, or the new covenant, these are not merely future from the apostolic perspective—they are being fulfilled.

**2. The NT Transforms How OT Texts Are Understood**

Because the messianic age has dawned, texts that seemed to await distant fulfillment now have present application. The church is living in the era the prophets longed to see ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 16|Matthew 13:16-17]]).

**3. Salvation Is New Creation**

[[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] demonstrates that to be saved is to be brought into the eschatological new creation:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Christ's resurrection is not simply a resuscitation of a corpse but the beginning of the end-time new creation. His resurrected body is the first installment of the new cosmos."</span>

### OT Foundation for Eschatological Location

The concept of "latter days" is not NT innovation but rooted in the OT itself:

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 1|Genesis 49:1, 9-10]] - Jacob speaks of "the last days" and the coming ruler

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 24#Numbers 24 . 14|Numbers 24:14-19]] - Balaam prophesies what will happen "in the latter days"

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 3#Hosea 3 . 5|Hosea 3:5]] - Israel will return "in the latter days"

**See also**: [[- Hermeneutics#11. Inaugurated Eschatology|Hermeneutics: Inaugurated Eschatology]] | [[- G.K. Beale#1. The Central Thesis: Inaugurated Eschatology|Beale: Inaugurated Eschatology]]

## 3. Divine Authorship Behind Human Authors

<span style="color: #1e90ff; font-weight: bold;">Because God stands behind the human writers, texts can contain meanings and patterns that exceed what the original author consciously intended.</span>

### Key Texts

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 3#2 Timothy 3 . 16|2 Timothy 3:16]] - "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 1#2 Peter 1 . 20|2 Peter 1:20-21]] - "No prophecy of Scripture comes from someone's own interpretation. For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 10|1 Peter 1:10-12]] - "The prophets who prophesied about the grace that was to be yours searched and inquired carefully, inquiring what person or time the Spirit of Christ in them was indicating when he predicted the sufferings of Christ and the subsequent glories."

### The Doctrine of Dual Authorship

Scripture has both **human authors** (with historical contexts, intentions, and literary styles) and the **divine Author** (with ultimate design and unified purpose). As [[- Hermeneutics|the Hermeneutics document]] explains:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Both authorial intentions must be considered: the human author's **immediate meaning** in historical-grammatical context, and the divine Author's **ultimate meaning** as part of the canonical whole. This is not 'double meaning' but **organic development**—the divine intent includes and expands upon the human author's intent without contradicting it."</span>

### What Divine Authorship Enables

**1. Meanings That Exceed Conscious Intention**

[[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 10|1 Peter 1:10-12]] explicitly teaches that the prophets did not fully understand the implications of what they wrote. They "searched and inquired carefully" about their own prophecies. The Spirit of Christ was indicating truths through them that they could only dimly perceive.

**2. Patterns Embedded in History**

Because God sovereignly orchestrates history, He can embed typological patterns in actual events, persons, and institutions. These patterns are real because God designed them, even if the human authors did not consciously construct them.

[[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] articulates this:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Types are not arbitrary human discoveries but part of God's intentional design in redemptive history... Human ingenuity cannot create types. Only what God has embedded in Scripture through divine design qualifies as legitimate typology."</span>

**3. Canonical Unity**

Because all Scripture shares the same ultimate divine Author, later texts can legitimately interpret earlier texts. The NT authors' interpretations are not impositions but recognitions of the divine intent that was always present.

### How Divine Authorship Works

[[- Geerhardus Vos|Vos]] explains that inspiration is organic, not mechanical:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"If God be personal and conscious, then the inference is inevitable that in every mode of self-disclosure He will make a faultless expression of His nature and purpose. He will communicate His thought to the world with the stamp of divinity on it."</span>

This means:

  ▸ God worked **through** the human authors' personalities, contexts, and purposes—not bypassing them

  ▸ The human author's meaning is **true** and forms the foundation for further divine meaning

  ▸ Divine meaning **includes and expands** human meaning without contradicting it

  ▸ The fullness of divine meaning may only become clear in retrospect, from the vantage point of fulfillment

### The Relationship of Human and Divine Meaning

| Human Author's Meaning | Divine Author's Meaning |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| Immediate historical context | Canonical context |
| Understood by original audience | May await later revelation for clarity |
| Foundation for typological meaning | Includes typological and prophetic dimensions |
| Never contradicted by later revelation | Expanded and clarified by later revelation |

### Practical Implications

**1. Typological and Prophetic Readings Are Legitimate**

Because God stands behind the text, we can recognize patterns and prophecies that the human author may not have fully grasped. This is not allegory (arbitrary spiritual meanings) but recognition of divine design.

**2. The Human Author's Meaning Remains Foundational**

[[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] insists: "Our first care must be, according to the principles already established, to ascertain the views and impressions which, as parts of an existing religion, they were fitted to awaken in the ancient worshipper."

The typological meaning **builds upon**, never **replaces**, the original meaning.

**3. Later Scripture Interprets Earlier Scripture**

Because of common divine authorship, we should expect later revelation to clarify and expand earlier revelation. The NT's interpretation of the OT is authoritative precisely because it comes from the same ultimate Author.

### Vos on Plenary Inspiration

[[- Geerhardus Vos|Vos]] argues that this principle requires plenary (full) inspiration:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Whenever the New Testament speaks about the inspiration of the Old, it is always in the most absolute, comprehensive terms... It is either 'plenary inspiration' or nothing at all."</span>

**See also**: [[- Hermeneutics#2. Dual Authorship|Hermeneutics: Dual Authorship]] | [[- Geerhardus Vos#Principle #2: Objectivity of Revelation|Vos: Objectivity of Revelation]]

## 4. Typological Correspondence

<span style="color: #1e90ff; font-weight: bold;">Historical persons, events, and institutions genuinely prefigure later realities. God orchestrates history itself to create patterns—Adam/Christ, Exodus/New Exodus, Temple/Christ's body.</span>

### Key Texts

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 14|Romans 5:14]] - "Adam, who was a type of the one who was to come."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 6|1 Corinthians 10:6, 11]] - "Now these things took place as examples [τύποι] for us... they were written down for our instruction."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 5|Hebrews 8:5]] - "They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 17|Colossians 2:17]] - "These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ."

### Definition of Typology

[[- Hermeneutics|The Hyperlinked Bible's Hermeneutics document]] defines typology as:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The study of analogical correspondences between earlier and later escalated events, persons, institutions, and so forth within the historical framework of biblical revelation, and which sometimes from a retrospective viewpoint are perceived to have a prophetic function."</span>

### This Is Not Allegory

**The critical distinction**: Allegory finds "hidden meanings" unrelated to historical reality. Typology recognizes God's design in **actual history**.

[[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] distinguishes carefully:

| Feature | Typology | Allegory |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Historical basis | Real events/persons | May be fictional |
| Divine design | God-ordained patterns | Human invention |
| Correspondence | Essential features match | Arbitrary connections |
| Escalation | Antitype greater than type | No escalation required |
| Context-respecting | Honors original meaning | Ignores context |

### The Five Essential Characteristics of a Valid Type

From [[- Hermeneutics#Five Essential Characteristics of a Valid Type|the Hermeneutics document]]:

**1. Analogical Correspondence**

The type and antitype share essential features—structural, functional, and thematic similarity.

*Example*: David as type of Christ—both anointed kings, both shepherd God's people, both establish God's kingdom.

**2. Historicity**

Both type and antitype are historical realities, grounded in God's actual acts in space and time.

**3. Escalation (Heightening)**

The antitype is **greater** than the type—not mere repetition but intensification.

*Example*: The temple—OT: physical building in Jerusalem; NT: Christ's body, the church, new creation. From one place → everywhere God's people are.

**4. Pointing-Forwardness (Predictiveness)**

The type has a prospective orientation, designed by God to foreshadow what was coming.

**5. Retrospective Interpretation**

The typological connection becomes fully clear from the vantage point of fulfillment.

### Fairbairn's Principle of Escalation

[[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] emphasizes this critical principle:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The antitype is always higher than the type; and the work of grace transcends in excellence and glory the work of nature."</span>

Every valid type must demonstrate clear escalation:

| Type | Antitype | How Escalated |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Animal sacrifices | Christ's sacrifice | Repeated → once-for-all; animal → human; imperfect → perfect |
| Aaron's priesthood | Christ's priesthood | Mortal → immortal; Levitical → Melchizedekian |
| Physical temple | Christ/Church | One place → everywhere; stone → living stones |
| Exodus from Egypt | Redemption in Christ | Physical bondage → spiritual; temporal → eternal |
| Manna | Christ as bread of life | Daily bread → eternal sustenance; physical → spiritual |

### How to Validate Typology

[[- Hermeneutics#Determining Authorial Intent: Micro and Macro-Level Indicators|The Hermeneutics document]] draws on James Hamilton's criteria for establishing author-intended typology:

**Micro-Level Indicators**:
1. Reuse of significant terms (lexical points of contact)
2. Quotations of phrases or lines
3. Repeated sequences of events
4. Similarity in salvation-historical/covenantal significance

**Macro-Level Indicators**:
1. Chiastic positioning of corresponding units
2. Thematic parallels in matching structural positions
3. Consistent patterns across multiple literary structures

### Forward-Looking vs. Backward-Looking Types

[[- How to Study the Bible's Use of the Bible|Schnittjer & Harmon]] distinguish:

**Forward-Looking Types (Expectational)**:
- Contain indicators within the original context pointing to future fulfillment
- Often marked by explicit prospective language
- *Examples*: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 18#Deuteronomy 18 . 15|Deuteronomy 18:15-19]] ("a prophet like me"); [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 110#Psalms 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] ("a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek")

**Backward-Looking Types (Prefigurative)**:
- Not inherently expectational in original context
- Recognized as typological only in light of later fulfillment
- *Examples*: Jonah's three days; the bronze serpent

### Major Biblical Types

| Category | Examples | Antitype |
|----------|----------|----------|
| **Personal Types** | Adam, Noah, Moses, Aaron, David, Melchizedek, Solomon | Christ |
| **Event Types** | Passover, Red Sea Crossing, Wilderness Manna, Bronze Serpent | Christ's work |
| **Institution Types** | Sabbath, Sacrifices, Priesthood, Tabernacle, Feasts | Christ and His benefits |
| **Object Types** | Ark, Altar, Lampstand, Veil, Showbread | Christ and His body |

## 5. Corporate Solidarity

<span style="color: #1e90ff; font-weight: bold;">Individuals can represent and embody groups. The king embodies Israel; the Servant represents the remnant; Christ recapitulates Adam. This underwrites how messianic psalms and Servant songs find their fulfillment in Jesus.</span>

### Key Texts

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12-21]] - Adam's sin brought death to all; Christ's righteousness brings life to all who believe.

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 21|1 Corinthians 15:21-22]] - "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45-49]] - "The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 3|Isaiah 49:3-6]] - The Servant is called "Israel" yet has a mission to restore Israel.

### The One and the Many

In Semitic thought, the **one and the many oscillate**—an individual represents a group such that what the one does, the many are viewed as having done.

[[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] explains:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"In Semitic thought, the one and the many oscillate—an individual represents a group such that what the one does, the many are viewed as having done."</span>

### Biblical Examples of Corporate Solidarity

| Representative | Group Represented | Significance |
|----------------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Adam** | All humanity | His sin = all humanity's sin ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12\|Rom 5:12-21]]) |
| **Christ as Last Adam** | Believers | His righteousness = believers' righteousness |
| **Kings of Israel** | The nation | Their actions brought blessing/judgment on whole nation |
| **High Priest** | All Israel | Bore names of 12 tribes on breastplate |
| **The Servant** | True Israel | Both individual and corporate identity |

### The Suffering Servant: Individual Yet Israel

[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 3|Isaiah 49:3-6]] presents a profound paradox that only corporate solidarity can explain:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"He said to me, 'You are my Servant, **Israel**, in whom I will show my glory.'... 'It is too small a thing that you should be my Servant to **raise up the tribes of Jacob and to restore the preserved ones of Israel**...'"</span>

[[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] unpacks the logic:

1. The Servant is called "Israel" (v. 3)
2. Yet the Servant's mission is to restore Israel (v. 6)
3. The Servant cannot be the whole nation (a nation cannot restore itself)
4. The Servant cannot be the remnant (the remnant is still sinful and needs restoration)
5. **Therefore**: The Servant must be an **individual messianic figure** who represents and sums up true Israel

### How Corporate Solidarity Explains Messianic Interpretation

This principle explains how texts addressed to Israel or the Davidic king can be fulfilled in Jesus:

**1. Hosea 11:1 → Matthew 2:15**

  ▸ Original context: "Out of Egypt I called my son" refers to Israel's exodus

  ▸ Matthew's application: Fulfilled in Jesus' return from Egypt

  ▸ Explanation: Jesus, as true Israel, recapitulates Israel's story. What was true of Israel is fulfilled in Him.

**2. Davidic Psalms → Christ**

The Davidic king was the corporate representative of Israel. Royal psalms (2, 45, 72, 89, 110) find their ultimate fulfillment in Christ because He is the ultimate Davidic king who perfectly represents and embodies Israel.

**3. Adam → Christ**

[[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 14|Romans 5:14]] identifies Adam as "a type of the one who was to come." Adam was the covenant head of all humanity; Christ is the covenant head of the new humanity.

### Fairbairn on Headship and the Need for a Second Adam

[[- Fairbairn|Fairbairn]] observes that even in the primeval period, thoughtful believers could perceive the need for a second Adam:


<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The thought was not far to seek, that the same constitution might somehow have place in connection with the work of recovery... A fallen head could give birth only to a fallen offspring—so the righteousness of Heaven had decreed; and the prospect of rising again to the possession of immortal life and blessing, seemed, by its very announcement, to call for the institution of another head, unfallen and yet human, through whom the prospect might be realized."</span>

The promise of the "seed of the woman" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]) pointed toward a second Adam who would undo the first Adam's ruin.

### Christ as True Israel and True Adam

[[- G.K. Beale|Beale]] demonstrates that these identities are two sides of the same coin:

| Title | Applied to Adam | Applied to Israel | Applied to Christ |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Son of God** | [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 38\|Luke 3:38]] | [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22\|Exod 4:22]]; [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 11#Hosea 11 . 1\|Hos 11:1]] | [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 17\|Matt 3:17]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 4\|Rom 1:4]] |
| **Son of Man** | [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 2#Ezekiel 2 . 1\|Ezek 2:1]] (=human) | [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 13\|Dan 7:13-14]] (=Israel) | [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62\|Mark 14:62]] (=Messiah) |
| **Firstborn** | — | [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 22\|Exod 4:22]]; [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 9\|Jer 31:9]] | [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 89#Psalms 89 . 27\|Ps 89:27]]; [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15\|Col 1:15, 18]] |
| **Image of God** | [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26\|Gen 1:26-27]] | (implicit in calling) | [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 4\|2 Cor 4:4]]; [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15\|Col 1:15]] |

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"To be identified with true Israel is not a narrow parochial identification—it's identification with what it means to be true humanity (true Adam). To be part of eschatological Israel is to recover the Adamic commission to reflect God's image and rule over creation."</span> — [[- G.K. Beale|Beale]]

### The Church's Identification with Israel Through Christ

Because Christ is true Israel, all who are united to Him by faith become part of true Israel:

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 29|Galatians 3:29]] - "If you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise."

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 16|Galatians 6:16]] - "The Israel of God"

  ▸ [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]] - "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession"

This is not "replacement theology" but **fulfillment theology**: the church is the continuation of faithful Israel, expanded to include Gentiles through the Messiah.

## How the Five Principles Work Together

These five principles form an integrated hermeneutical framework. Each depends upon and reinforces the others:

![[Resources/First_Principles_Diagram.png]]

**1. The Christological Axiom** provides the goal—all Scripture points to Christ.

**2. Eschatological Location** explains when we are in the story—the last days have begun; fulfillment is present reality.

**3. Divine Authorship** guarantees the validity of finding patterns that exceed human author's conscious intent.

**4. Typological Correspondence** shows the how—God designs history to create patterns that foreshadow Christ.

**5. Corporate Solidarity** explains the mechanism—Christ represents and embodies Israel/humanity, so what the OT says of them applies to Him.

---

## Practical Application: Reading Scripture with These Principles

When approaching any OT text, ask:

1. **Christological**: How does this text prepare for, anticipate, or connect to Christ?

2. **Eschatological**: Where does this fit in the already/not yet pattern? How is this being fulfilled in the present age?

3. **Divine Authorship**: What patterns or meanings might the divine Author have embedded that the human author may not have fully grasped?

4. **Typological**: Is there a historical correspondence that points forward to something greater? Does it meet the five criteria (correspondence, historicity, escalation, predictiveness, retrospection)?

5. **Corporate**: Who is the representative figure? How does Christ fulfill this role as true Israel or second Adam?

## Why These Principles Matter

[[- How to Study the Bible's Use of the Bible|Schnittjer & Harmon]] conclude:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The goal is not to impose external frameworks on Scripture, but to read Scripture according to its own self-interpreting patterns—the Bible's use of the Bible."</span>

These five principles are not foreign impositions but arise from Scripture itself. Jesus taught them on the road to Emmaus ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 27|Luke 24:27]]). The apostles practiced them throughout their writings. And they remain normative for how we should interpret the Bible today.

As [[- Hermeneutics|The Hyperlinked Bible's Hermeneutics document]] states:

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The interpretive perspective of biblical authors is both **valid** (correct) and **normative** (authoritative for how we should interpret). We should embrace the habits of mind, patterns of thought, and interpretive practices that the biblical authors model in their writings."</span>
