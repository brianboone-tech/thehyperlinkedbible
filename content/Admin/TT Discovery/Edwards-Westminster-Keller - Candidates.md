## Executive Summary

These three modern/contemporary works are **methodological** rather than **typological**. Unlike the Puritan/Victorian sources (Mather, Fairbairn, Gill, Pink) which catalog specific biblical types, these books teach **how to preach Christ from all Scripture** but provide **few new typological candidates**.

**Key Finding:** These are **hermeneutical** books, not **typological** catalogs. They explain the Redemptive-Historical Method (RHM) but assume readers will apply it to discover types themselves.

**Result:** Only **2-3 genuinely new candidates** identified across all three books.

## Book-by-Book Analysis

### 1. Jonathan Edwards - History of Redemption

**Character:** Redemptive-historical overview from creation to consummation
**Structure:** Three periods (Adam to Christ, Christ's work, Christ to End)
**Approach:** Grand narrative framework, not granular typology

#### Typological Content

**Strengths:**
- Emphasizes progressive revelation structure
- Shows Christ as center of all history
- Demonstrates "longitudinal themes" development
- Excellent on covenant progression

**Weaknesses for TT Discovery:**
- **No specific type-by-type analysis**
- Focuses on macro-narrative, not micro-types
- Assumes reader knows individual types
- Examples are illustrative, not exhaustive

#### Specific Mentions

Edwards references types **in passing** to illustrate redemptive history:
- **Creation Week** as pattern for redemption work (6,000 years)
- **Noah's Ark** as salvation through judgment
- **Passover/Exodus** as redemption pattern
- **Tabernacle** as God's dwelling progression
- **David's Kingdom** as type of Christ's kingdom
- **Prophetic Ministry** (Elijah/Elisha) as signs of kingdom

**Assessment:** All these already have trajectory tables. Edwards provides **0 new candidates**.

---

### 2. Westminster Seminary - Seeing Christ in All of Scripture

**Character:** Hermeneutical essays on Christ-centered interpretation
**Structure:** Principles for OT/NT interpretation, seminary methodology
**Approach:** Defends Christocentric reading against historical-critical method

#### Typological Content

**Strengths:**
- Articulates **theological basis** for typology
- Defends "types and shadows" (p.51, 03 - OT Hermeneutics)
- Emphasizes progressive clarity from shadow to substance
- Shows OT saints understood gospel "in types and shadows" (Gal 3:8 discussion)

**Weaknesses for TT Discovery:**
- **Purely methodological** - teaches principles, not examples
- No catalog of specific types
- Examples are generic (temple, sacrifice, covenant)
- Assumes reader knows Westminster Standards typology

#### Specific Mentions

Types mentioned **as hermeneutical examples only:**
- Types and shadows (general concept)
- Fulfillment of OT in Christ (eschatological)
- Gospel preached to Abraham (Gal 3:8)

**Assessment:** Provides **0 new candidates**. Valuable for methodology, not content.

### 3. Timothy Keller - Preaching Christ in a Postmodern World

**Character:** Practical preaching course on Christ-centered exposition
**Structure:** How to preach Christ from every text, avoid moralism
**Approach:** Redemptive-Historical Method applied to sermon preparation

#### Typological Content

**Strengths:**
- Most **practical** of the three books
- Includes **sermon examples** showing typological reading
- Demonstrates "moralistic vs. Christocentric" readings
- Best for **how types function pastorally**

**Weaknesses for TT Discovery:**
- Examples are **illustrative**, not comprehensive
- Same types repeated across chapters
- Focus on method, not catalog

#### Specific Examples Used

Keller provides **worked examples** of Christocentric readings:

1. **David and Goliath** (Chapter 4, p.194-206)
   - **Moralistic:** "Be brave like David against your giants"
   - **Christocentric:** David as substitute-champion whose victory is imputed to Israel
   - **Already covered:** David Trajectory Table exists

2. **Abraham and Isaac** (Chapter 4, p.259)
   - **Moralistic:** "Sacrifice everything for God"
   - **Christocentric:** God provides the substitute (ram/Christ)
   - **Already covered:** Abraham and Isaac tables exist

3. **Jacob Wrestling Angel** (Chapter 4, p.262)
   - **Moralistic:** "Wrestle with God, never give up"
   - **Christocentric:** Blessing through weakness/wounding
   - **Already covered:** Jacob Trajectory Table exists

4. **Elijah on Mt. Carmel** (Chapter 1, p.186-203)
   - Elijah doesn't "cut himself" like Baal prophets because God provides
   - Points to Christ who was "cut to pieces for us"
   - **Already covered:** Elijah Trajectory Table exists

5. **Naaman in Jordan** (Chapter 1, p.331)
   - Salvation through weak/marginal (servant girl, weak country)
   - Submission to revealed salvation (must wash in Jordan)
   - **Possible new candidate:** See below

6. **Bronze Serpent** (Chapter 1, p.162)
   - Jesus explicitly identifies himself (John 3)
   - "It only takes a look" - faith, not works
   - **Already covered:** Bronze Serpent in existing tables

7. **Good Samaritan** (mentioned negatively as allegory example, p.340)
   - Keller uses this to show what NOT to do (allegorizing)
   - **Not a type**

**Assessment:** Keller provides **1 potential new candidate** (Naaman).

## Processing Notes

### 1. Why So Few Candidates?

**These books serve a different purpose than Mather/Fairbairn/Gill:**

| Puritan/Victorian | Modern/Contemporary |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| Catalog types | Teach method |
| Exhaustive lists | Illustrative examples |
| What to preach | How to preach |
| Content-focused | Hermeneutic-focused |

**Analogy:**
- Mather = Encyclopedia of types
- Edwards/Westminster/Keller = How to use the encyclopedia

### 2. Value of These Books

**Critical value despite few new candidates:**

1. **Edwards** - Shows **dispensational structure** for trajectory tables
   - How to organize by redemptive epochs
   - Progressive revelation framework
   - "Already-but-not-yet" kingdom understanding

2. **Westminster** - Provides **theological foundation**
   - Defends typology against historical-critical objections
   - "Types and shadows" language (biblical warrant)
   - Divine authorship vs. human authorship balance

3. **Keller** - Demonstrates **pastoral application**
   - How to avoid moralism when preaching types
   - Expulsive power of new affection (Chalmers)
   - "True virtue" vs. "common virtue" (Edwards)
   - Gospel-centered application from types

### 3. Integration with Existing Work

**How to use these books:**

1. **Creating New Trajectory Tables**

   - Use Keller for **pastoral tone**

## Appendix: Hermeneutical Insights (For Reference)

### Edwards' Three Periods of Redemption

1. **Period One:** Fall to Incarnation
   - Preparation for Christ's coming
   - Progressive revelation through types

2. **Period Two:** Christ's Humiliation
   - Purchase of redemption
   - Types fulfilled

3. **Period Three:** Resurrection to End
   - Application of redemption
   - Types understood retrospectively

### Westminster's Four Principles (from 03 - OT Hermeneutics)

1. Christ is center of all Scripture
2. OT had message for original hearers (not just us)
3. OT writers sometimes didn't fully understand what they described
4. Gospel was preached in OT "in types and shadows"

### Keller's Difference Between Type and Allegory

**Type:**
- Based on author's symbolic intent
- Focuses on macro-patterns (prophet, priest, king)
- Connects to biblical-theological themes
- Example: Isaac as firstborn (redemptive significance)

**Allegory:**
- Ignores author's original intent
- Fixes on micro-features (numbers, colors)
- Makes arbitrary connections
- Example: Two coins = two sacraments (eisegesis)
