## The Analytical Method

### Step 1: Identify the Theological Concept/Theme

**Example**: "Last Days" (Hebrew: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים *bĕʾaḥărît hayyāmîm* / Greek: ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν *ep' eschatōn tōn hēmerōn*)

This concept appears across Scripture in these key passages:
- Genesis 49:1 - Jacob's prophecy about his sons
- Numbers 24:14 - Balaam's oracle
- Deuteronomy 4:30; 31:29 - Israel's restoration after exile
- Isaiah 2:2 - Nations streaming to Zion
- Isaiah 39:6 - Babylonian captivity
- Jeremiah 23:20 - Understanding God's anger
- Daniel 2:28, 29; 10:14 - Kingdom visions
- Micah 4:1 - Mountain of the Lord's house
- Acts 2:17 - Spirit poured out
- 1 Timothy 4:1 - End-time apostasy
- 2 Timothy 3:1 - Difficult times
- Hebrews 1:2 - God speaking through His Son

### Step 2: Extract Common Lexical Elements

**Goal**: Identify the Hebrew/Greek words that create thematic unity across these verses.

#### Hebrew Terminology (OT Passages)

**Primary phrase**: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (*bĕʾaḥărît hayyāmîm*)
- **Root**: אַחֲרִית (*ʾaḥărît*) = "end, latter part, future"
- **Component**: יָמִים (*yāmîm*) = "days"
- **Literal**: "in the end of the days"

**Occurrences**:
- Genesis 49:1 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים
- Numbers 24:14 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)
- Deuteronomy 4:30 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)
- Deuteronomy 31:29 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)
- Isaiah 2:2 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)
- Jeremiah 23:20 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)
- Micah 4:1 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical)

**Variations**:
- Daniel 2:28 - בְּאַחֲרִית יוֹמַיָּא (*bĕʾaḥărît yômayāʾ*) - Aramaic form
- Daniel 10:14 - בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical to Hebrew)

#### Greek Terminology (LXX & NT)

**Primary LXX translation**: ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν (*ep' eschatōn tōn hēmerōn*)
- **Root**: ἔσχατος (*eschatos*) = "last, final, end"
- **Component**: ἡμέρα (*hēmera*) = "day"
- **Literal**: "in/at the last of the days"

**LXX Renderings**:
- Genesis 49:1 LXX - ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν
- Numbers 24:14 LXX - ἐπ' ἐσχάτου τῶν ἡμερῶν
- Deuteronomy 4:30 LXX - ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν
- Isaiah 2:2 LXX - ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν
- Micah 4:1 LXX - ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν

**NT Usage**:
- Acts 2:17 - ἐν ταῖς ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (*en tais eschatais hēmerais*) = "in the last days"
- Hebrews 1:2 - ἐπ' ἐσχάτου τῶν ἡμερῶν τούτων (*ep' eschatou tōn hēmerōn toutōn*) = "at the end of these days"
- 2 Timothy 3:1 - ἐν ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (*en eschatais hēmerais*) = "in last days"
- James 5:3 - ἐν ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (*en eschatais hēmerais*) = "in last days"

**Key observation**: The Hebrew phrase בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים is consistently translated by LXX using ἔσχατος (*eschatos*), establishing lexical continuity from OT → LXX → NT.

### Step 3: Apply the Ninefold Methodology to Each Reference

For EACH verse where "last days" appears, work through all nine steps from `hermeneutics.md`:

#### **Genesis 49:1** - First occurrence

**Step 1 - Identify OT Reference**: Foundation text (no earlier Scripture to reference)

**Step 2 - Analyze Broad NT Context**:
- Quoted in Acts 2:17 (Pentecost sermon)
- Alluded to in Revelation 5:5 ("Lion of Judah")
- Background to Romans 16:25-26 (mystery revealed)

**Step 3 - Analyze OT Context**:
- Jacob blessing his twelve sons before death
- Focus on Judah (vv. 8-10): kingship, lion, scepter
- "Until Shiloh comes" - messianic expectation
- "Obedience of peoples" - universal dominion

**Step 4 - Jewish Backgrounds**:
- Targum Onqelos: "When King Messiah comes"
- 4Q252 (DSS): "Until the Messiah of Righteousness comes"
- Testament of Judah: Messianic interpretation

**Step 5 - Compare Text Forms**:
- MT: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים
- LXX: ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν (exact equivalent)

**Step 6 - Textual Use**: LXX provides standard eschatological terminology adopted by NT

**Step 7 - Hermeneutical Use**: Typological - Jacob's prophecy finds escalated fulfillment in Christ

**Step 8 - Theological Use**: Christology (Messiah from Judah), Eschatology (end-time kingdom)

**Step 9 - Rhetorical Use**: Establishes expectation of future messianic king who brings eschatological age

#### **Numbers 24:14** - Development of Genesis 49

**Step 1 - Identify OT Reference**: Directly references Genesis 49:1 (same wording)

**Step 2 - Analyze Broad NT Context**:
- Revelation 22:16 - "I am the bright morning star" (allusion to Num 24:17)

**Step 3 - Analyze OT Context**:
- Balaam's oracle about Israel's future
- "Star from Jacob, scepter from Israel" (v. 17)
- Defeat of enemies (Moab, Edom)
- Parallel to Genesis 49:9-10

**Step 4 - Jewish Backgrounds**:
- Bar Kokhba revolt (AD 132-135) - "Son of the Star"
- Damascus Document (CD 7:18-20) - "Star is the Interpreter of the Law"

**Step 5 - Compare Text Forms**:
- MT: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (identical to Gen 49:1)
- LXX: ἐπ' ἐσχάτου τῶν ἡμερῶν

**Step 6 - Textual Use**: Balaam intentionally echoes Genesis 49:1 vocabulary

**Step 7 - Hermeneutical Use**: Intertextual development - Balaam interprets Jacob's prophecy

**Step 8 - Theological Use**: Messianic king defeats enemies; universal dominion

**Step 9 - Rhetorical Use**: Confirms Jacob's prophecy through independent Gentile prophet

#### **Acts 2:17** - NT Fulfillment

**Step 1 - Identify OT Reference**: Quotes Joel 2:28-32 (Hebrew: אַחֲרֵי־כֵן / "after this"; LXX: μετὰ ταῦτα / "after these things")

**Step 2 - Analyze Broad NT Context**:
- Pentecost - Holy Spirit poured out
- Peter's sermon explaining the phenomenon
- Inauguration of new covenant age

**Step 3 - Analyze OT Context (Joel 2)**:
- Spirit poured on "all flesh"
- Prophetic gifting universalized
- "Before the great and awesome day of the LORD"

**Step 4 - Jewish Backgrounds**:
- Expectation of eschatological Spirit outpouring
- Linked to new covenant (Jeremiah 31; Ezekiel 36)

**Step 5 - Compare Text Forms**:
- Joel 2:28 MT: אַחֲרֵי־כֵן (*ʾaḥărê-kēn*) = "after this"
- Joel 3:1 LXX: μετὰ ταῦτα (*meta tauta*) = "after these things"
- Acts 2:17: **ἐν ταῖς ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις** (*en tais eschatais hēmerais*) = "in the last days"

**Step 6 - Textual Use**: **CRITICAL** - Peter CHANGES Joel's "after this" to "in the last days"

**Why the change?**
- Peter interprets Joel's "after this" as "in the last days"
- Links Joel to Genesis 49:1, Numbers 24:14 terminology
- **Claims**: The eschatological "latter days" have NOW arrived in Christ

**Step 7 - Hermeneutical Use**: Direct fulfillment - Joel's prophecy inaugurated at Pentecost

**Step 8 - Theological Use**: Inaugurated eschatology - "last days" BEGIN with Christ's first coming

**Step 9 - Rhetorical Use**: Explains Pentecost as eschatological event, not random miracle

### Step 4: Trace LXX Translation Patterns

**Key Finding**: The LXX consistently translates בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים as ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν

**Significance**:
- Establishes standard eschatological vocabulary
- NT writers inherit this LXX terminology
- Creates lexical network spanning OT → LXX → NT

**Variations to note**:
- Sometimes ἐπ' ἐσχάτου (singular) vs. ἐπ' ἐσχάτων (plural)
- NT uses ἐν ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (with preposition ἐν instead of ἐπί)
- All variations use ἔσχατος (*eschatos*) root

### Step 5: Create Thematic Network Linking All Verses

**Thematic Network**: "Last Days"

**Structure**:

1. **Foundation** - Genesis 49:1 (first occurrence)
2. **Development** - Numbers 24:14 (echoes Genesis)
3. **Exile Context** - Deuteronomy 4:30; 31:29 (latter days = restoration after judgment)
4. **Prophetic Expansion** - Isaiah 2:2; Micah 4:1 (temple exaltation, nations streaming)
5. **Apocalyptic Vision** - Daniel 2:28; 10:14 (kingdom visions)
6. **NT Inauguration** - Acts 2:17; Hebrews 1:2 (Christ inaugurates last days)
7. **Church Age** - 1 Timothy 4:1; 2 Timothy 3:1 (ongoing last days realities)

**Lexical Unity**:
- Hebrew: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (7 exact OT occurrences)
- LXX: ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν (consistent translation)
- NT: ἐν ταῖς ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (inherited terminology)

## Application to Thematic Reference Files

### What This Means for Our Work:

When creating thematic reference files, follow this **lexically-focused** analytical approach:

1. **Identify common Hebrew/Greek words** - Show the lexical threads connecting verses
2. **Document LXX translation patterns** - How does LXX render the Hebrew?
3. **Apply ninefold methodology to EACH verse** - Work through all 9 steps systematically
4. **Create thematic networks** - Link all verses sharing the theme
5. **Show NT fulfillment lexically** - How NT inherits OT/LXX terminology

### For "Last Days" Network Specifically:

**Entry format for each verse in the network**:

#### Genesis 49:1

**Hebrew**: בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים (*bĕʾaḥărît hayyāmîm*) - "in the end of the days"

**LXX**: ἐπ' ἐσχάτων τῶν ἡμερῶν (*ep' eschatōn tōn hēmerōn*)

**Context**: Jacob's deathbed prophecy; focus on Judah's kingship and universal dominion (vv. 8-10)

**Connections**:
- **TO**: None (foundational text)
- **FROM OT**: Numbers 24:14 (Balaam develops); Deuteronomy 4:30; 31:29 (exile/restoration pattern)
- **FROM NT**: Acts 2:17 (inaugurated); Hebrews 1:2 (Christ brings); Revelation 5:5 (Lion of Judah)

**Ninefold Analysis**:
- **Hermeneutical Use**: Typological foundation
- **Theological Use**: Messianic kingship from Judah
- **Jewish Background**: Targums identify "King Messiah"; 4Q252 "Messiah of Righteousness"

**Christological Connection**: Christ is the Lion of Judah whose first coming inaugurated the "last days" eschatological age.

---

#### Acts 2:17

**Greek**: ἐν ταῖς ἐσχάταις ἡμέραις (*en tais eschatais hēmerais*) - "in the last days"

**OT Source**: Joel 2:28 (Hebrew: אַחֲרֵי־כֵן = "after this")

**LXX Joel 3:1**: μετὰ ταῦτα (*meta tauta*) = "after these things"

**Peter's Change**: Replaces Joel's "after this" with "in the last days" - **interpretive rendering**

**Context**: Pentecost sermon; Spirit poured out on all flesh

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 49:1 (foundation); Joel 2:28-32 (quoted)
- **Thematic Network**: Links to all OT "latter days" texts by using standardized eschatological terminology

**Ninefold Analysis**:
- **Text Form**: Peter modifies Joel's text to emphasize eschatological fulfillment
- **Hermeneutical Use**: Direct fulfillment (Joel's prophecy) + typological (Genesis 49 inaugurated)
- **Theological Use**: Inaugurated eschatology - "last days" have BEGUN
- **Rhetorical Use**: Explains Pentecost as eschatological event, not anomaly

**Christological Connection**: Christ's ascension and Spirit's descent mark the arrival of the "last days" prophesied throughout the OT.
