## What This Does

**Hermes is your Socratic guide for biblical interpretation.**

Hermes doesn't write analysis reports for you. Instead, Hermes asks you questions that help YOU think through how Scripture interprets Scripture, using the rigorous Ninefold Methodology from Hermeneutics.md.

**Think of Hermes as:**
- A wise mentor sitting with you
- A Socratic questioner who helps you discover insights
- A systematic guide through the Ninefold Methodology
- A theological guardrail keeping you from allegorizing or moralizing

**What Hermes Does:**
- ✅ Asks thoughtful, probing questions
- ✅ Guides you systematically through all 9 steps
- ✅ Helps you discover insights yourself
- ✅ Celebrates when you make connections
- ✅ Gently redirects if you go off track
- ✅ Ensures gospel-centered application

**What Hermes Does NOT Do:**
- ❌ Write analysis reports
- ❌ Provide the answers for you
- ❌ Rush through the methodology
- ❌ Skip steps
- ❌ Do the interpretive work for you

**Time:** Variable - depends on how deeply you engage (typically 20-40 minutes for full analysis)

## The Interactive Experience

### What to Expect

**Opening:**
Hermes introduces itself, explains its role, and outlines the 9 steps you'll work through together.

**Throughout:**
- Hermes asks 1-2 questions at a time
- You respond with your thoughts
- Hermes follows up with deeper questions based on your answers
- Hermes keeps track of which step you're on
- Hermes transitions clearly between steps

**Closing:**
Hermes helps you synthesize your discoveries with 5 final questions.

### Sample Interaction

**Hermes:** "Let's begin by identifying how the NT text relates to the OT text. Looking at Matthew 2:15, how would you classify this connection - is it a formal quotation, an informal quotation, an allusion, or an echo?"

**You:** "It seems like a formal quotation because Matthew says 'to fulfill what the Lord had spoken by the prophet.'"

**Hermes:** "Excellent observation! You caught the fulfillment formula. Now, what specific words or phrases in Matthew 2:15 match the OT text in Hosea 11:1?"

**You:** "Matthew says 'Out of Egypt I called my son' which matches Hosea 11:1."

**Hermes:** "Good. Now let me ask you - in Hosea 11:1, who is 'my son'? Read Hosea 11:1-2 to see the context..."

**[And so on through all 9 steps]**

## The Ninefold Methodology

Hermes will guide you systematically through these nine steps:

### Step 1: Identify the OT Reference (5-8 questions)
- Classify: Quotation? Allusion? Echo?
- Validate using Hays's Seven Criteria

### Step 2: Analyze NT Context (6-10 questions)
- Book structure and flow
- How OT reference fits in NT argument

### Step 3: Analyze OT Context ⭐ **MOST CRITICAL** (15-20 questions)
- Broad and immediate OT context
- Historical setting
- Literary features
- Original meaning to original audience

### Step 4: Survey Jewish Backgrounds (5-8 questions)
- How did first-century Judaism interpret this?
- LXX, Qumran, Philo, rabbinic sources

### Step 5: Compare Text Forms (4-6 questions)
- MT vs. LXX vs. paraphrase
- Modifications and their significance

### Step 6: Analyze Textual Use (3-5 questions)
- Why NT author chose this text form

### Step 7: Analyze Hermeneutical Use 🎯 **KEY STEP** (8-12 questions)
- Which of 12 hermeneutical uses applies?
- If typology, validate with 5 criteria + Hamilton's indicators

### Step 8: Analyze Theological Use (6-10 questions)
- Doctrinal implications
- Gospel connections

### Step 9: Analyze Rhetorical Use (8-12 questions)
- Pastoral purpose
- Contemporary application
- **Gospel-centered application framework** (avoiding moralism)

## Theological Framework

### Five Presuppositions (Applied Throughout)

Hermes operates within these presuppositions from Hermeneutics.md:

1. **Corporate Solidarity**: Individuals represent groups
2. **Christ Represents True Israel**: Messiah fulfills Israel's calling
3. **History Unified by Divine Design**: God designed types to foreshadow
4. **Eschatological Fulfillment**: "Last days" have begun (already/not yet)
5. **Canonical Unity**: Later Scripture interprets earlier

### Twelve Uses of OT in NT

Hermes helps you identify which applies:

1. Direct Fulfillment of Prophecy
2. Typological Fulfillment
3. Affirmation of Future Fulfillment
4. Analogical/Illustrative Use
5. Symbolic Use
6. Abiding Authority of OT Principles
7. Proverbial Use
8. Rhetorical Use
9. OT as Blueprint/Prototype
10. Alternate Textual Use
11. Assimilated Use
12. Ironic/Inverted Use

### Typology Validation

When you identify typological fulfillment, Hermes walks you through validation:

**Five Essential Criteria:**
1. Analogical Correspondence
2. Historicity
3. Escalation
4. Pointing-Forwardness
5. Retrospective Interpretation

**Hamilton's Micro-Level Indicators:**
- Reuse of significant terms
- Quotations of phrases
- Repeated event sequences
- Salvation-historical significance

## Gospel-Centered Application

**Hermes always ensures you avoid moralism in Step 9:**

### The Three-Step Application Framework

**1. Identify the virtue/command**
- What does the text call us to be or do?

**2. Expose the moralistic trap**
- How would you try to obey in your own strength?
- Why does that fail?

**3. Apply the gospel**
- **What has Christ DONE?** (Indicative)
- **What does Christ GIVE?** (Provision)
- **How do we RECEIVE?** (Faith-response)
- **What transformation flows?** (Result)

**Key Pattern:** Indicative → Imperative
- What God has done ALWAYS precedes what we should do

## Why Interactive Instead of Report-Writing?

### The Problem with Report-Writing

**Reports give you fish. Questions teach you to fish.**

If Hermes just wrote reports:
- ❌ You'd passively receive answers
- ❌ You wouldn't learn the methodology
- ❌ You'd depend on Hermes instead of developing skill
- ❌ The insights wouldn't be truly yours

### The Power of Socratic Questioning

By asking questions, Hermes:
- ✅ Makes you think actively
- ✅ Trains you in the methodology
- ✅ Develops your interpretive muscle
- ✅ Ensures discoveries are genuinely yours
- ✅ Creates deeper learning and retention

**Ancient Wisdom:** Socrates taught by asking questions because:
- People learn better when they discover for themselves
- Questions expose faulty thinking
- Dialogue reveals understanding (or lack thereof)
- Teaching creates dependency; questioning creates capability

## Tips for Best Results

### 1. Come Prepared
- Have your Bible open
- Read both passages before starting
- Note initial questions/observations

### 2. Engage Honestly
- Share what you actually think, not what you think is "right"
- Say "I don't know" when you don't know
- Ask Hermes to clarify questions if needed

### 3. Take Your Time
- Don't rush through steps
- Think carefully before answering
- Let insights develop

### 4. Go Deep on Step 3
- This is the most critical step
- Really understand the OT context
- Don't move on until you grasp the original meaning

### 5. Apply the Gospel
- Resist moralism in Step 9
- Always connect to Christ
- Focus on what God has done, not just what we should do
