**Sermon #308**
**Delivered in 1860**
**By The Rev. C. H. Spurgeon**
**At New Park Street Chapel, Southwark**

---

##### Text
*<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"While a large crowd was gathering and people were coming to Jesus from town after town, He told them this parable: 'A farmer went out to sow his seed. And as he was sowing, some seed fell along the path, where it was trampled, and the birds of the air devoured it. Some fell on rocky ground, and when it came up, the seedlings withered because they had no moisture. Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up with it and choked the seedlings. Still other seed fell on good soil, where it sprang up and produced a crop—a hundredfold.' As Jesus said this, He called out, 'He who has ears to hear, let him hear.'"</span>—[[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 8#Luke 8 . 4|Luke 8:4-8]].*

---

##### Summary

In this parable, Christ reveals Himself as the divine Sower who scatters the seed of God's Word across the hearts of men. Just as the seed's fruitfulness depends entirely upon the soil that receives it, so the Word's power to save is manifest only in hearts prepared by the Spirit. The Sower is none other than Christ Himself, who came from heaven's granary to sow the imperishable seed of the gospel—a seed that, when it falls on good ground, produces the hundredfold harvest of eternal life. Spurgeon calls hearers to examine what manner of soil they are, urging them to receive the Word not with hardened, shallow, or thorny hearts, but with the honest and good heart that alone brings forth fruit unto perfection.
