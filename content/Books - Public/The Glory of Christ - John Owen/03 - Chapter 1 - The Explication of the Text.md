<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am; that they may behold my glory."</span> — **John 17:24**

**Christian Reader,**

The subject of this discourse is the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ, as revealed in the Scripture and proposed as the principal object of our faith, love, delight, and admiration. Yet after our utmost and most diligent inquiries, we must say with Job, "How little a portion is it of him that we can understand!" His glory is incomprehensible, and his praises are unutterable.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Imperfection of Our Present Vision</span>

That real view which we may have of Christ and his glory in this world by faith, however weak and obscure that knowledge which we may attain of them by divine revelation, is inexpressibly to be preferred above all other wisdom, understanding, or knowledge whatever.

Our sight of Christ's glory here is like seeing through a glass darkly, subject to many limitations and interruptions. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"We see now through a glass, darkly; but then face to face."</span> By the aid of such glasses, men will discover stars or heavenly lights which, by reason of their distance from us, the eye of itself is no way able to discern. Such a glass is the gospel, without which we can make no discovery of Christ at all; but in the use of it we are far enough from beholding him in the just dimensions of his glory.

The apostle adds another intimation of this imperfection, in an allusion unto the way whereby things are proposed and conveyed unto the minds and apprehensions of men. This is by words, which are either plain, proper, and direct, or dark, figurative, and parabolical. This latter way makes the conception of things to be difficult and imperfect; and by reason of the imperfection of our view of the glory of Christ by faith in this world, the apostle says it is "ainigmati", in "a riddle."

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Nature of Gospel Revelation</span>

But here it must be observed, that the description and representation of the Lord Christ and his glory in the gospel is not absolutely or in itself either dark or obscure; yea, it is perspicuous, plain, and direct. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">Christ is therein evidently set forth crucified, exalted, glorified.</span> But the apostle does not here discourse concerning the way or means of the revelation of it unto us, but of the means or instrument whereby we comprehend that revelation. This is our faith, which, as it is in us, being weak and imperfect, we comprehend the representation that is made unto us of the glory of Christ as men do the sense of a dark saying, a riddle, a parable; that is, imperfectly, and with difficulty.

On the account hereof we may say at present, how little a portion is it that we know of him! as Job speaks of God. How imperfect are our conceptions of him! How weak are our minds in their management! There is no part of his glory that we can fully comprehend. And what we do comprehend, there is a comprehension in faith, we cannot abide in the steady contemplation of.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Blessing of Present Revelation</span>

For ever blessed be that sovereign grace, whence it is that He who <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"commanded light to shine out of darkness has shined into our hearts, to give us the light of the knowledge of his own glory in the face of Jesus Christ,"</span> and therein of the glory of Christ himself; that he has so revealed him unto us, as that we may love him, admire him, and obey him: but constantly, steadily, and clearly to behold his glory in this life we are not able; <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"for we walk by faith, and not by sight."</span>

Hence our sight of him here is as it were by glances, liable to be clouded by many interpositions. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Behold, he standeth behind the wall, he looketh forth at the windows, showing himself through the lattice,"</span> Song of Solomon 2:9. There is a great interposition between him and us, as a wall; and the means of the discovery of himself unto us, as through a window and lattice, include a great instability and imperfection in our view and apprehension of him.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Promise of Perfect Vision</span>

But in heaven we shall clearly and constantly behold his glory, without anything to cloud our sight of him or divert us from the contemplation of him. We shall be freed from the obscurity of this world, from our own weakness and distempers, that here so much hinder us in the direct view of his glory. We shall, by a direct vision of his person and glory, be blessed in the eternal fruition of him. This is that we look for, the blessed hope and appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Meditation on Glory as Present Duty</span>

In the meantime it is our duty, and is the proper work of faith, to get the best view or apprehensions that we can of his glory. This is that which will purify our hearts, transform our souls into his likeness, encourage us in all our approaches unto God by him, give us peace and boldness in his presence, raise us up above the troubles and terrors of this life, and sweeten our passage into eternity.

This is the work of grace in us here in this world, first to enable us to behold the glory of Christ by faith, and then to transform us into the same image and likeness. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"We all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord."</span> This is the highest honor we can attain unto in this world, and is the very life and being of our faith.

The great design of the apostle in the verse that contains the text was to encourage the disciples to peace and courage under all that opposition, reproach, and persecution which they were to meet withal in this world. For this end he declares unto them what he had designed for them in a future state: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"In the world ye shall have tribulation; but be of good cheer: I have overcome the world,"</span> John 16:33. And both these he farther confirms in our text.

This verse may be considered as containing either a prayer, or the assertion of a privilege. As a prayer, it includes a vehement desire or longing for the accomplishment of that which is requested. Our Lord Jesus, being now in the state of his deepest humiliation, foreseeing and knowing what he should undergo immediately in his sufferings and death, expresses with what earnest longing he expected the glory that was set before him, when his disciples should be received into the same glory that he was to be received into. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For the joy that was set before him he endured the cross, despising the shame."</span> And this joy was principally in the salvation and glory of his elect.

Or it may be taken as the assertion of a privilege which he claimed for his disciples: namely, that in his glorified state he would take them to be with him, and would give them to behold his glory. And so it is a revelation of that grace and love which he had towards them; as also a promise unto them of what he would do for them, to encourage them against the troubles and persecutions they were to meet with in the world.
