## <span style="color: #008080;">The Voluntary Undertaking</span>

The third thing wherein we may behold the glory of Christ is in **his susception of the office of a mediator**, and first, in that infinite condescension whereby he undertook and entered upon this office.

That the Lord Christ did not assume this office unto himself, but was called and appointed unto it by his Father, is expressly declared in the Scripture. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"And no man taketh this honor unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron. So also Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, today have I begotten thee,"</span> Hebrews 5:4-5.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Nature of This Condescension</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">I. The Eternal Covenant</span>

This susception or undertaking of the mediatorial office was founded in the eternal covenant between the Father and the Son. Before the world was made, before sin entered, there was a covenant transaction between the persons of the Trinity concerning the salvation of the elect.

In this covenant:
- The **Father** appointed the Son to be the mediator and redeemer
- The **Son** voluntarily undertook the work assigned to him
- The **Holy Spirit** engaged to apply the redemption purchased by the Son

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek,"</span> Psalm 110:4.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Surety of the Covenant</span>

In this eternal transaction, the Lord Jesus became **the surety of a better testament**, Hebrews 7:22. He engaged to fulfill all the conditions required for the salvation of his people, and to make satisfaction for all their sins.

What infinite condescension was this! The eternal Son of God, equal with the Father, undertook to become responsible for sinful, rebellious creatures, and to answer for all their debts and obligations.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Manifestation of This Condescension</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">II. In His Incarnation</span>

The first great evidence of this condescension appeared in his incarnation. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich,"</span> 2 Corinthians 8:9.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Depths of His Descent</span>

Consider from what height he descended, and to what depth! From the highest throne in heaven to the lowest condition on earth:

- From **infinite glory** to the form of a servant
- From **being worshipped by angels** to being despised by men
- From **dwelling in unapproachable light** to being wrapped in swaddling clothes
- From **the bosom of the Father** to a manger among the beasts

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"He made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men,"</span> Philippians 2:7.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Voluntary Nature</span>

This was not forced upon him, but was his own voluntary act of love and grace. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God,"</span> Hebrews 10:7.

No creature could have compelled the Son of God to take our nature, or to undertake our redemption. It was pure, free, sovereign grace that moved him to this condescension.

### <span style="color: #008080;">III. In His Subjection to the Law</span>

Having taken our nature, he subjected himself to the law under which we were bound. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,"</span> Galatians 4:4.

The law could have no claim upon him as the eternal Son of God. He was the lawgiver, not subject to law. Yet for our sakes he put himself under the obligation of the law, that he might:

- **Fulfill all righteousness** in our stead
- **Bear the curse** due to our transgressions
- **Redeem us** from under the law

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Perfection of His Obedience</span>

His obedience to the law was perfect in every respect:

- **Complete** in that he fulfilled every commandment
- **Voluntary** in that he was not bound to obey as we are
- **Active** in that he did all that the law required
- **Passive** in that he suffered all that our disobedience deserved

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him,"</span> 2 Corinthians 5:21.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Glory of This Condescension</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">IV. The Display of Divine Attributes</span>

In this condescension of Christ, we see a glorious display of the divine attributes:

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Infinite Love</span>

What love is here! That God should so love the world as to give his only begotten Son, and that the Son should so love us as to give himself for us.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends. Ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you,"</span> John 15:13-14.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. Amazing Grace</span>

Grace reigns triumphant in this condescension. It was not justice that moved God to send his Son, for justice demanded our punishment. It was not our merit, for we had none. It was grace—free, sovereign, amazing grace.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. Infinite Wisdom</span>

The wisdom of God shines forth in devising this way of salvation. How to be just and yet justify the ungodly, how to uphold the honor of the law and yet save lawbreakers—this wisdom is seen in the condescension of Christ.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God,"</span> Ephesians 3:10.

### <span style="color: #008080;">V. The Pattern for Us</span>

This condescension of Christ is set before us as a pattern for our own lives:

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Humility</span>

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation,"</span> Philippians 2:5-7.

If he who was so high could stoop so low, how much more should we, who are but dust and ashes, humble ourselves before God and man!

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. Love and Service</span>

As Christ came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, so should we be ready to serve others, even those who are beneath us in station or ability.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"By love serve one another,"</span> Galatians 5:13.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. Self-Denial</span>

Christ pleased not himself, but gave himself for others. This spirit of self-denial should characterize all his followers.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Encouragement to Sinners</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">VI. What This Condescension Teaches Us</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Christ's Willingness to Save</span>

Such condescension speaks loudly of Christ's willingness to save sinners. If he was willing to stoop so low to undertake our salvation, surely he is willing to receive all who come unto him.

Would he have taken our nature if he were not willing to save our souls? Would he have subjected himself to the law if he were not ready to justify lawbreakers?

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. The Completeness of His Work</span>

This condescension assures us that his work is complete. He who undertook so much will not leave his work unfinished.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ,"</span> Philippians 1:6.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. The Certainty of Our Acceptance</span>

Since Christ has condescended to take our nature and fulfill all righteousness in our stead, we may be certain that all who believe in him are accepted with God.

There is no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.

## <span style="color: #008080;">Practical Application</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Unbelievers</span>

Let this condescension of Christ melt your hearts and draw you to him. If love like this cannot win you, what will? If condescension like this cannot humble you, what can?

Despise not this great salvation. He who humbled himself for sinners will exalt all who humble themselves before him.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Believers</span>

Let this condescension fill you with:

- **Wonder** at such amazing love
- **Gratitude** for such undeserved mercy
- **Humility** following such an example
- **Confidence** in such a Savior
- **Zeal** to serve such a Master

Crown him Lord of all! Let every thought be brought into captivity to the obedience of Christ.

---

*What tongue can express, what heart can conceive, the glory of this condescension! The eternal Son of God, before whom angels veil their faces, stooped to take our nature and bear our sins. Here is love beyond comprehension, grace beyond expression, glory beyond imagination.*
