
## <span style="color: #008080;">The Foundation of All Glory</span>

The second thing wherein we may behold the glory of Christ, is the mysterious constitution of his person, as he is God and man in one person, by the hypostatical union of the divine and human natures in the person of the Son of God.

This is the greatest mystery in religion, yea, the foundation and corner-stone of the whole fabric of Christianity. Take this away, and the whole building falls to the ground. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh,"</span> 1 Timothy 3:16.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Nature of This Mystery</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">I. The Union of Two Distinct Natures</span>

In the person of Christ there is a union of natures absolutely distinct, the divine and human nature; and these are united in one person, that of the eternal Son of God. Herein lies the mystery—not that two natures may be in one person, for that is natural to us all; but that natures so infinitely distinct should be united in one person, that the same individual person should be both finite and infinite, temporal and eternal, mortal and immortal.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The Word was made flesh,"</span> John 1:14. The Word, which is the eternal Son of God, took our nature into personal union with his own divine person. This is the great foundation of all our hope and salvation.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Divine Nature</span>

In his divine nature, Christ is <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"the brightness of the Father's glory, and the express image of his person,"</span> Hebrews 1:3. He is equal with the Father, being <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"in the form of God,"</span> and <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"thought it not robbery to be equal with God,"</span> Philippians 2:6. All the fullness of the Godhead dwells in him bodily, Colossians 2:9.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Human Nature</span>

In his human nature, he is <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"made of a woman, made under the law,"</span> Galatians 4:4. He took upon him our nature, sin only excepted. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same,"</span> Hebrews 2:14. He was made in all things like unto his brethren, sin only excepted.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Glory of This Union</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">II. The Manner of This Union</span>

This union of the two natures in the person of Christ is hypostatical—that is, it is in his person, not in his nature. The divine and human natures are not confounded or mixed, but remain distinct; yet they are so united in one person that whatever belongs to either nature may be attributed to the person.

Hence Christ may be said both to be omnipotent and to have suffered, to be eternal and to have died, to be infinite and to have grown in wisdom and stature. These things are spoken of the same person under different considerations of his natures.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Communication of Properties</span>

From this personal union arises what divines call the communication of properties. The properties of both natures are attributed to the one person. Thus we read:

- <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"They crucified the Lord of glory,"</span> 1 Corinthians 2:8
- <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"God purchased the church with his own blood,"</span> Acts 20:28
- <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"No man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven,"</span> John 3:13

These expressions would be contradictory unless we understand them as spoken of the one person of Christ in whom both natures subsist.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Glory Manifested in This Constitution</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">III. The Wisdom of God Displayed</span>

Herein is the manifold wisdom of God gloriously displayed. Consider:

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. The Purpose of This Union</span>

This union was necessary for our redemption. It was needful:

- That he should be God, that his obedience and sufferings might be of infinite value and merit
- That he should be man, that he might obey the law in our nature and suffer the punishment due to our sins
- That he should be both in one person, that there might be a communication between what he did and suffered in either nature

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus,"</span> 1 Timothy 2:5.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. The Condescension Involved</span>

What infinite condescension is here! That the eternal Son of God should take our nature into union with his divine person! This is the great mystery of love. He who is the heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds, took upon him the form of a servant.

The distance between the divine and human nature is infinite. Yet behold, they are united in one person! The Creator takes the creature into personal union with himself.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Effects of This Glorious Union</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">IV. What This Union Accomplishes</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. The Dignity of Human Nature</span>

By this union, human nature is advanced to the highest dignity possible for a creature. It is exalted above all principalities and powers, above every name that is named, not only in this world but also in that which is to come.

Our nature, which by sin was debased lower than the beasts of the field, is in Christ exalted above the highest angels. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son?"</span> But to our nature in Christ he says, <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Sit on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool."</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. The Foundation of All Mediation</span>

This constitution of Christ's person is the foundation of all his mediatorial work. Being God, he could satisfy divine justice; being man, he could suffer in our stead; being both in one person, he could be a mediator between God and man.

All his offices of Prophet, Priest, and King depend upon this union of natures in his person. Take away this personal union, and you overthrow his whole mediatorial work.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. The Ground of Our Access to God</span>

It is by virtue of this union that we have access to God through Christ. The human nature is the veil, but the divine nature is our way into the holiest of all. <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh."</span>

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Practical Importance</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">V. What This Means for Believers</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. For Our Faith</span>

This mystery is the proper object of our faith. We are to believe in Christ as he is—God and man in one person. Faith that does not embrace both natures united in his person is not saving faith.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"This is his commandment, That we should believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ,"</span> 1 John 3:23. To believe on his name is to believe on him as he has revealed himself to be.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. For Our Worship</span>

We are to worship Christ as God-man. The worship due to his divine nature, and the honor due to his human nature as exalted above all creatures, are both to be given to his one person.

Let all the angels of God worship him. And every knee shall bow, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. For Our Hope</span>

Our hope of glory rests on this union. Because our nature is united to the divine nature in the person of Christ, we have hope of eternal life and glory. He has taken our nature to heaven, and will bring us thither also.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Incomprehensible Glory</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">VI. The Limits of Our Understanding</span>

While we are to believe this mystery, we cannot comprehend it. The manner of this union is beyond our understanding. We know that it is, but we cannot understand how it is.

Let us not attempt to be wise above what is written. It is sufficient that we believe what God has revealed, and adore what we cannot comprehend.

The ancient church confessed: "We acknowledge one and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, at once complete in Godhead and complete in manhood, truly God and truly man, of one substance with the Father as regards his Godhead, and at the same time of one substance with us as regards his manhood."

## <span style="color: #008080;">Practical Application</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Those Who Doubt</span>

If any are ready to say, "How can these things be?" let them remember that with God all things are possible. The same power that made the world of nothing can unite the divine and human natures in one person.

Reason must bow before revelation. What God has declared, we are bound to believe, though we cannot understand.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Believers</span>

Let believers rejoice in this glorious mystery. Here is the foundation of all your hope and comfort. Your Redeemer is both God and man. As God, he is able to save to the uttermost; as man, he has fellow-feeling with your infirmities.

What manner of persons ought you to be, who have such a Savior! How should you love him, serve him, and live to his glory!

---

*In this mysterious union of the divine and human natures in the one person of Christ, we behold such glory as angels desire to look into. Here is the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom which God ordained before the world unto our glory.*
