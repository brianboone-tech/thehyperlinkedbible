## <span style="color: #008080;">The Great Contrast</span>

Having considered the various aspects of Christ's glory that we may behold by faith in this world, we now turn to examine **the differences between our present sight of his glory by faith and the vision we shall have of it in heaven**.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known,"</span> 1 Corinthians 13:12.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Nature of Our Present Vision</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">I. Faith as Our Present Means of Sight</span>

In this life, we behold the glory of Christ through faith, which is our spiritual eye for seeing invisible realities.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Character of Faith's Vision</span>

Faith's sight of Christ's glory is:
- **Spiritual** rather than physical
- **Mediate** through the Word rather than immediate
- **Partial** rather than complete
- **Obscure** rather than clear
- **Interrupted** rather than constant

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"(For we walk by faith, not by sight:)"</span> 2 Corinthians 5:7.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Limitations of Faith</span>

Though faith is a glorious grace, it has inherent limitations in this life:

- It sees through **instruments** (Word, sacraments, prayer)
- It comprehends **gradually** and **partially**
- It is **weakened** by remaining corruption
- It is **hindered** by earthly distractions
- It **fluctuates** in strength and clarity

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord,"</span> 2 Corinthians 3:18.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The First Great Difference: Mediate vs. Immediate</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">II. Our Present Mediate Vision</span>

The first great difference is that our present sight of Christ's glory is **mediate**—it comes through means and instruments—while our future sight will be **immediate** and direct.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Glass of the Gospel</span>

Paul uses the metaphor of seeing "through a glass" or mirror:

- The **Gospel** is our mirror reflecting Christ's glory
- **Scripture** is the glass through which we see him
- **Preaching** holds up this mirror before us
- **Sacraments** represent his glory to our senses
- **Prayer** opens our eyes to behold him

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord,"</span> 2 Corinthians 3:18.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Nature of Mirror Images</span>

Ancient mirrors were made of polished metal and gave:
- **Dim** reflections compared to reality
- **Distorted** images that lacked clarity
- **Partial** views that missed details
- **Dark** appearances in poor light
- **Uncertain** representations of the truth

So our present sight of Christ through the gospel mirror, though real and transforming, is limited and imperfect.

### <span style="color: #008080;">III. The Instruments of Our Present Vision</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. The Written Word</span>

Scripture is our primary means of seeing Christ:
- It **reveals** his person and work
- It **declares** his excellencies
- It **records** his words and deeds
- It **promises** his presence and power
- It **directs** us to his glory

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me,"</span> John 5:39.

Yet Scripture itself acknowledges its mediate nature—it points beyond itself to Christ himself.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. The Preached Word</span>

Through faithful preaching:
- Christ is **evidently set forth**
- His glory is **proclaimed** and **explained**
- Hearts are **opened** to see him
- Faith is **kindled** and **strengthened**
- Souls are **drawn** to behold him

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness; But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God,"</span> 1 Corinthians 1:23-24.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. The Sacraments</span>

In baptism and the Lord's Supper:
- Christ's work is **visibly represented**
- His benefits are **sealed** to believers
- His presence is **spiritually enjoyed**
- His love is **freshly apprehended**
- Faith is **confirmed** and **nourished**

#### <span style="color: #008080;">4. Prayer and Meditation</span>

Through communion with God:
- Christ **manifests** himself to seeking souls
- Hearts are **prepared** to receive divine light
- Spiritual **appetite** is awakened for heavenly things
- The **veil** is taken away from spiritual eyes
- **Sweet fellowship** is enjoyed with the Savior

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"At that day ye shall know that I am in my Father, and ye in me, and I in you,"</span> John 14:20.

### <span style="color: #008080;">IV. The Limitations of Mediate Vision</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Dependency on External Means</span>

Our present sight depends upon:
- **Having** access to Scripture
- **Hearing** faithful preaching
- **Participating** in ordinances
- **Maintaining** spiritual disciplines
- **Living** where the gospel is known

When these means are absent or neglected, our vision grows dim.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. Susceptibility to Interruption</span>

This mediate vision can be:
- **Hindered** by sin and worldliness
- **Clouded** by trials and sorrows
- **Weakened** by spiritual negligence
- **Disturbed** by false teaching
- **Interrupted** by various causes

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine unto them,"</span> 2 Corinthians 4:4.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. Requiring Constant Renewal</span>

Because it is mediate, this vision needs:
- **Daily** renewal through the Word
- **Regular** refreshing through prayer
- **Frequent** participation in ordinances
- **Continual** watchfulness against hindrances
- **Persistent** seeking after clearer sight

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Future Immediate Vision</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">V. The Nature of Heavenly Sight</span>

In heaven, our sight of Christ's glory will be **immediate**—direct, unmediated, and face to face.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">No Longer Through Instruments</span>

In glory we shall:
- See Christ **himself**, not merely representations of him
- Behold his **actual** glory, not just descriptions of it
- Experience his **presence** directly, not through means
- Know him **immediately**, not through learning
- Enjoy him **constantly**, not intermittently

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God,"</span> Revelation 21:3.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">Face to Face Vision</span>

The apostle promises:
- We shall see him **face to face**
- We shall know **even as we are known**
- We shall see him **as he is**
- We shall be **like him** in that sight
- We shall **never lose** this vision

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is,"</span> 1 John 3:2.

### <span style="color: #008080;">VI. The Advantages of Immediate Vision</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Perfect Clarity</span>

No more:
- **Dim** reflections in mirrors
- **Obscure** representations in types
- **Partial** understanding through teaching
- **Confused** apprehensions through weakness
- **Uncertain** glimpses through faith

But perfect, clear, unmistakable sight of Christ as he truly is.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. Complete Knowledge</span>

We shall know:
- **Fully** what now we know only in part
- **Perfectly** what now we understand imperfectly
- **Immediately** what now requires study
- **Certainly** what now we believe by faith
- **Eternally** what now we grasp temporarily

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. Uninterrupted Fellowship</span>

This immediate vision will be:
- **Constant** without any breaks
- **Undisturbed** by earthly cares
- **Unclouded** by remaining sin
- **Unhindered** by physical limitations
- **Unchanging** throughout eternity

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"And so shall we ever be with the Lord,"</span> 1 Thessalonians 4:17.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Practical Implications</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">VII. For Our Present Experience</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. Value the Means of Grace</span>

Since our present sight is mediate, we must:
- **Prize** the Word of God highly
- **Attend** faithfully upon preaching
- **Participate** regularly in sacraments
- **Maintain** constant prayer and meditation
- **Use** all appointed means diligently

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. Be Patient with Limitations</span>

Understanding that our present vision is limited helps us:
- **Bear** with the dimness of our sight
- **Continue** seeking despite difficulties
- **Persevere** through times of darkness
- **Look forward** to perfect vision
- **Not despair** when sight seems weak

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. Long for the Better Vision</span>

This comparison should create:
- **Holy desire** for heaven
- **Earnest longing** for Christ's return
- **Patient waiting** for glory
- **Faithful use** of present means
- **Joyful anticipation** of perfect sight

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal,"</span> 2 Corinthians 4:17-18.

## <span style="color: #008080;">Practical Application</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Those Who Neglect the Means</span>

If you neglect the Word, prayer, and worship, you cannot expect to see Christ's glory clearly. God has appointed these means for our present seeing.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Those Discouraged by Dim Sight</span>

Do not despair if your sight seems weak. Even the strongest faith sees only dimly compared to the vision we shall have in glory.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For All Believers</span>

Use well your present means while longing for perfect sight. What you see now by faith will be sight forever in glory.

---

*How great is the difference between our present mediate sight and our future immediate vision! Now we see through instruments; then we shall see face to face. Now we know in part; then we shall know even as we are known. Let us value our present means while longing for perfect sight, and use well our glass until we see without it in the glory of heaven.*
