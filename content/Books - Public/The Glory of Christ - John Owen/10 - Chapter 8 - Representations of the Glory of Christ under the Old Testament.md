
## <span style="color: #008080;">The Prophetic Preparation</span>

The seventh thing wherein we may behold the glory of Christ is in **the representations that were made of him and his glory under the Old Testament**, before his actual incarnation. These were given to prepare the church for his coming and to sustain faith in the promised Messiah.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins,"</span> Acts 10:43.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Types and Shadows</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">I. The Sacrificial System</span>

The entire Old Testament sacrificial system was designed to represent Christ and his atoning work.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Daily Sacrifices</span>

Every sacrifice pointed to Christ:
- The **morning and evening** sacrifices spoke of his continual intercession
- The **burnt offerings** represented his perfect obedience
- The **sin offerings** portrayed his bearing our guilt
- The **peace offerings** signified the reconciliation he would make
- The **trespass offerings** showed his satisfaction for specific sins

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins,"</span> Hebrews 10:4. These sacrifices could not actually remove sin, but they pointed to him who could.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Great Day of Atonement</span>

The annual Day of Atonement was a comprehensive type of Christ's atoning work:

- The **high priest** represented Christ our great High Priest
- The **holy garments** signified his perfect righteousness
- The **blood** sprinkled on the mercy seat typified his precious blood
- The **scapegoat** bearing sins into the wilderness showed how Christ removes our guilt
- The **entrance into the holiest** prefigured his opening heaven for us

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building; Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us,"</span> Hebrews 9:11-12.

### <span style="color: #008080;">II. The Tabernacle and Temple</span>

The structure and furniture of the tabernacle and temple were rich in typical significance pointing to Christ.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Tabernacle Structure</span>

- The **outer court** represented this world where Christ lived and died
- The **holy place** signified the church militant on earth
- The **holy of holies** typified heaven itself
- The **veil** between them represented Christ's flesh, which when rent opened the way to God

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh,"</span> Hebrews 10:19-20.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Sacred Furniture</span>

Each piece of furniture spoke of Christ:

- The **brazen altar** where sacrifices were offered pointed to the cross
- The **brazen laver** for washing represented his cleansing work
- The **golden candlestick** symbolized Christ as the light of the world
- The **table of showbread** typified Christ as the bread of life
- The **altar of incense** represented his intercession
- The **ark of the covenant** signified God's presence in Christ
- The **mercy seat** showed how God meets sinners in mercy through Christ's blood

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ,"</span> Colossians 2:17.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Personal Types</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">III. Typical Persons</span>

Many Old Testament figures were types of Christ, representing various aspects of his person and work.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">Adam - The First Man</span>

Adam was a type of Christ as:
- The **head** of a race
- The **representative** of his people
- The one through whom came either **life or death**

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"And so it is written, The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit,"</span> 1 Corinthians 15:45.

But Christ far excels Adam:
- Where Adam **failed**, Christ **succeeded**
- Where Adam brought **condemnation**, Christ brings **justification**
- Where Adam brought **death**, Christ brings **life**

#### <span style="color: #008080;">Melchizedek - King and Priest</span>

Melchizedek prefigured Christ in his dual office:
- **King of righteousness** by interpretation of his name
- **King of peace** (Salem means peace)
- **Priest of the most high God** combining royal and priestly functions
- **Without father or mother** in the record, pointing to Christ's eternal generation
- **Having neither beginning nor end** of days, signifying Christ's eternal priesthood

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek,"</span> Psalm 110:4.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">Moses - The Great Prophet</span>

Moses typified Christ as:
- **Prophet** declaring God's will
- **Lawgiver** giving divine commandments
- **Mediator** between God and the people
- **Deliverer** leading from bondage
- **Intercessor** pleading for the people

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken,"</span> Deuteronomy 18:15.

Yet Christ infinitely exceeds Moses in glory and authority.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">David - The King</span>

David represented Christ as:
- **Anointed king** chosen by God
- **Shepherd** of his people
- **Warrior** defeating enemies
- **Sweet psalmist** leading worship
- **Man after God's own heart**

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"I have found David my servant; with my holy oil have I anointed him,"</span> Psalm 89:20. But this ultimately pointed to great David's greater Son.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Prophetic Utterances</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">IV. Direct Prophecies</span>

The prophets spoke directly of Christ's coming and work, often in remarkable detail.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">His Birth and Early Life</span>

The prophets foretold:
- His **virgin birth**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel,"</span> Isaiah 7:14
- His **birthplace**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel,"</span> Micah 5:2
- The **slaughter of innocents**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel weeping for her children,"</span> Jeremiah 31:15

#### <span style="color: #008080;">His Ministry and Character</span>

The prophets described:
- His **anointing with the Spirit**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek,"</span> Isaiah 61:1
- His **gentle nature**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"A bruised reed shall he not break, and the smoking flax shall he not quench,"</span> Isaiah 42:3
- His **rejection**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief,"</span> Isaiah 53:3

#### <span style="color: #008080;">His Suffering and Death</span>

Isaiah 53 gives the most detailed prophetic account of Christ's atoning work:
- He would be **wounded for our transgressions**
- He would be **bruised for our iniquities**
- The **chastisement of our peace** would be upon him
- By his **stripes we would be healed**
- The **Lord would lay on him the iniquity of us all**

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all,"</span> Isaiah 53:6.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">His Resurrection and Exaltation</span>

The prophets also spoke of his triumph:
- His **resurrection**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption,"</span> Psalm 16:10
- His **ascension**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Thou hast ascended on high, thou hast led captivity captive,"</span> Psalm 68:18
- His **session at God's right hand**: <span style="color: #1e90ff;">"The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool,"</span> Psalm 110:1

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Ceremonial Observances</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">V. The Feasts and Holy Days</span>

The appointed feasts were prophetic of Christ's work:

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Passover</span>

This pointed directly to Christ as our Passover Lamb:
- The **lamb without blemish** represented his sinless nature
- The **blood on the doorposts** prefigured his blood sheltering us from judgment
- The **eating of the lamb** signified feeding on Christ by faith
- The **deliverance from Egypt** typified our salvation from sin

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us,"</span> 1 Corinthians 5:7.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Day of Pentecost</span>

This feast pointed to:
- The **firstfruits** of the harvest, representing Christ's resurrection
- The **giving of the Spirit** fifty days after Christ's resurrection
- The **wave offering** showing the acceptance of the offering

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Day of Atonement</span>

This most solemn day represented:
- The **once-for-all sacrifice** of Christ
- The **cleansing from all sin**
- The **access to God's presence**

#### <span style="color: #008080;">The Feast of Tabernacles</span>

This joyful feast pointed to:
- **God dwelling with his people** (Immanuel)
- The **ingathering** of all the elect
- The **final rest** in the heavenly Canaan

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Glory Revealed in These Representations</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">VI. What These Types Accomplished</span>

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. They Sustained Faith</span>

These representations kept alive the hope of the coming Messiah. Believers could look forward to the reality through the shadows.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad,"</span> John 8:56.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. They Revealed the Plan of Salvation</span>

Through types and prophecies, God gradually unfolded his plan of redemption, preparing minds to understand Christ's work when he came.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. They Demonstrated Unity of Scripture</span>

The perfect fulfillment of all these types in Christ proves the divine authorship of Scripture and the unity of God's plan throughout history.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">4. They Magnified Christ's Glory</span>

By showing how all the Old Testament pointed to Christ, these representations demonstrate his supreme importance and glory.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself,"</span> Luke 24:27.

## <span style="color: #008080;">The Superiority of the Reality</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">VII. Christ Exceeds All the Types</span>

While the types were glorious, the reality in Christ far surpasses them all:

#### <span style="color: #008080;">1. In Substance vs. Shadow</span>

The types were shadows; Christ is the substance. Shadows are valuable for pointing to reality, but the reality is infinitely greater.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">2. In Permanence vs. Temporality</span>

The types were temporary institutions; Christ's work is eternal. The sacrifices were repeated daily; his sacrifice was once for all.

#### <span style="color: #008080;">3. In Effectiveness vs. Limitation</span>

The types could not actually save; they only pointed to salvation. Christ actually accomplishes what the types could only represent.

<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect,"</span> Hebrews 10:1.

## <span style="color: #008080;">Practical Application</span>

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Understanding Scripture</span>

Study the Old Testament with Christ in view. Look for him in every type, prophecy, and institution. The key to understanding the Old Testament is Christ.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Strengthening Faith</span>

Let these manifold testimonies to Christ strengthen your faith. If God prepared for his coming through so many ages and in so many ways, surely Christ is the centerpiece of all history and religion.

### <span style="color: #008080;">For Worship and Adoration</span>

Marvel at the wisdom of God in preparing the world for Christ's coming. Worship him who was:
- **Promised** to Adam
- **Typified** in Abel's sacrifice
- **Prefigured** in all the ceremonial law
- **Prophesied** by all the prophets
- **Revealed** in the fullness of time

---

*What abundant testimony the Old Testament bears to Christ! Every sacrifice pointed to his cross, every priest to his intercession, every king to his kingdom, every prophet to his revelation. The whole Old Testament is pregnant with Christ. In him all the promises of God are yea and amen. He is the sum and substance of all divine revelation.*
