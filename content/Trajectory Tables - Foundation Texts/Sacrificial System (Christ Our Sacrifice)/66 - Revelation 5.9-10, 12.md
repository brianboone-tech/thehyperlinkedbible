### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 9|Revelation 5:9-10, 12]]

- [[Lexicon/G4969-4970#G4969|ἐσφάγης]] (*esphagēs*) - "you were slain, slaughtered" (aorist passive indicative, 2nd singular)

- [[Lexicon/G129-130#G129|αἵματί]] (*haimati*) - "by blood" (dative singular)

**Context**: Revelation 5:9-10, 12 presents heaven's worship of the Lamb who was slain. John sees a scroll sealed with seven seals (v. 1); no one in creation is worthy to open it (vv. 2-4). Then appears "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (v. 6). The twenty-four elders fall before the Lamb singing: "Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation, and you have made them a kingdom and priests to our God" (vv. 9-10). Myriads of angels join: "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing!" (v. 12). The sacrificial Lamb becomes the enthroned Lamb, eternally worshiped for His redemptive death.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 9|Revelation 5:9-10]], 12 presents the climactic vision of Christ as the slain Lamb eternally worshiped, consummating the entire biblical sacrificial trajectory. The **Lamb standing as slain** (arnion hestēkos hōs esphagmenon, v. 6) combines death and resurrection in one image. He was slain (esphagmenon, perfect passive participle—completed action with abiding results), yet He stands (hestēkos, perfect active participle—risen and established). This paradox defines the gospel: Christ died for sins and rose for justification ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 25|Romans 4:25]]). The eternal scars (wounds visible forever) testify to the sacrifice's reality—Thomas touched them ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 20#John 20 . 27|John 20:27]]); heaven beholds them forever. The **Lion-Lamb paradox** (v. 5-6) reveals how Messiah conquers. Jewish expectation was militaristic—the Lion of Judah would devour enemies ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 9|Genesis 49:9-10]]). But the Lion is a Lamb—conquering through sacrifice, not slaughter; winning through weakness, not strength. Paul's theology of the cross pervades: "the weakness of God is stronger than men" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 25|1 Corinthians 1:25]]); "he was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 13#2 Corinthians 13 . 4|2 Corinthians 13:4]]). The **worthy** acclamation (axios, vv. 9, 12) declares Christ's exclusive qualification to execute God's plan. No angel, saint, or creature is worthy—only the Lamb. His worthiness derives from His sacrifice: "you were slain" (esphagēs, v. 9). The aorist passive "were slain" points to Calvary's definite historical event—God's eternal plan ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 4|Ephesians 1:4]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 20|1 Peter 1:20]]) accomplished in space and time ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 4|Galatians 4:4]]). The **blood redemption** (v. 9: "by your blood you ransomed," en tō haimati sou ēgorasas) fulfills Levitical atonement. [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 17#Leviticus 17 . 11|Leviticus 17:11]] taught: "the life of the flesh is in the blood... it is the blood that makes atonement by the life." Animal blood atoned provisionally; Christ's blood redeems eternally. The verb agorazō ("purchase, ransom") appears in Paul: "you were bought with a price" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 20|1 Corinthians 6:20]]; 7:23). Peter echoes: "you were ransomed (elytrōthēte)... with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]]). The **universal scope** (v. 9: "from every tribe and language and people and nation") fulfills Abraham's promise: "in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]). The Passover lamb saved one household ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 3|Exodus 12:3]]); Christ's sacrifice saves believers globally. The phrase echoes [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 14|Daniel 7:14]]: "to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him." The **kingdom and priests** (v. 10) realizes [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]'s promise. At Sinai, God declared: "you shall be to me a kingdom of priests (mamleket kohanim) and a holy nation." Israel failed; Christ succeeds, making believers "a kingdom and priests (basileian, hiereis) to our God" (v. 10). Peter applies this: "you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood (basileion hierateuma)" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]). The Levitical priesthood, restricted to Aaron's line, expands to all believers through Christ's sacrifice. The **sevenfold doxology** (v. 12: power, wealth, wisdom, might, honor, glory, blessing) assigns complete worship to the Lamb. Seven attributes signify totality—the Lamb deserves infinite praise. This is divine worship; in Judaism, only God receives such adoration. The Lamb's reception of worship alongside God (5:13: "To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honor and glory and might") proves His deity. The **eternal celebration** (v. 9: "they sang a new song") replaces Moses' old song (Exodus 15) with the Lamb's song. Moses sang after Red Sea deliverance; heaven sings after cosmic redemption. The "new song" appears in Psalms (96:1; 98:1; 149:1)—songs of God's redemption and kingship. Revelation's new song celebrates the Lamb's definitive victory, His sacrifice securing eternal salvation. The trajectory culminates: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]] promises God will provide the Lamb → Exodus 12 prescribes Passover lamb → [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] prophesies Suffering Servant like a lamb → [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]] identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God → [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]] declares believers ransomed by the Lamb's blood → [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6-14]] unveils the slain Lamb enthroned, eternally worshiped → [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22|Revelation 21:22-23]] shows the Lamb as heaven's temple and light. The sacrifice is finished ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 30|John 19:30]]: tetelestai), but its celebration continues forever. What began in eternity past ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 4|Ephesians 1:4]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 20|1 Peter 1:20]]) was accomplished at Calvary and will be worshiped in eternity future. The Lamb who was slain remains the center of heaven's adoration, His blood the eternal ground of redemption, His worthiness the perpetual theme of creation's praise.
