### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G286-287#G286|ἀμνὸς]] (*amnos*) - "lamb"
- [[Lexicon/G2316-2317#G2316|θεοῦ]] (*theou*) - "of God" (genitive of theos)
- [[Lexicon/G142-143#G142|αἴρων]] (*airōn*) - "taking away, removing, lifting up" (present active participle of airō)
- [[Lexicon/G266-267#G266|ἁμαρτίαν]] (*hamartian*) - "sin" (accusative singular)
- [[Lexicon/G2889-2890#G2889|κόσμου]] (*kosmou*) - "of the world" (genitive of kosmos)
- [[Lexicon/G2396-2397#G2396|ἴδε]] (*ide*) - "behold, look, see" (aorist imperative of horaō)

**Context**: John 1:29 records John the Baptist's dramatic identification of Jesus: "The next day he saw Jesus coming toward him, and said, 'Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!'" This is the first public announcement of Jesus' identity and mission in the Fourth Gospel. The Baptist synthesizes the entire Old Testament sacrificial system into one declaration—Jesus is the Lamb of God (ton amnon tou theou), the divinely provided sacrifice who accomplishes what all previous lambs could not: taking away (airōn) the world's sin, not merely covering it. The present participle airōn suggests ongoing action—the Lamb who continually removes sin, whose work is both completed (cross) and continuing (application).

- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]] (God will provide for himself the lamb for a burnt offering), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 3|Exodus 12:3-13]] (each household shall take a lamb... when I see the blood, I will pass over you), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 10|Leviticus 14:10-13]] (male lamb without blemish for the guilt offering)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]'s identification of Jesus as "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" condenses the entire Old Testament sacrificial system into one declarative sentence. Every lamb ever offered—from Abel's acceptable sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 4|Genesis 4:4]]) to the temple's final tamid before Rome's destruction (AD 70)—pointed to this Lamb. The phrase "Lamb of God" (ton amnon tou theou) fulfills [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]]'s prophetic promise. When Isaac asked Abraham, "where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" Abraham replied: "God will provide (yir'eh) for himself the lamb (haśśeh)." The ram caught in the thicket provided immediate substitution, but John the Baptist declares the ultimate fulfillment: Jesus is the Lamb God provides for Himself to accomplish redemption. The Passover typology is unmistakable. Exodus 12 required each household to take "a lamb" (śeh) without blemish, slaughter it at twilight, apply its blood for protection from judgment, and eat its flesh for strength. Christ fulfills every detail: He is "without blemish" (amōmos, [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]), dies at Passover time ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]]), His blood protects from wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]), His flesh spiritually feeds believers ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 53|John 6:53-56]]). Paul makes the typology explicit: "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]). The daily tamid offering—two lambs perpetually, morning and evening—revealed the sacrificial system's incompleteness through its very continuity. Why repeat if the work is finished? Christ's "once for all" (ephapax, [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 27|Hebrews 7:27]]; 9:12; 10:10) sacrifice renders daily repetition obsolete. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]'s Suffering Servant "like a lamb led to slaughter" is applied to Jesus by the Ethiopian eunuch and Philip ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 8#Acts 8 . 32|Acts 8:32-35]]). The silent lamb becomes the speaking Word ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 1|John 1:1]]), yet silent before His accusers ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 12|Matthew 27:12-14]]), fulfilling prophecy. The verb "takes away" (airōn) is crucial. OT sacrifices covered (kaphar) sin temporarily; Christ removes (airō) sin permanently. The present tense suggests both accomplished work (His death) and continuing application (His blood continually cleanses, [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]). The object is "the sin" (tēn hamartian)—not merely individual sins but sin's unified reality, its power and penalty. The scope "of the world" (tou kosmou) universalizes atonement beyond Israel: "he is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for the sins of the whole world" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 2#1 John 2 . 2|1 John 2:2]]). Revelation consummates the Lamb imagery: the slain Lamb stands at heaven's throne ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]]), receives worship (5:12-13), opens the sealed scroll (5:9), leads the redeemed (7:17), conquers enemies (17:14), and lights the new creation (21:23). The Lamb's wrath judges unbelievers (6:16), but the Lamb's book of life saves believers (13:8). The trajectory moves from promise to fulfillment: God will provide a lamb ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]]) → Passover lamb protects from judgment (Exodus 12) → daily lambs offered perpetually (Exodus 29) → Suffering Servant like a lamb to slaughter ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]) → John declares: "Behold, the Lamb of God!" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]) → Christ crucified as Passover Lamb ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]], 36) → Paul proclaims: "Christ our Passover has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]) → Lamb enthroned in heaven forever ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6-14]]). What all the lambs prefigured, Christ fulfills. What they could not accomplish—taking away sin—He achieves. One Lamb replaces all lambs, one sacrifice supersedes all sacrifices, once for all time perfecting those being sanctified.
