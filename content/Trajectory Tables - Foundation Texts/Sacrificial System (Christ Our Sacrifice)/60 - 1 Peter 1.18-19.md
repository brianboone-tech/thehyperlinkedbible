### [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3084-3085#G3084|ἐλυτρώθητε]] (*elytrōthēte*) - "you were ransomed, redeemed" (aorist passive indicative)
- [[Lexicon/G5349-5350#G5349|φθαρτοῖς]] (*phthartois*) - "perishable, corruptible"
- [[Lexicon/G694-695#G694|ἀργυρίῳ]] (*arguriō*) - "silver" (dative singular)
- [[Lexicon/G5553-5554#G5553|χρυσίῳ]] (*chrusiō*) - "gold" (dative singular)
- [[Lexicon/G5093-5094#G5093|τιμίῳ]] (*timiō*) - "precious, valuable, costly" (dative singular)
- [[Lexicon/G129-130#G129|αἵματι]] (*haimati*) - "blood" (dative singular)
- [[Lexicon/G286-287#G286|ἀμνοῦ]] (*amnou*) - "of a lamb" (genitive singular)
- [[Lexicon/G299-300#G299|ἀμώμου]] (*amōmou*) - "without blemish, unblemished"
- [[Lexicon/G784-785#G784|ἀσπίλου]] (*aspilou*) - "without spot, spotless"

**Context**: 1 Peter 1:18-19 declares the priceless cost of redemption: "knowing that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot." Peter contrasts earthly ransoms (silver, gold—corruptible, perishable) with the incomparable price paid for believers' redemption: Christ's blood, described with Levitical sacrificial language ("lamb without blemish or spot"). The redemption metaphor (lytrōō) evokes OT slave redemption and Passover deliverance; the lamb imagery recalls both Passover (Exodus 12) and Isaiah 53's Suffering Servant; the "without blemish" requirement echoes Leviticus' unblemished sacrifice regulations.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5]] (your lamb shall be without blemish), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 1#Leviticus 1 . 3|Leviticus 1:3]] (male without blemish), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 22#Leviticus 22 . 19|Leviticus 22:19-21]] (it must be a male without blemish to be accepted)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] (like a lamb that is led to the slaughter), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 6#Exodus 6 . 6|Exodus 6:6]] (I will redeem you with an outstretched arm), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 49#Psalm 49 . 7|Psalm 49:7-8]] (no man can by any means redeem his brother)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]] (Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 20|1 Corinthians 6:20]] (you were bought with a price), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 7|Ephesians 1:7]] (in him we have redemption through his blood)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]]'s declaration that believers "were ransomed... with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot" synthesizes the Old Testament's redemption and sacrificial themes into christological fulfillment. The **redemption metaphor** (elytrōthēte, "you were ransomed") evokes Israel's exodus deliverance. [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 6#Exodus 6 . 6|Exodus 6:6]] promised: "I will redeem (padah) you with an outstretched arm"—God ransomed Israel from Egyptian slavery, not with silver or gold but with mighty power. Christ accomplishes the greater exodus, ransoming believers not from earthly slavery but from sin and death. The **blood emphasis** fulfills [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 17#Leviticus 17 . 11|Leviticus 17:11]]: "the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls." Animal blood atoned temporarily; Christ's blood redeems eternally. The contrast "not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with... blood" establishes blood's infinite value. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 49#Psalm 49 . 7|Psalm 49:7-8]] states the problem: "no man can redeem his brother, or give to God a ransom for him—for the ransom of their life is costly, and can never suffice." Human resources cannot pay sin's debt; only divine blood suffices. The **lamb imagery** combines Passover and Levitical sacrifice. [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5]] required "a lamb without blemish (tamim), a male a year old"—perfect, no defect. Leviticus repeats "without blemish" (tamim) over 70 times for acceptable sacrifices. The LXX translates tamim as amōmos (unblemished, [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 1#Leviticus 1 . 3|Leviticus 1:3]], 10; 3:1, 6; 4:3, 23, 28, 32). Peter applies this requirement christologically: Christ is the "lamb without blemish (amōmou) or spot (aspilou)"—absolutely sinless, morally perfect, qualifying Him as the acceptable sacrifice. The phrase "without spot" (aspilou) adds ceremonial perfection to moral perfection—not merely sinless but untainted by any defilement. The **precious blood** language (timiō haimati) creates stunning paradox. Blood in Leviticus is ritually defiling (12:4-5; 15:19-33); it must be carefully managed, never consumed (17:10-14). Yet Christ's blood is "precious" (timios)—invaluable, costly beyond measure. One drop of His blood outweighs all silver and gold in human history. The word timios appears in [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 7|1 Peter 2:7]] for Christ Himself ("precious to you who believe") and [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 19|Revelation 21:19]] for heaven's foundations' precious stones—Christ's blood is as valuable as Christ Himself, building heaven's eternal city. The **Suffering Servant connection** links to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]: "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent." Peter quotes Isaiah 53 extensively ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 22|1 Peter 2:22-25]]), applying the Servant's suffering to Christ. The trajectory unifies: the Servant is led like a lamb ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]) → the Servant's soul makes an offering for guilt (53:10) → Christ is the Lamb of God ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]) → believers ransomed by the Lamb's precious blood ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]) → the Lamb stands slain yet enthroned ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]]). The **once-for-all nature** is implied: "you were ransomed" (elytrōthēte, aorist passive) indicates completed redemption. Unlike Levitical sacrifices requiring endless repetition, Christ's blood redeems permanently. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]] makes it explicit: "he entered once for all into the holy places, not by means of the blood of goats and calves but by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption." What Peter states declaratively, Hebrews expounds theologically: Christ's blood accomplishes what animal blood never could—eternal, complete, perfect redemption. The trajectory moves from type to antitype: Passover lamb's blood protects from temporal judgment (Exodus 12) → Levitical lambs' blood covers sin temporarily (Leviticus 1-7) → Isaiah's Servant-lamb bears sin vicariously (Isaiah 53) → Christ the Lamb's precious blood redeems eternally ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]]) → the Lamb's blood cleanses robes ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]]) → the Lamb is heaven's light (21:23). What every unblemished lamb foreshadowed, Christ fulfilled; what all their blood symbolized, His blood accomplished—precious, perfect, permanent redemption.
