### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 33|Exodus 26:33-35]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6532-6533#H6532|פָּרֹכֶת]] (*pārōket*) - "veil, curtain" (that which separates)
- [[Lexicon/H6944-6945#H6944|קֹדֶשׁ]] (*qōdeš*) - "holy place"
- [[Lexicon/H6944-6945#H6944|קֹדֶשׁ הַקֳּדָשִׁים]] (*qōdeš haqqŏdāšîm*) - "Holy of Holies, Most Holy Place"
- [[Lexicon/H3742-3744#H3742|כְּרוּב]] (*kᵉrûb*) - "cherub" (angelic guardian)
- [[Lexicon/H4501-4502#H4501|מְנוֹרָה]] (*mᵉnôrāh*) - "lampstand"
- [[Lexicon/H7979-7980#H7979|שֻׁלְחָן]] (*šulḥān*) - "table" (for showbread)

**Context**: The veil divides the tabernacle into two chambers—the Holy Place where priests minister daily, and the Most Holy Place containing only the ark of the covenant where God's presence dwells. This curtain embroidered with cherubim symbolically recreates Eden's guarding cherubim (Genesis 3:24), barring sinful humanity from direct access to God's throne. The veil's beauty (blue, purple, scarlet, fine linen, skillfully worked cherubim) contrasts with its terrible function—keeping sinners from instant death in God's holy presence. Only the high priest passes through this veil, and only once annually on the Day of Atonement.

**Christological Connection**: The veil separating the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, embroidered with cherubim guarding God's presence, finds its profound fulfillment in Christ whose torn body opened the way into God's presence. As cherubim barred Eden's tree of life after the fall ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]), the veil's cherubim symbolically continued that exclusion—sinful humanity cannot approach holy God directly. The high priest's once-yearly entry through the veil on the Day of Atonement ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2-15]]) with blood foreshadowed Christ who "entered once for all into the holy places... by means of his own blood, thus securing eternal redemption" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]). The veil's tearing at Christ's crucifixion "from top to bottom" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]])—divine action, not human—dramatically fulfilled what the veil typologically prefigured. Hebrews makes the typology explicit: "we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, *that is, through his flesh*" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-20]]). Christ's body, torn on the cross, is the antitype of the torn veil—His suffering opened access to God's throne that the veil restricted for centuries. The furnishings' arrangement reveals progressive access: bronze altar (outer court—all approach), lampstand and table (Holy Place—priests enter), ark (Most Holy Place—high priest alone annually), but in Christ "we have access in one Spirit to the Father" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 18|Ephesians 2:18]]). What once only the high priest could do once yearly, every believer now does continually through Christ our great high priest who has "passed through the heavens" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 14|Hebrews 4:14]]). The veil that separated now connects—Christ's torn flesh is the "new and living way" into God's presence forever.
