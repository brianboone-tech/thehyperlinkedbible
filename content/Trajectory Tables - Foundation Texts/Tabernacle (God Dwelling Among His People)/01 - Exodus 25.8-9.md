### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8-9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4720-4725#H4720|מִקְדָּשׁ]] (*miqdāš*) - "sanctuary, holy place"
- [[Lexicon/H7931-7932#H7931|שָׁכַן]] (*šākan*) - "dwell, tabernacle, abide"
- [[Lexicon/H8432-8433#H8432|תָּוֶךְ]] (*tāwek*) - "midst, among, middle"
- [[Lexicon/H8403-8404#H8403|תַּבְנִית]] (*tabnît*) - "pattern, model, form"
- [[Lexicon/H4908-4910#H4908|מִשְׁכָּן]] (*miškān*) - "tabernacle, dwelling place"
- [[Lexicon/H3627-3629#H3627|כְּלִי]] (*kᵉlî*) - "vessel, article, utensil"

**Context**: After redeeming Israel from Egypt through the Passover lamb's blood and establishing covenant at Sinai, God now commands construction of a sanctuary where He will dwell among His people. This divine directive appears at the mountain's summit where Moses receives the pattern shown by God. The tabernacle represents God's gracious condescension—the Holy One choosing to dwell with sinful people—and establishes the principle that approach to God requires divinely prescribed means, not human innovation.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 8|Genesis 3:8]] (God walked in the garden with man before sin), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 4|Exodus 19:4-6]] (kingdom of priests, holy nation), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 45|Exodus 29:45-46]] (purpose of redemption: "I will dwell among the people of Israel")
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 1|1 Kings 6:1]] (Solomon's temple fulfills tabernacle), [[Readable Bible/13 - 1 Chronicles/1 Chronicles 28#1 Chronicles 28 . 19|1 Chronicles 28:19]] (David receives temple pattern from God), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 37#Ezekiel 37 . 27|Ezekiel 37:27]] (eschatological promise: "my dwelling place shall be with them"), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 43#Exodus 43 . 7|Ezekiel 43:7]] (glory returns to temple)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 5|Hebrews 8:5]] (Moses warned to make everything according to pattern), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]] (Word became flesh and "tabernacled" among us), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]] (God's dwelling with man in new creation)

**Christological Connection**: God's command to "make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst" inaugurates the grand trajectory of divine indwelling that culminates in Christ and extends to the new creation. The tabernacle's primary function—providing place for God's presence among sinful people—anticipates the incarnation where "the Word became flesh and *tabernacled* among us" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). John's choice of Greek verb (*eskēnōsen*, "pitched his tent") deliberately evokes [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]], declaring Jesus as the true tabernacle. The "pattern shown on the mountain" (v. 9) which [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 5|Hebrews 8:5]] identifies as "copy and shadow of the heavenly things" reveals that every tabernacle element—altar, lampstand, showbread, incense, veil, ark—pointed to heavenly realities fulfilled in Christ's person and work. Christ is the greater sanctuary "not made with hands" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 11|Hebrews 9:11]]), the place where God's presence dwells fully: "in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 9|Colossians 2:9]]). The tabernacle's centrality in Israel's camp—tribes positioned around it—prefigures Christ as center of God's people, through whom all approach the Father ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 18|Ephesians 2:18]]). The restriction to prescribed pattern underscores that access to God comes only through divinely appointed means—ultimately Christ who declared "I am the way... no one comes to the Father except through me" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]]). The tabernacle's portable nature—journeying with Israel through wilderness—foreshadows Christ's pilgrim ministry and the church's mission advancing through history carrying God's presence to all nations. The trajectory reaches consummation in [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]] where the angel declares "Behold, the *tabernacle* of God is with man. He will dwell with them"—what began as gracious accommodation in [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]] becomes eternal reality in new creation, with Christ as mediator making intimate divine presence possible forever.
