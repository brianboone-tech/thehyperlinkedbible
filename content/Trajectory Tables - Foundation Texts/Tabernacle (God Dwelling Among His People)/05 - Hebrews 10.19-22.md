### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-22]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3954-3955#G3954|παρρησία]] (*parrēsia*) - "boldness, confidence, openness"
- [[Lexicon/G1529-1530#G1529|εἴσοδος]] (*eisodos*) - "entrance, entry"
- [[Lexicon/G0039-0040#G0039|ἅγιον]] (*hagion*) - "holy place, sanctuary"
- [[Lexicon/G2537-2538#G2537|καινός]] (*kainos*) - "new" (in quality, fresh)
- [[Lexicon/G2198-2199#G2198|ζάω]] (*zaō*) - "living, alive"
- [[Lexicon/G2665-2666#G2665|καταπέτασμα]] (*katapetasma*) - "veil, curtain"
- [[Lexicon/G4314-4315#G4314|προσέρχομαι]] (*proserchomai*) - "draw near, approach"

**Context**: Following his exposition of Christ's once-for-all sacrifice that perfected believers (10:10-18), the writer issues a pastoral exhortation: "Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus..." This passage applies tabernacle typology directly to Christian life—what was restricted (access to God's presence) is now opened through Christ. The veil that separated worshipers from God's throne has been torn through Christ's flesh, creating "a new and living way" for believers to approach God boldly. This represents one of the NT's clearest statements on how Christ's death transforms worship from external ritual to intimate relational access.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-22]] applies Christ's fulfillment of tabernacle typology to believers' present experience, declaring that "we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus" (v. 19)—access previously restricted to the high priest alone once yearly ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2]]) now belongs to all believers continually. The "new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh" (v. 20) explicitly identifies Christ's torn body with the torn temple veil ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]]), revealing profound typology: as the veil separated worshipers from God's presence, Christ's flesh crucified removes the barrier. The veil embroidered with cherubim ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31]]) recalled Eden's guardian cherubim barring access to the tree of life ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]); Christ's death tears down that ancient barrier, granting access to eternal life. The phrase "living way" contrasts with the dead animals whose blood provided only temporary covering—Christ lives forever to make intercession ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). Where old covenant worshipers approached with fear, Christ's finished work allows believers to "draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith" (v. 22)—transformation from terror to confidence. The "great priest over the house of God" (v. 21) is Christ who, having "passed through the heavens" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 14|Hebrews 4:14]]), now ministers in the true tabernacle ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 2|Hebrews 8:2]]). The call to "draw near" echoes the priestly approach to God's presence, but democratized—all believers are now priests with direct access ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]: "a royal priesthood"). What the tabernacle's structure restricted for centuries, Christ's cross opened forever—the veil that said "keep out" now invites "come in" through Christ's torn flesh.
