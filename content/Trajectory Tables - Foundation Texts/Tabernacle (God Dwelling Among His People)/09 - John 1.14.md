**Context**: John's Gospel opens with creation theology ("In the beginning was the Word") and climaxes in verse 14 with incarnation theology: "the Word became flesh." This verse announces Christianity's most staggering claim—the eternal, divine Word through whom all things were made took on human nature and dwelt among humanity. John's verb choice (*skēnoō*, "tabernacled") deliberately evokes Exodus 40:34 where God's glory filled the tabernacle. The incarnation fulfills what the tabernacle foreshadowed: God dwelling visibly, tangibly, personally among His people. What Israel experienced in shadow (God's presence veiled in tent), believers experience in substance (God's presence revealed in Christ).

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]'s declaration that "the Word became flesh and *tabernacled* among us" identifies Christ as the ultimate fulfillment of God's promise to dwell with His people, surpassing all previous manifestations of divine presence. The verb *skēnoō* ("tabernacled") deliberately evokes [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 40#Exodus 40 . 34|Exodus 40:34]] where "the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle"—as God's glory dwelt in the wilderness tent, now "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory." What the tabernacle represented symbolically (God's presence among His people), Christ accomplishes actually: "in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 9|Colossians 2:9]]). The glory Moses could only glimpse partially ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 23|Exodus 33:23]]: "you shall see my back, but my face shall not be seen") believers now behold fully in Christ's face ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 6|2 Corinthians 4:6]]: "the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ"). The tabernacle's portable nature—journeying with Israel through wilderness—prefigures Christ's incarnational movement: "he went about doing good" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]]), moving among people as God-with-us. The description "full of grace and truth" echoes God's self-revelation to Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 6|Exodus 34:6]]: "abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness"), identifying Jesus with Yahweh who met Moses at the tabernacle. Where the tabernacle required blood at the bronze altar before approach, Christ Himself becomes both tabernacle and sacrifice—the place of meeting and the means of access. The veil separating the Holy Place from God's presence finds fulfillment in Christ's flesh which, when torn, opened the way to God ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]). What began with God's promise "I will dwell in their midst" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]) reaches climax in "the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us"—Immanuel, God with us ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 23|Matthew 1:23]]).
