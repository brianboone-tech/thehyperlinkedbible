### [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5401-5500#G5485|χάριτι]] (*chariti*) - "by grace" (dative of means)
- [[Lexicon/G4601-4700#G4982|σεσῳσμένοι]] (*sesōsmenoi*) - "having been saved" (perfect passive participle)
- [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4102|πίστεως]] (*pisteōs*) - "of faith" (genitive)
- [[Lexicon/G1401-1500#G1435|δῶρον]] (*dōron*) - "gift"
- [[Lexicon/G2001-2100#G2041|ἐξ ἔργων]] (*ex ergōn*) - "from works, as a result of works"
- [[Lexicon/G2701-2800#G2744|καυχήσηται]] (*kauchēsētai*) - "might boast" (subjunctive)

**Context**: Paul declares that salvation is entirely by grace through faith, not from works, so that no one can boast. This foundational gospel statement establishes rest from works-righteousness as central to Christian faith, fulfilling the Sabbath's rest principle.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 2|Genesis 2:2-3]] (God's rest from works), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]] (Abraham believed and it was counted as righteousness)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 27|Romans 3:27-28]] (boasting excluded, justified by faith), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 4|Romans 4:4-5]] (grace not wages), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 6|Romans 11:6]] (if by grace, not works), [[Readable Bible/56 - Titus/Titus 3#Titus 3 . 5|Titus 3:5]] (not by works of righteousness)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]] articulates the Sabbath rest principle in gospel terms. The Sabbath commanded ceasing from work one day weekly, trusting God's provision. Paul declares believers cease from works-righteousness entirely, trusting Christ's provision exclusively. This is Sabbath rest applied soteriologically—resting in Christ's finished work rather than one's own efforts. Jesus' invitation "Come to me... and I will give you rest" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28]]) finds theological explanation here: rest comes through grace, accessed by faith, excluding works. Hebrews connects this to Sabbath: "whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 10|Hebrews 4:10]]). As God rested after completing creation, believers rest after Christ completed redemption. The exclusion of boasting fulfills Sabbath's egalitarian principle—in rest, all are equal; no one boasts of superior resting ability. Weekly Sabbath provided external rest; grace provides internal rest—rest of conscience ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]), assurance of salvation, freedom from endless striving. Paul's "not a result of works" doesn't produce antinomianism but grateful obedience ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 10|Ephesians 2:10]]: "created in Christ Jesus for good works"). The pattern is rest first (salvation by grace), then works (fruit of salvation), reversing merit-theology's order. This Sabbath rest-principle reaches consummation when believers "rest from their labors" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]]) eternally, their works following as grace-fruit, not merit-earning efforts.
