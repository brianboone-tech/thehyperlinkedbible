### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 7|Hebrews 4:7]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3601-3700#G3724|ὁρίζει]] (*horizei*) - "he designates, appoints"
- [[Lexicon/G4501-4600#G4594|σήμερον]] (*sēmeron*) - "today" (emphatic present urgency)
- [[Lexicon/G3101-3200#G3326|μετὰ τοσοῦτον χρόνον]] (*meta tosouton chronon*) - "after so long a time"
- [[Lexicon/G4401-4500#G4645|σκληρύνητε]] (*sklērynēte*) - "harden" (subjunctive)
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2588|καρδίας]] (*kardias*) - "hearts"

**Context**: The author of Hebrews argues that God appointed another day called "Today" through David in Psalm 95 (written long after Joshua's conquest). This proves that rest remains available and must be entered through faith today, not postponed. The urgency of "today" combats procrastination and unbelief.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 7|Psalm 95:7-8]] (Today, if you hear his voice), [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 21#Joshua 21 . 43|Joshua 21:43-45]] (Joshua gave them rest—yet insufficient)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 7|Hebrews 3:7-11]] (Psalm 95 quoted), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 13|Hebrews 3:13]] (exhort one another every day, "Today"), [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 2|2 Corinthians 6:2]] (now is the day of salvation)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 7|Hebrews 4:7]]'s "today" defines the age of salvation inaugurated by Christ. What was unavailable in Joshua's era—true soul rest—Christ provides now ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28]]: "Come to me... and I will give you rest"). The "today" is not a 24-hour period but the entire gospel age from Christ's first coming until His return, the "acceptable time" and "day of salvation" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 2|2 Corinthians 6:2]]). God appoints this extended "today" through the gospel proclamation—wherever Christ is preached, "today" sounds forth. The urgency stems from two realities: (1) Death may end opportunity ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 27|Hebrews 9:27]]: "appointed for man to die once"); (2) Repeated rejection hardens hearts irreversibly, as Israel demonstrated in the wilderness. Entering rest "today" means ceasing from works-righteousness now ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 10|Hebrews 4:10]]), trusting Christ's finished work presently, not postponing faith. The author's pastoral concern is evident—believers must exhort "one another every day, as long as it is called 'today'" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 13|Hebrews 3:13]]) lest anyone drift through procrastination or unbelief. The Sabbath principle—cease working, enter rest—applies today soteriologically: stop striving for self-righteousness, rest in Christ's righteousness now. The eschatological tension is present: "today" extends until final rest ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]]), but entrance occurs in this life through faith. Those who postpone "until tomorrow" risk wilderness generation's fate—dying outside rest through unbelief. Christ opens rest's door; "today" is the time to enter.
