### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0601-0700#G0620|ἀπολείπεται]] (*apoleipetai*) - "remains, is left"
- [[Lexicon/G4501-4600#G4520|σαββατισμός]] (*sabbatismos*) - "Sabbath rest" (only NT occurrence)
- [[Lexicon/G2801-2900#G2992|λαῷ τοῦ θεοῦ]] (*laō tou theou*) - "people of God"
- [[Lexicon/G1501-1600#G1525|εἰσελθών]] (*eiselthōn*) - "having entered"
- [[Lexicon/G2601-2700#G2664|κατέπαυσεν]] (*katepausen*) - "rested, ceased"

**Context**: The author of Hebrews concludes his exposition of Psalm 95 by declaring that a Sabbath rest (σαββατισμός) remains for God's people. Entering God's rest means ceasing from works as God did from His, pointing to rest from works-righteousness through faith in Christ's finished work.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 2|Genesis 2:2-3]] (God rested from works), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 11|Psalm 95:11]] (they shall not enter my rest)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 7|Hebrews 3:7-4:11]] (rest exposition), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]] (not of works), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 5|Romans 4:5]] (believes in him who justifies)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]] applies Sabbath theology christologically. The "Sabbath rest" that remains is entered through Christ who perfectly fulfilled the law ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 17|Matthew 5:17]]), accomplishing what Adam, Moses, and Joshua couldn't. Christ's cry "It is finished" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 30|John 19:30]]) echoes God's completion in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 2|Genesis 2:2]], after which He rested. Christ's Sabbath rest in the tomb before resurrection Sunday inaugurates the new creation rest. Believers who "have entered" God's rest have "rested from their works as God did from his"—this doesn't mean cessation of good works but of works-righteousness, trusting Christ's work rather than one's own for justification ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]]: "not a result of works"). The already/not-yet tension reflects salvation's structure: believers presently rest in Christ's finished work while anticipating eternal rest. The Sabbath principle—cease working, trust God's provision—now operates soteriologically: cease self-effort, trust Christ's provision. The ultimate Sabbath rest reaches consummation in the new creation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]]) where believers "rest from their labors" eternally, their works following them not as grounds for acceptance but as fruit of grace.
