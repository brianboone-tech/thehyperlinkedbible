### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28-30]]

- [[Lexicon/G0001-0100#G0372|ἀνάπαυσιν]] (*anapausin*) - "rest, refreshment"

**Context**: Jesus invites all who are weary and burdened to come to Him, promising rest for their souls. He offers His yoke as easy and His burden as light, contrasting with the heavy burdens imposed by Pharisaic interpretations of the law. This is rest for souls, not merely physical rest.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28-30]] presents Christ as the fulfillment of the Sabbath's rest principle. What the Sabbath symbolized—ceasing from works and trusting God's provision—Christ provides actually. The weary and burdened are those crushed under sin's weight and law's demands ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 7#Romans 7 . 24|Romans 7:24]]: "Wretched man that I am!"). Christ invites them to cease striving and find rest in His finished work. Hebrews connects this to Sabbath theology: believers enter God's rest by ceasing from their own works ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 10|Hebrews 4:10]]), resting in Christ's completed redemption. The "yoke" of discipleship paradoxically brings freedom ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 1|Galatians 5:1]]) because Christ's demands are accompanied by His enabling grace. Where the Sabbath commanded physical rest one day weekly, Christ provides soul rest continually. This isn't abolishing Sabbath but fulfilling it—the principle of ceasing from self-effort and trusting God's provision now finds expression in resting in Christ's righteousness rather than earning one's own. Paul's teaching that believers are "not under law but under grace" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 14|Romans 6:14]]) flows from this rest-invitation. The ultimate Sabbath rest awaits in the new creation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]]: "Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord... that they may rest from their labors"), but believers enter it proleptically now through union with Christ who is their rest.
