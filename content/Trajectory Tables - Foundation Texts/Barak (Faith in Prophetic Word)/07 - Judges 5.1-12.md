### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 1|Judges 5:1-12]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7891|H7891]] שִׁיר (šîr) - "sing" - Deborah and Barak sang
- [[Lexicon/H5068|H5068]] נָדַב (nāḏaḇ) - "volunteer" - when the people volunteer
- [[Lexicon/H1288|H1288]] בָּרַךְ (bāraḵ) - "bless" - bless the LORD
- [[Lexicon/H6737|H6737]] צָדַק (ṣāḏaq) - "righteous acts" - the righteous acts of the LORD
- [[Lexicon/H5782|H5782]] עוּר (ʿûr) - "awake" - Awake, awake, O Deborah! Awake, awake, sing a song! Arise, O Barak!

**Context**: After victory, Deborah and Barak sang together. The Song of Deborah is one of the oldest Hebrew poems, celebrating God's intervention. It praises those who volunteered (5:2, 9), recalls God's theophanic march from Sinai (5:4-5), describes Israel's desperate condition (5:6-8), and calls both Deborah and Barak to arise (5:12). The song attributes victory entirely to God while honoring faithful human participation.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Victory songs are pattern: Moses (Exodus 15), David (2 Samuel 22), Hezekiah (Isaiah 38)
- The theophanic description (5:4-5) echoes Sinai and anticipates later prophetic theophanies
- The call "Awake, awake" echoes in [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 51#Isaiah 51 . 9|Isaiah 51:9]]; 52:1

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Exodus 15 - Song of the Sea after Red Sea victory
  - [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 33#Deuteronomy 33 . 2|Deuteronomy 33:2]] - Similar theophanic language
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 51#Isaiah 51 . 9|Isaiah 51:9]] - ; 52:1 - "Awake, awake" calls to Zion; Psalm 68 - Similar divine warrior imagery
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 15#Revelation 15 . 3|Revelation 15:3]] - Song of Moses and the Lamb
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 19|Ephesians 5:19]] - Singing in response to God's work
**Type Classification**: Providential (victory song preserved as canonical instruction), Forward-Looking (patterns for celebrating divine deliverance)

**Christological Connection**: The Song of Deborah anticipates the ultimate victory song. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 15#Revelation 15 . 3|Revelation 15:3]] describes the redeemed singing "the song of Moses... and the song of the Lamb." As Deborah and Barak sang after defeating Sisera, the church will sing after Christ's final victory. The Divine Warrior who marched from Sinai now marches from Calvary, and His conquest of sin and death calls forth eternal praise.
