### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32-34]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4102|G4102]] πίστις (pistis) - "faith" - through faith
- [[Lexicon/G2610|G2610]] καταγωνίζομαι (katagōnizomai) - "conquer" - conquered kingdoms
- [[Lexicon/G2038|G2038]] ἐργάζομαι (ergazomai) - "work/accomplish" - administered justice
- [[Lexicon/G1849|G1849]] ἐπιτυγχάνω (epitynchanō) - "obtain" - gained what was promised
- [[Lexicon/G772|G772]] ἀσθένεια (astheneia) - "weakness" - gained strength from weakness
- [[Lexicon/G1415|G1415]] δυνατός (dynatos) - "mighty/powerful" - became mighty in battle

**Context**: The author of Hebrews, running short of time, lists six faith heroes by name: Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, and Samuel. Then he describes what "through faith" they accomplished: conquered kingdoms, administered justice, obtained promises, shut lions' mouths, quenched fire, escaped the sword, gained strength from weakness, became mighty in battle, put foreign armies to flight. Barak's explicit inclusion confirms his faith was genuine despite Judges 4:8's record of his hesitation.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The list spans the Judges period (Barak, Gideon, Samson, Jephthah) plus early monarchy (David, Samuel)
- "Conquered kingdoms" and "put foreign armies to flight" specifically fit Barak's Sisera victory
- "Gained strength from weakness" may particularly apply to Barak's hesitant start

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Judges 4-5 - Barak's narrative
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 1|Hebrews 11:1-2]] - Definition of faith that frames this list
- **FROM OT**:
  - All the OT passages about each named hero
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 1|Hebrews 12:1]] - These witnesses form a "cloud" encouraging perseverance
**Type Classification**: NT Commendation (Barak's faith explicitly validated and held up as exemplary)

**Christological Connection**: The faith of Barak and the others was faith looking forward to Christ, the "author and perfecter of faith" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]]). They "did not receive what was promised" in full (11:39) because God planned "something better for us, so that together with us they would be made perfect" (11:40). Barak's faith in God's prophetic word anticipates Christian faith in God's ultimate Word, Jesus Christ. His weakness made strong foreshadows how Christ perfects His power in our weakness.
