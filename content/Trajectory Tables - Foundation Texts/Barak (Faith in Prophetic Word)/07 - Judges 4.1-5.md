### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 1|Judges 4:1-5]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7451|H7451]] רַע (raʿ) - "evil" - did evil in the sight of the LORD
- [[Lexicon/H4376|H4376]] מָכַר (māḵar) - "sell" - the LORD sold them
- [[Lexicon/H2388|H2388]] חָזַק (ḥāzaq) - "strong/oppress" - he harshly oppressed
- [[Lexicon/H5031|H5031]] נְבִיאָה (neḇîʾâ) - "prophetess" - Deborah, a prophetess
- [[Lexicon/H8199|H8199]] שָׁפַט (šāp̄aṭ) - "judge" - was judging Israel

**Context**: This passage sets the stage for Barak's story. After Ehud's death, Israel reverted to evil, and God "sold" them to Jabin king of Canaan—a strong term indicating judicial transfer to an oppressor. Sisera's 900 iron chariots represented overwhelming military superiority. After twenty years, Israel cried out. God's answer came through Deborah, the only female judge explicitly called a prophetess, who sat under her palm and judged Israel.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The Judges cycle (sin → oppression → cry → deliverance) repeats throughout the book
- Deborah as prophetess follows Miriam ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 20|Exodus 15:20]]) and anticipates Huldah ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 22#2 Kings 22 . 14|2 Kings 22:14]])
- Jabin king of Hazor echoes [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 1|Joshua 11:1]], suggesting ongoing Canaanite resistance

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 11#Joshua 11 . 1|Joshua 11:1]] - Earlier Jabin defeated by Joshua
  - [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 30|Judges 3:30]] - Previous judge Ehud's death triggers cycle
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 7|1 Samuel 8:7-8]] - Pattern of Israel's rebellion
  - [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 22#2 Kings 22 . 14|2 Kings 22:14]] - Huldah as later prophetess
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32]] - Barak commended despite this troubled context
**Type Classification**: Providential (context for Barak's faith), Backward-Looking (faith dynamics recognized from NT perspective)

**Christological Connection**: The oppression under Jabin/Sisera typifies bondage to sin and Satan. Israel's inability to free themselves (twenty years of harsh oppression) points to humanity's need for a deliverer. God's raising up Deborah and Barak anticipates Christ, the ultimate Prophet-Judge who delivers His people from oppression. The pattern of crying out and receiving salvation prefigures repentance and faith.
