### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 4#Judges 4 . 8|Judges 4:8-9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3212|H3212]] הָלַךְ (hālaḵ) - "go" - if you will go with me, I will go
- [[Lexicon/H8597|H8597]] תִּפְאָרָה (tip̄ʾārâ) - "honor/glory" - the road you are taking will bring you no honor
- [[Lexicon/H4376|H4376]] מָכַר (māḵar) - "sell" - the LORD will be selling Sisera
- [[Lexicon/H802|H802]] אִשָּׁה (ʾiššâ) - "woman" - into the hand of a woman

**Context**: Barak's response to Deborah's command reveals both faith and weakness. He will go—but only if Deborah accompanies him. This is neither pure courage nor pure cowardice. Deborah agrees to go but announces a consequence: "the road you are taking will bring you no honor, because the LORD will be selling Sisera into the hand of a woman." Barak accepts even this diminished honor and proceeds. His faith is real but not without its struggles.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Moses similarly expressed inadequacy when called ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 11|Exodus 3:11]]; 4:10-13)
- Gideon requested signs despite God's clear command ([[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 36|Judges 6:36-40]])
- The pattern: God works through imperfect, struggling faith

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 10|Exodus 4:10-13]] - Moses' similar hesitation
  - [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 15|Judges 6:15]] - Gideon's self-deprecation
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 17|1 Samuel 15:17]] - Samuel reminds Saul he was small in his own eyes
  - [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 103#Psalm 103 . 14|Psalm 103:14]] - God knows our frame
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32]] - Despite this hesitation, Barak is commended
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 14#Matthew 14 . 31|Matthew 14:31]] - Jesus saves Peter's "little faith"
**Type Classification**: Providential (faith accompanied by weakness), Backward-Looking (NT commendation despite OT presentation of weakness)

**Christological Connection**: Barak's need for prophetic presence anticipates the believer's need for Christ. Just as Barak wouldn't go without Deborah, we cannot face spiritual battles without Christ's presence. Yet Christ's response is more gracious than Deborah's—He promises "I am with you always" without diminishing our reward. Christ sympathizes with our weaknesses ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]) and intercedes for struggling faith.
