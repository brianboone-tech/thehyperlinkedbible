### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 1|Exodus 28:1-43]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- בִּגְדֵי־קֹדֶשׁ (*bigdei-qodesh*) - "holy garments" - sacred vestments restoring Adamic glory (28:2)
- כָּבוֹד (*kavod*) - "glory" - splendor, weightiness (28:2, 40)
- תִּפְאֶרֶת (*tif'eret*) - "beauty" - majesty, honor (28:2, 40)
- צִּיץ (*tsits*) - "plate, flower" - golden headplate inscribed "Holy to the LORD" (28:36)
- אֵפוֹד (*ephod*) - "ephod" - ornate vest containing twelve precious stones (28:6-14)

**Context**: Instructions for tabernacle construction (Exodus 25-31). Chapter 28 details priestly garments for Aaron and sons. Follows tabernacle structure/furniture instructions (chapters 25-27); precedes consecration ceremony (Leviticus 8).

**Christological Connection**: High priest's garments foreshadow Christ, the true Image-Bearer. Where Aaron wore golden plate reading "Holy to the LORD," Christ **is** holy, "set apart from sinners, exalted above the heavens" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]). Where Aaron bore twelve tribes' names on breastplate, Christ bears His people's names eternally, interceding at Father's right hand ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 34|Romans 8:34]]). Where Aaron's garments were "for glory and for beauty," Christ possesses intrinsic glory: "we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). **Escalation**: Aaron's external garments → Christ's inherent glory; Aaron's annual entrance → Christ's once-for-all entrance; Aaron's mortal priesthood → Christ's eternal priesthood "after the order of Melchizedek" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 17|Hebrews 7:17]]). The trajectory reveals priestly vocation fulfilled in Christ, who clothes believers in His righteousness ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 61#Isaiah 61 . 10|Isaiah 61:10]]; [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 8|Revelation 19:8]]), restoring image lost in Adam.
