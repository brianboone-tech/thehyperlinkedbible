### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 13|Isaiah 52:13-53:12]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- עֶבֶד (*eved*) - "servant" - chosen one who accomplishes God's will (52:13; 53:11)
- מָשַׁחַת (*mashchat*) - "marred, disfigured" - appearance beyond human recognition (52:14)
- מַכְאֹבוֹת (*mak'ovot*) - "sorrows, pains" - suffering vicariously borne (53:3-4)
- אָשָׁם (*asham*) - "guilt offering" - priestly sacrifice for sin (53:10)
- יַצְדִּיק (*yatsdiq*) - "make righteous, justify" - forensic declaration (53:11)

**Context**: Fourth Servant Song (after 42:1-9; 49:1-13; 50:4-11). Climax of Isaiah's servant prophecies. Follows announcement "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news" (52:7-10); precedes barren woman's joy (54:1-17).

**Christological Connection**: Christ is the Suffering Servant who perfectly bears God's image through obedience unto death. Where Adam grasped at being like God ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 5|Genesis 3:5]]), Christ "though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself... and became obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-8]]). **Escalation**: OT priests offered animal sacrifices repeatedly; Christ offers Himself once for all ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26-28]]). High priest's guilt offering was external; Christ **becomes** the guilt offering. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10-11]] anticipates resurrection and vindication - fulfilled when God "highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9]]). From marred beyond human semblance → to glorified Image at Father's right hand. The Servant's priestly work restores the image: believers justified (53:11), healed (53:5), and brought back as straying sheep (53:6; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 25|1 Peter 2:25]]). The trajectory reveals image-bearing fulfilled not through human achievement but divine substitution.
