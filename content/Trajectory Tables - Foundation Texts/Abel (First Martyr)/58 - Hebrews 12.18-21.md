### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 18|Hebrews 12:18-21]]

**Context**: The author contrasts two mountains: Sinai (vv.18-21) and Zion (vv.22-24). Sinai represents old covenant terror—fire, darkness, gloom, tempest, trumpet, voice so terrifying that even Moses said "I tremble with fear." This sets up the climactic Abel reference in v.24: believers have not come to this terrifying inaccessibility but to "sprinkled blood that speaks better than the blood of Abel." The contrast is between terror and grace, judgment and mercy.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ψηλαφωμένῳ (*psēlaphōmenō*) - "that can be touched"—physical, tangible mountain
- κεκαυμένῳ πυρί (*kekaumenō pyri*) - "blazing with fire"—consuming holiness
- γνόφῳ καὶ ζόφῳ καὶ θυέλλῃ (*gnophō kai zophō kai thyellē*) - "darkness, gloom, and storm"—terrifying phenomena
- ἔκφοβός εἰμι καὶ ἔντρομος (*ekphobos eimi kai entromos*) - "I am terrified and trembling"—Moses' response

**Christological Connection**: The Sinai theophany represents old covenant's inaccessibility—even Moses was terrified. This context makes the Abel reference (v.24) all the more powerful: Abel's blood (like Sinai's terror) cried for justice and judgment; Christ's blood (like Zion's grace) speaks welcome and mercy. The trajectory: Sinai (approach means death) → Abel's blood (cries for judgment) → Christ's blood (speaks better—grants access). Believers come not to mountain where touching means death but to city where Jesus' blood grants entrance to God's presence.
