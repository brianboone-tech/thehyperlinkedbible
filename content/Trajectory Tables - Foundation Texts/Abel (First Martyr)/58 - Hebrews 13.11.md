### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11]]

**Context**: "For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest as a sacrifice for sin are burned outside the camp." This references Day of Atonement protocol (Leviticus 16:27) and sets up the application to Jesus (v.12): "So Jesus also suffered outside the gate." The "outside" location connects to Abel trajectory: Abel died "in the field" (outside), Levitical bodies burned "outside the camp," Jesus crucified "outside the gate."

**Greek Key Terms**:
- τὸ αἷμα περὶ ἁμαρτίας (*to haima peri hamartias*) - "the blood for sin"—sin offering blood
- εἰς τὰ ἅγια (*eis ta hagia*) - "into the holy places"—blood brought into sanctuary
- ἔξω τῆς παρεμβολῆς (*exō tēs parembolēs*) - "outside the camp"—place of exclusion
- κατακαίεται (*katakaieta*) - "are burned"—complete destruction

**Christological Connection**: The "outside" location unites Abel, Levitical sacrifice, and Christ. Abel was murdered "in the field"—away from home, in excluded space. Day of Atonement sin offerings were burned "outside the camp"—excluded space where impurity belongs. Jesus suffered "outside the gate" of Jerusalem—the place of shame and exclusion. Yet this excluded location paradoxically becomes the locus of atonement. Christ bore the exclusion of sin so sinners could enter God's presence. The trajectory: Abel (died outside) → sacrifices (burned outside) → Christ (suffered outside) → believers (go to Him outside, bearing His reproach, v.13).
