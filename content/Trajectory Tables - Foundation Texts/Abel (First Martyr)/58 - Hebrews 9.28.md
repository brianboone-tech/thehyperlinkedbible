### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]]

**Context**: "So Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him." This quotes Isaiah 53:12 ("bore the sin of many") and transforms the martyr pattern: Abel died innocently for himself; Christ died innocently bearing others' sins. The once-for-all nature of Christ's sacrifice contrasts with repeated Levitical offerings.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἅπαξ προσενεχθείς (*hapax prosenechtheis*) - "having been offered once"—unrepeatable sacrifice
- πολλῶν ἀνενεγκεῖν ἁμαρτίας (*pollōn anenenkein hamartias*) - "to bear the sins of many"—Isaiah 53:12 allusion
- ἐκ δευτέρου (*ek deuterou*) - "a second time"—Christ's return
- χωρὶς ἁμαρτίας (*chōris hamartias*) - "apart from sin"—not to deal with sin again

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]] transforms Abel's martyr pattern from tragedy to soteriology. Abel's innocent death accomplished nothing for others—his blood cried only for his own justice. Christ's innocent death "bore the sins of many"—His blood accomplishes forgiveness for the very sinners who shed it. The contrast could not be sharper: Abel's blood speaks judgment on one person (Cain); Christ's blood speaks salvation for many. The trajectory: Abel (innocent sufferer, blood cries vengeance for self) → Christ (innocent sufferer, blood provides atonement for others). Christ's return will not be to deal with sin again but to bring final salvation.
