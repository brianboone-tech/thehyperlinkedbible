### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 5|Jeremiah 7:5-6]]

**Context**: The core demand of Jeremiah's temple sermon: "If you truly amend your ways and your deeds, if you truly execute justice one with another, if you do not oppress the sojourner, the fatherless, or the widow, or shed innocent blood in this place..." The phrase "shed innocent blood" directly invokes the Abel narrative—innocent blood shed while people maintain religious pretense. This was precisely Cain's pattern: offering sacrifice while harboring murderous intent.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- הֵיטֵב תֵּיטִיבוּ (*hetev tetivu*) - "truly amend"—emphatic improvement required
- מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*) - "justice"—right judgment/relationships
- דָּם נָקִי (*dam naqi*) - "innocent blood"—blood of guiltless persons
- אַל־תִּשְׁפְּכוּ (*'al-tishpekhu*) - "do not shed"—prohibition against bloodshed

**Christological Connection**: Jeremiah indicts Judah for shedding innocent blood while maintaining temple worship—exactly Cain's pattern of religious pretense combined with murder. The religious leaders who condemned Jesus epitomized this: meticulous about ritual purity while plotting innocent blood. Judas confessed he "betrayed innocent blood" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 4|Matthew 27:4]]); Pilate washed hands of "this innocent blood" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 24|Matthew 27:24]]). The trajectory: Abel's innocent blood (shed by his worshipping brother) → Israel's innocent blood (shed while trusting temple) → Christ's innocent blood (shed by religious leaders at Passover). Yet Christ's innocent blood accomplishes what all others could not—forgiveness for those who shed it.
