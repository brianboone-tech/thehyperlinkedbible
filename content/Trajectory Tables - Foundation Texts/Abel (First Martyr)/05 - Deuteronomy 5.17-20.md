### [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 5#Deuteronomy 5 . 17|Deuteronomy 5:17-20]]

**Context**: Deuteronomy restates the Decalogue for the new generation entering Canaan. The sixth commandment "You shall not murder" (v.17) appears with prohibitions against adultery, theft, and false witness (v.18-20). Jeremiah's temple sermon (7:5-6, 9) indicts Judah for violating these very commands—including murder and false witness—while presuming on temple worship. This combination echoes Cain: religious pretense while harboring murderous intent, and lying to cover guilt.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- לֹא תִרְצָח (*lo tirtsach*) - "you shall not murder" (v.17)
- לֹא תִנְאָף (*lo tin'aph*) - "you shall not commit adultery" (v.18)
- לֹא תִגְנֹב (*lo tignov*) - "you shall not steal" (v.19)
- לֹא תַעֲנֶה... עֵד שָׁוְא (*lo ta'aneh... 'ed shav'*) - "you shall not bear false witness" (v.20)

**Christological Connection**: The combination of murder and false witness in Deuteronomy connects to both Cain and Christ's trial. Cain murdered, then bore false witness ("I do not know"). Jesus was convicted through false witnesses ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 59|Matthew 26:59-60]]), then murdered. The religious leaders who condemned Jesus violated the very commands they taught—murder through false witness. Christ fulfilled these commands perfectly and bore their penalty for violators. The trajectory: Cain's murder/lying → law prohibits both → Jesus murdered through false witness → Jesus' blood covers both sins.
