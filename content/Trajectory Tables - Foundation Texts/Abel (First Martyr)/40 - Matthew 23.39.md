### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 39|Matthew 23:39]]

**Context**: Jesus concludes His woes against the religious leaders: "For I tell you, you will not see me again, until you say, 'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord'" (quoting Psalm 118:26). This immediately follows the climactic Abel reference (v.35): "upon you may come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah." Jesus identifies Himself as the culmination of the persecuted-righteous pattern and prophesies Jerusalem's desolation until future recognition.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- οὐ μή με ἴδητε (*ou mē me idēte*) - "you will certainly not see me"—emphatic departure
- ἀπ' ἄρτι (*ap' arti*) - "from now on"—beginning of new era
- ἕως ἂν εἴπητε (*heōs an eipēte*) - "until you say"—condition for future seeing
- εὐλογημένος ὁ ἐρχόμενος (*eulogēmenos ho erchomenos*) - "blessed is the one who comes"—Psalm 118:26 quotation

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 39|Matthew 23:39]] is the turning point in the Abel trajectory. Jesus declares Jerusalem will experience desolation for rejecting and killing Him (culminating the Abel-to-Zechariah pattern), but also anticipates future restoration when Israel welcomes Him. The trajectory does not end in perpetual judgment but in eschatological hope. Where Abel's blood cried only for vengeance, Christ's blood enables the very persecutors to repent and welcome Him. Acts 2 shows this beginning on Pentecost: "this Jesus whom you crucified" is now Lord and Christ—3,000 from that generation repented. The pattern transforms from endless bloodguilt to offered forgiveness.
