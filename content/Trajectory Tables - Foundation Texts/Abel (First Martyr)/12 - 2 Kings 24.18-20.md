### [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 24#2 Kings 24 . 18|2 Kings 24:18-20]]

**Context**: Zedekiah's reign and rebellion against Babylon leads to Jerusalem's destruction. The parallel in 2 Chronicles 36:11-17 adds crucial detail: God sent prophets repeatedly whom the people mocked and despised "until the wrath of the LORD rose against his people, until there was no remedy" (v.16). This pattern of persecuting God's messengers connects to the Abel trajectory—righteous prophets murdered by covenant people, accumulating bloodguilt until judgment falls.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וַיַּעַשׂ הָרַע (*vayya'as hara'*) - "and he did evil"—covenant unfaithfulness
- וַיִּמְרֹד (*vayyimrod*) - "and he rebelled"—Zedekiah's treachery against Babylon
- אַף־יְהוָה (*aph-YHWH*) - "anger of the LORD"—divine wrath at accumulated guilt

**Christological Connection**: Jerusalem's fall in 586 BC resulted from accumulated bloodguilt—generations of persecuting prophets until "no remedy" remained. Jesus identifies this pattern in [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 35|Matthew 23:35-37]]: "upon you will come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from Abel to Zechariah." The generation that rejected Christ received this accumulated judgment (AD 70). The trajectory: Abel's blood (first martyr) → prophets' blood (persecuted through centuries) → Christ's blood (culminating murder) → judgment on Jerusalem (within one generation). Yet Christ's blood also speaks mercy—the same generation that shed His blood could repent ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 36|Acts 2:36-41]]).
