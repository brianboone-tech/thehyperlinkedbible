### [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 36#2 Chronicles 36 . 11|2 Chronicles 36:11-17]]

**Context**: Chronicles' account of Jerusalem's fall emphasizes the prophetic dimension: "The LORD, the God of their fathers, sent persistently to them by his messengers... but they kept mocking the messengers of God, despising his words and scoffing at his prophets, until the wrath of the LORD rose against his people, until there was no remedy" (v.15-16). This pattern of persecuting righteous messengers connects directly to Jesus' citation of "all righteous blood from Abel to Zechariah" (Matthew 23:35).

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- מַשְׁכֵּם וְשָׁלוֹחַ (*mashkem veshalowach*) - "rising early and sending"—God's persistent warning
- מַלְאָכָיו (*mal'akhayw*) - "his messengers"—prophets as God's representatives
- מַלְעִבִים (*mal'ibim*) - "mocking"—contemptuous treatment of prophets
- עַד־אֵין מַרְפֵּא (*'ad-'eyn marpe'*) - "until there was no remedy"—point of no return

**Christological Connection**: This text shows the climax of the persecuted-prophet pattern Abel inaugurated. God sent messengers; they were mocked and killed; judgment fell. Jesus places Himself as the culmination: after sending prophets whom they killed, God sent His Son—and they killed Him too ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 33|Matthew 21:33-39]]). Stephen explicitly charges his accusers with this pattern ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 52|Acts 7:52]]). The trajectory: Abel (first righteous martyr) → prophets (centuries of persecution) → Christ (final messenger, Son of God) → judgment (AD 70). Yet the pattern transforms: where prophet blood brought judgment, Christ's blood offers forgiveness to the very generation that shed it.
