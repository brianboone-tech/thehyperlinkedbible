### [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 28#Job 28 . 1|Job 28:1]]

**Context**: Job 28 begins a wisdom poem: "Surely there is a mine for silver, and a place for gold that they refine." The chapter contrasts human ability to extract precious metals from the earth with inability to find wisdom—only God knows wisdom's place. The connection to Luke 11:49-51 (which mentions Abel) is not immediately clear and may indicate a textual/thematic link requiring further investigation. The broader context of Job as righteous sufferer connects to Abel trajectory.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- מוֹצָא (*motsa'*) - "source/mine"—place of origin
- כֶּסֶף (*keseph*) - "silver"—precious metal from earth
- זָהָב (*zahav*) - "gold"—valuable treasure
- יְזֹקְקוּ (*yezokkequ*) - "they refine"—purification process

**Connections**:

  - [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 16#Job 16 . 18|Job 16:18]] - "O earth, cover not my blood"
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 8#Proverbs 8 . 10|Proverbs 8:10-11]] - wisdom more precious than silver/gold
  - [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 7#Ecclesiastes 7 . 23|Ecclesiastes 7:23-24]] - wisdom far off
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 11#Luke 11 . 49|Luke 11:49-51]] - wisdom of God will send prophets—from Abel to Zechariah
  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 30|1 Corinthians 1:30]] - Christ our wisdom

**Christological Connection**: While the direct connection to Abel is unclear, Job 28's theme connects: humanity searches the earth for treasure but cannot find wisdom. Job himself is a type of righteous sufferer like Abel. The link to [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 11#Luke 11 . 49|Luke 11:49]] may be through the "Wisdom of God" who sends prophets from Abel onward—God's wisdom in redemptive history involves the suffering of the righteous, culminating in Christ who "became to us wisdom from God" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 30|1 Corinthians 1:30]]). The pattern of righteous suffering that begins with Abel finds its purpose in Christ, who is both the wisdom of God and the ultimate righteous sufferer.
