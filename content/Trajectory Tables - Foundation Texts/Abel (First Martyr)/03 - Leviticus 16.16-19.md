### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 16|Leviticus 16:16-19]]

**Context**: The Day of Atonement ritual describes blood purifying the sanctuary from Israel's uncleanness and transgressions. The high priest sprinkles blood on the mercy seat and altar to cleanse them from defilement accumulated through Israel's sins. Where Abel's blood polluted the ground (Genesis 4:10-11), Levitical blood purifies sacred space. Hebrews 9:23 cites this purification to show Christ's blood cleanses the heavenly sanctuary itself.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וְכִפֶּר (*vekhipper*) - "and he shall make atonement"—blood covers/purifies
- טֻמְאֹת (*tum'ot*) - "uncleannesses"—ritual and moral defilement
- פְּשָׁעֵיהֶם (*pish'ehem*) - "their transgressions"—willful rebellion
- מִזְבֵּחַ (*mizbeach*) - "altar"—place requiring purification

**Christological Connection**: The Day of Atonement reverses what happened with Abel. Abel's innocent blood polluted the ground; Levitical blood purifies sacred space. But this annual repetition showed its inadequacy—true cleansing required better blood. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 23|Hebrews 9:23]] explains: "the heavenly things themselves" required purification with "better sacrifices" than animal blood. Christ's blood accomplishes what Levitical blood symbolized: permanent purification not just of earthly sanctuary but of heaven itself, and not just of sacred space but of human conscience ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]). The trajectory: polluting blood (Abel) → purifying blood (Leviticus) → perfecting blood (Christ).
