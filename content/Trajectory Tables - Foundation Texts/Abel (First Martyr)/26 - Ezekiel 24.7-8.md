### [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 24#Ezekiel 24 . 7|Ezekiel 24:7-8]]

**Context**: God condemns Jerusalem's bloodguilt: "For the blood she has shed is in her midst; she set it on bare rock; she did not pour it out on the ground to cover it with dust. To rouse my wrath, to take vengeance, I have set her blood on the bare rock, that it may not be covered." This directly develops Abel's blood theology: exposed, uncovered blood cries for vengeance. Jerusalem's brazen exposure of innocent blood—contrary to Levitical protocol (Leviticus 17:13)—provokes divine wrath.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- דָּמָהּ (*damah*) - "her blood"—blood Jerusalem has shed
- צְחִיחַ סֶלַע (*tsechichach sela'*) - "bare rock"—exposed, uncovered surface
- לֹא שְׁפָכַתְהוּ עַל־הָאָרֶץ (*lo shephakhhathu 'al-ha'arets*) - "she did not pour it on the ground"—violation of Leviticus 17:13
- לְכַסּוֹת עָלָיו עָפָר (*lekhassot 'alayw 'aphar*) - "to cover it with dust"—proper blood disposal

**Connections**:
- **TO**:

  - [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 17#Leviticus 17 . 13|Leviticus 17:13]] - blood must be covered with earth
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 16#Job 16 . 18|Job 16:18]] - "O earth, cover not my blood"
  - [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 35#Numbers 35 . 33|Numbers 35:33]] - blood pollutes land, only atonement cleanses
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]] - sprinkled blood speaking better—properly applied blood speaks mercy

**Christological Connection**: Ezekiel presents Jerusalem as worse than Cain: at least Cain didn't deliberately expose Abel's blood on bare rock to provoke God. Jerusalem brazenly displayed innocent blood as if daring God to act. This deliberate exposure contrasts with Christ's blood "sprinkled" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]])—properly applied in the heavenly sanctuary. The trajectory: Abel's blood (exposed through murder, cries vengeance) → Jerusalem's blood (deliberately exposed, provokes wrath) → Christ's blood (properly sprinkled, speaks mercy). Where exposed blood cries for judgment, sprinkled blood provides forgiveness.
