### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 16|Exodus 19:16-22]]

**Context**: The Sinai theophany establishes the terrifying nature of God's holy presence under the old covenant. Thunder, lightning, thick cloud, and trumpet blast accompanied God's descent. The people trembled; even Moses said "I am terrified and trembling" (Hebrews 12:21). Boundaries were set—touching the mountain meant death. This sets up the Abel trajectory contrast in Hebrews 12:18-24: believers have not come to this terrifying mountain but to Mount Zion, where Christ's blood speaks better than Abel's.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- קֹלֹת (*qolot*) - "thunders/voices" (v.16)—divine manifestation
- בְּרָקִים (*beraqim*) - "lightnings" (v.16)—visible judgment
- עָנָן כָּבֵד (*anan kabed*) - "thick cloud" (v.16)—God's concealed presence
- קֹל שֹׁפָר (*qol shophar*) - "sound of trumpet" (v.16)—covenant announcement

**Christological Connection**: The Sinai theophany represents the old covenant's terrifying inaccessibility—even Moses feared. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 18|Hebrews 12:18-24]] uses this as contrast to introduce the Abel trajectory's climax: believers approach not Sinai's terror but Zion's grace, specifically "sprinkled blood that speaks better than Abel's." Under the old covenant, God's holiness produced fear and distance. Under the new covenant, Christ's blood provides access. Where Sinai said "stay back or die," Christ's blood says "draw near with confidence." The trajectory moves from Abel's blood (crying vengeance) through Sinai's terror (death for approach) to Christ's blood (speaking welcome).
