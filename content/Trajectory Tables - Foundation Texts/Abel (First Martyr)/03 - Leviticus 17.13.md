### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 17#Leviticus 17 . 13|Leviticus 17:13]]

**Context**: When hunting game, Israelites must pour out the blood and cover it with earth. This law develops Abel's blood theology: Abel's blood cried from the ground because it was not properly handled—exposed blood demands justice. The requirement to cover blood with earth shows respect for blood's sacred nature (life is in the blood, v.11). Ezekiel 24:7-8 condemns Jerusalem for deliberately leaving blood exposed on bare rock, violating this principle.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וְשָׁפַךְ (*vesaphakh*) - "and he shall pour out"—proper blood disposal
- וְכִסָּהוּ בֶּעָפָר (*vekhissahu be'aphar*) - "and cover it with earth"—respectful concealment
- הַדָּם הוּא הַנֶּפֶשׁ (*haddam hu hannephesh*) - "the blood is the life" (v.14)—blood's sacred significance

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 10|Genesis 4:10-11]] - Abel's blood cried from ground—uncovered, demanding justice
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 24#Ezekiel 24 . 7|Ezekiel 24:7-8]] - Jerusalem's blood on bare rock as provocation
  - [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 16#Job 16 . 18|Job 16:18]] - earth, cover not my blood
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]] - sprinkled blood speaking better than Abel

**Christological Connection**: This blood-covering law shows how blood should be handled—with reverence, properly disposed of. Abel's blood was left exposed (not his choice—he was murdered), crying for vengeance. Ezekiel condemns Jerusalem for intentionally exposing blood—brazen disregard provoking God's wrath. Christ's blood, by contrast, was "sprinkled" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]])—properly applied in the heavenly sanctuary. Where exposed blood cries vengeance and covered blood shows respect, sprinkled blood accomplishes atonement. The trajectory: Abel's exposed blood (crying vengeance) → Levitical covered blood (showing respect) → Christ's sprinkled blood (speaking mercy).
