### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 1|Jeremiah 7:1-2]]

**Context**: The introduction to Jeremiah's temple sermon: "Stand in the gate of the LORD's house, and proclaim there this word." This sermon (ch. 7) indicts Judah for covenant violations including shedding innocent blood (v.6) while trusting in the temple for protection. Chapter 26 reveals that this sermon nearly cost Jeremiah his life—the religious leaders demanded his death. This exemplifies the Abel pattern: the righteous prophet who condemns innocent bloodshed is himself targeted for murder.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- שַׁעַר בֵּית־יְהוָה (*sha'ar bet-YHWH*) - "gate of the LORD's house"—public temple entrance
- קְרָא שָׁם (*qera' sham*) - "proclaim there"—prophetic confrontation
- הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה (*haddavar hazzeh*) - "this word"—divine oracle

**Christological Connection**: Jeremiah's temple sermon confronted religious leaders with their bloodguilt—and they responded by attempting murder. This exemplifies the pattern from Abel: confronting wickedness results in the wicked attacking the righteous messenger. Jesus delivered His own "temple sermon" (cleansing, teaching) and was killed by religious leaders. The trajectory: Abel (righteous murdered for righteousness) → prophets like Jeremiah (nearly murdered for confronting bloodguilt) → Christ (murdered by religious leaders He confronted). Jeremiah escaped; Christ did not—but His death was not defeat. His blood speaks better.
