### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 26#Jeremiah 26 . 1|Jeremiah 26:1-2]]

**Context**: The narrative account of Jeremiah's temple sermon (the sermon itself is in chapter 7). "Stand in the court of the LORD's house, and speak to all the cities of Judah... all the words that I command you; do not hold back a word." This chapter reveals what chapter 7 doesn't: this sermon provoked murderous rage from priests and prophets who demanded Jeremiah's death (v.11). Jeremiah exemplifies the pattern Abel established—the righteous messenger endangered for speaking truth.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- בַּחֲצַר בֵּית־יְהוָה (*bachatsar bet-YHWH*) - "in the court of the LORD's house"—public temple area
- אַל־תִּגְרַע דָּבָר (*'al-tigra' davar*) - "do not hold back a word"—complete faithful proclamation
- כָּל־עָרֵי יְהוּדָה (*kol-'are Yehudah*) - "all the cities of Judah"—comprehensive audience

**Christological Connection**: Jeremiah 26 shows the prophet endangered for the same sermon that condemned innocent bloodshed (ch. 7). The irony is profound: confronting people about shedding innocent blood results in them attempting to shed the prophet's innocent blood. This is the Abel pattern: righteousness provokes murderous hatred. Jeremiah escaped (v.24); Jesus did not. Yet Jesus' death was not defeat—it was the culmination of the pattern and its transformation. Where prophets' deaths accumulated judgment, Christ's death provided atonement. The trajectory: Abel (martyred for righteousness) → prophets (endangered/killed for truth) → Christ (killed—blood speaks mercy).
