### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 20#Exodus 20 . 13|Exodus 20:13]]

**Context**: The sixth commandment "You shall not murder" (לֹא תִרְצָח) formalizes God's prohibition against taking human life, rooted in humanity's creation in God's image (Genesis 9:6). This command responds to the Cain-Abel narrative—God's question "What have you done?" (Genesis 4:10) becomes codified law. The prohibition protects innocent blood and establishes that murderers violate covenant. Prophets like Jeremiah (7:5-6, 9) indict Israel for violating this command while maintaining religious pretense.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- לֹא תִרְצָח (*lo tirtsach*) - "you shall not murder"—deliberate taking of human life
- רָצַח (*ratsach*) - "murder" (distinct from killing in war or capital punishment)

**Christological Connection**: The sixth commandment formally prohibits what Cain did to Abel. Yet the command itself cannot prevent murder or atone for it. The religious leaders who condemned Jesus were expert in this commandment yet committed the ultimate murder—killing the righteous Son of God. Jesus intensified the command, showing that anger constitutes heart-murder ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 21|Matthew 5:21-22]]). Christ fulfilled the law's demand perfectly (never murdering in act or heart) and bore the penalty for all who violated it. The trajectory: Abel murdered → law prohibits murder → law cannot prevent murder → Christ murdered by law-keepers → Christ's blood atones for murderers.
