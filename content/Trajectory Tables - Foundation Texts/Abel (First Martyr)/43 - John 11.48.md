### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 48|John 11:48]]

**Context**: After Jesus raises Lazarus, the religious leaders convene: "If we let him go on like this, everyone will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation." Their fear of losing "our place" (temple) echoes Jeremiah 7:14's threat. The tragic irony: they decided to kill Jesus to save the temple (v.50, 53), but this murder of innocent blood ensured the very destruction they feared (AD 70). They repeated Jerusalem's ancient pattern—shedding innocent blood while trusting the temple.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀφῶμεν αὐτὸν οὕτως (*aphōmen auton houtōs*) - "let him go on like this"—allow Jesus to continue
- πάντες πιστεύσουσιν (*pantes pisteusousin*) - "everyone will believe"—fear of widespread faith
- τὸν τόπον ἡμῶν (*ton topon hēmōn*) - "our place"—the temple (same term as Jeremiah 7:14 LXX)
- τὸ ἔθνος (*to ethnos*) - "the nation"—national existence under Rome

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 48|John 11:48]] shows religious leaders deciding to shed innocent blood to preserve the temple—precisely what Jeremiah 7 condemned. They became the culmination of the pattern from Cain: offering religious worship while committing murder. Their strategy was self-defeating: the innocent blood they shed brought the judgment they feared. The temple was destroyed in AD 70, one generation later ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 36|Matthew 23:36]]). The trajectory: Cain (murdered Abel to protect his standing) → religious leaders (murdered Jesus to protect temple) → temple destroyed anyway. Yet Christ's blood opened a better temple—access to God's presence through His flesh ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-20]]).
