### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 9|Jeremiah 7:9]]

**Context**: Jeremiah's rhetorical indictment: "Will you steal, murder, commit adultery, swear falsely, burn incense to Baal, and go after other gods that you have not known...?" This echoes Hosea 4:2 and the Decalogue (Exodus 20:13-15). The people violate multiple commandments—including murder—then enter the temple presuming God's protection. This mirrors Cain: religious activity while guilty of innocent blood. Murder among covenant violations shows bloodshed as symptom of comprehensive apostasy.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- הֲגָנֹב (*haganov*) - "will you steal?"—rhetorical question on Decalogue violation
- רָצֹחַ (*ratsoach*) - "murder"—deliberate killing
- נָאֹף (*na'oph*) - "commit adultery"—sexual covenant violation
- הִשָּׁבֵעַ לַשֶּׁקֶר (*hishave' lashsheqer*) - "swear falsely"—lying under oath

**Christological Connection**: Jeremiah exposes the hypocrisy of combining covenant violations with religious observance. Murder appears among comprehensive sins—then people enter God's house expecting protection. This is Cain's pattern: worship while harboring murderous guilt. The religious leaders who killed Jesus exemplified this: scrupulous about Sabbath, tithes, and purity laws while plotting murder. Jesus repeatedly exposed this disconnect (Matthew 23). The trajectory: Cain (murderer at worship) → Israel (murderers at temple) → religious leaders (murderers at Passover) → Christ's blood exposes and forgives hypocrisy.
