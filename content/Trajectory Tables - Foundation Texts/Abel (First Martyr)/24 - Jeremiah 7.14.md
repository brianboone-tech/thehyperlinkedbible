### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 14|Jeremiah 7:14]]

**Context**: God's judgment declaration: "I will do to the house that is called by my name, and in which you trust, and to the place that I gave to you and to your fathers, as I did to Shiloh." The temple that Israel trusted for protection while shedding innocent blood would be destroyed like Shiloh. John 11:48 echoes this fear: religious leaders worry Romans will destroy "our place" (temple) if Jesus' movement grows. Ironically, their murder of Jesus to save the temple ensured its destruction.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- הַבַּיִת (*habbayit*) - "the house"—the temple
- אֲשֶׁר־קֻרָא שְׁמִי עָלָיו (*'asher-qura' shemi 'alayw*) - "which is called by my name"—temple's divine association
- שִׁלוֹ (*Shilo*) - "Shiloh"—previous sanctuary God destroyed (see 1 Samuel 4)
- בֹּטְחִים (*botechim*) - "trusting"—misplaced confidence

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 14|Jeremiah 7:14]] shows God destroys His own sanctuary when His people shed innocent blood while trusting that sanctuary. [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 48|John 11:48]] shows religious leaders fearing this very thing—so they shed innocent blood (Jesus) to prevent it. Their strategy backfired completely: the innocent blood they shed ensured the destruction they feared (AD 70). The trajectory: Shiloh destroyed (Israel's sin) → Jerusalem threatened (Jeremiah 7) → Jerusalem destroyed 586 BC (innocent blood accumulated) → Jerusalem destroyed AD 70 (Christ's blood). Yet Christ's blood also opened access to God without physical temple ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-20]]).
