### [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 24#2 Kings 24 . 4|2 Kings 24:4]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1801-1900#H1818|H1818]] דָּם (dām) - blood
- [[Lexicon/H5001-5100#H5355|H5355]] נָקִי (nāqî) - innocent, guiltless, clean
- [[Lexicon/H8001-8100#H8210|H8210]] שָׁפַךְ (šāpaḵ) - to pour out, shed (blood)

**Context**: Explaining Judah's exile, the author states: "Including the innocent blood he [Manasseh] had shed, for he had filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the LORD was unwilling to forgive." Manasseh's shedding of innocent blood became unpardonable sin requiring national judgment.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 19#Deuteronomy 19 . 10|Deuteronomy 19:10]], 13 - "So innocent blood will not be shed in your land... You must purge from Israel the guilt of shedding innocent blood"
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 7#Jeremiah 7 . 6|Jeremiah 7:6]]; 22:3, 17 - Repeated prophetic warnings: "Do not shed innocent blood in this place"
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 26#Jeremiah 26 . 15|Jeremiah 26:15]] - "If you put me to death, you will bring the guilt of innocent blood on yourselves"
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 22#Ezekiel 22 . 3|Ezekiel 22:3-4]] - Jerusalem's bloodshed brings judgment: "You are guilty of the blood you have shed"
- [[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 3#Joel 3 . 19|Joel 3:19]] - Egypt and Edom desolate "because of violence done to Judah, in whose land they shed innocent blood"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 10|Genesis 4:10]] - Abel's innocent blood cries from ground (establishes pattern: innocent blood demands justice)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 35|Matthew 23:35-36]] - "All the righteous blood shed on earth... will come upon this generation"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 24|Matthew 27:24-25]] - Pilate: "I am innocent of this man's blood"; Crowd: "His blood be on us and on our children"

**Type Classification**: **Providential** (historical judgment) / **Forward-Looking** (prophets use bloodshed as warning of coming judgment)

**Christological Connection**: The pattern of innocent blood crying for vengeance reaches its climax when Israel sheds Christ's innocent blood. Where Manasseh's shedding of innocent blood brought exile despite Josiah's reforms, Israel's shedding of Messiah's blood brings the ultimate judgment (AD 70). Yet here is the profound reversal: Christ's innocent blood, unlike Abel's and the prophets', does not finally cry for vengeance but speaks forgiveness ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 23#Luke 23 . 34|Luke 23:34]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]]). The blood that should have brought irrevocable judgment instead offers irrevocable mercy. God was "unwilling to forgive" innocent blood under the old covenant; God is supremely willing to forgive through Christ's innocent blood under the new covenant.
