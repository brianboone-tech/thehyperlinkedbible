### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]]

**Context**: The Day of Atonement sin offering was burned "outside the camp"—its blood brought into the holy place, but its body disposed of in exclusion. This "outside" location becomes significant for the Abel trajectory: Abel was murdered "in the field" (outside domestic space), Levitical bodies burned outside the camp, and Christ suffered "outside the gate" of Jerusalem (Hebrews 13:11-12). The excluded location paradoxically becomes the locus of atonement.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- מִחוּץ לַמַּחֲנֶה (*michuts lamachaneh*) - "outside the camp"—place of exclusion/uncleanness
- יִשְׂרְפוּ (*yisrephu*) - "they shall burn"—complete destruction
- חַטָּאת (*chattat*) - "sin offering"—sacrifice bearing sin's penalty

**Christological Connection**: The "outside the camp" location unites Abel's murder, Levitical sacrifice, and Christ's crucifixion. Abel died in the field (excluded space); the Day of Atonement offering was burned outside the camp (excluded space); Christ suffered outside Jerusalem's gate (excluded space). [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11-12]] explicitly connects these: "the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest... are burned outside the camp. So Jesus also suffered outside the gate." The excluded location—where sin and death belong—becomes precisely where atonement occurs. Christ bears sin's exclusion to bring sinners inside God's presence.
