**Context**: On the night of Israel's final deliverance from Egypt, God institutes the Passover—the foundational redemptive event of the Old Testament. Each household must take an unblemished lamb, slaughter it at twilight, apply its blood to the doorposts and lintel, and eat its flesh. When the LORD passes through Egypt striking down every firstborn, He will "pass over" houses marked with blood, sparing those within. This is to be observed annually as perpetual memorial.

**Christological Connection**: The Passover lamb God instituted in Exodus 12 is the most explicit OT type of Christ's atoning sacrifice. Every detail points to Christ with stunning precision. The lamb must be "without blemish" (*tāmîm*, v. 5), anticipating Christ's sinless perfection—Peter explicitly calls him "a lamb without blemish or spot" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]). The lamb's slaughter "at twilight" on the 14th of Nisan prefigures the exact day and hour Christ died centuries later ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]]: "It was the day of Preparation of the Passover"). The blood applied to doorposts protected from judgment; Christ's blood shields believers from God's wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]: "saved by him from the wrath of God"). As the LORD "passed over" houses marked with blood, sparing the firstborn, God's judgment passes over those covered by Christ's blood—"there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]). The lamb's flesh eaten for strength on the journey foreshadows Christ as spiritual food: "unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man... you have no life in you" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 53|John 6:53]]). Paul makes the typology explicit: "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]])—the fulfillment of what Passover always foreshadowed. As Israel's Passover deliverance from Egypt led to Sinai covenant, Christ's Passover death establishes the new covenant "in my blood" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 20|Luke 22:20]]). The annual memorial ("throughout your generations," v. 14) pointed to the once-for-all sacrifice that "appears once for all... to put away sin" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]).
