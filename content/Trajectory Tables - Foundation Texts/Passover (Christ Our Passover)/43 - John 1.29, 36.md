### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29, 36]]

**Context**: John the Baptist's ministry centers on preparing the way for Messiah. When Jesus approaches, John makes his definitive declaration twice (verses 29, 36): "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." This proclamation occurs at the Jordan River where John baptizes, early in Jesus' public ministry, before any miracles or teaching. The Baptist's testimony identifies Jesus' essential mission—sacrificial atonement—from the outset. The second proclamation (v. 36) prompts two disciples to follow Jesus, initiating His disciple-gathering.

**Christological Connection**: John the Baptist's declaration "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" provides the NT's foundational identification of Jesus as the Passover lamb. This proclamation, occurring before Jesus performed any miracles or taught publicly, establishes His primary mission: sacrificial atonement. The title "Lamb of God" (*amnos tou theou*) deliberately evokes Passover imagery—for 1,400 years, Israel had slaughtered Passover lambs annually, commemorating deliverance from Egypt through blood. Now John announces that Jesus is the ultimate Passover lamb, God's own provision, who accomplishes not merely temporal deliverance but removal of sin. The phrase "takes away the sin of the world" expands Passover's scope from national to universal—Christ's sacrifice extends to all humanity, fulfilling the Abrahamic promise that through his seed "all nations shall be blessed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 18|Genesis 22:18]]). John synthesizes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] ("like a lamb that is led to the slaughter") with Exodus 12's Passover lamb, revealing that the Suffering Servant prophecy and Passover typology converge in Jesus. The Baptist's testimony shapes the entire Fourth Gospel—John structures Jesus' ministry around Passover festivals, climaxing with crucifixion precisely when Passover lambs are slaughtered ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]]: "It was the day of Preparation of the Passover, about the sixth hour"). The crucifixion fulfills Passover requirements with precision: Jesus is "without blemish" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]), no bone is broken ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 36|John 19:36]]), His blood provides protection from divine wrath. Paul makes explicit what John announced: "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]). The Baptist's double proclamation (verses 29, 36) emphasizes its centrality—this is not peripheral detail but core identity. When John says "Behold," he demands recognition of what God has provided: the final, perfect sacrifice who "by a single offering... has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 14|Hebrews 10:14]]). The lamb slain from the foundation of the world ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 13#Revelation 13 . 8|Revelation 13:8]]) now walks among His people, soon to accomplish the redemption toward which every Passover pointed.
