### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 9#Numbers 9 . 12|Numbers 9:12]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7604-7605#H7604|שָׁאַר]] (*šāʾar*) - "remain, be left over"
- [[Lexicon/H1242-1243#H1242|בֹּקֶר]] (*bōqer*) - "morning"
- [[Lexicon/H6106-6107#H6106|עֶצֶם]] (*ʿeṣem*) - "bone"
- [[Lexicon/H7665-7666#H7665|שָׁבַר]] (*šābar*) - "break, shatter"
- [[Lexicon/H2708-2709#H2708|חֻקָּה]] (*ḥuqqâ*) - "statute, ordinance"
- [[Lexicon/H6453-6454#H6453|פֶּסַח]] (*pesaḥ*) - "Passover"

**Context**: Numbers 9 addresses the "second Passover" provision for those ceremonially unclean or traveling during the regular Passover. God establishes that such individuals may observe Passover one month later, following all original regulations. Verse 12 reiterates key Passover requirements: nothing shall remain until morning, no bone shall be broken, and all statutes must be observed exactly as prescribed. These seemingly minor details carry profound typological significance, pointing to Christ's crucifixion.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 46|Exodus 12:46]] (original Passover bone-breaking prohibition), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 10|Exodus 12:10]] (nothing remain until morning)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 34#Psalm 34 . 20|Psalm 34:20]] (he keeps all his bones; not one is broken)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 33|John 19:33-36]] (did not break his legs; not a bone broken), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]] (Christ our Passover)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 9#Numbers 9 . 12|Numbers 9:12]]'s seemingly mundane regulation—"they shall not break any of its bones"—emerges as profound Christological prophecy in [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 33|John 19:33-36]]. On the cross, Roman soldiers broke the legs of the two crucified alongside Jesus to hasten death before Sabbath. Standard crucifixion practice, this bone-breaking (*crurifragium*) caused asphyxiation within minutes. When soldiers reached Jesus, they found Him already dead: "But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 33|John 19:33]]). John interprets this: "For these things took place that the Scripture might be fulfilled: 'Not one of his bones will be broken'" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 36|John 19:36]]). The timing is critical—Jesus died precisely when Passover lambs were being slaughtered in the temple, at the exact hour the Passover commemorated Israel's deliverance from Egypt. Jesus is "Christ, our Passover lamb" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]), and every detail of the ancient Passover finds fulfillment in Him. The unblemished lamb prefigured Christ's sinlessness ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]); the blood on doorposts prefigured blood that shields from wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]); the timing at twilight on Nisan 14 prefigured Christ's death hour. Now [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 9#Numbers 9 . 12|Numbers 9:12]]'s prohibition against breaking bones demonstrates God's meticulous planning—centuries before crucifixion was invented, God embedded this marker into Passover ritual. The soldiers' decision not to break Jesus' legs was not humanitarian mercy but divine providence, fulfilling Scripture with precision. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 34#Psalm 34 . 20|Psalm 34:20]], which David wrote about God's protection of the righteous ("He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken"), finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ, the perfectly righteous one whose bones God preserved. The unbroken bones signify the sacrifice's perfection and completeness—nothing compromised, nothing deficient, the whole Christ offered for our redemption.
