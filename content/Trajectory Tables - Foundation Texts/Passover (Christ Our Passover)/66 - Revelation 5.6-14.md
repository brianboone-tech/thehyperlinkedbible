**Context**: Revelation 5 depicts heavenly throne room worship. The vision begins with God holding a sealed scroll that no one in creation can open. John weeps until an elder announces: "the Lion of the tribe of Judah... has conquered." But when John looks, he sees not a lion but "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain." This paradoxical image—slaughtered lamb alive and triumphant—reveals Christ's exaltation through suffering. The Lamb takes the scroll, prompting universal worship as creatures sing of His worthiness to rule because He was slain and by His blood ransomed people for God.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6-14]] presents the climactic vision of Christ as "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain," consummating the Passover typology introduced in Exodus 12 and developed throughout Scripture. The elder announces "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" has conquered ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 9|Genesis 49:9]]'s Messianic prophecy), but John sees a Lamb—revealing that Messianic victory came through sacrificial death, not military conquest. This is the paradox of the gospel: Christ triumphed by being slain, conquered by surrendering, won by losing. The Lamb is "standing" (*hestēkos*)—risen from death—yet "as though it had been slain" (*hōs esphagmenon*), eternally bearing crucifixion marks. The resurrection didn't erase the cross; glorified Christ forever displays His wounds, testifying to redemption's cost. The Lamb's seven horns (complete power) and seven eyes (complete knowledge) reveal His divine attributes—this is no weak lamb but the omnipotent, omniscient God-Man. The worship song declares why the Lamb is "worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals": "for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation" (v. 9). Christ's sacrificial death—fulfilling Passover typology—earned Him authority over history and creation. As Passover lamb blood ransomed Israel's firstborn from death, Christ's blood ransomed a multinational people from sin and death. John the Baptist's proclamation "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]) finds cosmic fulfillment—the Lamb didn't merely save Israel but purchases people "from every tribe and language and people and nation," fulfilling Abraham's promise that through his seed "all nations shall be blessed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 18|Genesis 22:18]]). Paul's declaration "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]) reaches its destiny—the sacrificed Lamb now reigning, worshiped by all creation. The universal worship crescendos: four living creatures, twenty-four elders, myriads of angels, and finally "every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea" (v. 13) ascribe "blessing and honor and glory and might forever and ever" to the Lamb. What began as one household's lamb in Egypt, protecting one firstborn child, culminates in the cosmic Lamb whose blood ransoms multitudes and whose reign is eternal. Peter's description of Christ as "a lamb without blemish or spot" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]) echoes in Revelation's vision—the perfect sacrifice now enthroned. The slaughtered Passover lamb pointed all along to this consummation: the Lamb on the throne, "worthy... to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing" (v. 12), ruling eternally over the kingdom purchased with His blood.
