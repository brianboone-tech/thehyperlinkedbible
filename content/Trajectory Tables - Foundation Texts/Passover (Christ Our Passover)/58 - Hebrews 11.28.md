**Context**: Hebrews 11, the great "Faith Hall of Fame," catalogs OT believers whose trust in God's promises brought salvation. Verse 28 addresses Moses' Passover observance, framing Israel's foundational redemption as act of faith. Moses trusted God's promise that blood on doorposts would protect from the Destroyer striking Egypt's firstborn. This faith-emphasis reveals that from the beginning, Passover effectiveness depended not on ritual mechanics but on trusting God's provision.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 28|Hebrews 11:28]]'s presentation of Passover as faith-act reveals that from the beginning, redemption came through trusting God's blood-provision, not through ritual mechanics or human effort. Moses "by faith kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood, so that the Destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them"—this faith believed God's promise that applied blood would protect from judgment. The same pattern applies to Christ's Passover sacrifice: believers by faith appropriate His blood for deliverance from God's wrath. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 25|Romans 3:25]] explains that God "put forward [Christ] as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith"—faith and blood together accomplish salvation. Just as Moses' faith trusted that the lamb's blood would shield Israel's firstborn from the Destroyer, Christian faith trusts that Christ's blood shields believers from divine judgment: "Since, therefore, we have now been justified by his blood, much more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]). The "Destroyer" (*olothreuōn*) who struck Egypt's firstborn represents God's judgment against sin—holy wrath that must be satisfied. The blood on doorposts didn't magically protect but demonstrated trust in God's provision of substitute. Similarly, Christ's blood doesn't automatically save all humanity but delivers those who trust in its efficacy. The Passover principle—blood provides protection from judgment for those who apply it by faith—operates identically in the gospel. Paul declares "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]), and that sacrifice becomes effective through faith. Hebrews' emphasis on faith corrects potential misunderstanding: Passover blood wasn't effective because of ritual precision but because God promised protection, which Moses believed. The author's point to persecuted Christians: just as Moses' faith in blood-protection proved valid when judgment fell, your faith in Christ's blood will prove valid at final judgment. The original Passover demonstrated that "without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 22|Hebrews 9:22]]), and Moses' faith trusted this principle before theological explanation. Believers now trust Christ's superior blood that "purifies our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]), not through sight but through faith.
