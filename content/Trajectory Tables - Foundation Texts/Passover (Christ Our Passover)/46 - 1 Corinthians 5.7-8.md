**Context**: Paul addresses moral corruption in the Corinthian church—a member engaged in sexual immorality (sleeping with his father's wife) while the church boasted rather than mourned. After commanding church discipline (vv. 2-5), Paul uses Passover and Unleavened Bread typology to exhort holiness. Christ's sacrifice as Passover lamb creates new reality requiring moral purity. The Christian life is continuous Passover festival, demanding removal of sin's "leaven."

**Christological Connection**: Paul's declaration "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" provides the NT's most explicit Passover-Christ identification. What Exodus 12's lamb foreshadowed, Christ fulfilled definitively. The perfect tense "has been sacrificed" (*etythē*) emphasizes completed action with permanent results—Christ's once-for-all death accomplished what annual Passover lambs could never achieve: actual removal of sin. For 1,400 years, Israel slaughtered Passover lambs annually, commemorating deliverance from Egypt, but these sacrifices were "a reminder of sins every year" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 3|Hebrews 10:3]]), unable to "take away sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 4|Hebrews 10:4]]). Christ's sacrifice, by contrast, "offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]), accomplishing eternal redemption. The Passover typology operates comprehensively: the lamb must be unblemished ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5]])—Christ was "without blemish or spot" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]); the blood protected from judgment ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 13|Exodus 12:13]])—Christ's blood saves "from the wrath of God" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 9|Romans 5:9]]); the lamb was slain at twilight on Nisan 14 ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 6|Exodus 12:6]])—Christ died at that exact hour ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 14|John 19:14]]); not a bone was broken ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 46|Exodus 12:46]])—fulfilled literally at crucifixion ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 36|John 19:36]]). Paul's application transforms Passover observance: instead of annual one-night celebration, the Christian life is continuous Passover festival—"let us therefore celebrate the festival." This ongoing celebration requires moral purity, removing sin's "old leaven" to live in "sincerity and truth." The indicative-imperative pattern mirrors salvation structure: because Christ our Passover has been sacrificed (indicative), we must cleanse out the leaven (imperative). Believers are "a new lump, as you really are unleavened" (v. 7)—positionally pure through Christ's blood, commanded to practical purity. The Passover-Unleavened Bread sequence illustrates salvation's progression: Passover lamb's blood delivers from judgment; unleavened bread represents subsequent holy living. As Israel couldn't remain in Egypt after Passover but journeyed to promised land, believers cannot remain in sin after Christ's sacrifice but must pursue holiness. The feast Paul describes celebrates Christ's accomplished deliverance, characterized not by "malice and evil" but by "sincerity and truth"—authentic transformation that Passover prefigured now realized through "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]).
