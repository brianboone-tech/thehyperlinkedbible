**Context**: At the crucifixion, Jewish leaders request that crucified victims' legs be broken to hasten death before Sabbath. Roman soldiers break the legs of the two criminals crucified alongside Jesus, but finding Him already dead, they don't break His legs. Instead, one soldier pierces Jesus' side with a spear, releasing blood and water. John interprets these events as fulfilling two Scriptures: "Not one of his bones will be broken" (Exodus 12:46/Numbers 9:12/Psalm 34:20) and "They will look on him whom they have pierced" (Zechariah 12:10). This passage provides the climactic Passover typology fulfillment in John's Gospel.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 33|John 19:33-37]] provides the Gospel's climactic Passover typology fulfillment. The soldiers' decision not to break Jesus' legs—despite standard crucifixion practice—fulfills the Passover regulation given to Israel 1,400 years earlier: "they shall not break any of its bones" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 46|Exodus 12:46]]; [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 9#Numbers 9 . 12|Numbers 9:12]]). This detail, seemingly incidental in Exodus, emerges as divine marker identifying Christ as the true Passover lamb. Roman soldiers unknowingly fulfilled Scripture with precision, demonstrating God's sovereign orchestration of redemptive history. The timing is critical—Jesus died at the exact hour Passover lambs were being slaughtered in the temple, fulfilling John the Baptist's proclamation "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]). The unbroken bones signify the sacrifice's perfection and completeness—nothing compromised, nothing deficient, the whole Christ offered for redemption. Paul's declaration "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]) is validated by this precise fulfillment. The spear-piercing releases blood and water (v. 34), testifying to Christ's real death while pointing to the sacraments (baptism and Eucharist) that flow from His sacrifice. John's citation of [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 12#Zechariah 12 . 10|Zechariah 12:10]]—"They will look on him whom they have pierced"—adds prophetic dimension: those who pierced Him will one day recognize Him, when "all Israel will be saved" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 26|Romans 11:26]]). The piercing reveals both sin's brutality (we pierced Him) and grace's triumph (His wounds heal us, [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 5|Isaiah 53:5]]). Peter declares believers were redeemed "with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]), applying Passover's unblemished lamb requirement to Christ. Every detail of the ancient Passover—timing, unblemished lamb, blood protection, unbroken bones—finds fulfillment in Christ's crucifixion, demonstrating that from the beginning, Passover pointed beyond Egypt's deliverance to eternal deliverance through the Lamb slain before the foundation of the world ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 13#Revelation 13 . 8|Revelation 13:8]]).
