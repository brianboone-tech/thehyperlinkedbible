### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 15#Acts 15 . 1|Acts 15:1]]

**Context**: "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved." This teaching from Judean visitors sparked the Jerusalem Council. The issue was whether Gentile converts must receive the Abrahamic covenant sign (circumcision) and keep the Mosaic Law. The council's decision (v.19-20) affirmed justification by faith alone—the same way Abraham was justified (Genesis 15:6) before circumcision (Genesis 17).

**Greek Key Terms**:
- περιτμηθῆτε (*peritēthēte*) - "you be circumcised"—Abrahamic covenant sign
- τῷ ἔθει (*tō ethei*) - "according to the custom"—Mosaic regulation
- οὐ δύνασθε σωθῆναι (*ou dynasthe sōthēnai*) - "you cannot be saved"—salvation requirement claim

**Christological Connection**: The Jerusalem Council's decision is grounded in Abrahamic theology. Paul's argument (elaborated in Romans 4 and Galatians 3): Abraham was justified by faith ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]]) before circumcision (Genesis 17), proving circumcision is not necessary for justification. The council affirmed: God gave the Spirit to uncircumcised Gentiles ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 15#Acts 15 . 8|Acts 15:8]]), cleansing their hearts by faith (v.9). The trajectory: Abraham justified by faith → circumcision as sign (not means) of righteousness → Gentiles justified by faith without circumcision → spiritual circumcision in Christ ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 11|Colossians 2:11]]).
