### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 12|Hebrews 11:12]]

**Context**: "Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars of heaven and as many as the innumerable grains of sand by the seashore." This celebrates the result of Abraham and Sarah's faith: despite physical impossibility (Abraham "as good as dead," Sarah's barren womb), God produced innumerable descendants. This required resurrection faith—believing God could bring life from death.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀφ' ἑνός (*aph' henos*) - "from one"—single source
- νενεκρωμένου (*nenekrōmenou*) - "as good as dead"—physical impossibility
- καθὼς τὰ ἄστρα τοῦ οὐρανοῦ (*kathōs ta astra tou ouranou*) - "as the stars of heaven"—Genesis 15:5; 22:17
- ὡς ἡ ἄμμος (*hōs hē ammos*) - "as the sand"—Genesis 22:17

**Christological Connection**: Abraham's faith was resurrection faith—he believed God could produce life from his "dead" body and Sarah's dead womb. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 17|Romans 4:17]] makes this explicit: Abraham believed God "who gives life to the dead and calls into existence the things that do not exist." This is the same faith required for justification: believing in the God who raised Jesus from the dead ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 24|Romans 4:24]]). The trajectory: Abraham believes God for life from death → innumerable physical descendants (Israel) → Christ raised from death → innumerable spiritual descendants (church, [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]). The faith that justified Abraham is the faith that justifies us—resurrection faith.
