### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 2|Acts 7:2-53]]

**Context**: Stephen's defense before the Sanhedrin is a masterful rehearsal of salvation history, beginning with "The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia" (v.2). Stephen emphasizes that God called Abraham before the temple existed, that Abraham never possessed the land, yet believed God's promises. The speech demonstrates that rejection of God's messengers (including Jesus) follows the pattern of Israel's history, not true Abrahamic faith.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ὁ θεὸς τῆς δόξης (*ho theos tēs doxēs*) - "the God of glory"—divine initiative
- ὤφθη (*ōphthē*) - "appeared"—theophany to Abraham
- τῷ πατρὶ ἡμῶν Ἀβραάμ (*tō patri hēmōn Abraam*) - "to our father Abraham"—shared ancestor
- μετοικίσαι (*metoikisai*) - "to migrate"—Abraham left by faith

**Christological Connection**: Stephen structures Israel's history around Abraham to make a christological point: God's purposes have never been tied to physical temple or land. Abraham had no temple; Moses received revelation in the wilderness; Solomon knew God doesn't dwell in houses made with hands (v.48). The religious leaders who rejected Jesus (like their fathers who rejected Moses and the prophets) proved they were not Abraham's true children. The trajectory: Abraham believed God → Israel repeatedly rejected God's messengers → Sanhedrin rejected Jesus → true children of Abraham are those who receive God's Righteous One (v.52).
