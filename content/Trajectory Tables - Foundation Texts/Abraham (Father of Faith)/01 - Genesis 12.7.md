### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 7|Genesis 12:7]]

**Context**: "Then the LORD appeared to Abram and said, 'To your offspring I will give this land.'" This is the first explicit land promise, connecting seed and land. Abraham responds by building an altar—worship in the place of promise. Stephen quotes this verse (Acts 7:2-5) to show that God's promises preceded any temple or fixed location, emphasizing that salvation depends on God's promise, not human religious structures.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וַיֵּרָא יְהוָה (*vayyera' YHWH*) - "the LORD appeared"—divine theophany initiating revelation
- לְזַרְעֲךָ (*lezar'akha*) - "to your offspring/seed"—singular, pointing to Christ (Galatians 3:16)
- אֶתֵּן (*'etten*) - "I will give"—divine promise, unconditional grant
- הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת (*ha'arets hazzo't*) - "this land"—Canaan, typologically pointing to new creation

**Christological Connection**: The land promise to Abraham's "seed" finds its ultimate fulfillment not in physical Canaan but in Christ and the new creation. Paul expands the promise: Abraham was not merely heir of Canaan but "of the world" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 13|Romans 4:13]]). Hebrews explains that Abraham looked beyond Canaan to "the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 10|Hebrews 11:10]]). The trajectory: land promised to seed → partial fulfillment in Israel's conquest → Christ the seed inherits → believers as co-heirs inherit the new creation ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 17|Romans 8:17]]; Revelation 21-22).
