### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 18|Genesis 15:18]]

**Context**: God makes a formal covenant with Abraham through the ancient ceremony of cutting animals and passing between the pieces: "On that day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, 'To your offspring I give this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates.'" The boundaries define the promised land at its greatest extent. Notably, only God (as smoking fire pot and flaming torch, v.17) passes between the pieces—the covenant's fulfillment depends entirely on God's faithfulness.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- כָּרַת יְהוָה אֶת־אַבְרָם בְּרִית (*karat YHWH 'et-'Avram berit*) - "the LORD cut a covenant with Abram"
- מִנְּהַר מִצְרַיִם עַד־הַנָּהָר הַגָּדֹל (*minnehar Mitsrayim 'ad-hannahar haggadol*) - "from the river of Egypt to the great river"—boundaries
- לְזַרְעֲךָ נָתַתִּי (*lezar'akha natatti*) - "to your offspring I give"—present tense certainty of future fulfillment

**Christological Connection**: The covenant ceremony is striking: only God passes through the pieces. In ancient covenants, both parties passed through, invoking the fate of the animals on themselves if they broke covenant. Here God alone takes the oath—the covenant's fulfillment depends solely on divine faithfulness. This prefigures the new covenant: Christ alone fulfills the conditions, bearing the curse of covenant-breaking ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]) so that believers inherit the promises. The land boundaries find ultimate fulfillment not in physical geography but in the new creation where Christ's people inherit "the world" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 13|Romans 4:13]]).
