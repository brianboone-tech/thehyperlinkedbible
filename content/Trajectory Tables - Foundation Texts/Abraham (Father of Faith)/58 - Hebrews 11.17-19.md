### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 17|Hebrews 11:17-19]]

**Context**: "By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac, and he who had received the promises was in the act of offering up his only son, of whom it was said, 'Through Isaac shall your offspring be named.' He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back." The author interprets the Aqedah as the supreme test of faith, revealing Abraham's belief in resurrection.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- πειραζόμενος (*peirazomenos*) - "when he was tested"—divine testing
- τὸν μονογενῆ (*ton monogenē*) - "his only [son]"—same term as John 3:16
- ἐκ νεκρῶν ἐγείρειν δυνατὸς ὁ θεός (*ek nekrōn egeirein dynatos ho theos*) - "God was able to raise from the dead"
- ἐν παραβολῇ (*en parabolē*) - "figuratively/as a type"—typological return

**Christological Connection**: Hebrews' interpretation of the Aqedah reveals its typological significance. Abraham believed God's promise required Isaac's survival, so if Isaac died, God must raise him. Abraham received Isaac back "as a type" (ἐν παραβολῇ)—this foreshadowed Christ's actual death and resurrection. The escalation: Abraham was willing to offer; God actually offered. Isaac was spared from death; Christ died. Isaac returned "figuratively" from death; Christ rose literally from death. The Aqedah is the OT's most profound type of the Father offering the Son and receiving Him back through resurrection.
