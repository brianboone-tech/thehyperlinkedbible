### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 13#Genesis 13 . 15|Genesis 13:15]]

**Context**: After Lot chooses the well-watered Jordan plain, God reaffirms the land promise to Abraham: "for all the land that you see I will give to you and to your offspring forever." The promise is eternal ("forever") and tied to "offspring" (seed). Paul makes the exegetical point in Galatians 3:16 that this singular "seed" refers to Christ—not merely to physical descendants but to the one descendant through whom all promises find their "Yes."

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- כָּל־הָאָרֶץ (*kol-ha'arets*) - "all the land"—comprehensive promise
- לְךָ אֶתְּנֶנָּה וּלְזַרְעֲךָ (*lekha 'ettenennah ulezar'akha*) - "to you I will give it and to your seed"
- עַד־עוֹלָם (*'ad-'olam*) - "forever"—eternal duration of promise

**Christological Connection**: Paul's argument in [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 16|Galatians 3:16]] depends on the singular "offspring" (σπέρμα, sperma): "It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' who is Christ." This exegetical precision shows that the Abrahamic promises were always pointing to one specific descendant—Christ. The "forever" duration of the promise finds fulfillment not in Israel's temporary possession of Canaan but in Christ's eternal inheritance that believers share ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 11|Ephesians 1:11]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 4|1 Peter 1:4]]).
