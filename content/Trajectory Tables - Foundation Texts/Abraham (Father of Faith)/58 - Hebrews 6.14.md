### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 6#Hebrews 6 . 14|Hebrews 6:14]]

**Context**: "Saying, 'Surely I will bless you and multiply you.'" The author quotes God's post-Aqedah oath to Abraham (Genesis 22:17). The point is certainty: "When God made a promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself" (v.13). This oath provides believers with "strong encouragement" (v.18)—the same God who kept His oath to Abraham will keep His promises to us.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- εὐλογῶν εὐλογήσω σε (*eulogōn eulogēsō se*) - "surely I will bless you"—emphatic future blessing
- πληθύνων πληθυνῶ σε (*plēthynōn plēthynō se*) - "surely I will multiply you"—emphatic future multiplication
- ὤμοσεν καθ' ἑαυτοῦ (*ōmosen kath' heautou*) - "he swore by himself"—highest possible guarantee

**Christological Connection**: God's oath to Abraham after the Aqedah provides the basis for Christian assurance. If God swore by Himself—the highest possible guarantee—and kept His oath to Abraham (despite impossibilities), He will keep His promises to believers. The oath was made after Abraham demonstrated willingness to offer Isaac—a test of faith that prefigured God's actual offering of Christ. The trajectory: Abraham passes the test → God swears irrevocable oath → God keeps oath (innumerable descendants) → believers trust the same oath-keeping God → our hope is anchored in God's faithfulness ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 6#Hebrews 6 . 19|Hebrews 6:19]]).
