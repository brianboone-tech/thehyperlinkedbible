### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-21]]

**Context**: Isaac is born to Sarah in her old age, fulfilling God's impossible promise. When conflict arises between Ishmael and Isaac, Sarah demands Ishmael's expulsion: "Cast out this slave woman with her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not be heir with my son Isaac" (v.10). Though painful for Abraham, God confirms this: the covenant promise runs through Isaac, not Ishmael. Paul allegorizes this in Galatians 4:21-31: Ishmael represents flesh/law/slavery; Isaac represents promise/Spirit/freedom.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- פָּקַד יְהוָה (*paqad YHWH*) - "the LORD visited"—divine intervention fulfilling promise
- צְחֹק (*tsechoq*) - "laughter"—Isaac's name (יִצְחָק, Yitschaq) derives from this
- גָּרֵשׁ (*garesh*) - "cast out"—expulsion of slave woman and son
- לֹא יִירַשׁ (*lo' yirash*) - "shall not inherit"—inheritance through promise, not flesh

**Christological Connection**: The Ishmael-Isaac contrast establishes a fundamental biblical principle: the children of God are born not by human effort but by divine promise. Ishmael was born "according to the flesh" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 23|Galatians 4:23]]); Isaac was born "through promise." Paul applies this to the gospel: those who seek righteousness through law (flesh/works) are like Ishmael—they will not inherit. Those who receive righteousness through faith (promise/Spirit) are like Isaac—they are heirs. The trajectory: promise over flesh → Isaac inherits, not Ishmael → believers inherit through faith in Christ, not through works.
