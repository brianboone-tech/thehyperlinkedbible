### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 35|John 8:35]]

**Context**: Jesus tells Jewish opponents: "The slave does not remain in the house forever; the son remains forever." This alludes to Genesis 21:10 where Sarah says, "Cast out this slave woman with her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not be heir with my son Isaac." Jesus uses the Ishmael-Isaac typology to distinguish between those who are merely Abraham's physical descendants (slaves) and those who are truly free (sons through faith in Christ).

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ὁ δοῦλος (*ho doulos*) - "the slave"—representing Ishmael's status
- εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα (*eis ton aiōna*) - "forever"—permanent vs. temporary standing
- ὁ υἱός (*ho huios*) - "the son"—Isaac's status, applied to believers in Christ
- ἐλευθερώσῃ (*eleutherōsē*) - "set free" (v.36)—freedom through the Son

**Christological Connection**: Jesus applies the Ishmael-Isaac typology to spiritual reality. Physical descent from Abraham doesn't guarantee inheritance; only those born through promise (like Isaac) are true heirs. Paul develops this in [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 21|Galatians 4:21-31]]: Hagar represents the covenant of slavery (Sinai/law); Sarah represents the covenant of freedom (promise/Spirit). Those in Christ are Isaac's heirs—children of the free woman, born by promise. The trajectory: Ishmael (slave) cast out, Isaac (son) inherits → law produces slaves, promise produces sons → Christ sets free → believers are sons who remain forever.
