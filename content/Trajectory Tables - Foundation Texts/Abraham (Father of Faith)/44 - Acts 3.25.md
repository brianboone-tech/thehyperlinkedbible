### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 3#Acts 3 . 25|Acts 3:25]]

**Context**: Peter addresses the crowd after healing the lame man: "You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with your fathers, saying to Abraham, 'And in your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed.'" Peter explicitly quotes Genesis 12:3/22:18 and identifies Jesus as the fulfillment—"God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, to bless you" (v.26). This is apostolic interpretation: Jesus is Abraham's seed who brings blessing to all nations.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- τῆς διαθήκης (*tēs diathēkēs*) - "of the covenant"—Abrahamic covenant
- ἐν τῷ σπέρματί σου (*en tō spermati sou*) - "in your offspring/seed"—singular, pointing to Christ
- ἐνευλογηθήσονται (*eneulogēthēsontai*) - "shall be blessed"—passive, receiving blessing
- πᾶσαι αἱ πατριαὶ τῆς γῆς (*pasai hai patriai tēs gēs*) - "all the families of the earth"—universal scope

**Christological Connection**: Peter's sermon is explicit: the Abrahamic covenant promise of blessing to all nations finds fulfillment in Jesus. "God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, to bless you by turning every one of you from your wickedness" (v.26). The blessing Abraham was promised is now available through Jesus—not merely material prosperity but redemption from sin. The trajectory: God promises Abraham → promises confirmed to his seed → Jesus is that seed → Jesus risen and sent brings the blessing → first to Jews, then to all nations ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 16|Romans 1:16]]).
