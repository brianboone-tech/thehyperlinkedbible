### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 1|Genesis 15:1-7]]

**Context**: God appears to Abraham in a vision, promising to be his "shield" and "very great reward." When Abraham questions how promises can be fulfilled without an heir, God takes him outside, shows him the stars, and promises innumerable descendants. Verse 6 is foundational: "And he believed the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness." This is the first explicit statement of justification by faith in Scripture, quoted by Paul (Romans 4:3; Galatians 3:6) and James (James 2:23).

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וְהֶאֱמִן בַּיהוָה (*vehe'emin baYHWH*) - "and he believed in the LORD"—trust in God's promise
- וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ לּוֹ צְדָקָה (*vayyachshveha lo tsedaqah*) - "and He counted/credited it to him as righteousness"
- מָגֵן (*magen*) - "shield"—divine protection
- שְׂכָרְךָ (*sekharekha*) - "your reward"—God Himself is the reward

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]] is the cornerstone of the justification-by-faith doctrine. Paul argues extensively in Romans 4 and Galatians 3 that Abraham's faith—not works—was the basis for his righteous standing before God. This occurred before circumcision (Genesis 17) and before the Law (430 years later, [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 17|Galatians 3:17]]), proving that justification has always been by grace through faith. Abraham believed God's promise of a seed; believers believe God's promise fulfilled in Christ. The trajectory: Abraham believed promise → counted righteous → same pattern for all who believe in Christ ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 23|Romans 4:23-25]]).
