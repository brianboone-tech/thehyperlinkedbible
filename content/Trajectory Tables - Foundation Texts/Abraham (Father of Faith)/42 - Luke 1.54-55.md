### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 54|Luke 1:54-55]]

**Context**: Mary's Magnificat concludes: "He has helped his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy, as he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and to his offspring forever." Mary identifies Jesus' coming as the fulfillment of God's promises to Abraham. The phrase "to Abraham and to his offspring forever" echoes Genesis 17:7-8 and shows that Mary understood Jesus' birth as the climax of covenant history beginning with Abraham.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀντελάβετο (*antelabeto*) - "has helped"—came to the aid of
- μνησθῆναι ἐλέους (*mnēsthēnai eleous*) - "in remembrance of mercy"—covenantal remembrance
- πρὸς τοὺς πατέρας ἡμῶν (*pros tous pateras hēmōn*) - "to our fathers"—patriarchal promises
- τῷ Ἀβραὰμ καὶ τῷ σπέρματι αὐτοῦ (*tō Abraam kai tō spermati autou*) - "to Abraham and to his offspring"

**Christological Connection**: Mary's Magnificat explicitly identifies Jesus as the fulfillment of the Abrahamic promises. God "helped his servant Israel" by sending Jesus—the seed promised to Abraham. The phrase "to his offspring forever" points to the eternal nature of the fulfillment: Jesus is not merely one descendant among many but the seed in whom all promises find their "Yes" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 1#2 Corinthians 1 . 20|2 Corinthians 1:20]]). The trajectory: God promises Abraham → God remembers His mercy → God sends Jesus as the promised seed → believers are Abraham's offspring through faith in Christ ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 29|Galatians 3:29]]).
