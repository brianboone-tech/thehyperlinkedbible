### [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 6|Galatians 3:6]]

**Context**: Paul quotes Genesis 15:6: "Abraham 'believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness.'" This is the cornerstone of Paul's argument against the Judaizers who claimed Gentiles must be circumcised and keep the Law. Paul's point: Abraham was justified by faith—before circumcision (Genesis 17) and 430 years before the Law (Galatians 3:17). Therefore, justification is by faith alone, not by works of law.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἐπίστευσεν τῷ θεῷ (*episteusen tō theō*) - "believed God"—trust in God's promise
- ἐλογίσθη αὐτῷ (*elogisthē autō*) - "was counted/credited to him"—divine reckoning
- εἰς δικαιοσύνην (*eis dikaiosynēn*) - "as righteousness"—righteous status

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 6|Galatians 3:6]] establishes the pattern for all justification: faith in God's promise, not works of law. Abraham believed the promise of a seed ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 4|Genesis 15:4-5]]); God credited righteousness. Believers believe God's promise fulfilled in Christ; God credits righteousness. The object of faith differs (promise vs. fulfillment) but the pattern is identical: trust God's word, receive righteousness. The trajectory: Abraham believed promise → credited righteous → believers believe Christ → credited righteous. "Those who are of faith are blessed along with Abraham, the man of faith" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 9|Galatians 3:9]]).
