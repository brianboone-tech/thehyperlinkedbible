### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 13|Genesis 15:13-14]]

**Context**: God reveals to Abraham the future suffering and deliverance of his descendants: "Know for certain that your offspring will be sojourners in a land that is not theirs and will be servants there, and they will be afflicted for four hundred years. But I will bring judgment on the nation that they serve, and afterward they shall come out with great possessions." This covenant prediction includes both suffering and redemption, establishing the pattern: God's promises include affliction followed by deliverance.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- גֵּר יִהְיֶה זַרְעֲךָ (*ger yihyeh zar'akha*) - "your offspring will be sojourners"—aliens in a foreign land
- וַעֲבָדוּם וְעִנּוּ אֹתָם (*va'avadum ve'innu 'otam*) - "and they will serve them and they will afflict them"
- אַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת שָׁנָה (*'arba' me'ot shanah*) - "four hundred years"—specified duration
- וְאַחֲרֵי־כֵן יֵצְאוּ (*ve'acharey-khen yetse'u*) - "and afterward they shall come out"—promised deliverance

**Christological Connection**: God's covenant with Abraham included suffering before glory—a pattern that applies to Christ and believers. Israel's Egyptian bondage and Exodus deliverance typify humanity's bondage to sin and deliverance through Christ. Stephen quotes this passage ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 6|Acts 7:6-7]]) in his speech showing that God's redemptive plan always included suffering followed by salvation. The trajectory: Abraham's offspring enslaved → God delivers with mighty acts → Christ's people enslaved to sin → God delivers through Christ's mighty work → suffering then glory for believers ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 17|Romans 8:17-18]]).
