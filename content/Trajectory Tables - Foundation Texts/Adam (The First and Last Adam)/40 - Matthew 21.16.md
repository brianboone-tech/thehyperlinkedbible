### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 16|Matthew 21:16]]

**Context**: When children cry "Hosanna to the Son of David" in the temple and the chief priests object, Jesus quotes Psalm 8:2: "Out of the mouth of infants and nursing babies you have prepared praise." Since Psalm 8 meditates on Genesis 1:26-28's creation of humanity in God's image with dominion over creation, Jesus' application of this Psalm to Himself implicitly claims to be the true man who fulfills what Adam failed to accomplish.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- νηπίων καὶ θηλαζόντων (*nepion kai thelazondon*) - "infants and nursing babies" (Psalm 8:2 LXX)
- αἶνον (*ainon*) - "praise" (proper response to the true King)
- κατηρτίσω (*katertiso*) - "you have prepared/perfected" (God ordaining praise from unlikely sources)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' quotation of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 8#Psalm 8 . 2|Psalm 8:2]] in the temple context is richly christological. Psalm 8 is David's meditation on [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-28]]—the dignity and dominion given to humanity ("What is man that you are mindful of him?"). By applying this Psalm to the children's praise of Himself, Jesus implicitly claims to be the "son of man" (בֶּן־אָדָם, ben-adam) who fulfills humanity's calling. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 6|Hebrews 2:6-9]] makes this explicit: we do not see all things subject to humanity, "but we see Jesus... crowned with glory and honor." The first Adam received the dominion mandate but forfeited it through sin. Jesus as last Adam receives the praise due to the true man—even from infants—because He is accomplishing what the first Adam failed to do. The children's Hosanna recognizes what the religious leaders reject: Jesus is the Son of David who fulfills Psalm 8's vision of human dignity and divine glory.
