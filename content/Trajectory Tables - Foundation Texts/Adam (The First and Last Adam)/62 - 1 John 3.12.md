### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]]

**Context**: John identifies Cain as "of the evil one" who "murdered his brother because his own deeds were evil and his brother's righteous." This reads Genesis 4 typologically through Genesis 3:15's lens—Cain belongs to the serpent's seed, Abel to the woman's seed. The passage develops the conflict introduced by Adam's fall: enmity between the two lineages, with Cain as the first example of the serpent's seed persecuting the righteous.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἐκ τοῦ πονηροῦ (*ek tou ponērou*) - "of the evil one" (spiritual paternity, belonging to Satan's lineage)
- ἔσφαξεν (*esphaxen*) - "slew/slaughtered" (violent murder, sacrificial language)
- πονηρά (*ponēra*) - "evil" (Cain's deeds characterized his spiritual identity)
- δίκαια (*dikaia*) - "righteous" (Abel's deeds marked him as woman's seed)

**Christological Connection**: John's identification of Cain as "of the evil one" is a typological reading of Genesis 4 through [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]. The enmity God promised between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed manifests immediately after Eden in Cain's murder of Abel. This establishes the pattern: throughout history, the serpent's seed persecutes the woman's seed, with righteous sufferers anticipating the ultimate righteous sufferer—Christ. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]] draws the christological contrast: Abel's blood cried from the ground for vengeance; Christ's blood "speaks a better word"—mercy, not vengeance. The first murder was Cain's attack on Abel; the climactic murder was the serpent's seed (the religious leaders, called "brood of vipers" in [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 33|Matthew 23:33]]) attacking Christ. But where Abel's death ended in tragedy, Christ's death ended in victory—the seed of the woman crushing the serpent's head even as His heel was bruised.
