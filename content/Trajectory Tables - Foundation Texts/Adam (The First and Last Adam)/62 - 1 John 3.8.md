### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 8|1 John 3:8]]

**Context**: John states "The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil," directly fulfilling Genesis 3:15's protoevangelium where the seed of the woman crushes the serpent's head. This is explicit Adam typology: Christ as the promised seed (last Adam) comes to defeat the serpent who deceived the first Adam, reversing the fall and destroying Satan's works that have plagued humanity since Eden.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἐφανερώθη (*ephanerōthē*) - "was manifested/appeared" (incarnation as the decisive divine intervention)
- λύσῃ (*lysē*) - "to destroy/loose/undo" (subjunctive, purposeful undoing of Satan's work)
- τὰ ἔργα τοῦ διαβόλου (*ta erga tou diabolou*) - "the works of the devil" (everything Satan accomplished through Adam's fall)

**Christological Connection**: John's statement is the clearest NT declaration of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]'s fulfillment. The devil's "works" encompass everything he accomplished through Adam's fall: sin, death, condemnation, alienation from God, the reign of darkness. Christ appeared "to destroy" these works—the Greek λύσῃ means to loose, dissolve, undo. This is precisely what [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] promised: the seed of the woman would crush the serpent's head. Where the first Adam succumbed to the serpent's temptation, the last Adam (tempted in the wilderness and at Gethsemane) resisted and conquered. Christ's death defeated the devil ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]]), His resurrection broke death's power, and His return will bring Satan's final destruction ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 10|Revelation 20:10]]). The trajectory from [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] to [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 8|1 John 3:8]] is the story of redemption: the devil's victory over Adam is overturned by Christ's victory over the devil.
