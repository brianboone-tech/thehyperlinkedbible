### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 1|John 1:1]]

**Context**: John's Gospel opens "In the beginning was the Word," deliberately echoing Genesis 1:1 "In the beginning God created." This establishes Christ's preexistence and role as divine agent in creation—crucial for Adam typology because it shows the last Adam existed before and created the first Adam. Christ is not merely another man but the eternal Word through whom Adam himself was made, now incarnate to redeem.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἐν ἀρχῇ (*en archē*) - "in the beginning" (deliberate echo of Genesis 1:1 LXX)
- ὁ λόγος (*ho logos*) - "the Word" (divine speech through which creation occurred)
- ἦν (*ēn*) - "was" (imperfect tense indicating continuous existence before creation)
- πρὸς τὸν θεόν (*pros ton theon*) - "with God" (distinct person in relationship)
- θεὸς ἦν (*theos ēn*) - "was God" (divine nature affirmed)

**Christological Connection**: John's echo of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]] establishes the fundamental christological truth underlying Adam typology: the last Adam is the Creator who made the first Adam. "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 3|John 1:3]]). This demolishes any notion that Christ is merely a superior human replacing an inferior one—He is the eternal Word who spoke creation into existence, including Adam himself. The incarnation is thus not God sending another creature but the Creator entering His own creation. Where the first Adam was formed from dust ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 7|Genesis 2:7]]), the last Adam is "from heaven" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 47|1 Corinthians 15:47]]). Where the first Adam received life, the last Adam is "life" itself ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 4|John 1:4]]). The trajectory shows radical asymmetry: Christ's superiority to Adam is not merely moral but ontological—He is the Creator condescending to become creature in order to redeem creatures from the curse their first representative brought upon them.
