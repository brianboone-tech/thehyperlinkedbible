### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 19#Matthew 19 . 4|Matthew 19:4-5]]

**Context**: Jesus appeals to Genesis 1:27 and 2:24 to establish marriage's permanence against the Pharisees' question about divorce. By grounding ethics in "the beginning" of creation, Jesus shows that Adam's pre-fall creation establishes normative patterns for humanity. This develops Adam typology by demonstrating that redemption aims to restore God's original design which Adam's fall corrupted.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀπ' ἀρχῆς (*ap' arches*) - "from the beginning" (appeal to creation order as normative)
- ἄρσεν καὶ θῆλυ (*arsen kai thely*) - "male and female" (quoting Genesis 1:27 LXX)
- σάρκα μίαν (*sarka mian*) - "one flesh" (quoting Genesis 2:24, marital union)
- ὁ κτίσας (*ho ktisas*) - "the One who created" (God as author of marriage)

  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 6#1 Corinthians 6 . 16|1 Corinthians 6:16]] - cites one-flesh union

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' appeal to Genesis reveals that the creation order—established through Adam before the fall—remains normative despite sin's corruption. As the last Adam, Christ does not abolish the creation order but restores and fulfills it. [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 31|Ephesians 5:31-32]] takes this further: Paul quotes [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 24|Genesis 2:24]]'s one-flesh union and declares "this mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church." The original Adam's marriage to Eve was always pointing forward to the last Adam's union with His bride. Jesus' teaching in Matthew 19 thus operates on two levels: He upholds creation's pattern for human marriage, and He is Himself the bridegroom who will accomplish the ultimate one-flesh union with His people—fulfilling what the first marriage typified.
