### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 7|Genesis 2:7]]

**Context**: This verse describes Adam's creation in vivid detail: God forms him from dust and breathes life into his nostrils. Paul quotes this verse directly in 1 Corinthians 15:45 as the foundation for his Adam-Christ typology: "The first man Adam became a living being." The contrast Paul develops—first Adam as "living soul" versus last Adam as "life-giving spirit"—is central to understanding Christ's superiority and the nature of resurrection life.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- וַיִּיצֶר (*vayyitser*) - "and He formed" (divine craftsmanship, like a potter)
- עָפָר (*aphar*) - "dust" (Adam's earthly origin, also his destiny after the fall: "to dust you shall return")
- נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים (*nishmat chayyim*) - "breath of life" (divine animation distinguishing living from non-living)
- נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (*nephesh chayyah*) - "living being/soul" (the result: animate, conscious existence)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Establishes humanity's dual nature—earthly body, divine breath; connects to [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 19|Genesis 3:19]]'s curse of returning to dust
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 3#Ecclesiastes 3 . 20|Ecclesiastes 3:20]] - ; 12:7 reflect on dust-to-dust mortality
  - [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 103#Psalm 103 . 14|Psalm 103:14]] - notes God remembers we are dust
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45-49]] - quotes this verse to contrast first and last Adam
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12-21]] - develops federal headship

**Christological Connection**: Paul's quotation in [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45]] establishes the fundamental Adam-Christ contrast: "Thus it is written, 'The first man Adam became a living being'; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit." The first Adam received life; the last Adam gives life. The first Adam was formed from earth and is "earthy" (χοϊκός); the last Adam is "from heaven" and is heavenly (ἐπουράνιος). The first Adam's sin brought death to all connected to him; the last Adam's resurrection brings life to all united to Him. This verse is the textual anchor for Paul's entire Adam typology—both the solidarity of humanity in Adam and the hope of resurrection in Christ. Where Adam became a living soul subject to death, Christ as life-giving Spirit imparts resurrection life that conquers death forever.
