### [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 3#Ecclesiastes 3 . 20|Ecclesiastes 3:20-21]]

**Context**: The Preacher reflects on the mortality shared by humans and animals: "All go to one place. All are from the dust, and to dust all return." This directly echoes Genesis 3:19's curse on Adam: "you are dust, and to dust you shall return." Ecclesiastes develops the universal human condition resulting from Adam's fall—death reigns over all his descendants. This "under the sun" perspective establishes the problem that only resurrection through the last Adam can solve.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- עָפָר (*aphar*) - "dust" (origin and destiny of mortal flesh, echoing Genesis 2:7 and 3:19)
- מָקוֹם אֶחָד (*maqom echad*) - "one place" (death as the great equalizer)
- רוּחַ (*ruach*) - "spirit/breath" (the Preacher questions if human spirit ascends while animal descends)

**Christological Connection**: Ecclesiastes' meditation on dust-to-dust mortality establishes the problem of Adamic death that only Christ can solve. The Preacher's observation that "all are from the dust, and to dust all return" is the experiential reality of [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12]]: "sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men." This universal mortality demonstrates Adam's federal headship—his one act of disobedience subjected all his posterity to death. But Paul's gospel answers the Preacher's despair: "For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 22|1 Corinthians 15:22]]). The last Adam's resurrection breaks the cycle of dust-to-dust, transforming perishable bodies into imperishable glory ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 42|1 Corinthians 15:42-44]]). Where Adam's fall made dust our destiny, Christ's resurrection makes glory our inheritance.
