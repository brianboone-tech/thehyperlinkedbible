### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 22|Isaiah 66:22]]

**Context**: Isaiah's final chapter promises that "the new heavens and the new earth that I make shall remain before me." The language echoes Genesis 1:1's original creation but adds the crucial element of permanence—this new creation will endure forever. This develops Adam typology eschatologically: the first creation through Adam was mutable and fell; the new creation through Christ as last Adam is immutable and eternal.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- שָׁמַיִם חֲדָשִׁים (*shamayim chadashim*) - "new heavens" (renewed cosmic order)
- אֶרֶץ חֲדָשָׁה (*erets chadashah*) - "new earth" (renewed terrestrial realm)
- עֹמְדִים (*omdim*) - "standing/remaining" (permanence, stability)
- לְפָנָי (*lephanai*) - "before me" (in God's presence, under His eternal gaze)

**Christological Connection**: The contrast between the first creation's mutability and the new creation's permanence is central to Adam typology. The first heavens and earth, where Adam was placed, proved vulnerable to corruption through sin—the fall brought curse upon the entire created order ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 20|Romans 8:20]]). But the new heavens and earth "shall remain" eternally. This escalation—not mere restoration but glorification—is accomplished through Christ as last Adam. Hebrews develops this theme: God will shake the created order "so that the things that cannot be shaken may remain" (12:27). The perishable gives way to imperishable, the mortal to immortal ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 53|1 Corinthians 15:53]]). What the first Adam lost through sin, the last Adam secures forever through righteousness. The new creation's eternal permanence demonstrates Christ's superiority: where Adam's creation fell, Christ's creation stands forever.
