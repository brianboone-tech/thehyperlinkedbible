### [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 9|Colossians 3:9-10]]

**Context**: Paul instructs believers to "put off the old self with its practices and put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its Creator." This echoes Genesis 1:26-27's creation in God's image and applies Adam typology directly to Christian living. Believers strip off the old Adam (fallen nature) and put on the new Adam (Christ's resurrection life), being restored to the image marred by Adam's fall.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀπεκδυσάμενοι (*apekdysamenoi*) - "having put off" (stripping away like clothing)
- τὸν παλαιὸν ἄνθρωπον (*ton palaion anthrōpon*) - "the old man" (the Adamic self, fallen nature)
- ἐνδυσάμενοι (*endysamenoi*) - "having put on" (clothing oneself with)
- τὸν νέον (*ton neon*) - "the new [man]" (the self in Christ, new creation)
- ἀνακαινούμενον (*anakainoumenon*) - "being renewed" (ongoing transformation)
- κατ' εἰκόνα (*kat' eikona*) - "according to the image" (echoing Genesis 1:26-27)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's "put off/put on" language directly applies Adam typology to Christian ethics. The "old self" is the Adamic nature inherited from the first Adam—characterized by the vices Paul lists (v.5-9). The "new self" is the nature received in union with Christ, the last Adam. The transformation is described as "being renewed in knowledge after the image of its Creator"—a deliberate echo of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 26|Genesis 1:26-27]]. Adam was created in God's image but that image was corrupted through sin. In Christ, believers are being restored to that original image-bearing purpose. This is not merely moral improvement but participation in Christ's resurrection life. The trajectory is creation in God's image (Genesis 1) → image corrupted in Adam (Genesis 3) → image restored in Christ (Colossians 3). Believers are caught up in this trajectory, progressively transformed "from one degree of glory to another" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 3#2 Corinthians 3 . 18|2 Corinthians 3:18]]) as the last Adam reverses what the first Adam ruined.
