### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 25|Isaiah 65:25]]

**Context**: In Isaiah's new creation vision, the wolf and lamb feed together, the lion eats straw like the ox, and "dust shall be the serpent's food." This final phrase deliberately echoes Genesis 3:14's curse on the serpent while notably omitting other curses. The new creation reverses Adam's fall except for the serpent's judgment—a theologically precise statement that the promised seed (Genesis 3:15) will crush the serpent while liberating creation from the curse.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- זְאֵב (*ze'ev*) - "wolf" (predator now at peace with prey)
- טָלֶה (*taleh*) - "lamb" (vulnerable creature now safe)
- נָחָשׁ (*nachash*) - "serpent" (the cursed enemy, still eating dust)
- עָפָר (*aphar*) - "dust" (serpent's food, echoing Genesis 3:14)

**Christological Connection**: The selective retention of the serpent's curse in Isaiah's new creation vision is theologically precise. Other effects of Genesis 3's curse are removed—predation ceases, harmony returns to creation. But "dust shall be the serpent's food" remains, pointing to the serpent's permanent defeat. This develops [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]'s protoevangelium: the seed of the woman (Christ as last Adam) will crush the serpent's head. The new creation doesn't redeem the serpent but displays his eternal humiliation. Christ's victory over Satan through the cross ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]]) and His future defeat of the serpent ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 10|Revelation 20:10]]) fulfill what Isaiah envisions. Where Adam's fall gave the serpent temporary victory, the last Adam's triumph ensures the serpent's eternal defeat while liberating creation from the curse Adam brought.
