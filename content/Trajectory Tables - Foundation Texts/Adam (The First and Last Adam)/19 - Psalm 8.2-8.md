### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 8#Psalm 8 . 2|Psalm 8:2-8]]

**Context**: Psalm 8 is David's meditation on Genesis 1:26-28, marveling at humanity's dignity and dominion. Looking at the moon and stars (echoing Genesis 1:16), David asks "What is man that you are mindful of him?" and answers with humanity's glory: crowned with honor, given dominion over all creatures. This Psalm is central to NT Adam typology—Hebrews 2:6-9 quotes it to show Christ as the true man who fulfills what Adam failed to accomplish.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- אֱנוֹשׁ (*enosh*) - "man/mortal" (emphasizing human frailty)
- בֶּן־אָדָם (*ben-adam*) - "son of man/son of Adam" (generic for humanity, becomes messianic title)
- כָּבוֹד וְהָדָר (*kavod v'hadar*) - "glory and honor" (royal dignity bestowed on humanity)
- מָשַׁל (*mashal*) - "to rule/have dominion" (echoing Genesis 1:28's mandate)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 5|Hebrews 2:5-9]] is the authoritative christological reading of Psalm 8. The author notes that while David speaks of humanity's glory and dominion, "at present, we do not yet see everything in subjection to him" (v.8)—Adam's fall left the dominion mandate unfulfilled. "But we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death" (v.9). Christ is the true "son of man/son of Adam" who fulfills what the first Adam forfeited. He was made lower than angels through incarnation, crowned with glory through resurrection, and will have all things under His feet at the consummation. Psalm 8's meditation on Adam's dignity finds its ultimate fulfillment not in fallen humanity but in the last Adam, Jesus Christ.
