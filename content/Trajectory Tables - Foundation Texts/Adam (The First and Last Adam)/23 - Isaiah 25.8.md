### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 8|Isaiah 25:8]]

**Context**: Isaiah's eschatological vision declares that God "will swallow up death forever" and wipe away tears from all faces. Paul quotes this verse in 1 Corinthians 15:54 to celebrate the resurrection victory over death. This prophecy directly addresses the curse introduced through Adam's fall—death entered through one man's sin (Romans 5:12), but the last Adam's resurrection defeats death permanently.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- בִּלַּע (*billa*) - "swallow up" (complete consumption/destruction)
- הַמָּוֶת (*hammavet*) - "the death" (death personified as an enemy)
- לָנֶצַח (*lanetsach*) - "forever" (permanent, perpetual victory)
- דִּמְעָה (*dim'ah*) - "tear" (grief associated with death removed)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's quotation of [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 8|Isaiah 25:8]] in [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 54|1 Corinthians 15:54]] is climactic: "When the perishable puts on the imperishable, and the mortal puts on immortality, then shall come to pass the saying that is written: 'Death is swallowed up in victory.'" This prophecy directly addresses the Adamic curse. Death entered through the first Adam's disobedience ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12]]; [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 19|Genesis 3:19]]), reigning over all his descendants. But Christ as last Adam defeats death through His own death and resurrection. Where Adam's sin gave death its reign, Christ's obedience ends death's reign. Isaiah's vision finds its fulfillment in the resurrection: death—the great enemy introduced through Adam—is finally and forever destroyed. "The last enemy to be destroyed is death" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 26|1 Corinthians 15:26]]), and [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 8|Isaiah 25:8]] is God's promise that this destruction is certain.
