### [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 10#Mark 10 . 6|Mark 10:6-8]]

**Context**: Jesus employs Genesis 1:27 and 2:24 to interpret marriage law, using earlier Scripture (creation ordinances) to establish normative ethics. "From the beginning of creation, God made them male and female" grounds Jesus' teaching in Adam's pre-fall creation. This demonstrates that as the last Adam, Jesus restores and upholds the creation order that the first Adam violated through sin.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἀπὸ δὲ ἀρχῆς κτίσεως (*apo de arches ktiseos*) - "but from the beginning of creation" (creation order as interpretive key)
- ἄρσεν καὶ θῆλυ (*arsen kai thely*) - "male and female" (Genesis 1:27)
- προσκολληθήσεται (*proskollethēsetai*) - "shall be joined/cleave" (Genesis 2:24, marital permanence)
- μία σάρξ (*mia sarx*) - "one flesh" (union language from Genesis 2:24)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 27|Genesis 1:27]] - male and female
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 24|Genesis 2:24]] - one flesh); creation order before fall
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 2#Malachi 2 . 15|Malachi 2:15-16]] - on marriage covenant
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 19#Matthew 19 . 4|Matthew 19:4-5]] - parallel
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 31|Ephesians 5:31-32]] - applies marriage typology to Christ and church

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' hermeneutical method—appealing to creation order to interpret later law—reveals His authority as the last Adam who embodies and restores God's original design. The Pharisees asked about Mosaic divorce provisions (which accommodate sin's effects); Jesus points behind the law to creation's intent. This pattern is crucial: the first Adam's sin introduced the conditions requiring divorce legislation, but the last Adam directs attention to the Creator's purpose. Moreover, the one-flesh union Jesus defends ultimately typifies His own union with the church ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 31|Ephesians 5:31-32]]). Adam's marriage to Eve pointed forward to Christ's marriage to His bride. The last Adam thus fulfills what the first Adam's marriage symbolized—the permanent, faithful, one-flesh union between Christ and His redeemed people.
