### [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 7|Ecclesiastes 12:7]]

**Context**: The Preacher's conclusion on mortality: "the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it." This verse directly echoes both Genesis 2:7 (God breathed life into dust) and Genesis 3:19 (to dust you shall return). It completes the trajectory of Adamic death—the body dissolves back to earth while the spirit returns to its divine source. This establishes the anthropology underlying Paul's resurrection teaching in 1 Corinthians 15.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- עָפָר (*aphar*) - "dust" (the body's earthly origin and mortal destiny)
- רוּחַ (*ruach*) - "spirit" (the animating breath from God that returns to Him)
- נָתַן (*natan*) - "gave" (God as the giver of life-breath)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/21 - Ecclesiastes/Ecclesiastes 12#Ecclesiastes 12 . 7|Ecclesiastes 12:7]] describes the dissolution that characterizes Adamic humanity—body to dust, spirit to God. This is the universal experience under the reign of death introduced by Adam's fall. Paul's resurrection theology in 1 Corinthians 15 addresses this directly: the first Adam's body was "natural" (ψυχικόν, from the earth), subject to death and dissolution. But Christ as last Adam brings a "spiritual body" (σῶμα πνευματικόν) that is imperishable, glorious, and powerful. Where Ecclesiastes traces the separation of dust and spirit at death, Paul proclaims their reunification in resurrection: "this perishable body must put on the imperishable, and this mortal body must put on immortality" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 53|1 Corinthians 15:53]]). The last Adam's resurrection transforms the human condition from dust-bound mortality to glorified immortality.
