### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 4|Genesis 4:4-15]]

**Context**: The Cain and Abel narrative develops the Genesis 3:15 conflict between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed. Abel offers acceptable sacrifice by faith (Hebrews 11:4); Cain murders his brother in the pattern of the serpent's hostility. John identifies Cain as "of the evil one" (1 John 3:12), reading this narrative through the lens of the two-seed typology. This passage shows the immediate outworking of Adam's fall: sin's reign, the first murder, and the enmity between the godly and ungodly lines.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- מִנְחָה (*minchah*) - "offering" (both brothers bring offerings, but God regards Abel's)
- בְּכֹרוֹת (*bekhorot*) - "firstborn" (Abel offers the best of his flock)
- חַטָּאת (*chattat*) - "sin" (crouching at Cain's door like a predator)
- שֹׁמֵר (*shomer*) - "keeper" (Cain's sarcastic "Am I my brother's keeper?")

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Follows immediately from Genesis 3's fall; develops serpent-seed vs woman-seed conflict of 3:15
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]] - shows Seth replacing Abel in the godly line; the pattern continues through Noah, Abraham
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 4|Hebrews 11:4]] - commends Abel's faith; 12:24 contrasts Abel's blood with Christ's
  - [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]] - identifies Cain as "of the evil one"
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 23#Matthew 23 . 35|Matthew 23:35]] - references "righteous Abel"

**Christological Connection**: The Cain-Abel narrative develops the protoevangelium typologically. Abel stands as the first of the righteous seed persecuted by the serpent's seed—a pattern culminating in Christ. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 24|Hebrews 12:24]] draws the contrast explicitly: Abel's blood cried out for vengeance from the ground, but Jesus' blood "speaks a better word than the blood of Abel." Where Abel's death demanded justice against Cain, Christ's death secures mercy for sinners. John's identification of Cain as "of the evil one" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 12|1 John 3:12]]) confirms the [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] reading: enmity exists between the two seeds, and Cain manifests the murderous character of his spiritual father the devil ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 44|John 8:44]]). Abel thus becomes the first martyr in the line leading to Christ, the ultimate righteous sufferer at the hands of the serpent's seed.
