### [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 54|1 Corinthians 15:54-55]]

**Context**: Paul combines Isaiah 25:8 ("Death is swallowed up in victory") with Hosea 13:14 ("O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?") to celebrate the resurrection triumph. This directly addresses the Adamic curse—death entered through one man's sin (Romans 5:12), but the last Adam's resurrection defeats death permanently. The taunt against death marks the reversal of Adam's fall.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- κατεπόθη (*katepothē*) - "is swallowed up" (aorist passive, decisive defeat)
- νῖκος (*nikos*) - "victory" (complete triumph over death)
- κέντρον (*kentron*) - "sting" (death's weapon—sin—now disarmed)
- θάνατε (*thanate*) - "O death" (vocative, directly addressing and taunting defeated enemy)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's triumphant quotation climaxes his Adam-Christ argument in 1 Corinthians 15. Death entered through Adam—"in Adam all die" (v.22)—and has reigned ever since as humanity's great enemy. But Christ as last Adam defeats death through resurrection: "in Christ shall all be made alive" (v.22). The taunt "O death, where is your sting?" declares this victory complete. Death's sting was sin (v.56)—it was Adam's sin that gave death its power. Christ removes the sting by dealing with sin on the cross, then demonstrates death's powerlessness by rising from the grave. The trajectory from Adam's death sentence to Christ's resurrection victory is the entire gospel: what the first Adam brought upon humanity, the last Adam has overcome. "Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" (v.57).
