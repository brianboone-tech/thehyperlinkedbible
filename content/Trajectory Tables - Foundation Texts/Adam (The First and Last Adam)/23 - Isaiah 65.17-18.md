### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 17|Isaiah 65:17-18]]

**Context**: Isaiah prophesies new heavens and new earth where "the former things shall not be remembered." The deliberate echo of Genesis 1:1 ("In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth") establishes eschatological hope for creation's restoration. This is central to Adam typology: original creation → fall through Adam → new creation through Christ as last Adam that surpasses Eden. The curse introduced in Genesis 3 is reversed, and joy replaces sorrow.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- בּוֹרֵא (*bore*) - "creating" (participle, God as active Creator of new heavens and earth)
- חֲדָשִׁים (*chadashim*) - "new" (qualitatively new, not merely renewed)
- שָׂשׂוֹן (*sason*) - "joy/gladness" (characterizes new creation vs. sorrow of fallen creation)
- גִּילָה (*gilah*) - "rejoicing" (exultation in God's completed work)

**Christological Connection**: Isaiah's new creation prophecy develops Adam typology eschatologically. The original creation of [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]] was "very good" but vulnerable—Adam's fall brought curse, death, and sorrow upon the entire created order ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17-19]]; [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 20|Romans 8:20-22]]). Through Christ as last Adam, God accomplishes not mere restoration but escalation: "new heavens and new earth" surpassing Eden's glory. The trajectory is creation → fall → re-creation in Christ. Paul develops this in [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]: "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." What is true spiritually for believers now will be true cosmically at Christ's return—the groaning creation finally liberated ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 21|Romans 8:21]]), former things forgotten, and everlasting joy characterizing the redeemed order. The last Adam accomplishes what the first Adam forfeited and more.
