### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 36|Judges 6:36-40]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1492|H1492]] גִּזָּה (gizzâ) - "fleece" - a fleece of wool
- [[Lexicon/H2919|H2919]] טַל (ṭal) - "dew" - if there is dew only on the fleece
- [[Lexicon/H3045|H3045]] יָדַע (yāḏaʿ) - "know" - then I will know
- [[Lexicon/H2734|H2734]] חָרָה (ḥārâ) - "be angry" - do not be angry with me
- [[Lexicon/H5254|H5254]] נָסָה (nāsâ) - "test" - let me test one more time

**Context**: After receiving divine commission, signs, and even the Spirit's empowerment (6:34), Gideon still requested confirming signs—the famous fleece tests. "If You are going to save Israel by my hand, as You have said..." implies doubt. He asked for wet fleece on dry ground, then dry fleece on wet ground. God graciously accommodated each request without rebuke.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Requesting signs after divine commission echoes Moses ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 1|Exodus 4:1-9]]) and Ahaz ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 7#Isaiah 7 . 10|Isaiah 7:10-12]], though Ahaz refused)
- God's patient accommodation of weak faith appears throughout Scripture
- The pattern of double confirmation appears with Joseph's dreams and Pharaoh's dreams

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 4#Exodus 4 . 1|Exodus 4:1-9]] - Moses given signs for weak faith
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 7#Isaiah 7 . 10|Isaiah 7:10-12]] - Ahaz told to ask for a sign
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 36|1 Kings 18:36-38]] - Elijah's test with fire
  - [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 103#Psalm 103 . 14|Psalm 103:14]] - God knows our frame
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 14#Matthew 14 . 31|Matthew 14:31]] - "O you of little faith, why did you doubt?" yet Jesus saves Peter
**Type Classification**: Providential (God's accommodation of weak faith), Backward-Looking (NT recognizes this as genuine faith despite struggle)

**Christological Connection**: God's patient response to Gideon's fleece tests anticipates Christ's patience with His disciples. Jesus didn't reject Peter for sinking but saved him; He didn't abandon Thomas for doubting but showed him His wounds. The Gideon pattern reveals a God who works with imperfect faith rather than waiting for perfect faith—ultimately revealed in Christ who "will not break a bruised reed" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 3|Isaiah 42:3]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 20|Matthew 12:20]]).
