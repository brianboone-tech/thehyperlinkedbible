### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 2|Judges 7:2-8]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7227|H7227]] רַב (rab) - many, too many
- [[Lexicon/H6286|H6286]] פָּאַר (pa'ar) - to glorify oneself, boast
- [[Lexicon/H3027|H3027]] יָד (yad) - hand ("my own hand has saved me")

**Context**: God tells Gideon the army is "too many" for Him to give victory—He deliberately removes human strength. The army is reduced from 32,000 to 10,000 (fearful dismissed) to 300 (water-lapping test). God explicitly states His purpose: "lest Israel boast over me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.'"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 8#Deuteronomy 8 . 17|Deuteronomy 8:17-18]] - Warning against saying "My power and might of my hand"
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 4|Isaiah 9:4]] - "As on the day of Midian"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 7|2 Corinthians 4:7]] - "To show that the surpassing power belongs to God"

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (explicitly prophetic in Isaiah 9:4); Direct Type

**Christological Connection**: God's removal of human strength establishes the pattern for gospel ministry. Just as Gideon could not boast in military might, believers cannot boast in human wisdom or power ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 27|1 Corinthians 1:27-29]]). Christ Himself came in weakness—born in a manger, crucified in shame—so that salvation would exclude all human boasting. "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 31|1 Corinthians 1:31]]).
