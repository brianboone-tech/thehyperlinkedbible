### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 1|Isaiah 9:1-2, 6-7]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2822|H2822]] חֹשֶׁךְ (choshek) - darkness
- [[Lexicon/H216|H216]] אוֹר (or) - light
- [[Lexicon/H3206|H3206]] יֶלֶד (yeled) - child
- [[Lexicon/H1121|H1121]] בֵּן (ben) - son
- [[Lexicon/H6382|H6382]] פֶּלֶא (pele') - wonder, wonderful

**Context**: Isaiah prophesies light dawning on those in darkness—specifically naming Zebulun and Naphtali, the region of Gideon's victory over Midian. The passage climaxes with the birth announcement of a Child/Son whose names reveal deity ("Mighty God," "Everlasting Father") and whose kingdom will be eternal. This light-in-darkness imagery connects to Gideon's torches shining forth when the clay jars were broken.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 6#Judges 6 . 35|Judges 6:35]] - Zebulun and Naphtali called to battle with Gideon
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 7#Isaiah 7 . 14|Isaiah 7:14]] - Virgin birth prophecy precedes this passage
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 15|Matthew 4:15-16]] - Jesus fulfills this in the same geography

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking prophecy; Messianic direct prediction

**Christological Connection**: The geography is significant—Zebulun and Naphtali were where Gideon's torches shone in darkness, defeating Midian. Isaiah prophesies that in this same region, a greater light will dawn: the Messiah Himself. [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 15|Matthew 4:15-16]] records Jesus deliberately beginning His ministry there, proclaiming "the kingdom of heaven is at hand." The torch of Gideon becomes the Light of the World; the child born in Bethlehem is the "Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God" who breaks oppression "as on the day of Midian."
