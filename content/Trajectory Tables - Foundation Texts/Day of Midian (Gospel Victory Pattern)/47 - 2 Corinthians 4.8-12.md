### [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 8|2 Corinthians 4:8-12]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2346|G2346]] θλίβω (thlibō) - to afflict, press hard
- [[Lexicon/G1377|G1377]] διώκω (diōkō) - to persecute, pursue
- [[Lexicon/G2598|G2598]] καταβάλλω (kataballō) - to strike down, cast down
- [[Lexicon/G3500|G3500]] νέκρωσις (nekrōsis) - death, dying (carrying death of Jesus)
- [[Lexicon/G5319|G5319]] φανερόω (phaneroō) - to manifest, reveal

**Context**: Paul describes the "smashing of the clay jars"—the suffering that reveals Christ's light. Believers are "afflicted... perplexed... persecuted... struck down" but not destroyed. They "always carry in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be manifested." The principle: "death is at work in us, but life in you."

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 19|Judges 7:19-20]] - Smashing the jars to reveal the torches
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 5|Isaiah 53:5]] - By His wounds we are healed
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 10|Philippians 3:10]] - Fellowship of His sufferings

**Type Classification**: NT application of Gideon typology to Christian experience

**Christological Connection**: The smashing of Gideon's clay jars becomes a pattern for Christian suffering. Paul's list—afflicted, perplexed, persecuted, struck down—describes the "breaking" that reveals Christ's light. The principle is profound: "death is at work in us, but life in you." As the 300's broken jars released torchlight to defeat Midian, believers' suffering releases Christ's life to bring others to salvation. We "carry in the body the death of Jesus" so His life may be manifested through our mortal flesh.
