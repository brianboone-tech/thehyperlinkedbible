### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 4|Isaiah 9:4]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5923|H5923]] עֹל (ol) - yoke (of burden/oppression)
- [[Lexicon/H4294|H4294]] מַטֶּה (matteh) - staff, rod (of the oppressor)
- [[Lexicon/H4080|H4080]] מִדְיָן (Midyan) - Midian
- [[Lexicon/H7665|H7665]] חָתַת (chatat) - to break, shatter

**Context**: In the midst of Isaiah's great Messianic prophecy (9:1-7), verse 4 declares that the coming Deliverer will break the yoke of oppression "as on the day of Midian." This explicit reference to Judges 6-7 identifies Gideon's victory as a divinely intended type of Messianic deliverance. The method of victory—unconventional, displaying divine power through weakness—will characterize how the Messiah breaks spiritual bondage.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 19|Judges 7:19-22]] - The "day of Midian" victory
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 10#Isaiah 10 . 26|Isaiah 10:26]] - "As when he struck Midian at the rock of Oreb"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 15|Matthew 4:15-16]] - Fulfillment of [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 1|Isaiah 9:1-2]]

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (explicit prophetic typology); Direct Type (divinely declared pattern)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 4|Isaiah 9:4]] is the hermeneutical key that unlocks the Gideon typology. By explicitly stating that Messiah will break oppression "as on the day of Midian," Isaiah declares Gideon's victory to be a divinely intended pattern. The Child who will be born (v.6)—Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God—will conquer not by military force but by the same unconventional means: divine power displayed through apparent weakness. Christ's victory through the cross perfectly fulfills this pattern.
