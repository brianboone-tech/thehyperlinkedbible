### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 7#Judges 7 . 16|Judges 7:16-22]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7782|H7782]] שׁוֹפָר (shophar) - trumpet, ram's horn (proclamation)
- [[Lexicon/H3537|H3537]] כַּד (kad) - jar, pitcher (clay vessel)
- [[Lexicon/H3940|H3940]] לַפִּיד (lappid) - torch, flame (light)
- [[Lexicon/H7665|H7665]] שָׁבַר (shabar) - to break, shatter (the jars)

**Context**: Gideon divides his 300 men into three companies, equipping them with trumpets, empty clay jars, and torches inside the jars. At his signal, they blow the trumpets, smash the jars, hold up the torches, and cry "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!" The Midianite camp panics and destroys itself—"the LORD set every man's sword against his comrade."

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 6#Joshua 6 . 20|Joshua 6:20]] - Jericho fell to trumpets and shouts, not swords
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 4|Isaiah 9:4]] - Victory "as on the day of Midian"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 7|2 Corinthians 4:7]] - "Treasure in jars of clay"

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (Isaiah 9:4 explicitly references this); Direct Type

**Christological Connection**: The three elements—trumpets, torches, and clay jars—prefigure gospel ministry: (1) **Trumpets** = proclamation of the gospel message; (2) **Torches** = "the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ" (2 Cor 4:4); (3) **Clay jars** = weak human vessels containing the treasure. When the jars are "smashed" through suffering, the light shines forth. Victory comes not through the sword of human effort but through the Word proclaimed by broken vessels.
