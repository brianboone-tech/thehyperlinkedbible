### [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9, 15]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1019-1025#G1019|βραδύνει]] (*bradynei*) - "is slow, delays"
- [[Lexicon/G3114-3120#G3115|μακροθυμεῖ]] (*makrothymei*) - "is patient, longsuffering"
- [[Lexicon/G1014-1020#G1014|βούλομαι]] (*boulomai*) - "will, desire"
- [[Lexicon/G0622-0625#G0622|ἀπολέσθαι]] (*apolesthai*) - "to perish, be destroyed"
- [[Lexicon/G3340-3345#G3341|μετάνοια]] (*metanoia*) - "repentance"
- [[Lexicon/G5562-5565#G5562|χωρῆσαι]] (*chōrēsai*) - "to come, arrive at, reach"
- [[Lexicon/G3115-3120#G3115|μακροθυμίαν]] (*makrothymian*) - "patience, forbearance" (v. 15)
- [[Lexicon/G4991-5000#G4991|σωτηρίαν]] (*sōtērian*) - "salvation" (v. 15)

**Context**: Peter addresses scoffers who mock the delay of Christ's return, asking "where is the promise of his coming?" (v. 4). He explains that God's perceived slowness is actually patience—he desires all to reach repentance before judgment falls. Verse 15 reinforces this: "count the patience of our Lord as salvation," giving opportunity for more to be saved before the Day arrives.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 3|Genesis 6:3]] (My Spirit shall not abide in man forever... his days shall be 120 years), [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 20|1 Peter 3:20]] (God's patience waited in days of Noah)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 4|Romans 2:4]] (God's kindness leads to repentance), [[Readable Bible/54 - 1 Timothy/1 Timothy 2#1 Timothy 2 . 4|1 Timothy 2:4]] (God desires all to be saved), [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 4|2 Peter 3:4]] (scoffers ask "where is the promise?")

**Christological Connection**: God's patience before Noah's flood, providing 120 years for repentance while the ark was prepared, profoundly anticipates the present age of gospel proclamation before Christ's return. As God's patience "waited in the days of Noah" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 20|1 Peter 3:20]]), giving opportunity to enter the ark, so God's patience now waits while the church (Christ's ark) is gathered. Peter explicitly states "count the patience of our Lord as salvation" (v. 15)—every day Jesus delays his return is mercy, allowing more to come to faith. As Noah preached righteousness for 120 years before judgment fell, the church proclaims Christ throughout this age before final judgment ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 14|Matthew 24:14]]: "this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed... and then the end will come"). God's patience is not weakness but sovereign grace—he is "not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance" (v. 9), with "all" referring to his elect whom Christ came to save ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 39|John 6:39]]: "I should lose nothing of all that he has given me"). As the flood's delay ended abruptly when God shut the ark's door ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 16|Genesis 7:16]]), Christ's return will come suddenly when the full number of the elect is complete ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 25|Romans 11:25]]: "until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in"). The pattern warns against presumption—the generation before the flood continued in sin "until the day when Noah entered the ark, and the flood came and destroyed them all" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 17#Luke 17 . 27|Luke 17:27]]). So Christ's return will catch the unprepared by surprise ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 44|Matthew 24:44]]: "the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect"). God's patience is salvation for those who enter Christ, the true ark, while the door remains open.
