### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 17#Luke 17 . 26|Luke 17:26-27]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2250-2260#G2250|ἡμέραι Νῶε]] (*hēmerai Nōe*) - "days of Noah"
- [[Lexicon/G2064-2070#G2064|ἔρχεται]] (*erchetai*) - "is coming" (present tense for future: Son of Man's coming)
- [[Lexicon/G2068-2070#G2068|ἤσθιον]] (*ēsthion*) - "they were eating"
- [[Lexicon/G4095-4100#G4095|ἔπινον]] (*epinon*) - "they were drinking"
- [[Lexicon/G1060-1065#G1060|ἐγάμουν]] (*egamoun*) - "they were marrying"
- [[Lexicon/G1548-1550#G1548|ἄχρι]] (*achri*) - "until"
- [[Lexicon/G2627-2630#G2627|κατακλυσμός]] (*kataklysmos*) - "flood, deluge"
- [[Lexicon/G0622-0625#G0622|ἀπώλεσεν]] (*apōlesen*) - "destroyed"

**Context**: Jesus warns his disciples about his second coming by comparing it to Noah's flood. As people in Noah's day continued in ordinary activities (eating, drinking, marrying) until the flood suddenly came and destroyed them all, so will people be unprepared when the Son of Man is revealed. The emphasis is on the suddenness and unexpectedness of judgment upon those living carelessly.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 5|Genesis 6:5-7]] (wickedness was great, God determined to destroy), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 21|Genesis 7:21-23]] (all flesh died), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 7|Genesis 7:7]] (Noah entered the ark)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 37|Matthew 24:37-39]] (parallel account), [[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 5#1 Thessalonians 5 . 2|1 Thessalonians 5:2-3]] (sudden destruction), [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 13|Matthew 25:13]] (watch, you know neither the day nor the hour)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus's comparison of his return to Noah's flood establishes himself as the eschatological Judge and Savior. As the flood was universal judgment that destroyed "all" except those in the ark, Christ's return will bring universal judgment that condemns all except those "in Christ" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]: "no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"). The suddenness emphasized—"until the day when Noah entered the ark, and the flood came"—mirrors Christ's unexpected return: "the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 44|Matthew 24:44]]). As people in Noah's day continued normal life oblivious to coming judgment despite 120-year warning, so people today continue in sin despite 2000+ years of gospel proclamation ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 3|2 Peter 3:3]]: "scoffers will come... saying, 'Where is the promise of his coming?'"). The pattern of divine discrimination—Noah and family saved, world destroyed—foreshadows final separation: believers gathered to Christ, unbelievers cast into judgment ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 31|Matthew 25:31-46]]). As God shut the ark's door ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 16|Genesis 7:16]]), sealing Noah's salvation and the world's doom, so there comes a point of no return when Christ returns—the master will "rise and shut the door" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 13#Luke 13 . 25|Luke 13:25]]). The comprehensive destruction—"destroyed them all" (v. 27)—anticipates the lake of fire where death and Hades are thrown ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 14|Revelation 20:14]]). Yet as God preserved Noah through judgment waters, Christ preserves his people: "Because you have kept my word... I will keep you from the hour of trial" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 3#Revelation 3 . 10|Revelation 3:10]]). The call is clear: enter the ark (Christ) while the door remains open, for "now is the day of salvation" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 2|2 Corinthians 6:2]]).
