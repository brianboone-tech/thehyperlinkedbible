### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 15|Genesis 8:15-22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4196-4200#H4196|מִזְבֵּחַ]] (*mizbēaḥ*) - "altar"
- [[Lexicon/H5930-5931#H5930|עֹלָה]] (*ʿōlāh*) - "burnt offering"
- [[Lexicon/H7381-7382#H7381|רֵיחַ נִיחֹחַ]] (*rêaḥ nîḥōaḥ*) - "soothing aroma, pleasing scent"
- [[Lexicon/H7043-7044#H7043|קָלַל]] (*qālal*) - "curse" (God promises not to curse again, v. 21)
- [[Lexicon/H5221-5222#H5221|נָכָה]] (*nākāh*) - "strike, smite" (God promises not to strike again, v. 21)
- [[Lexicon/H2233-2234#H2233|זֶרַע]] (*zeraʿ*) - "seed, offspring"

**Context**: After the flood subsides, God commands Noah to exit the ark and repopulate the earth. Noah's immediate response is worship—he builds an altar and offers burnt offerings from the clean animals. God smells the pleasing aroma and makes an internal vow never again to curse the ground or destroy all life, despite humanity's unchanged sinful nature. This establishes the post-flood world order on the basis of sacrifice and covenant promise.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17]] (ground cursed), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-4]] (Cain and Abel's offerings)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 9|Isaiah 54:9-10]] ("this is like the days of Noah to me"), [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 33#Jeremiah 33 . 20|Jeremiah 33:20-22]] (covenant with day and night)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 15|Hebrews 13:15-16]] (sacrifice of praise), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]] (Christ's offering as fragrant aroma), [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 5|2 Peter 3:5-7]] (new heavens and earth)

**Christological Connection**: Noah's emergence from the ark to build an altar and offer sacrifice profoundly anticipates Christ's work establishing new creation. As Noah's first act in the renewed world was sacrificial worship, Christ's resurrection inaugurates the new creation through his self-sacrifice. The "pleasing aroma" of Noah's burnt offering prefigures Christ's offering, which Paul explicitly calls "a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]). God's promise never again to curse the ground (v. 21) despite human sin's persistence anticipates the gospel paradox: God maintains justice while extending mercy through the substitute sacrifice of Christ. The Noahic covenant's stability—guaranteeing seedtime and harvest "while the earth remains" (v. 22)—demonstrates God's commitment to preserve creation until Christ brings final redemption. The animals Noah sacrificed died as substitutes; Christ is the ultimate substitute who "appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]). The new world Noah entered through judgment and sacrifice foreshadows the new creation believers enter through Christ's death and resurrection—Peter explicitly connects the flood to baptism and "the resurrection of Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 21|1 Peter 3:21]]). As God smelled the pleasing aroma and committed to sustain creation, the Father accepted Christ's sacrifice and seated him at his right hand, securing the new covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]: "when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down"). The permanence of natural cycles (v. 22) assures that God will preserve creation until Christ returns to consummate the new heavens and new earth ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 13|2 Peter 3:13]]).
