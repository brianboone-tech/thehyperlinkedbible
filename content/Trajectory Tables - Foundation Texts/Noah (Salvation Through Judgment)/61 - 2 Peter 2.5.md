### [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 2#2 Peter 2 . 5|2 Peter 2:5]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0744-0750#G0744|ἀρχαίου κόσμου]] (*archaiou kosmou*) - "ancient world"
- [[Lexicon/G5339-5350#G5339|ἐφείσατο]] (*epheisato*) - "spared" (negated: did not spare)
- [[Lexicon/G1343-1345#G1343|δικαιοσύνης]] (*dikaiosynēs*) - "righteousness"
- [[Lexicon/G2783-2785#G2783|κήρυκα]] (*kēryra*) - "herald, preacher"
- [[Lexicon/G5442-5445#G5442|ἐφύλαξεν]] (*ephylaxen*) - "preserved, guarded"
- [[Lexicon/G2627-2630#G2627|κατακλυσμὸν]] (*kataklysmon*) - "flood, deluge"
- [[Lexicon/G0765-0770#G0765|ἀσεβῶν]] (*asebōn*) - "ungodly, impious"

**Context**: Peter uses Noah's flood as an example of God's pattern in judgment: he did not spare the ungodly ancient world but preserved Noah, "a herald of righteousness," with seven others when bringing the flood upon the world. This establishes that God knows how to rescue the godly from trials while keeping the unrighteous under punishment.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 13|Genesis 6:13]] (God announces judgment), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 1|Genesis 7:1]] (you alone I have seen to be righteous), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 23|Genesis 7:23]] (only Noah and those with him remained)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 6|2 Peter 3:6-7]] (world deluged with water, now reserved for fire), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 7|Hebrews 11:7]] (Noah condemned the world), [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 2#2 Peter 2 . 9|2 Peter 2:9]] (the Lord knows how to rescue the godly)

**Christological Connection**: Peter's depiction of Noah as "herald of righteousness" whom God preserved through judgment while destroying the ungodly world profoundly anticipates Christ and his people. As Noah preached righteousness for 120 years to a hostile world that perished, Christ proclaimed the kingdom to Israel, which rejected him and faced AD 70 judgment ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 19#Luke 19 . 41|Luke 19:41-44]]). As God "did not spare the ancient world," the Father "did not spare his own Son" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 32|Romans 8:32]])—but for opposite reasons: the world for wickedness, Christ for our wickedness. Noah and seven others were preserved through the flood; the church, a "little flock" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 12#Luke 12 . 32|Luke 12:32]]), is preserved through tribulation to inherit the kingdom. The pattern "did not spare the ungodly... but preserved the righteous" operates throughout redemptive history and climaxes at Christ's return: God will not spare the unbelieving world ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 7|2 Peter 3:7]]: "the present heavens and earth... are reserved for fire") but will preserve those in Christ. As flood waters destroyed the old world while bearing the ark to safety, final judgment will destroy this present evil age while believers—united to Christ—pass through safely to new heavens and new earth ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 13|2 Peter 3:13]]). The "eighth" person (Noah) anticipates the "eighth day" resurrection—Christ rose on the first day of the new week, the "eighth day," inaugurating new creation. As Noah emerged from the ark to a cleansed world and offered sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 20|Genesis 8:20]]), believers will emerge from this age through Christ's sacrifice into the world made new ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]]: "Behold, I am making all things new").
