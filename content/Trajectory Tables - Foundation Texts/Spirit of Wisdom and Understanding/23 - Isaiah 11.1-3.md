### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1-3]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- **choter** (חֹטֶר) - rod, shoot, branch
- **netser** (נֵצֶר) - branch, sprout
- **ruach YHWH** (רוּחַ יְהוָה) - Spirit of the LORD
- **chokmah** (חָכְמָה) - wisdom
- **binah** (בִּינָה) - understanding, discernment
- **etsah** (עֵצָה) - counsel
- **geburah** (גְּבוּרָה) - might, strength
- **da'at** (דַּעַת) - knowledge
- **yir'at YHWH** (יִרְאַת יְהוָה) - fear of the LORD
- LXX: **pneuma theou** (πνεῦμα θεοῦ) - Spirit of God
- LXX: **sophia** (σοφία) - wisdom
- LXX: **synesis** (σύνεσις) - understanding, intelligence
- LXX: **gnosis** (γνῶσις) - knowledge
- LXX: **empiplemi** (ἐμπίπλημι) - to fill

**Context**: Isaiah 11 follows the prophecy of Assyria's judgment (10:33-34), where the forest is cut down. In contrast, a shoot will sprout from Jesse's stump. The Davidic line, seemingly ended with the exile, will produce the Messiah. This prophecy was given during King Hezekiah's reign, when Assyria threatened Judah's existence.

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - God's promise to David of an eternal dynasty ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]]); shoot from Jesse, David's father
- **FROM OT**:
  - Jeremiah's "righteous Branch" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 23#Jeremiah 23 . 5|Jeremiah 23:5]]); Zechariah's "Branch" ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 3#Zechariah 3 . 8|Zechariah 3:8]]; 6:12)
- **FROM NT**:
  - Jesus descended from Jesse through David ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1-17]]
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 12|Romans 15:12]] - ); Spirit descends on Jesus at baptism ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 16|Matthew 3:16-17]]); Jesus filled with the Spirit ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 1|Luke 4:1]], 14, 18
**OT Context**: This Messianic prophecy comes during a time of political crisis. Assyria has devastated the northern kingdom (Israel) and threatens Judah. Human kings have failed—even good kings like Hezekiah have limitations. Isaiah contrasts failed human leadership with the coming Spirit-anointed Davidic king who will rule with perfect wisdom and righteousness. The sevenfold Spirit (vv. 2-3) recalls Bezalel's triad (wisdom, understanding, knowledge) but expands it, showing the Messiah's complete empowerment.

**Jewish Backgrounds**: Rabbinic tradition identified this passage with the Messiah (*Mashiach ben David*). The Targum Jonathan renders v. 1: "A king shall come forth from the sons of Jesse, and the Messiah shall be exalted from his sons' sons." The sevenfold Spirit became associated with the menorah's seven lamps, symbolizing perfect illumination. Post-exilic Jews longed for this prophecy's fulfillment as they experienced continued subjugation under Persians, Greeks, and Romans.

**Text Form**: The structure is carefully crafted:
- v. 1: Messiah's origin (from Jesse's stump)
- v. 2a: Messiah's empowerment (Spirit **rests** on Him)
- v. 2b-c: Messiah's attributes (threefold pairs: wisdom/understanding, counsel/might, knowledge/fear of the LORD)
- v. 3a: Messiah's disposition (delight in fearing the LORD)
- v. 3b: Messiah's judgment (not by appearance)

The verb "rest" (*nuach*) implies permanence—unlike temporary OT fillings, the Spirit remains on the Messiah. The LXX adds that the Spirit will "fill" (*empiplemi*) him (v. 3), using the same verb as Exodus 31:3, 35:31.

**Hermeneutical Use**: This is one of the clearest Messianic prophecies, quoted by Paul in Romans 15:12: "The root of Jesse will come, even he who arises to rule the Gentiles; in him will the Gentiles hope." The NT sees Jesus' baptism as fulfillment—the Spirit descending and remaining on Him (John 1:32-33). The sevenfold Spirit influenced Revelation 1:4; 3:1; 4:5; 5:6, where the "seven Spirits of God" represent the Holy Spirit's fullness.

**Theological Use**:
1. **Messianic Hope** - Despite dynastic collapse, God will raise a Davidic king
2. **Spirit's Permanent Indwelling** - The Spirit **rests** (not temporarily fills) on Messiah
3. **Perfect Wisdom for Governance** - The Messiah will judge with divine, not human, wisdom
4. **Escalation from Previous Leaders** - Bezalel had wisdom for craftsmanship; Messiah has wisdom for cosmic rule

**Rhetorical Use**: Isaiah uses this prophecy to:
1. **Comfort** Judah with hope during Assyrian threat
2. **Contrast** failed human kings with the perfect future king
3. **Assure** that God's covenant with David stands despite appearances
4. **Call** the people to wait faithfully for God's timing

**Christological Connection**: This prophecy finds direct fulfillment in Jesus Christ:

1. **Jesus as the Branch from Jesse** - Matthew's genealogy traces Jesus through David to Jesse ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5-6]]). Paul quotes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 10|Isaiah 11:10]] in [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 12|Romans 15:12]], applying it to Christ who brings salvation to Gentiles. Jesus is the "Root and Offspring of David" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 16|Revelation 22:16]]).

2. **Spirit Rests on Jesus** - At Jesus' baptism, "the Spirit of God descended like a dove and came to rest on him" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 16|Matthew 3:16]]). John the Baptist testifies, "I saw the Spirit descend from heaven like a dove, and it remained on him" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 32|John 1:32]]). The verb "remained" (*menō*) fulfills Isaiah's "rest" (*nuach*)—permanent, not temporary.

3. **Sevenfold Spirit Fulfilled** - Isaiah's description anticipates Jesus' ministry:
   - **Wisdom and understanding**: "I am gentle and lowly in heart" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 29|Matthew 11:29]]); "Never man spoke like this man!" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 46|John 7:46]])
   - **Counsel and might**: Multiplies loaves ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 14#Matthew 14 . 19|Matthew 14:19-21]]), calms storms ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 4#Mark 4 . 39|Mark 4:39]]), casts out demons ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 1#Mark 1 . 27|Mark 1:27]])
   - **Knowledge and fear of the LORD**: "I do nothing on my own authority, but speak just as the Father taught me" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 28|John 8:28]]); "I always do the things that are pleasing to him" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 29|John 8:29]])

4. **Perfect Judgment Not by Appearance** - Jesus declares, "Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 24|John 7:24]]). He judges not by external reputation but by the heart ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 2#Revelation 2 . 23|Revelation 2:23]]).

5. **Escalation**:
   - **Bezalel** ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 31#Exodus 31 . 3|Exodus 31:3]]): Spirit-filled for temporary construction
   - **David** ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 13|1 Samuel 16:13]]): Spirit came upon him for kingship, but he still sinned
   - **Messiah** ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 2|Isaiah 11:2]]): Spirit **rests** permanently, enabling perfect righteousness
   - **Fulfillment** (Jesus): "God gives the Spirit without measure" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 34|John 3:34]]); completely sinless ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]])

**Promise-Fulfillment**: Isaiah 11:1-3's Messiah, endowed with wisdom, finds perfect fulfillment in Christ (Matthew 12:18-21, quoting Isaiah 42). Jesus doesn't merely possess wisdom; He IS "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24). The temporary filling of craftsmen and kings gives way to the permanent, complete indwelling in the Messiah.

**Contrast** (per PDF analysis): OT fillings were temporary and task-specific; in Christ, wisdom is permanent and transformational (Colossians 1:9-10). Bezalel was filled for tabernacle work; the Messiah is filled for eternal, righteous governance of all creation.
