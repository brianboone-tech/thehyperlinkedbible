### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 2#Leviticus 2 . 2|Leviticus 2:2]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4503|H4503]] מִנְחָה (*minchah*) - "grain offering, tribute, gift"
- [[Lexicon/H5560|H5560]] סֹלֶת (*solet*) - "fine flour"
- [[Lexicon/H3828|H3828]] לְבֹנָה (*levonah*) - "frankincense"
- [[Lexicon/H7381|H7381]] רֵיחַ (*reach*) - "scent, fragrance, aroma"
- [[Lexicon/H5207|H5207]] נִיחֹחַ (*nichoach*) - "soothing, pleasing"

**Context**: The grain offering (מִנְחָה) is unique among the Levitical sacrifices as the only bloodless offering. Composed of fine flour, oil, and frankincense, it accompanied burnt offerings and peace offerings, never standing alone. The priest burns a memorial portion on the altar, producing a "pleasing aroma to the LORD," while the remainder becomes food for the priests. This offering symbolizes the dedication of daily labor and the fruit of human industry to God, acknowledging that all productivity comes from Him and belongs to Him.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The term מִנְחָה originally meant "gift" or "tribute" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-5]]; 32:13-21; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 15|Judges 3:15]])
- Isaiah rebukes grain offerings when accompanied by wickedness ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 1#Isaiah 1 . 13|Isaiah 1:13]])
- Malachi prophesies a pure offering among the Gentiles, using מִנְחָה terminology ([[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 1#Malachi 1 . 11|Malachi 1:11]])
- Chronicles records the grain offering's role in temple restoration ([[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 29#2 Chronicles 29 . 27|2 Chronicles 29:27]])

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking Direct Type

**Christological Connection**: The grain offering's fine flour represents Christ's perfect humanity—unblemished, refined, and wholly dedicated to the Father. The oil mixed with the flour symbolizes the Holy Spirit's anointing, while the frankincense points to the fragrance of His righteous life. Unlike animal sacrifices emphasizing substitutionary death, the grain offering highlights Christ's obedient life—His active righteousness offered on our behalf. Just as the grain offering never stood alone but always accompanied blood sacrifices, Christ's perfect life is inseparable from His atoning death. Both together constitute the "pleasing aroma" that secures our acceptance before God ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]).
