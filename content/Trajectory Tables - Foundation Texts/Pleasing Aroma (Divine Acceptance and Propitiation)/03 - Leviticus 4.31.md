### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 31|Leviticus 4:31]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2403|H2403]] חַטָּאת (*chatat*) - "sin offering, purification offering"
- [[Lexicon/H2459|H2459]] חֵלֶב (*chelev*) - "fat, richest part"
- [[Lexicon/H7381|H7381]] רֵיחַ (*reach*) - "scent, fragrance, aroma"
- [[Lexicon/H5207|H5207]] נִיחֹחַ (*nichoach*) - "soothing, pleasing"
- [[Lexicon/H3722|H3722]] כָּפַר (*kapar*) - "to cover, atone, make atonement"

**Context**: The sin offering (חַטָּאת) addresses unintentional sins and ritual impurity, providing ceremonial cleansing and atonement. Leviticus 4:31 concludes the instructions for a common person's sin offering, stating that when the fat is burned on the altar "as a pleasing aroma to the LORD," the priest makes atonement and the sinner is forgiven. Remarkably, even the sin offering—which deals explicitly with human guilt—produces a "pleasing aroma" when offered according to God's prescribed method. This demonstrates that God Himself has provided the means of reconciliation, and He delights in the restoration of sinners through substitutionary sacrifice.

**Christological Connection**: The sin offering's dual nature—dealing with defilement yet producing a "pleasing aroma"—perfectly anticipates Christ's atoning work. Just as the sin offering bore the penalty of transgression while satisfying divine justice, Christ "who knew no sin became sin for us" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]). The offering's acceptance by God, signified by the pleasing aroma, prefigures the Father's acceptance of Christ's perfect sacrifice. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10]] says "it pleased the LORD to crush Him," language echoing the "pleasing aroma" motif. What the repeated sin offerings could only cover ceremonially, Christ's once-for-all sacrifice removes definitively ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]). The fragrant smoke ascending from the sin offering pointed forward to Christ's sacrifice that turns away wrath and secures eternal forgiveness.
