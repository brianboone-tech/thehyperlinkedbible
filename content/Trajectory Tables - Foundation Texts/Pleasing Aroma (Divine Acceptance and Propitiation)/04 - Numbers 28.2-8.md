### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 28#Numbers 28 . 2|Numbers 28:2-8]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H8548|H8548]] תָּמִיד (*tamid*) - "continual, perpetual, regular"
- [[Lexicon/H5930|H5930]] עֹלָה (*olah*) - "burnt offering"
- [[Lexicon/H3532|H3532]] כֶּבֶשׂ (*kebes*) - "male lamb"
- [[Lexicon/H7381|H7381]] רֵיחַ (*reach*) - "scent, fragrance"
- [[Lexicon/H5207|H5207]] נִיחֹחַ (*nichoach*) - "pleasing, soothing"
- [[Lexicon/H801|H801]] אִשֶּׁה (*isheh*) - "fire offering"

**Context**: This passage establishes the daily burnt offering (תָּמִיד עֹלַת, *olat tamid*) as the foundation of Israel's worship. Every morning and evening, a year-old male lamb without blemish must be offered along with grain and drink offerings. This twice-daily sacrifice, producing a "pleasing aroma to the LORD," maintained perpetual access to God and formed the baseline upon which all festival and special offerings were added. The daily burnt offering symbolized Israel's continual consecration to Yahweh and their constant need for atonement. Its regularity—365 days per year, regardless of circumstances—demonstrated that worship is not optional or sporadic but the defining rhythm of covenant life.

**Christological Connection**: The daily burnt offering's perpetual nature highlights both the inadequacy of repeated animal sacrifices and their prophetic function. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]] states that priests "stand daily offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins," but this repetition demonstrated the ongoing need that only Christ could fulfill. Where the Levitical system required twice-daily sacrifices producing temporary acceptance, Christ offered Himself once, producing eternal redemption ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]). Yet the daily rhythm also anticipates believers' continuous consecration to God through Christ's ongoing intercession ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). The morning and evening lambs pointed forward to the Lamb of God whose single sacrifice provides perpetual access to the throne of grace ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 16|Hebrews 4:16]]).
