### [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 2#2 Corinthians 2 . 14|2 Corinthians 2:14-16]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2358|G2358]] θριαμβεύω (*thriambeuō*) - "to lead in triumphal procession"
- [[Lexicon/G3744|G3744]] ὀσμή (*osmē*) - "fragrance, aroma, odor"
- [[Lexicon/G1108|G1108]] γνῶσις (*gnōsis*) - "knowledge"
- [[Lexicon/G2175|G2175]] εὐωδία (*euōdia*) - "sweet smell, fragrance, pleasing aroma"
- [[Lexicon/G4991|G4991]] σωτηρία (*sōtēria*) - "salvation, deliverance"

**Context**: Paul uses striking imagery from Roman triumphal processions to describe God's work through apostolic ministry. When a victorious general returned to Rome, the procession included captives and incense bearers spreading fragrance throughout the city. Paul identifies believers as the "aroma of Christ" spread everywhere through gospel proclamation. Remarkably, this same fragrance has a dual effect—it is "the aroma of life to those being saved" but "the aroma of death to those perishing." The gospel's message, like the Levitical sacrifices, produces a pleasing aroma to God regardless of human response, while simultaneously revealing the spiritual state of those who encounter it.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The Levitical "pleasing aroma" sacrifices were acceptable to God regardless of individual worshiper's ultimate fate
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10]] says "it pleased the LORD to crush Him"—God delighted in Christ's sacrifice
- Song of Solomon uses fragrance imagery for love and intimacy (1:3, 12; 4:10)
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 20#Ezekiel 20 . 41|Ezekiel 20:41]] promises God will accept His people "as a pleasing aroma"

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking Providential Type (recognized as fulfillment from NT perspective)

**Christological Connection**: This passage demonstrates the "pleasing aroma" trajectory's application to believers through union with Christ. Just as Christ Himself is a "fragrant offering" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]), those united to Him become "the aroma of Christ" in the world. The Levitical sacrifices produced a pleasing fragrance that ascended to God; now, through gospel proclamation, Christ's fragrance spreads through His ambassadors. The dual effect of the aroma—life to some, death to others—reflects the gospel's polarizing nature: Christ is either the cornerstone or the stumbling stone ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 7|1 Peter 2:7-8]]). What the OT sacrifices symbolized externally, believers now embody spiritually as living sacrifices ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 1|Romans 12:1]]) spreading Christ's fragrance wherever they go.
