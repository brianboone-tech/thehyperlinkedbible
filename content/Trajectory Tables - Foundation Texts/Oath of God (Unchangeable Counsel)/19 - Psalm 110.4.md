### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7601-7700#H7650|H7650]] שָׁבַע (shābaʿ) - "to swear" (Niphal: "The LORD has sworn," v. 4).
- [[Lexicon/H3501-3600#H3548|H3548]] כֹּהֵן (kōhēn) - "priest" (v. 4). Royal priesthood after the order of Melchizedek.

**Context**: Psalm 110 is the most-quoted OT text in the NT (cited or alluded to over 30 times). Verse 4's oath establishes a priesthood distinct from and superior to the Levitical order—a royal priesthood "after the order of Melchizedek" (cf. Gen 14:18-20).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 14#Genesis 14 . 18|Genesis 14:18-20]]** - Melchizedek, king of Salem and "priest of God Most High," blessed Abraham. [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] invokes this mysterious figure as the model for the Messianic priest-king.
- **[[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 6#Zechariah 6 . 12|Zechariah 6:12-13]]** - The prophet envisions a coming figure called "the Branch" who will "be a priest on His throne," combining royal and priestly roles (cf. Ps 110:1, 4).

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 14#Genesis 14 . 18|Genesis 14:18-20]] - Melchizedek blesses Abraham); 2 Samuel 7 (Davidic dynasty
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 6#Zechariah 6 . 12|Zechariah 6:12-13]] - "He will be a priest on His throne"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 6|Hebrews 5:6]] - , 10; 6:20; 7:17, 21 (extensive exposition of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] as Christological oath establishing Jesus' eternal priesthood
**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (explicit divine oath) and **Forward-Looking** (the oath explicitly appoints a future priest-king "forever").

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 20|Hebrews 7:20-22]] quotes [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] to demonstrate that Jesus' priesthood is superior to Aaron's because it is oath-confirmed. The oath makes Jesus "the guarantee [ἔγγυος] of a better covenant" (Heb 7:22). This is the only priesthood in Scripture appointed by divine oath, underscoring its eternal, unchangeable nature.
