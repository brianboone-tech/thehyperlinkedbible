### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 16|Genesis 22:16-18]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7601-7700#H7650|H7650]] שָׁבַע (shābaʿ) - "to swear, take an oath" (v. 16). A denominative verb from "seven," suggesting the completeness and solemnity of oath-taking. In the Niphal, "to swear by oneself" (of God).
- [[Lexicon/H1-100#H1288|H1288]] בָּרַךְ (bārak) - "to bless" (v. 17). Intensive form: "I will surely bless you" (אָבָרֵךְ אֲבָרֶכְךָ, infinitive absolute + verb for emphasis).
- [[Lexicon/H2201-2300#H2233|H2233]] זֶרַע (zeraʿ) - "seed, offspring, descendants" (v. 17-18). Both collective (multiplied descendants) and singular Messianic seed (Gal 3:16).

**Context**: After Abraham's willing obedience in offering Isaac (Gen 22:1-14), the angel of the LORD calls from heaven a second time (v. 15) with God's oath-confirmed promise. This is the climactic moment of the Abrahamic covenant, grounding all subsequent promises in God's self-sworn oath.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 26#Genesis 26 . 3|Genesis 26:3]]** - Oath renewed to Isaac: "I will confirm the oath I swore to your father Abraham"
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 6#Exodus 6 . 8|Exodus 6:8]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 8|Deuteronomy 7:8]]** - The Exodus is executed to fulfill "the oath I swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob"
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 105#Psalm 105 . 8|Psalm 105:8-11]]** - "He remembers His covenant forever... the covenant He made with Abraham, the oath He swore to Isaac"

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (God's explicit self-oath is a divinely ordained act of revelation) and **Forward-Looking** (Gen 22:18 "through your offspring all nations will be blessed" contains built-in futurity; Hebrews identifies this as intentionally pointing forward to Christ and the Gentile mission).

**Christological Connection**: Christ is the ultimate "offspring" (Gal 3:16) through whom the oath to Abraham finds fulfillment. Jesus is both the recipient of blessing (as Davidic-Melchizedekian priest-king appointed by oath, Ps 110:4; Heb 7:21) and the mediator of blessing to all nations (as the seed in whom Jew and Gentile are united, Gal 3:28-29). God's oath to Abraham is ultimately an oath to give His Son for the salvation of the world (Rom 8:32 alludes to Gen 22:16: "He who did not spare His own Son"). The immutability of God's counsel, demonstrated in the Abrahamic oath, secures the perseverance and final salvation of all who are in Christ (Heb 6:17-20).
