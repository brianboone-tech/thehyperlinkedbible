### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 17|Hebrews 10:17-18]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3403|G3403]] μιμνῄσκομαι (mimnēskomai) - "remember" (negated: "remember no more")
- [[Lexicon/H266|G266]] ἁμαρτία (hamartia) - "sin" - what is forgotten
- [[Lexicon/G458|G458]] ἀνομία (anomia) - "lawlessness" - transgression of God's law
- [[Lexicon/G859|G859]] ἄφεσις (aphesis) - "forgiveness" - release from debt

**Context**: Hebrews quotes Jeremiah 31:34's new covenant promise: "Their sins and lawless acts I will remember no more." This divine forgetfulness is the ultimate fulfillment of the scapegoat's removal. Sins are not just carried away to the wilderness but erased from divine memory.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 22|Leviticus 16:22]] - Scapegoat carries sins to a remote place
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]] - New covenant promise of sins remembered no more
- [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 7#Micah 7 . 19|Micah 7:19]] - Sins cast into the sea

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 22|Leviticus 16:22]] - Sins removed to uninhabited wilderness
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 34|Jeremiah 31:34]] - "I will forgive their wickedness and remember their sins no more"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]] - "No condemnation"
  - [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 14|Colossians 2:14]] - Certificate of debt canceled
**Type Classification**: NT Superiority - Divine forgetfulness exceeds physical removal

**Christological Connection**: The scapegoat carried sins to the wilderness where they were "out of sight"; Christ carries sins to divine forgetfulness where they are "out of mind." Physical removal symbolized; spiritual obliteration accomplished. God's "remembering no more" is not cognitive limitation but covenantal commitment. What the scapegoat pictured—sins gone, never to return—Christ achieves eternally.
