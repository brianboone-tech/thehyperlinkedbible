### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 4|Isaiah 53:4-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5375|H5375]] נָשָׂא (nasa) - "bore" - carrying away (scapegoat term)
- [[Lexicon/H5445|H5445]] סָבַל (sabal) - "carried" - bearing burden
- [[Lexicon/H6293|H6293]] פָּגַע (paga) - "laid on/caused to fall upon" - imputation
- [[Lexicon/H5771|H5771]] עָוֹן (avon) - "iniquity" - the guilt transferred

**Context**: Isaiah's fourth Servant Song describes the Suffering Servant who bears the sins of many. The language directly echoes the scapegoat ritual: sins are "laid on" Him (imputation), He "bears" them (carrying), and this results in healing for those whose sins He carries.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 21|Leviticus 16:21-22]] - High priest confesses sins over scapegoat, which carries them away
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10-12]] - The Servant makes His soul a guilt offering
- Psalm 22 - The suffering righteous one

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking prophetic fulfillment, explicating what the scapegoat typified

**Christological Connection**: Isaiah 53 is the scapegoat prophecy. The laying on of iniquity (53:6, "the LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all") corresponds exactly to the high priest's hand-laying. The bearing (53:4, 11, 12) corresponds to the goat carrying sins away. But the Servant exceeds the type: He bears sin willingly, consciously, effectively—accomplishing what the animal could only symbolize.
