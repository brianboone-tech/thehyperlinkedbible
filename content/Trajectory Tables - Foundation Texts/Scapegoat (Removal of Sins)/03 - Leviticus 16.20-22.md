### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 20|Leviticus 16:20-22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5799|H5799]] עֲזָאזֵל (azazel) - "scapegoat" - meaning debated: "goat of removal" or "for Azazel"
- [[Lexicon/H5564|H5564]] סָמַךְ (samakh) - "lay/lean" - placing hands for transfer
- [[Lexicon/H3034|H3034]] יָדָה (yadah) - "confess" - verbal acknowledgment of sins
- [[Lexicon/H5375|H5375]] נָשָׂא (nasa) - "bear/carry" - the goat carrying sins
- [[Lexicon/H1509|H1509]] גְּזֵרָה (gezerah) - "solitary/cut-off place" - uninhabited wilderness

**Context**: Leviticus 16 prescribes the Day of Atonement ritual. After the slain goat's blood has been sprinkled on the mercy seat (propitiation), the high priest performs this second ritual with the live goat. The laying on of hands and confession transfers Israel's sins; the release into the wilderness removes them permanently.

**Type Classification**: Direct (divinely commanded ritual), Forward-looking (explicitly typological of Christ's work)

**Christological Connection**: Christ fulfills the scapegoat by receiving imputed sin: "God made Him who had no sin to be sin for us" (2 Cor 5:21). The confession over the goat parallels Christ's bearing "our sins in His body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24). But where the goat passively received and carried, Christ actively bore sin through death and resurrection, removing it not to a wilderness but to divine forgetfulness where it can never be recovered.
