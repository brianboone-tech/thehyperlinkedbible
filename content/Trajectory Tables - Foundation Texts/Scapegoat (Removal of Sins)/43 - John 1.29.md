### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G286|G286]] ἀμνός (amnos) - "lamb" - sacrificial animal
- [[Lexicon/G2316|G2316]] θεός (theos) - "God" - divine ownership/provision
- [[Lexicon/G142|G142]] αἴρω (airō) - "takes away/removes" - lifts up and carries off
- [[Lexicon/G266|G266]] ἁμαρτία (hamartia) - "sin" - singular, collective humanity's sin

**Context**: John the Baptist identifies Jesus to his disciples with this proclamation. The title combines Passover lamb (Exod 12) and scapegoat (Lev 16) imagery. The verb αἴρω specifically means to lift up and carry away—the scapegoat's function. Jesus is the one who removes the world's sin.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 21|Leviticus 16:21-22]] - The scapegoat carries sins into the wilderness
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] - "Like a lamb that is led to the slaughter"
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 5|Exodus 12:5-7]] - The Passover lamb slain and its blood applied

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 22|Leviticus 16:22]] - The scapegoat "carries" (נָשָׂא) sins away
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] - Lamb to slaughter
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]] - "God will provide... the lamb"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]] - "A Lamb, looking as if it had been slain"
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]] - "precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish"
**Type Classification**: NT Fulfillment announcement - Combining Passover and scapegoat typology

**Christological Connection**: John's declaration identifies Jesus as both the slain sacrifice (lamb blood applied) and the sin-removing scapegoat (taking away sin). The verb αἴρω specifically captures the scapegoat function—lifting up and carrying away. What the scapegoat did annually for Israel, Christ does once for all for the world. He doesn't merely cover sin temporarily but removes it permanently.
