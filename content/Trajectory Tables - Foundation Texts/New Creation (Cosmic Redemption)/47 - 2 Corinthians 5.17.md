### [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]

- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2537|G2537]] καινός (kainos) - new (in quality/freshness, not just recent in time)
- [[Lexicon/G2901-3000#G2936|G2936]] κτίζω (ktizo) - to create, form, shape
- [[Lexicon/G2901-3000#G2937|G2937]] κτίσις (ktisis) - creation, creature, created thing
- [[Lexicon/G3801-3900#G3854|G3854]] παρέρχομαι (parerchomai) - to pass away, pass by
- [[Lexicon/G1001-1100#G1096|G1096]] γίνομαι (ginomai) - to become, come into being

**Context**: In 2 Corinthians 5:14-21, Paul explains the transformative power of Christ's substitutionary death. "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come!" (v. 17). This declaration stands at the center of Paul's theology: union with Christ transfers believers from the old age of sin and death into the new age of righteousness and life. The phrase "new creation" (kaine ktisis) directly echoes Isaiah 65:17 (LXX: καινὸς οὐρανός, kainē gē). Paul applies Isaiah's cosmic new creation prophecy to the individual believer's transformation in Christ, showing that eschatological new creation has been inaugurated through the gospel.

**Type Classification**: **Direct** (Isaiah explicitly prophesied new creation) and **Forward-looking** (Isaiah's prophecy points forward to eschatological fulfillment, now inaugurated in Christ)

**Christological Connection**: Christ is both the agent and the substance of new creation. Just as the Father spoke creation into existence through the eternal Word ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 3|John 1:3]]), so He speaks new creation into existence through the gospel of Christ. Paul connects this explicitly to [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 3|Genesis 1:3]] in [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 6|2 Corinthians 4:6]]: "For God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ." The original creation's light-speaking prefigures gospel illumination. Union with Christ ("in Christ") is the locus of new creation—believers are transferred from Adam's old creation realm into Christ's new creation realm. This demonstrates Fairbairn's principle of escalation in multiple ways: (1) **Scope**: Original creation was "very good" but corruptible; new creation is eternal and incorruptible. (2) **Relationship**: Original creation knew God's presence in Eden; new creation features unmediated communion ("we will see his face"—[[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]]). (3) **Security**: Adam could fall; those in Christ "shall never perish" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 28|John 10:28]]). (4) **Glory**: Original creation reflected God's glory; new creation participates in Christ's glory ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 17|Romans 8:17]]—"heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ"). The "old has passed away; behold, the new has come" announces the most radical transformation imaginable—from death to life, from curse to blessing, from Adam to Christ, from this age to the age to come. New creation in Christ surpasses original creation as much as Christ surpasses Adam—infinitely.
