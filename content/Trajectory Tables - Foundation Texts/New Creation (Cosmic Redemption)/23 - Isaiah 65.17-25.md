### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 17|Isaiah 65:17-25]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1201-1300#H1254|H1254]] בָּרָא (bara) - to create (same verb as Genesis 1:1—divine creative activity)
- [[Lexicon/H2301-2400#H2319|H2319]] חָדָשׁ (chadash) - new, fresh
- [[Lexicon/H8001-8100#H8064|H8064]] שָׁמַיִם (shamayim) - heavens
- [[Lexicon/H701-800#H776|H776]] אֶרֶץ (erets) - earth
- [[Lexicon/H7201-7300#H7223|H7223]] רִאשׁוֹן (rishon) - former, first, previous

**Context**: Isaiah 65:17-25 is the climactic prophetic vision of new creation. God declares, "For behold, I will create (bara) new heavens and a new earth. The former things will not be remembered, nor will they come to mind" (v. 17). This new creation will feature: removal of weeping and crying (v. 19), extended lifespans (v. 20), security in dwelling and planting (vv. 21-22), fruitful labor (v. 23), intimate prayer access ("before they call, I will answer"—v. 24), and curse removal (v. 25—"the wolf and lamb will feed together... but the food of the serpent will be dust"). Isaiah uses the same verb (bara) as Genesis 1:1, signaling that God will perform a creative act as dramatic as the original creation—yet surpassing it.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]] - "In the beginning God created (bara) the heavens and the earth"—Isaiah deliberately echoes this
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 14|Genesis 3:14-19]] - Curse on serpent, woman, man, and ground—[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 25|Isaiah 65:25]] shows partial reversal (serpent still eats dust)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 6|Isaiah 11:6-9]] - Earlier Isaianic vision of curse removal (wolf with lamb, lion eating straw)
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 43#Isaiah 43 . 18|Isaiah 43:18-19]] - "Do not call to mind the former things... Behold, I am about to do something new"—connects new exodus to new creation
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 22|Isaiah 66:22]] - Immediately follows, confirming: "For just as the new heavens and the new earth which I make will endure before Me"

**Type Classification**: **Direct** (God explicitly commands/promises new creation) and **Forward-looking** (Isaiah prophetically announces what God will do; later NT authors recognize inaugurated and consummated fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: Christ is the agent and substance of the new creation Isaiah prophesies. Paul declares that "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]), using language directly from [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 17|Isaiah 65:17]]. Union with Christ brings believers into the age to come while they still live in this present evil age. Isaiah's vision of extended lifespans ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 20|Isaiah 65:20]]) points ultimately to resurrection life and immortality in Christ. The intimate prayer access ("before they call, I will answer"—[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 24|Isaiah 65:24]]) is fulfilled in Christ our Mediator who gives us access to the Father ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 18|Ephesians 2:18]]). The removal of weeping and crying ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 19|Isaiah 65:19]]) finds ultimate fulfillment when God "will wipe every tear from their eyes" in the New Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 4|Revelation 21:4]]). The partial curse removal in [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 25|Isaiah 65:25]] (serpent still eats dust) anticipates the total curse removal in [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 3|Revelation 22:3]] ("no longer any curse"). This demonstrates Fairbairn's principle of progressive clarity: Isaiah's prophecy was glorious but veiled; Christ reveals the full scope—new creation includes not just renewed earth but resurrected bodies, not just peace among animals but reconciliation between God and humanity, not just long life but eternal life. The escalation is infinite: Isaiah's new creation is wonderful, but Christ's new creation is the dwelling of God with humanity forever.
