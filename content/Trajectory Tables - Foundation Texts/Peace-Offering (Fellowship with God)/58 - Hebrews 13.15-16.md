### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 15|Hebrews 13:15-16]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2378-2379#G2378|θυσίαν αἰνέσεως]] (*thysian aineseōs*) - "sacrifice of praise" (genitive: praise as the sacrifice)
- [[Lexicon/G1223-1224#G1223|δι' αὐτοῦ]] (*di' autou*) - "through him" (instrumental: Christ the mediator)
- [[Lexicon/G1275-1276#G1275|διὰ παντός]] (*dia pantos*) - "continually, perpetually, always"
- [[Lexicon/G2590-2591#G2590|καρπὸν χειλέων]] (*karpon cheileōn*) - "fruit of lips" (Hebrew idiom for verbal praise)
- [[Lexicon/G3670-3671#G3670|ὁμολογούντων]] (*homologountōn*) - "confessing, acknowledging" (present participle: ongoing confession)
- [[Lexicon/G2842-2843#G2842|κοινωνίας]] (*koinōnias*) - "fellowship, sharing, generosity"
- [[Lexicon/G2100-2101#G2100|εὐαρεστεῖται]] (*euaresteitai*) - "is well-pleased with" (present passive: God delights in)

**Context**: Hebrews 13:15-16 concludes the letter's theological exposition by applying Christ's superior priesthood to believers' worship. After establishing that Christ offered the ultimate sacrifice "outside the gate" (v. 12), the author exhorts readers to offer spiritual sacrifices through Him. The passage transforms Old Testament sacrificial categories into New Testament worship: instead of animal offerings, believers offer praise (thanksgiving-offerings); instead of ceremonial rites, they practice good works and sharing (vow and freewill-offerings). The threefold application—praise, good deeds, sharing—corresponds to the three varieties of peace-offerings detailed in Leviticus 7:11-21. The key phrase "through him" (*di' autou*) establishes Christ as the mediator through whom all acceptable worship must pass. This spiritual sacrifice is "continually" (*dia pantos*)—not annually or occasionally but perpetually, reflecting Christ's once-for-all sacrifice that enables ongoing communion.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 15|Hebrews 13:15-16]]'s call to "offer up a sacrifice of praise to God... through him" fulfills the peace-offering's thanksgiving dimension. Where [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 7#Leviticus 7 . 12|Leviticus 7:12]]'s todah (thanksgiving-offering) expressed gratitude through animal sacrifice and accompanying cakes, believers now offer "the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name" (v. 15)—verbal praise through Christ's mediation. The peace-offering's blood, sprinkled on the altar, made the thanksgiving meal acceptable; Christ's blood, shed once for all, makes believers' praise perpetually acceptable. The threefold classification of peace-offerings (thanksgiving, vow, freewill) finds New Testament correspondence: praise (thanksgiving), doing good (vow-fulfillment), and sharing (freewill generosity). Where the thanksgiving-offering must be eaten the same day ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 7#Leviticus 7 . 15|Leviticus 7:15]]), enforcing temporal limits, Christ enables "continual" (*dia pantos*) worship—perpetual thanksgiving for perpetual grace. The vow-offering (neder) represented covenant faithfulness; Hebrews' call to "do good" fulfills this through obedient living. The freewill-offering (nedavah) expressed spontaneous devotion; "do not neglect... sharing" (*koinōnia*, v. 16) continues this voluntary generosity. Paul echoes this connection: "your gifts [are] a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 4#Philippians 4 . 18|Philippians 4:18]])—generosity as spiritual sacrifice. The peace-offering's "pleasing aroma to the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 3#Leviticus 3 . 5|Leviticus 3:5]]) anticipated these spiritual sacrifices' acceptability: "for such sacrifices are pleasing to God" (v. 16). Christ's role as mediator—"through him" (*di' autou*)—shows that what the Aaronic priesthood facilitated temporarily, Christ enables permanently. Where the peace-offering required priestly mediation for acceptability, Christ's priesthood guarantees believers' worship reaches God. The trajectory extends from Leviticus 3's ritual through Christ's finished work to the church's ongoing worship: thanksgiving without ceasing ([[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 5#1 Thessalonians 5 . 18|1 Thessalonians 5:18]]), doing good as those created in Christ Jesus for good works ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 10|Ephesians 2:10]]), and generous sharing from hearts transformed by God's grace ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 9#2 Corinthians 9 . 7|2 Corinthians 9:7]]). The peace-offering's joyful feast in God's presence becomes, through Christ, perpetual communion expressed in praise, obedience, and generosity—spiritual sacrifices continually offered and eternally accepted through the great High Priest.
