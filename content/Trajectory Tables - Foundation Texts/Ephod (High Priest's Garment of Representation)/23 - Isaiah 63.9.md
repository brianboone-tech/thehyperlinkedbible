### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 9|Isaiah 63:9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H06862|H06862]] צַר (*ṣar*) - "affliction, distress"
- [[Lexicon/H06440|H06440]] פָּנִים (*pānîm*) - "face, presence"
- [[Lexicon/H04397|H04397]] מַלְאָךְ (*mal'āḵ*) - "angel, messenger"
- [[Lexicon/H05375|H05375]] נָשָׂא (*nāśā'*) - "to lift, carry, bear"
- [[Lexicon/H03117|H03117]] יוֹם (*yôm*) - "day" (all the days of old)

**Context**: Isaiah 63:7-14 forms a hymn recounting God's saving acts in Israel's history, particularly the exodus. Verse 9 makes the profound statement that in Israel's affliction, God Himself was afflicted, and "the angel of his presence saved them." The verse concludes with covenant love language: "in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; he lifted them up and carried them all the days of old."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Echoes the wilderness wandering: "I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 4|Exodus 19:4]])
- Recalls [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 31|Deuteronomy 1:31]]: "the LORD your God carried you, as a man carries his son"
- The "angel of his presence" likely refers to the pre-incarnate Christ (cf. [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 20|Exodus 23:20-21]]; 33:14-15)
- This divine bearing is the reality that Aaron's ephod symbolized

**Type Classification**: Backward-Looking (Isaiah interprets exodus as divine bearing) and Forward-Looking (points to Christ's ultimate bearing).

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 9|Isaiah 63:9]] reveals that the ephod's symbolism pointed to divine reality: God Himself bears His people. Aaron's ephod bearing Israel's names foreshadowed the incarnation, where God takes human nature upon Himself. The "angel of his presence" is Christ pre-incarnate; the bearing and carrying anticipate Christ's redemptive work. Jesus bore our sins in His body ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]]), carries us as the Good Shepherd ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 15#Luke 15 . 5|Luke 15:5]]), and intercedes perpetually ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). The phrase "all the days of old" contrasts with Christ's eternal priesthood—Aaron bore Israel temporarily; Christ bears His elect forever. The shoulder stones on the ephod pointed to this greater truth: God incarnate bearing His people in strength and love.
