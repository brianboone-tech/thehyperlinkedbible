### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 6|Exodus 28:6-12]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H0646|H0646]] אֵפוֹד (*'ēp̄ôḏ*) - "ephod" (priestly vestment)
- [[Lexicon/H07718|H07718]] שֹׁהַם (*šōham*) - "onyx" (precious stone)
- [[Lexicon/H02146|H02146]] זִכָּרוֹן (*ziḵārôn*) - "memorial, remembrance"
- [[Lexicon/H07926|H07926]] שְׁכֶם (*šeḵem*) - "shoulder" (seat of strength)
- [[Lexicon/H08034|H08034]] שֵׁם (*šēm*) - "name" (personal identity)

**Context**: Exodus 28 details God's instructions for Aaron's high priestly garments, given while Israel camped at Sinai. The ephod is the foundational garment, described first (vv. 6-14), upon which the breastpiece will be attached. The ephod's distinctive feature is the two onyx stones mounted on the shoulder pieces, each engraved with six tribal names in birth order (vv. 9-10). Verse 12 provides the theological interpretation: "Aaron is to bear their names on his two shoulders as a memorial before the LORD."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 39#Exodus 39 . 2|Exodus 39:2-7]] reports the faithful execution of this command, repeating the "stones of remembrance" language verbatim
- The ephod becomes essential to priestly ministry ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 5|Exodus 29:5]]; [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 8#Leviticus 8 . 7|Leviticus 8:7]])
- Later references associate the ephod with seeking divine guidance ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 23#1 Samuel 23 . 9|1 Samuel 23:9-12]]; 30:7-8)
- [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]] records misuse—Gideon's ephod becomes an idol when divorced from Aaronic priesthood
- The ephod disappears after the exile, awaiting its fulfillment in the greater High Priest

**Type Classification**: Direct Type (explicitly commanded by God) and Forward-Looking (the "memorial before the LORD" language points beyond itself to perfect representation).

**Christological Connection**: The ephod bearing Israel's names on Aaron's shoulders points to Christ bearing His people before the Father in eternal intercession. Aaron's bearing was symbolic (stones), temporary (he died), and limited (Israel only). Christ's bearing is substantial (He knows His sheep personally), eternal ("always lives," [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]), and universal (elect from every nation). The shoulder signifies strength—Christ bears His people in the power of His mediatorial work. The memorial aspect finds fulfillment in Christ's perpetual remembrance of His own before the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 9|John 17:9]]: "I am praying for them... for those whom you have given me").
