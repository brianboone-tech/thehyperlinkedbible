### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 8#Judges 8 . 27|Judges 8:27]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H0646|H0646]] אֵפוֹד (*'ēp̄ôḏ*) - "ephod"
- [[Lexicon/H02181|H02181]] זָנָה (*zānâ*) - "to whore, commit fornication" (spiritual adultery)
- [[Lexicon/H04170|H04170]] מוֹקֵשׁ (*môqēš*) - "snare, trap"

**Context**: After defeating Midian, Gideon requests the golden earrings from the plunder. He makes an ephod weighing 1,700 shekels of gold (approximately 43 pounds) and places it in his hometown, Ophrah. Despite Gideon's earlier faithfulness, this ephod becomes an object of idolatrous worship: "all Israel whored after it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his family" (v. 27).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Demonstrates the danger of unauthorized worship, even with proper symbols
- Gideon was not from Levi, let alone Aaron's line—he had no authority to make an ephod
- The ephod divorced from Aaronic priesthood and tabernacle worship becomes an idol
- Foreshadows Israel's repeated cycle: deliverance → apostasy → judgment (Judges pattern)
- Contrasts with David's proper use of the ephod through Abiathar the priest ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 23#1 Samuel 23 . 9|1 Samuel 23:9]])

**Type Classification**: Negative example (misuse of type) demonstrating that typological significance depends on divine authorization.

**Christological Connection**: Gideon's unauthorized ephod demonstrates that legitimate priesthood and mediation come only through divine appointment. Aaron was chosen by God; Gideon appointed himself. This points forward to Christ, who "did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him, 'You are my Son'" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 5|Hebrews 5:5]]). Only Christ has divine authorization to bear God's people before the Father. All self-appointed mediators—whether Gideon's ephod, Micah's shrine (Judges 17), or modern false teachers—are snares. Christ alone is the way to the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]]); all other paths lead to spiritual adultery.
