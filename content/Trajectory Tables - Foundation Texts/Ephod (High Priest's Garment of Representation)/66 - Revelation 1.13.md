### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 13|Revelation 1:13]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3664|G3664]] ὅμοιος (*homoios*) - "like, similar to"
- [[Lexicon/G5207|G5207]] υἱός (*huios*) - "son"

- [[Lexicon/G5552|G5552]] χρυσός (*chrysos*) - "gold, golden"

**Context**: Revelation 1:9-20 records John's vision of the glorified Christ on Patmos. Verse 13 describes Christ standing "in the midst of the lampstands" (the seven churches, v. 20), clothed in a long robe with a golden sash around His chest. The imagery combines priestly (long robe, Exodus 28:4) and royal (golden sash, Daniel 10:5) elements, presenting Christ as eternal King-Priest.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Alludes to [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 13|Daniel 7:13]] (one like a son of man)
- Echoes [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 10#Daniel 10 . 5|Daniel 10:5]] (man clothed in linen with golden belt)
- Fulfills priestly garment imagery from Exodus 28 (ephod, robe, sash)
- Combines with royal imagery (Psalm 110—king and priest)

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Exodus 28:4, 39-40 (priestly robe and sash); Daniel 7:13 (Son of Man); Daniel 10:5 (angelic figure in priestly-royal attire); Zechariah 6:12-13 (Branch—priest on throne)
- **FROM OT**:
  - None (eschatological fulfillment)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]] - Lamb standing as though slain
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 16|Revelation 19:16]] - King of kings on thigh/robe
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 24|Hebrews 7:24-25]] - eternal priesthood
**Type Classification**: Eschatological fulfillment (Christ's eternal priestly glory).

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 13|Revelation 1:13]] shows the ephod's ultimate fulfillment—Christ glorified and eternally ministering as High Priest. Aaron wore the ephod bearing Israel's names temporarily; Christ appears in priestly robes bearing His churches (symbolized by stars in His hand, v. 16, 20) eternally. The long robe echoes the high priest's garments "for glory and beauty" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 2|Exodus 28:2]]), but Christ's glory infinitely surpasses Aaron's. The golden sash represents the binding of priestly and royal authority—Christ is both King and Priest forever. Where Aaron's ephod bore twelve tribal names on stones, Christ holds seven stars (the churches) in His right hand—His grip is secure, His knowledge perfect, His care perpetual. The ephod's memorial function ("before the LORD") finds consummation in Christ's ongoing presence "in the midst of the lampstands"—He ministers among His people in glory, interceding eternally, until all is complete in the new creation.
