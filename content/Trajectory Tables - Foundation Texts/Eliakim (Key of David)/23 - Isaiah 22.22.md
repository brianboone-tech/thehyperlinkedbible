### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 22#Isaiah 22 . 22|Isaiah 22:22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4601-4700#H4668|H4668]] מַפְתֵּחַ (maphtêaḥ) - "key, opener" - symbol of administrative authority to grant or deny access
- [[Lexicon/H7801-7900#H7926|H7926]] שְׁכֶם (šeḵem) - "shoulder" - place of burden-bearing and governmental responsibility
- [[Lexicon/H6601-6700#H6605|H6605]] פָּתַח (pâṯaḥ) - "to open" - to grant access, permission, opportunity
- [[Lexicon/H5401-5500#H5462|H5462]] סָגַר (sâḡar) - "to shut, close" - to deny access, exclude, forbid entry

**Greek Parallel** (Rev 3:7):
- [[Lexicon/G2801-2900#G2807|G2807]] κλείς (kleís) - "key" - John uses the same imagery, applying it directly to Christ

**Context**: This verse forms the theological center of the Eliakim oracle. The "key of the house of David" represents comprehensive authority over the royal household and, by extension, the Davidic covenant promises. Opening and shutting with finality signifies irrevocable decisions—what Eliakim establishes cannot be overturned by human authority.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The phrase "on his shoulder" creates an intertextual connection to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 6|Isaiah 9:6]]: "the government will be on His shoulders." Both texts point to ultimate Davidic authority.
- The "house of David" recalls God's covenant with David (2 Sam 7:11-16) that promised an eternal kingdom. Eliakim's authority over this "house" is provisional; the eternal King will come from David's line.
- [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 23|Judges 3:23-25]] provides background: keys could lock people in or out, making key-possession a matter of life and death.

**Type Classification**: Providential (Eliakim's appointment by divine sovereignty), Forward-looking (prophetic language exceeds historical fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 3#Revelation 3 . 7|Revelation 3:7]] explicitly applies this verse to Christ, demonstrating that Eliakim was a prophetic type. Where Eliakim held keys to an earthly palace, Christ holds "the key of David"—authority over the Davidic kingdom and Messianic promises. Where Eliakim could grant or deny access to the king's presence, Christ grants or denies access to the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]]). The "shoulder" imagery (Isa 22:22 and 9:6) shows that Christ bears the governmental weight that Eliakim could only temporarily symbolize. Christ's authority to "open and shut" extends to eternal salvation (Rev 1:18—keys of Death and Hades) and eschatological judgment (Rev 21:27—access to New Jerusalem).
