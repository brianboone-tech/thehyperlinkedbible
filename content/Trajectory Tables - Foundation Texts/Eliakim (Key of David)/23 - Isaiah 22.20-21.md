### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 22#Isaiah 22 . 20|Isaiah 22:20-21]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H401-500#H471|H471]] אֶלְיָקִים (ʼElyâqîym) - "God raises up, God sets up" - Eliakim's name itself is prophetic, pointing to one whom God establishes
- [[Lexicon/H5401-5500#H5650|H5650]] עֶבֶד (ʻebed) - "servant" - honorific title used for Moses, David, and the Messiah
- [[Lexicon/H4401-4500#H4475|H4475]] מֶמְשָׁלָה (memšâlâh) - "dominion, authority, rule" - administrative power over the royal household

**Context**: Isaiah 22:15-25 contrasts two palace administrators under Hezekiah: Shebna the unfaithful steward who prepared a grand tomb for himself (vv. 15-19), and Eliakim the faithful servant whom God would raise up to replace him. This oracle comes in the context of Jerusalem's deliverance from Assyrian invasion (701 BC), showing God's concern for faithful leadership during crisis.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The title "My servant" connects Eliakim to the faithful servant pattern in Isaiah: Moses (Num 12:7-8), David (2 Sam 7:5, 8), and ultimately the Suffering Servant (Isa 42:1; 52:13).
- The investiture with robe and sash recalls the high priestly garments (Ex 28:4, 39-40), suggesting Eliakim functions as a mediator between king and people.

**Type Classification**: Providential (God sovereignly appoints Eliakim), Forward-looking (language transcends the historical figure)

**Christological Connection**: Eliakim's appointment as "My servant" who will be "a father" to God's people points to Christ, the ultimate Servant (Phil 2:7) who is also "Everlasting Father" (Isa 9:6). Where Eliakim received delegated authority over Hezekiah's household, Christ possesses inherent authority over God's eternal household (Heb 3:6). The divine investiture with robe and authority foreshadows Christ's exaltation after His faithful service unto death (Phil 2:9-11).
