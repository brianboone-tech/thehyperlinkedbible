### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1-5]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2401-2500#H2415|H2415]] חֹטֶר (ḥōṭer) - "shoot, twig" - new growth from a cut-down tree
- [[Lexicon/H1401-1500#H1503|H1503]] גֶּזַע (gezaʻ) - "stump, stock" - the seemingly dead root of Jesse
- [[Lexicon/H5201-5300#H5342|H5342]] נֵצֶר (nêṣer) - "branch, sprout" - messianic term (cf. Matt 2:23, "Nazarene")
- [[Lexicon/H7301-7400#H7307|H7307]] רוּחַ (rûaḥ) - "Spirit" - sevenfold endowment of the Spirit of Yahweh

**Context**: Isaiah 11 continues the messianic trajectory from Isaiah 9:6-7, but adds crucial details: this King will come from David's line ("Jesse" = David's father) when that dynasty appears dead ("stump"). He will be endowed with the fullness of God's Spirit and will rule with perfect justice and righteousness—qualities no human king, including Eliakim, could achieve.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Isaiah's progression: 9:6-7 (divine Child-King) → 11:1-5 (Spirit-anointed Davidic Branch) → 22:22 (key-bearing steward). The "shoot from Jesse's stump" comes AFTER the dynasty's apparent collapse, connecting to post-exilic hope.
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 23#Jeremiah 23 . 5|Jeremiah 23:5-6]] and [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 3#Zechariah 3 . 8|Zechariah 3:8]]; 6:12 develop the "Branch" (נֵצֶר/צֶמַח) imagery further as a technical messianic term.
- The sevenfold Spirit (v. 2: wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, fear of the LORD) represents complete divine enablement beyond any human capacity.

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (direct messianic prophecy requiring supernatural fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: This prophecy demonstrates why Eliakim's stewardship was temporary and typological. Eliakim possessed delegated authority; the Branch possesses the fullness of God's Spirit (v. 2). Eliakim could administer an earthly household; the Branch "will not judge by what He sees... or decide by what He hears" (v. 3)—He possesses supernatural, perfect discernment. Eliakim could mediate justice for Jerusalem; the Branch "will judge the poor with righteousness and decide with equity for the humble of the earth" (v. 4)—universal, eschatological justice. The NT identifies Jesus as this Branch: anointed with the Spirit at baptism ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 22|Luke 3:22]]), descended from Jesse and David (Rom 1:3), exercising perfect judgment ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 22|John 5:22]], 30), bringing righteousness and peace (Eph 2:14-17). Where Eliakim wore a sash of authority (Isa 22:21), Christ's "sash will be righteousness, and faithfulness the belt around His hips" (Isa 11:5)—intrinsic, not delegated, perfection.
