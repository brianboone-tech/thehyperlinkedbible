### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 22#Isaiah 22 . 23|Isaiah 22:23-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3301-3400#H3489|H3489]] יָתֵד (yâṯêḏ) - "peg, tent peg, nail" - symbol of stability and permanence
- [[Lexicon/H4401-4500#H4725|H4725]] מָקוֹם (mâqôm) - "place, fixed location" - the peg is driven into a firm, immovable position
- [[Lexicon/H3401-3500#H3519|H3519]] כָּבוֹד (kâḇôḏ) - "glory, honor, weight" - all the household's honor rests on Eliakim
- [[Lexicon/H3601-3700#H3678|H3678]] כִּסֵּא (kissêʼ) - "throne, seat of authority" - Eliakim becomes a throne of glory for his father's house

**Context**: These verses complete the investiture oracle, showing both Eliakim's security (v. 23) and his comprehensive responsibility (v. 24). The imagery shifts from keys to construction—Eliakim will be like a peg driven firmly into a wall, secure enough to bear the weight of the entire household's "glory."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The "peg in a firm place" recalls the tent pegs that secured the tabernacle (Ex 27:19; 35:18; 38:20, 31)—foundational supports for God's dwelling place.
- [[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 9#Ezra 9 . 8|Ezra 9:8]] uses similar imagery: God has given Israel "a peg in His holy place"—secure standing in God's presence.
- However, [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 22#Isaiah 22 . 25|Isaiah 22:25]] adds a sobering note: "In that day... the peg driven into a firm place will give way." Even Eliakim's faithfulness is temporary. This points forward to One whose throne will never fail.

**Type Classification**: Providential (God sovereignly establishes Eliakim), Forward-looking (language exceeds historical fulfillment; v. 25 requires greater antitype)

**Christological Connection**: Where Eliakim was a "peg in a firm place," Christ is the eternal cornerstone (Isa 28:16; 1 Pet 2:6). Where all of Eliakim's father's house hung their glory on him (temporarily), all of God's household—the church—depends entirely on Christ eternally (Col 2:19). The warning in v. 25 that even Eliakim's peg will eventually fail demonstrates that no human mediator suffices. Christ alone is the foundation that "cannot be shaken" (Heb 12:28). Eliakim was a "throne of glory" for his household; Christ is "the throne of God and of the Lamb" for all eternity (Rev 22:3). The peg imagery finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ's resurrection—He was "driven into place" through death, but death could not hold Him. He is "alive forevermore" (Rev 1:18), the secure foundation upon which all believers' hope rests (Heb 6:19-20).
