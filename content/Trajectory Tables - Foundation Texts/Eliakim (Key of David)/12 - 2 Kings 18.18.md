### [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 18|2 Kings 18:18]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H401-500#H471|H471]] אֶלְיָקִים (ʼElyâqîym) - "God raises up" - Eliakim, son of Hilkiah
- [[Lexicon/H1-100#H1004|H1004]] בַּיִת (bayiṯ) - "house, household, palace" - Eliakim is "over the house"
- [[Lexicon/H5401-5500#H5608|H5608]] סֹפֵר (sōp̄êr) - "scribe, secretary" - Shebna demoted from steward to secretary

**Context**: This is the historical fulfillment of Isaiah's prophecy (Isa 22:20-23). By 701 BC, during Sennacherib's invasion of Judah, Eliakim has indeed replaced Shebna as palace administrator ("over the house"). The three-member delegation—Eliakim (palace administrator), Shebna (secretary), and Joah (recorder)—represents Hezekiah before Assyria's Rabshakeh, who blasphemes God and demands Jerusalem's surrender.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 22#Isaiah 22 . 15|Isaiah 22:15-25]] predicted Shebna's removal and Eliakim's promotion. [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 18|2 Kings 18:18]] confirms the prophecy's fulfillment.
- The phrase "over the house" (ʼăšer ʻal-habbayiṯ) echoes [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 39#Genesis 39 . 4|Genesis 39:4-5]] (Joseph over Potiphar's house) and [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 6|1 Kings 4:6]] (Ahishar over Solomon's palace)—a position of total administrative authority.
- The parallel account in [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 36#Isaiah 36 . 3|Isaiah 36:3]] is nearly identical, emphasizing the significance of this moment.

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly placed in office at critical moment), Forward-looking (points to ultimate faithful Steward)

**Christological Connection**: Eliakim's historical faithfulness as steward "over the house" during Judah's greatest crisis foreshadows Christ's perfect faithfulness as the Son "over God's house" (Heb 3:6). Where Eliakim stood between blasphemous empire and threatened people, Christ stands as eternal Mediator between holy God and sinful humanity (1 Tim 2:5). Where Eliakim tore his clothes in mourning over Assyria's blasphemy (2 Kgs 18:37), Christ bore the weight of all blasphemy and sin in His own body on the cross. Where Eliakim sought the prophet Isaiah for God's word (2 Kgs 19:2), Christ IS the Word incarnate ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). Eliakim's temporary stewardship points to Christ's eternal administration of God's household.
