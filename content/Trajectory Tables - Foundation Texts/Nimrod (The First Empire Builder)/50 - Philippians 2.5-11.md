### [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 5|Philippians 2:5-11]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3444|G3444]] μορφή (*morphē*) - "form, nature"
- [[Lexicon/G2316|G2316]] θεός (*theos*) - "God"
- [[Lexicon/G725|G725]] ἁρπαγμός (*harpagmos*) - "something grasped, seized"
- [[Lexicon/G2758|G2758]] κενόω (*kenoō*) - "to empty"
- [[Lexicon/G1401|G1401]] δοῦλος (*doulos*) - "slave, servant"
- [[Lexicon/G5013|G5013]] ταπεινόω (*tapeinoō*) - "to humble, make low"
- [[Lexicon/G5312|G5312]] ὑψόω (*hypsoō*) - "to exalt, lift up"
- [[Lexicon/G3686|G3686]] ὄνομα (*onoma*) - "name"

**Context**: Paul exhorts the Philippians to humility by pointing to Christ's example. This passage (likely an early Christian hymn) describes Christ's descent and exaltation: though existing in the "form of God" (μορφῇ θεοῦ), He did not consider equality with God "something to be grasped" (ἁρπαγμός), but "emptied Himself" (ἐκένωσεν), took the form of a servant, became human, and humbled Himself to death on a cross. Therefore God "highly exalted Him" (ὑπερύψωσεν) and gave Him "the name above every name"—so that every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- This passage inverts the pattern of [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 14#Isaiah 14 . 13|Isaiah 14:13-14]] (king of Babylon's five "I will ascend" statements).
- Where Adam grasped at being "like God" (Gen 3:5), Christ refused to grasp.
- The exaltation echoes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 45#Isaiah 45 . 23|Isaiah 45:23]] (every knee will bow) and 52:13 (the Servant will be "exalted and lifted up").

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 3:5 (grasping to be like God), Isaiah 14:13-14 (Babylon's king's pride), Isaiah 45:23 (every knee bows), Isaiah 52:13 (Servant exalted)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15|Colossians 1:15-20]] (Christ hymn), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]] (exact representation of God's nature)
- **PARALLEL**: John 13:3-5 (knowing He came from God, He washed feet)

**Type Classification**: Backward-Looking (Christ's humility recognized as inversion of Nimrod/Babylon pattern); Direct (deliberate contrast with Isaiah 14)

**Christological Connection**: Philippians 2 presents Christ as the anti-Nimrod. (1) **Grasping vs. Emptying**: Where Nimrod (and every empire-builder) grasps for power, Christ "did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, but emptied Himself" (vv. 6-7). This is the fundamental inversion of the Babylonian spirit. (2) **Ascending vs. Descending**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 14#Isaiah 14 . 13|Isaiah 14:13]]: "I will ascend to heaven." [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 7|Philippians 2:7-8]]: Christ "made Himself nothing... humbled Himself... even to death." True glory comes through descent, not ascent. (3) **Self-Exaltation vs. God's Exaltation**: Nimrod "began to be a mighty one on the earth" (Gen 10:8)—he made himself great. "Therefore God exalted Him" (v. 9)—Christ's exaltation comes from the Father, not self-assertion. (4) **Lord of Lords**: The name "above every name" (v. 9) means Christ now occupies the position every Nimrod sought but could not achieve. Universal sovereignty belongs to the Servant who died, not the hunter who conquered.
