### [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 2#Daniel 2 . 31|Daniel 2:31-45]]

**Aramaic/Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6755|H6755]] צֶלֶם (*tselem*) - "image, statue"
- [[Lexicon/H1722|H1722]] דְּהַב (*dehab*, Aramaic) - "gold"
- [[Lexicon/H3702|H3702]] כְּסַף (*kesaph*, Aramaic) - "silver"

- [[Lexicon/H69|H69]] אֶבֶן (*ʾeben*) - "stone"
- [[Lexicon/H4437|H4437]] מַלְכוּ (*malkû*, Aramaic) - "kingdom, sovereignty"

**Context**: Nebuchadnezzar's dream reveals the succession of world empires as a great statue: head of gold (Babylon), chest/arms of silver (Medo-Persia), belly/thighs of bronze (Greece), legs of iron (Rome), feet of iron and clay (divided kingdom). A stone "cut without hands" strikes the statue, destroys all human empire, and becomes "a great mountain that filled the whole earth" (v. 35). Daniel interprets this as God's eternal kingdom that will "crush all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, but it will itself endure forever" (v. 44).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Babylon as "head of gold" (v. 38) traces back to Nimrod's founding of Babylon (Gen 10:10).
- The progression from gold to iron-clay shows empire's decreasing glory and increasing instability.
- Daniel 7 presents the same succession as four beasts—emphasizing empire's beastly nature.
- The "stone not cut with hands" anticipates Christ (Ps 118:22; Isa 28:16) and His kingdom.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 10:10 (Nimrod founds Babylon), Isaiah 14:4 (Babylon judged)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 1|Daniel 7:1-28]] (parallel vision of four beasts), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 2#Psalm 2 . 9|Psalm 2:9]] (nations shattered)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 44|Matthew 21:44]] (stone that crushes), [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 20#Luke 20 . 18|Luke 20:18]] (falling on the stone), Revelation 17-18 (Babylon destroyed)

**Type Classification**: Forward-Looking (explicit prophecy of kingdom succession and divine kingdom); Providential (Babylon paradigmatic of human empire)

**Christological Connection**: The stone "cut without hands" finds fulfillment in Christ. (1) **Divine Origin**: The stone is "cut without hands"—not of human origin. Christ's kingdom is "not of this world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 18#John 18 . 36|John 18:36]]). (2) **Humble Beginnings, Universal End**: The small stone becomes "a great mountain that filled the whole earth" (v. 35). From Bethlehem's obscurity, Christ's kingdom spreads worldwide. (3) **Destruction of Empire**: The stone "strikes" and "crushes" all human kingdoms (vv. 34, 44-45). Christ's victory dismantles every "Nimrod" structure (1 Cor 15:24-25). (4) **Eternal Kingdom**: "It will itself endure forever" (v. 44). Unlike Babylon's dynasty or Rome's empire, Christ's kingdom has no end ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 33|Luke 1:33]]). (5) **Stone Christology**: Jesus identified Himself with this stone (Matt 21:44). Those who fall on it are broken (repentance); those it falls on are crushed (judgment). The Nimrods of history will be crushed by the Son of David.
