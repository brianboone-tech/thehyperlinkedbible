### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 14#Isaiah 14 . 4|Isaiah 14:4-15]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4912|H4912]] מָשָׁל (*mashal*) - "proverb, taunt song, parable"
- [[Lexicon/H4428|H4428]] מֶלֶךְ (*melek*) - "king"
- [[Lexicon/H894|H894]] בָּבֶל (*Babel*) - "Babylon"
- [[Lexicon/H1966|H1966]] הֵילֵל (*hêlēl*) - "morning star, shining one"
- [[Lexicon/H5927|H5927]] עָלָה (*ʿalah*) - "to ascend, go up"
- [[Lexicon/H7585|H7585]] שְׁאוֹל (*sheʾôl*) - "Sheol, the grave"
- [[Lexicon/H5945|H5945]] עֶלְיוֹן (*ʿelyon*) - "Most High"

**Context**: Isaiah 13-14 forms an oracle against Babylon, culminating in this taunt song (מָשָׁל) that Israel will recite over Babylon's fallen king. Though addressing a specific historical ruler (likely Nebuchadnezzar or a type of all Babylonian kings), the language transcends human kingship. The five "I will" statements (vv. 13-14) reveal the hubris driving all empire: self-deification. "Hêlēl, son of the dawn" (translated "Lucifer" in Latin) has been read as Satan, but the primary referent is the king of Babylon as heir to Nimrod's rebellious spirit.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- This taunt song echoes Genesis 3's temptation: "You will be like God" (Gen 3:5). Babylon's king embodies the serpent's promise.
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 13#Isaiah 13 . 19|Isaiah 13:19]] calls Babylon "the glory of kingdoms"—recalling Nimrod's founding of the first kingdom (Gen 10:10).
- Ezekiel 28's lament over Tyre's king uses similar motifs (Eden, precious stones, fall from height).
- Daniel 4 describes Nebuchadnezzar's actual hubris and divine humbling—a historical fulfillment of Isaiah's pattern.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 3:5 (be like God), Genesis 10:10 (Babylon founded), Genesis 11:4 (tower reaching heaven)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 28#Ezekiel 28 . 11|Ezekiel 28:11-19]] (parallel lament), [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 4#Daniel 4 . 30|Daniel 4:30-33]] (Nebuchadnezzar's pride and fall)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-11]] (Christ's opposite pattern), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 18#Revelation 18 . 7|Revelation 18:7]] (Babylon's self-exaltation)

**Type Classification**: Forward-Looking (prophecy anticipates all Babylon's downfall); Providential (Babylon exemplifies empire's pattern)

**Christological Connection**: Isaiah 14 establishes the contrast Christ fulfills. (1) **Ascending vs. Descending**: The king of Babylon says "I will ascend to heaven" (v. 13); Christ, though equal with God, "descended" to earth and even to death (Phil 2:6-8). Empire ascends by pride; Christ descends by love. (2) **Grasping vs. Emptying**: "I will make myself like the Most High" (v. 14) embodies grasping for deity. Christ "did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, but emptied Himself" (Phil 2:6-7). (3) **Cast Down vs. Exalted**: Babylon's king is "brought down to Sheol" (v. 15); the self-emptying Christ is "highly exalted" by God (Phil 2:9). Human pride leads to abasement; divine humility leads to glory. (4) **True Morning Star**: If "Hêlēl" means "morning star," then Christ is the true Morning Star (Rev 22:16)—not self-proclaimed but Father-given.
