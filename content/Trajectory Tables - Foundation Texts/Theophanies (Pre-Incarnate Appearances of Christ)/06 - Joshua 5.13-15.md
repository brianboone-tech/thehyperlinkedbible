### [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 5#Joshua 5 . 13|Joshua 5:13-15]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H376|H376]] אִישׁ (*ish*) - "a man" (5:13)
- [[Lexicon/H8025|H8025]] שָׁלַף (*shalaph*) - "drawn" (5:13, of sword)
- [[Lexicon/H8269|H8269]] שַׂר (*sar*) - "commander/prince" (5:14, 15)
- [[Lexicon/H6635|H6635]] צְבָא (*tseva*) - "army/host" (5:14, 15)
- [[Lexicon/H6944|H6944]] קֹדֶשׁ (*qodesh*) - "holy" (5:15)

**Context**:

Joshua was near Jericho, surveying Israel's first conquest in the Promised Land, when "he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man was standing before him with his drawn sword in his hand" (5:13). The unexpected appearance of an armed warrior prompted Joshua's challenge: "Are you for us, or for our adversaries?" The figure's reply transcends the question: "No; but I am the commander of the army of the LORD. Now I have come."

Joshua's immediate response reveals recognition of divine presence: he "fell on his face to the earth and worshiped" (5:14). This worship is accepted—contrasted with Revelation 19:10 and 22:8-9 where angels refuse worship, commanding "Worship God!" The Commander then speaks with divine authority: "Take off your sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy" (5:15)—identical to God's words to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:5).

**OT-to-OT Development**:

The verbal parallel to Exodus 3:5 is exact: "Take off (שַׁל, shal) your sandals (נְעָלֶיךָ, ne'aleyka) from your feet, for the place (הַמָּקוֹם, hammaqom) where you are standing is holy (קֹדֶשׁ, qodesh) ground." This identifies both figures as the same divine Person—the Angel of the LORD who makes ground holy by His presence.

The title "commander of the army of the LORD" (sar-tseva YHWH) appears elsewhere for Michael (Dan 10:21, 12:1), but Michael refuses worship and is called "one of the chief princes" (Dan 10:13), whereas this Commander stands alone and accepts worship. The "army of the LORD" refers to heavenly hosts (1 Kings 22:19, Psalm 103:21), identifying this Commander as leader of angelic forces—a role belonging to the Second Person who comes as divine warrior.

2 Kings 6:17 reveals what Elisha's servant saw: "the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha"—the heavenly army led by this divine Commander, protecting God's prophet.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged appearance) and **Forward-Looking** (points explicitly to Christ as divine warrior leading God's people to victory and rest).

**Christological Connection**:

This theophany reveals the pre-incarnate Christ in warrior form, anticipating His role as conquering King. Three factors confirm divine identity:

1. **Accepted worship** (5:14): Angels refuse worship (Rev 19:10, 22:9), but this Commander accepts Joshua's prostration without rebuke. Only God receives worship.

2. **Identical language to burning bush** (5:15): "Take off your sandals... holy ground" mirrors [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 5|Exodus 3:5]] exactly, identifying this Commander with the Angel who appeared to Moses. Both theophanies use personal presence to make ground holy.

3. **Supreme military authority**: The title "commander of the army of the LORD" places this figure above all angelic hosts. Michael is "one of the chief princes" (Dan 10:13), but this Commander stands alone as supreme leader of heaven's armies.

The trajectory from Joshua 5 to Revelation 19 is direct: the divine warrior who appeared with drawn sword before Jericho is the same who appears on white horse leading heaven's armies (Rev 19:11-16). The names reveal identity: Joshua (יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Yehoshua, "YHWH saves") is Hebrew for Jesus (Ἰησοῦς, Iesous). The Commander who led Joshua into rest is Jesus who leads His people into eternal rest (Heb 4:8-11).

As Owen observed: "Christ appeared as warrior to Joshua because His ultimate work includes not merely dying for sin but conquering all enemies. He who appeared with sword drawn before Jericho will appear on white horse, conquering and to conquer."
