### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 23|Revelation 21:23]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1391|G1391]] δόξα (doxa) - glory
- [[Lexicon/G5457|G5457]] φῶς (phōs) - light
- [[Lexicon/G3088|G3088]] λύχνος (lychnos) - lamp
- [[Lexicon/G721|G721]] ἀρνίον (arnion) - lamb

**Context**: John describes the New Jerusalem: "And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb." The eternal city needs no created light sources because God's glory—mediated through the Lamb—illuminates all things.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 19|Isaiah 60:19-20]] - "The LORD will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory"
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 21|Exodus 13:21]] - Pillar of fire giving light by night
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 43#Ezekiel 43 . 2|Ezekiel 43:2]] - Glory of God filling the temple
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 5|Revelation 22:5]] - "They will need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light"

**Type Classification**: Eschatological consummation

**Christological Connection**: The progression of theophanies reaches its eternal culmination here. What began as temporary, terrifying appearances (burning bush, Sinai thunder) becomes the permanent, life-giving light of the eternal city. The glory that Israel could not bear to look upon ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 30|Exodus 34:30]]) now illuminates the New Jerusalem through "the Lamb." The trajectory is complete: from fleeting theophanies to incarnation to eternal, unmediated presence. The Lamb who was slain is the lamp of the eternal city—the light that Isaiah promised (60:19) finds its fulfillment in Christ.
