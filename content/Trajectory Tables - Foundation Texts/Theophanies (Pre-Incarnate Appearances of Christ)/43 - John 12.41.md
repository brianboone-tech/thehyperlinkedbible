### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 41|John 12:41]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1391|G1391]] δόξα (doxa) - glory
- [[Lexicon/G3708|G3708]] ὁράω (horaō) - to see, perceive

**Context**: John quotes Isaiah 6:10 about Israel's hardened hearts, then makes the stunning declaration: "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him." John explicitly identifies the glory Isaiah saw in the temple (Isaiah 6:1-5) as the glory of Christ—the pre-incarnate Son.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 6#Isaiah 6 . 1|Isaiah 6:1-5]] - Isaiah's vision of the LORD seated on the throne, high and lifted up
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 1|Isaiah 53:1]] - "Who has believed what he heard from us?" (quoted in [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 38|John 12:38]])
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]] - "We have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father"

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking (recognized from NT vantage); Direct theophany

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 41|John 12:41]] is one of the clearest NT statements that OT theophanies were appearances of the pre-incarnate Christ. When Isaiah "saw the LORD sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up," he was seeing the Son in His eternal glory. This confirms that the God who appeared to Abraham, Moses, and Isaiah is the same One who "became flesh and dwelt among us" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). The pattern of divine revelation finds its ultimate expression in the incarnation.
