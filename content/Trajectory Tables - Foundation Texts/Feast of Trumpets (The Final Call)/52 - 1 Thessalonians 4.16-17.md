### [[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 4#1 Thessalonians 4 . 16|1 Thessalonians 4:16-17]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2758|κέλευσμα]] (*keleusma*) - "cry of command, shout"
- [[Lexicon/G5401-5500#G5456|φωνῇ ἀρχαγγέλου]] (*phōnē archangelou*) - "voice of an archangel"
- [[Lexicon/G4401-4500#G4536|σάλπιγγι θεοῦ]] (*salpingi theou*) - "trumpet of God"
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2597|καταβήσεται]] (*katabēsetai*) - "will descend"
- [[Lexicon/G3401-3500#G3498|νεκροὶ ἐν Χριστῷ]] (*nekroi en Christō*) - "dead in Christ"
- [[Lexicon/G0001-0100#G0450|ἀναστήσονται]] (*anastēsontai*) - "will rise"
- [[Lexicon/G0701-0800#G0726|ἁρπαγησόμεθα]] (*harpagēsometha*) - "will be caught up, snatched away"
- [[Lexicon/G3401-3500#G3507|νεφέλαις]] (*nephelais*) - "clouds"

**Context**: Paul comforts Thessalonian believers grieving their deceased loved ones by revealing Christ's return sequence: the Lord descends with commanding shout, archangel's voice, and God's trumpet; the dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together with them in clouds to meet the Lord. This provides resurrection hope—death doesn't separate believers from Christ's triumphant gathering.

**Connections**:

- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 30|Matthew 24:30-31]] (Son of Man coming with trumpet), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 52|1 Corinthians 15:52]] (last trumpet), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 9|Acts 1:9-11]] (taken up in cloud, will return same way), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 7|Revelation 1:7]] (coming with clouds)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 4#1 Thessalonians 4 . 16|1 Thessalonians 4:16-17]] describes Christ's glorious return as trumpet-announced gathering. Jesus ascended in clouds ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 9|Acts 1:9]]) and will return in clouds—the same Jesus who left will return the same way ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 11|Acts 1:11]]). The "trumpet of God" announces His arrival: divine trumpet for divine Person. The threefold sound (shout, archangel, trumpet) heralds the King of kings. His descent reverses His ascension—He came down in incarnation, ascended in glorification, descends again for consummation. The resurrection demonstrates Christ's power over death: "He will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body, by the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 21|Philippians 3:21]]). The dead rising "first" shows priority—those who died in faith aren't disadvantaged but honored. Then living believers are "caught up"—the rapture transforms and transports simultaneously. The meeting "in the air" recalls ancient custom of citizens greeting arriving dignitary and escorting him. Believers meet descending Christ and accompany Him in triumphal entry. The result: "always with the Lord"—permanent, unbroken, eternal fellowship. Death couldn't separate believers from Christ ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 38|Romans 8:38-39]]), and resurrection ensures they're never separated again. The trumpet that gathered Israel to temporary assembly gathers believers to eternal assembly. The Feast of Trumpets memorial becomes resurrection reality. What OT trumpets prefigured (gathering God's people), Christ's trumpet accomplishes finally. The "last trumpet" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 52|1 Corinthians 15:52]]) is final because after it, all believers are glorified, death is defeated, and Christ reigns with His gathered people forever. This is ultimate comfort: death is temporary separation, resurrection is permanent reunion, Christ's return is certain hope.
