### [[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 2#Joel 2 . 1|Joel 2:1]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H8601-8700#H8628|תִּקְעוּ שׁוֹפָר]] (*tiqʿû šôpār*) - "blow the trumpet"
- [[Lexicon/H6701-6800#H6726|בְּצִיּוֹן]] (*bəṣiyyôn*) - "in Zion"
- [[Lexicon/H7401-7500#H7321|הָרִיעוּ]] (*hārîʿû*) - "sound an alarm, shout"
- [[Lexicon/H7301-7400#H7264|יִרְגְּזוּ]] (*yirgəzû*) - "tremble, quake"
- [[Lexicon/H3101-3200#H3117|יוֹם יְהוָה]] (*yôm YHWH*) - "Day of the LORD"
- [[Lexicon/H7101-7200#H7138|קָרוֹב]] (*qārôb*) - "near, close"

**Context**: Joel commands trumpet blast in Zion as alarm because the Day of the LORD approaches—a day of divine judgment and intervention. The trumpet summons Israel to repentance before catastrophic locust plague (immediate judgment) and eschatological day of the LORD (ultimate judgment). The trumpet's alarm function warns of imminent danger, calling people to prepare for God's coming.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 10#Numbers 10 . 9|Numbers 10:9]] (blow alarm in battle), [[Readable Bible/36 - Zephaniah/Zephaniah 1#Zephaniah 1 . 14|Zephaniah 1:14-16]] (Day of LORD, trumpet and battle cry)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 2#Joel 2 . 15|Joel 2:15-16]] (blow trumpet, consecrate fast), [[Readable Bible/30 - Amos/Amos 3#Amos 3 . 6|Amos 3:6]] (Is trumpet blown in city and people not afraid?)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 29|Matthew 24:29-31]] (coming of Son of Man, trumpet call), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 6#Revelation 6 . 17|Revelation 6:17]] (great day of wrath has come), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 8#Revelation 8 . 7|Revelation 8:7-9:21]] (trumpet judgments)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 2#Joel 2 . 1|Joel 2:1]]'s alarm trumpet finds fulfillment in Christ's return announced by divine trumpet. Joel's "Day of the LORD" becomes "the day of our Lord Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 8|1 Corinthians 1:8]]). Jesus describes His return with Joel-like imagery: "They will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 30|Matthew 24:30-31]]). For believers, the trumpet brings salvation—resurrection and gathering to Christ. For unbelievers, it brings judgment—they will say, "The great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 6#Revelation 6 . 17|Revelation 6:17]]). Joel's locust plague prefigures Revelation's trumpet judgments (Revelation 8-9) that devastate earth progressively. The trumpet's alarm function creates present urgency: "Now is the day of salvation" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 6#2 Corinthians 6 . 2|2 Corinthians 6:2]]). Paul warns: "The Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance on those who do not know God" ([[Readable Bible/53 - 2 Thessalonians/2 Thessalonians 1#2 Thessalonians 1 . 7|2 Thessalonians 1:7-8]]). Yet for those in Christ, the Day brings hope: "The Lord himself will descend from heaven... with the sound of the trumpet of God" ([[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 4#1 Thessalonians 4 . 16|1 Thessalonians 4:16]]) to gather His own. Joel's "it is near" becomes believers' "Maranatha—Come, Lord Jesus!" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 20|Revelation 22:20]]). The trumpet that made Joel's generation tremble will make the final generation either rejoice (if in Christ) or despair (if rejecting Him). The alarm sounded—will you heed the warning?
