### [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 12|Romans 15:12]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4491|G4491]] ῥίζα (rhiza) - root
- [[Lexicon/G2421|G2421]] Ἰεσσαί (Iessai) - Jesse
- [[Lexicon/G450|G450]] ἀνίστημι (anistēmi) - to rise up, arise
- [[Lexicon/G757|G757]] ἄρχω (archō) - to rule, reign
- [[Lexicon/G1484|G1484]] ἔθνος (ethnos) - nation, Gentile
- [[Lexicon/G1679|G1679]] ἐλπίζω (elpizō) - to hope

**Context**: Paul concludes his argument for Jew-Gentile unity in the church (Romans 14:1-15:13) with a catena of four OT quotations demonstrating that Gentile inclusion was always God's plan. Romans 15:12 quotes Isaiah 11:10 (LXX) as the climax: the Root of Jesse will arise to rule Gentiles, and they will place their hope in Him.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Quotes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 10|Isaiah 11:10]] LXX, which interprets the Hebrew "banner/signal" (נֵס) as "to rule" (ἄρχειν)
- The LXX messianic reading emphasizes royal authority over nations
- "Root of Jesse" connects to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1]]'s Branch (נֵצֶר) imagery
- Paul sees Christ's resurrection as the "arising" (ἀνιστάμενος) Isaiah prophesied

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (Isaiah anticipated Gentile inclusion), Direct fulfillment in Christ

**Christological Connection**: Paul identifies Jesus as the Root of Jesse who has arisen (through resurrection) to rule the nations. The verb ἀνιστάμενος ("arising") carries resurrection overtones in Paul's usage. The Gentiles' hope in Christ fulfills Isaiah's vision of nations streaming to the Branch. Significantly, Paul places this quotation as the climax of his catena (after [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 18#Psalm 18 . 49|Psalm 18:49]]; [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 32#Deuteronomy 32 . 43|Deuteronomy 32:43]]; [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 117#Psalm 117 . 1|Psalm 117:1]]), showing that the Branch prophecy represents the fullest expression of God's plan for the nations. Christ's reign is not through military conquest but through the gospel - Gentiles willingly placing their hope in Him.
