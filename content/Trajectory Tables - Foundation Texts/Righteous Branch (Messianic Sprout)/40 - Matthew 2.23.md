### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 23|Matthew 2:23]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3478|G3478]] Ναζαρέτ (Nazaret) - Nazareth
- [[Lexicon/G3480|G3480]] Ναζωραῖος (Nazōraios) - Nazarene, one from Nazareth
- [[Lexicon/G4396|G4396]] προφήτης (prophētēs) - prophet (plural: "through the prophets")

**Hebrew Background**:
- [[Lexicon/H5342|H5342]] נֵצֶר (netser) - branch, shoot (Isaiah 11:1)

**Context**: Joseph settles in Nazareth after returning from Egypt, fulfilling what was spoken "through the prophets" (plural) that the Messiah would be called a Nazarene. No single OT text contains this exact phrase, indicating Matthew sees a composite or wordplay fulfillment.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1]] uses נֵצֶר (netser) for the Branch from Jesse's roots
- The phonetic similarity between נֵצֶר (netser) and Nazareth (Ναζαρέτ) creates a prophetic wordplay
- Nazareth's obscurity ("Can anything good come from Nazareth?" [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 46|John 1:46]]) reflects Isaiah's "stump" - glory from apparent insignificance
- The plural "prophets" suggests Matthew sees this as a pattern, not a single prediction

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1]] - netser/Branch
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 2|Isaiah 53:2-3]] - despised servant from dry ground
- **FROM OT**:
  - All Branch prophecies find geographical embodiment in Jesus' hometown
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 45|John 1:45-46]] - Nathanael's skepticism
  - [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 24#Acts 24 . 5|Acts 24:5]] - "sect of the Nazarenes"
**Type Classification**: Backward-looking (recognized as fulfillment only from NT perspective), Providential Type

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' identification as "the Nazarene" (ὁ Ναζωραῖος) becomes so prominent that early Christians are called "the sect of the Nazarenes" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 24#Acts 24 . 5|Acts 24:5]]). The wordplay reveals that even Jesus' geographical location proclaims His messianic identity. From the "stump" of Jesse, from the despised village of Nazareth, from apparent insignificance and poverty, God brought forth the glorious Branch. This pattern - glory through humility, exaltation through abasement - characterizes Christ's entire ministry and is reflected in His followers' experience.
