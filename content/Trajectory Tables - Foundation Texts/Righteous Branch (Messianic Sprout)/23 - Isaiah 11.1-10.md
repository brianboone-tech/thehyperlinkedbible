### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1-10]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2415|H2415]] חֹטֶר (choter) - rod, shoot, twig
- [[Lexicon/H5342|H5342]] נֵצֶר (netser) - branch, shoot, sprout
- [[Lexicon/H1503|H1503]] גֶּזַע (geza) - trunk, stump, stock
- [[Lexicon/H8328|H8328]] שֹׁרֶשׁ (shoresh) - root
- [[Lexicon/H3448|H3448]] יִשַׁי (Yishai) - Jesse (David's father)
- [[Lexicon/H7307|H7307]] רוּחַ (ruach) - spirit, breath, wind

**Context**: Following the Assyrian judgment (ch. 10), Isaiah prophesies hope through a new Davidic king. The "stump of Jesse" imagery suggests the Davidic dynasty cut down to apparent death, yet from it springs new life. The Spirit's sevenfold endowment (v. 2) enables righteous rule.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Uses נֵצֶר (netser) rather than צֶמַח (tsemach), providing the wordplay for "Nazarene"
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 4#Isaiah 4 . 2|Isaiah 4:2]]'s "Branch of the LORD" is here identified as from Jesse's line
- The peaceable kingdom (vv. 6-9) reverses the curse of Genesis 3
- Verse 10 extends the Branch's rule to Gentiles ("nations will seek Him")

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking, Providential Type

**Christological Connection**: Matthew's identification of Jesus as "Nazarene" (2:23) likely involves wordplay on נֵצֶר (netser/branch). At Jesus' baptism, the Spirit descends and remains on Him ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 32|John 1:32-33]]), fulfilling the sevenfold anointing. Jesus explicitly claims Isaiah 11's context when He reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18-21]]). Paul cites [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 10|Isaiah 11:10]] in [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 12|Romans 15:12]] to demonstrate that Christ's reign extends to Gentiles. In Revelation, Jesus claims both titles: "Root of David" (divine origin) and "Offspring of David" (human descent) - the paradox Isaiah presents is resolved in the incarnation.
