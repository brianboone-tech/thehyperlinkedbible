### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 6#Revelation 6 . 9|Revelation 6:9-11]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G101-200#G129|G129]] αἷμα (haima) - blood
- [[Lexicon/G1501-1600#G1556|G1556]] ἐκδικέω (ekdikeō) - to avenge, vindicate
- [[Lexicon/G2901-3000#G2919|G2919]] κρίνω (krinō) - to judge, decide
- [[Lexicon/G5401-5500#G5456|G5456]] φωνή (phōnē) - voice, sound

**Context**: At the fifth seal, John sees martyred souls under the altar crying out: "O Sovereign Lord, holy and true, how long before you will judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?" The blood of the faithful continues to speak, echoing Abel's cry.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 10|Genesis 4:10]] - Abel's blood "cries out" from the ground
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 24#Ezekiel 24 . 7|Ezekiel 24:7-8]] - Jerusalem's blood "to cause wrath to come up"
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 79#Psalm 79 . 10|Psalm 79:10]] - "Let the avenging of the outpoured blood of your servants be known"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 10|Genesis 4:10]] - Same motif: innocent blood crying for justice
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 7|Leviticus 4:7]] - Blood under the altar (martyrs "under the altar")
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 2|Revelation 19:2]] - "He has avenged... the blood of his servants"

**Type Classification**: **Eschatological** - The blood-speaks motif continues into final judgment

**Christological Connection**: The martyrs' blood-cry demonstrates that Abel's voice was not silenced but multiplied. Yet this cry exists within Christ's greater voice:

1. **Location**: The souls are "under the altar"—the place where Levitical blood was poured (Lev 4:7)
2. **Question**: "How long?"—acknowledging that vindication is certain but timing is God's
3. **Answer**: They are given white robes and told to wait—their blood will be avenged (19:2)

Christ's blood speaks mercy for believers; martyrs' blood speaks justice against persecutors. Both voices are heard; both will be answered. The cross establishes that innocent blood's cry will never go unheard—whether crying for pardon or for judgment.
