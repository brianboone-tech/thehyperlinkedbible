### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 3|John 10:3-4]]

**Context**: Jesus describes Himself as the Good Shepherd in contrast to thieves and hirelings. The shepherd "calls his own sheep by name and leads them out." This personal knowing—calling each sheep by its individual name—emphasizes the intimacy of Christ's knowledge of His elect. The sheep recognize His voice and follow because they belong to Him. This echoes Moses' prayer for a shepherd-leader in Numbers 27:15-18.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- τὰ ἴδια πρόβατα (*ta idia probata*) - "his own sheep" (possession, belonging)
- φωνεῖ κατ᾽ ὄνομα (*phōnei kat' onoma*) - "calls by name" (individual, personal knowledge)
- ἀκούουσιν τῆς φωνῆς (*akouousin tēs phōnēs*) - "hear his voice" (responsive recognition)
- ἀκολουθεῖ (*akolouthei*) - "follow" (discipleship, trust)

**Christological Connection**: "Calls his own sheep by name" is the pastoral expression of the book of life. Christ does not call anonymously; He knows each elect person individually, by name, from before the foundation of the world ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 13#Revelation 13 . 8|Revelation 13:8]]). The sheep "hear his voice" because their names are written in His book—they were given to Christ by the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 37|John 6:37]]; 17:6). This personal knowledge provides assurance: the Shepherd who calls you by name will never forget that name, never lose that sheep. "I know my own" (10:14) parallels "the Lord knows those who are his" ([[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 2#2 Timothy 2 . 19|2 Timothy 2:19]]). The book of life is not a cold registry but the Shepherd's intimate knowledge of every sheep entrusted to Him.
