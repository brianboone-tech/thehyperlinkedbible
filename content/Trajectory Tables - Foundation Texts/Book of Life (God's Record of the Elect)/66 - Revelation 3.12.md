### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 3#Revelation 3 . 12|Revelation 3:12]]

**Context**: Christ's promise to the overcomer in Philadelphia: "I will make him a pillar in the temple of my God... I will write on him the name of my God, and the name of the city of my God, the new Jerusalem... and my own new name." This threefold naming signifies permanent identification with God, His city, and Christ. The promise echoes Isaiah 62:2 where Zion receives "a new name that the mouth of the LORD will give."

**Greek Key Terms**:
- στῦλον (*stylon*) - "pillar" (permanent, load-bearing, immovable)
- γράψω (*grapsō*) - "I will write" (future, Christ inscribes His own)
- τὸ ὄνομα τοῦ θεοῦ (*to onoma tou theou*) - "the name of my God" (divine ownership)
- τὸ ὄνομά μου τὸ καινόν (*to onoma mou to kainon*) - "my own new name" (Christ's eschatological identity)

**Christological Connection**: The threefold naming in [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 3#Revelation 3 . 12|Revelation 3:12]] connects directly to the book of life. Those whose names are written in Christ's book receive His name inscribed on them. The relationship is reciprocal: Christ writes the believer's name in His book; Christ writes His name on the believer. This mutual inscription guarantees eternal security—"I will never blot his name out of the book of life" (3:5). The pillar imagery suggests permanence: unlike loose stones that can be removed, pillars are essential and immovable. Those named by Christ are fixed in God's temple forever, bearing the triune God's identity (Father's name, New Jerusalem's name, Christ's new name), sealed by their inclusion in the Lamb's book of life.
