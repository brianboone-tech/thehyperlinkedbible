### [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 19#1 Kings 19 . 10|1 Kings 19:10]]

**Context**: After his victory on Mount Carmel, Elijah flees from Jezebel's death threat and despairs at Mount Horeb. He believes himself alone: "I am the only one left, and they are seeking my life." God's response reveals that 7,000 remain who have not bowed to Baal (v. 18)—a faithful remnant unknown to Elijah but known to God. Paul cites this narrative in Romans 11:2-4 to prove that God always preserves a remnant "chosen by grace."

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- קַנֹּא קִנֵּאתִי (*qanno' qinneti*) - "I have been very zealous" (intensive verb form)
- בְּרִיתְךָ (*beriteka*) - "Your covenant" (Israel's relationship with God)
- נוֹתַרְתִּי אֲנִי לְבַדִּי (*notarti ani levaddi*) - "I alone am left" (Elijah's perception)
- שִׁבְעַת אֲלָפִים (*shiv'at alafim*) - "seven thousand" (God's hidden remnant, v. 18)

**Christological Connection**: The 7,000 whom God "reserved" for Himself ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 4|Romans 11:4]], κατέλιπον ἐμαυτῷ) demonstrates that God's book of life has names unknown even to prophets. Elijah saw apostasy everywhere; God saw 7,000 faithful. This pattern recurs throughout redemptive history: a visible church larger than the true church, but God knowing perfectly "those who are his" ([[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 2#2 Timothy 2 . 19|2 Timothy 2:19]]). Paul's application in Romans 11 establishes that the remnant exists "by grace"—not by human faithfulness but by sovereign preservation. The names in God's book are secure because God keeps them, not because they keep themselves. Christ's church may appear small, but the Lamb's book of life contains "a great multitude that no one could number" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]).
