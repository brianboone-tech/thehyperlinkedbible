### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 27#Numbers 27 . 15|Numbers 27:15-18]]

**Context**: Facing his impending death, Moses asks God to appoint a shepherd-leader "over the congregation, who may go out before them and come in before them, who may lead them out and bring them in, that the congregation of the LORD may not be as sheep that have no shepherd." God designates Joshua, "a man in whom is the Spirit." Jesus alludes to this passage in John 10 when He identifies Himself as the Good Shepherd who calls His sheep "by name" and leads them out.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- רֹעֶה (*ro'eh*) - "shepherd" (leader who knows and guides his flock)
- צֹאן (*tso'n*) - "sheep/flock" (God's people needing leadership)
- אֲשֶׁר־רוּחַ בּוֹ (*asher-ruach bo*) - "in whom is the Spirit" (divine empowerment)
- לִפְנֵיהֶם (*lifneyhem*) - "before them" (shepherd goes ahead of sheep)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 48#Genesis 48 . 15|Genesis 48:15]] - "The God who has been my shepherd all my life"
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 1|Exodus 3:1]] - Moses as shepherd, preparation for leading Israel
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 34#Ezekiel 34 . 23|Ezekiel 34:23]] - "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David"
  - [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 5#Micah 5 . 4|Micah 5:4]] - Messianic ruler will "shepherd his flock"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 3|John 10:3-4]] - "He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out... the sheep follow him, for they know his voice"

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' Good Shepherd discourse (John 10) echoes Moses' prayer. The shepherd "calls his own sheep by name"—not anonymously, but with personal knowledge of each one. This connects directly to the book of life: God knows His elect by name, having written their names before the foundation of the world. Just as Joshua was appointed to lead God's people into rest, Jesus (the same Hebrew name, יְהוֹשֻׁעַ/Yehoshua) leads His sheep to eternal pastures. The shepherd metaphor establishes that salvation is not mass evacuation but personal calling—each sheep known, named, and led individually by the Shepherd whose book contains their names.
