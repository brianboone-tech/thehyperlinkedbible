### [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 2|Romans 11:2-4]]

**Context**: Paul defends God's faithfulness to Israel despite widespread Jewish unbelief. His argument: "God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew." The "foreknew" (προέγνω) echoes Romans 8:29's predestination language. Paul then cites Elijah's experience: God reserved 7,000 who had not bowed to Baal. This proves that even in apparent apostasy, God preserves a remnant according to election.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- προέγνω (*proegnō*) - "foreknew" (aorist, God's eternal predetermining knowledge)
- κατέλιπον ἐμαυτῷ (*katelipon emautō*) - "I have reserved for myself" (divine preservation)
- κατ᾽ ἐκλογὴν χάριτος (*kat' eklogēn charitos*) - "according to election by grace" (v. 5)
- λεῖμμα (*leimma*) - "remnant" (those preserved by God's choice)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's argument proves that the book of life has always existed. God "foreknew" His people—not merely knowing facts about them but personally electing them before time. The 7,000 God "reserved for myself" were in His book; their perseverance was not self-achieved but divinely preserved. This pattern continues: "So too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace" (11:5). The book of life is the record of those foreknown, and God will not reject those He foreknew. Christ intercedes for those the Father gave Him ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 6|John 17:6-12]]), ensuring that everyone whose name is in the Lamb's book will be saved. Election guarantees perseverance because God keeps His own.
