### [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 2|Galatians 5:2-6]]

**Context**: Paul issues urgent warning: "Look: I, Paul, say to you that if you accept circumcision, Christ will be of no advantage to you... You are severed from Christ, you who would be justified by the law... For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but only faith working through love." This declares physical circumcision obsolete and dangerous—seeking justification through it severs from Christ. What once marked covenant entrance now indicates covenant rejection when trusted for righteousness.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 10|Genesis 17:10-14]] (circumcision as covenant sign), [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 6|Deuteronomy 30:6]] (God will circumcise your heart)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 2#Galatians 2 . 21|Galatians 2:21]] (if righteousness through law, Christ died for no purpose), [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 10|Galatians 3:10]] (all who rely on works of law are under curse), [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 15|Galatians 6:15]] (neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts, but new creation)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 2|Galatians 5:2-6]] declares "if you accept circumcision, Christ will be of no advantage to you," showing circumcision's obsolescence when trusted for righteousness. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 17#Genesis 17 . 10|Genesis 17:10-14]] established circumcision as covenant sign for Abraham's descendants—necessary marker of covenant membership. For millennia, males were circumcised on the eighth day, incorporated into Israel's covenant community. But circumcision was always sign pointing to spiritual reality, not ground of salvation. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 11|Romans 4:11]] calls it "sign and seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while uncircumcised"—Abraham was justified by faith before circumcision. Christ's coming fulfilled what circumcision symbolized. His death accomplished the "putting off the body of flesh" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 11|Colossians 2:11]]); His Spirit circumcises hearts ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 29|Romans 2:29]]); His righteousness provides what law-keeping couldn't—perfect standing before God. Seeking circumcision for righteousness implies Christ's work is insufficient, requiring supplementation by human ritual. This "severs from Christ" because it abandons grace for works. [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 2#Galatians 2 . 21|Galatians 2:21]] warns: "if righteousness were through the law, then Christ died for no purpose." The trajectory shows circumcision's purpose and obsolescence: covenant sign (Genesis 17) → prophetic promise of heart transformation ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 6|Deuteronomy 30:6]]) → Christ's accomplishment of spiritual circumcision ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 11|Colossians 2:11]]) → Spirit's application to believers ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 29|Romans 2:29]]) → physical circumcision obsolete when trusted for righteousness ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 2|Galatians 5:2]]). Paul doesn't condemn circumcision itself (he circumcised Timothy, [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 16#Acts 16 . 3|Acts 16:3]]) but trusting it for justification. "In Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but only faith working through love." What mattered as covenant marker becomes irrelevant in Christ; what was commanded finds fulfillment; what symbolized transformation is replaced by actual transformation through faith in Christ whose death and resurrection accomplish all the law demanded.
