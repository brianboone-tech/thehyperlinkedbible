### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3670-3671#G3670|ὁμολογῶμεν]] (*homologōmen*) - "we confess, acknowledge" (present subjunctive active)
- [[Lexicon/G0266-0267#G0266|ἁμαρτίας]] (*hamartias*) - "sins" (accusative plural)
- [[Lexicon/G4103-4104#G4103|πιστός]] (*pistos*) - "faithful, trustworthy" (adjective)
- [[Lexicon/G1342-1343#G1342|δίκαιος]] (*dikaios*) - "righteous, just" (adjective)
- [[Lexicon/G0863-0864#G0863|ἀφῇ]] (*aphē*) - "he may forgive" (aorist subjunctive active)
- [[Lexicon/G2511-2512#G2511|καθαρίσῃ]] (*katharisē*) - "he may cleanse" (aorist subjunctive active)
- [[Lexicon/G0093-0094#G0093|ἀδικίας]] (*adikias*) - "unrighteousness" (genitive)
- [[Lexicon/G3956-3957#G3956|πάσης]] (*pasēs*) - "all, every" (genitive)

**Context**: First John 1:9 provides new covenant remedy for sin's leprosy: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." The verse addresses believers (note "we"), not initial salvation but ongoing cleansing. Like Leviticus 13:45's leper crying "Unclean! Unclean!" believers must acknowledge sin (*homologōmen*, "confess"—lit. "say the same thing" God says about sin). But unlike lepers whose cry warned others of contagion, believers' confession brings cleansing through Christ's blood (v. 7). The dual promise—forgiveness (*aphē*) and cleansing (*katharisē*)—addresses both guilt and defilement. "Faithful and just" (*pistos kai dikaios*) grounds assurance: God's character guarantees response. Confession isn't earning forgiveness but appropriating what Christ's sacrifice secured.

**Christological Connection**: First [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 9|John 1:9]]'s promise—"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness"—transforms [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 13#Leviticus 13 . 45|Leviticus 13:45]]'s leper crying "Unclean! Unclean!" The OT leper's confession warned others of contagion but brought no healing; the NT believer's confession receives immediate forgiveness and cleansing through Christ's blood. Verse 7 provides the foundation: "the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin." The present tense *katharizei* ("cleanses") indicates ongoing purification—not one-time ceremonial act but continual washing. This fulfills Leviticus 14's elaborate cleansing ritual typologically: what required eight days, two birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop, multiple washings, and three sacrifices in OT finds complete fulfillment in Christ's single offering. The verb *homologōmen* ("confess") means "say the same thing"—agreeing with God's verdict on sin. Like the leper acknowledging leprosy, believers must acknowledge sin's reality, not deny or minimize it. Verse 8 warns: "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us." The dual promise—"forgive" (*aphē*) and "cleanse" (*katharisē*)—addresses both guilt (requiring forgiveness) and defilement (requiring cleansing). Forgiveness removes penalty; cleansing purifies character. The grounds for assurance are divine attributes: "faithful and just" (*pistos kai dikaios*). God is "faithful"—reliable, keeping promises; "just"—righteous, having satisfied justice through Christ's substitutionary death. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 3#Romans 3 . 25|Romans 3:25-26]] explains: God presented Christ "as a propitiation by his blood... This was to show God's righteousness... so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus." God's justice demands sin's punishment; Christ's death satisfies justice; God's faithfulness guarantees promised cleansing. The comprehensiveness—"from all unrighteousness" (*apo pasēs adikias*)—echoes Leviticus 14's thorough cleansing: washing, shaving, bathing, re-washing, re-shaving, re-bathing, sacrifices. What OT accomplished ceremonially, Christ achieves spiritually. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 22|Hebrews 10:22]] describes believers having "hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience"—ongoing cleansing enabling bold access to God. The trajectory shows: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 13#Leviticus 13 . 45|Leviticus 13:45]] requires leper crying "Unclean! Unclean!" (public acknowledgment without remedy) → Leviticus 14 prescribes elaborate cleansing after divine healing (multi-day ritual) → [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] prays "purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean" (spiritual application) → Christ's blood cleanses from all sin ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]) → confession brings immediate forgiveness and cleansing ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]). What required ceremonial isolation and complex ritual in OT becomes simple confession and instant cleansing through Christ's superior sacrifice.
