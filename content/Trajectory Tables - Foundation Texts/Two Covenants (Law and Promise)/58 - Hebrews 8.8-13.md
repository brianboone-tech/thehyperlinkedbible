### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 8|Hebrews 8:8-13]]

**Context**: The theological centerpiece of Hebrews' argument for Christ's superior priesthood and covenant. After establishing Christ as high priest after the order of Melchizedek (ch. 7), the author quotes Jeremiah 31:31-34 in full - the longest OT quotation in the NT - to prove the new covenant renders the Mosaic covenant obsolete.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Hebrews quotes [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]] (LXX) verbatim as the prophetic basis for new covenant theology
- This OT promise responds to Israel's covenant failure from Exodus through the exile
- The new covenant synthesizes Abrahamic promise (unconditional blessing) with Mosaic obedience (now enabled by Spirit)
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]] parallels Jeremiah's promise: new heart, new Spirit, enabling obedience

**Type Classification**: FORWARD-LOOKING DIRECT TYPE - Explicit divine promise of a future covenant that would replace the Mosaic covenant; Hebrews interprets this as direct prophecy fulfilled in Christ

**Christological Connection**: Hebrews' exposition of [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]] reveals Christ as the mediator of a "better covenant enacted on better promises" (v. 6). The four contrasts in Jeremiah 31 (internal vs. external; universal knowledge of God vs. limited priesthood; complete forgiveness vs. repeated sacrifices; permanent vs. breakable) find fulfillment in Christ's work:

1. **Internal Law** (v. 10): The Spirit writes God's law on hearts (Heb 10:16), fulfilling what external Sinai law could not accomplish
2. **Universal Knowledge** (v. 11): All in the new covenant "know the Lord" through direct access via Christ's blood (Heb 10:19-22)
3. **Final Forgiveness** (v. 12): Christ's once-for-all sacrifice secures complete forgiveness, unlike endless Levitical sacrifices (Heb 10:11-14)
4. **Permanence** (v. 13): The old covenant is "obsolete and growing old" and "ready to vanish away"; the new covenant is eternal (Heb 13:20)

The conclusion (v. 13) is decisive: "In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete." Not obsolete because God failed, but because God planned from eternity to replace the temporary, preparatory Mosaic covenant with the permanent, fulfilling new covenant in Christ's blood. The law was our "guardian until Christ came" (Gal 3:24); now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian but sons through faith in Christ Jesus (Gal 3:25-26).
