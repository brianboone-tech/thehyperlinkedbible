### [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2319|H2319]] חָדָשׁ (chadash) - new
- [[Lexicon/H1285|H1285]] בְּרִית (berit) - covenant
- [[Lexicon/H3820|H3820]] לֵב (leb) - heart, inner being
- [[Lexicon/H7130|H7130]] קֶרֶב (qereb) - inward parts, midst
- [[Lexicon/H5545|H5545]] סָלַח (salach) - forgive, pardon

**Context**: The most extensive and explicit promise of a new covenant in the OT. Written during the Babylonian siege of Jerusalem, Jeremiah prophesies that despite covenant failure and exile, God will make a "new covenant" that will be internal ("on their hearts"), universal ("all shall know me"), and secure final forgiveness ("I will remember their sin no more").

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- This promise responds to centuries of covenant failure documented from Exodus to 2 Kings
- Contrasts explicitly with the Sinai covenant: "not like the covenant that I made with their fathers" (v. 32)
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26-27]] parallels this promise: "new heart... new spirit... I will put my Spirit within you"
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 30#Deuteronomy 30 . 6|Deuteronomy 30:6]] anticipated this: "the LORD your God will circumcise your heart... so that you will love the LORD"

**Type Classification**: FORWARD-LOOKING DIRECT TYPE - Explicit divine promise of a future covenant that would replace the Mosaic covenant; directly and intentionally anticipates the new covenant in Christ's blood

**Christological Connection**: Jesus inaugurated the new covenant by His blood at the Last Supper: "This cup is the new covenant in my blood" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 20|Luke 22:20]]). His death secured the final forgiveness promised in [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 34|Jeremiah 31:34]] (Heb 10:17-18). The indwelling Holy Spirit, poured out at Pentecost, writes God's law on believers' hearts (2 Cor 3:3), enabling the obedience the Mosaic covenant demanded but could not produce. Hebrews 8-10 extensively expounds [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 31|Jeremiah 31:31-34]] to prove Christ is the mediator of a "better covenant" with "better promises" (Heb 8:6). The new covenant is "new" not in annulling God's moral law, but in providing internal transformation through the Spirit to fulfill what the external law could only command.
