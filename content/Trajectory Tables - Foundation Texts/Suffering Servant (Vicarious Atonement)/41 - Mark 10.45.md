### [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 10#Mark 10 . 45|Mark 10:45]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1247|G1247]] διακονέω (*diakoneo*) - "to serve, minister" - Jesus came to serve, not be served
- [[Lexicon/G3083|G3083]] λύτρον (*lytron*) - "ransom" - A price paid to redeem captives or slaves
- [[Lexicon/G473|G473]] ἀντί (*anti*) - "instead of, in place of" - Substitution—Jesus's life for many lives
- [[Lexicon/G4183|G4183]] πολλοί (*polloi*) - "many" - Echoes Isaiah 53:11-12 "bear the sin of many"

**Context**: Mark 10:45 concludes Jesus's teaching about greatness through servanthood, given after James and John request positions of honor. Jesus contrasts worldly authority with kingdom service, then declares His own mission in Servant language.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10-12]] provides the theological foundation: The Servant makes Himself a guilt offering (53:10), bears the sin of many (53:12), and through His suffering many are justified (53:11). Jesus's "ransom for many" directly echoes this language.

**Connections**:
- **TO (Earlier OT)**: Isaiah 52:13-53:12 (Servant gives life for many); Exodus 21:30 (ransom to redeem life); Leviticus 25:47-49 (kinsman redeemer)
- **FROM OT (Later OT)**: N/A (Isaiah 53 is the climactic OT revelation)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 20#Matthew 20 . 28|Matthew 20:28]] - parallel passage
  - [[Readable Bible/54 - 1 Timothy/1 Timothy 2#1 Timothy 2 . 6|1 Timothy 2:6]] - ransom for all
  - [[Readable Bible/56 - Titus/Titus 2#Titus 2 . 14|Titus 2:14]] - gave himself to redeem
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]] - bear sins of many
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]] - redeemed with precious blood
**Type Classification**: Jesus identifies Himself as the fulfillment of Isaiah's **forward-looking Direct Type** (the prophesied Servant)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus explicitly interprets His death through the lens of Isaiah 53. The "ransom for many" directly quotes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 11|Isaiah 53:11-12]] where the Servant "bears the sin of many" and "makes many to be accounted righteous." Jesus came not as conquering king (first advent) but as Suffering Servant—serving humanity by giving His life as the ransom price to redeem captives from sin and death. His use of "many" (polloi) rather than "all" reflects Semitic idiom (many = multitude, not excluding others) and echoes Isaiah's language. This saying is Jesus's clearest pre-passion statement of His atoning mission: His death is not tragic but purposeful, not forced but voluntary, not symbolic but substitutionary. He gives His life *anti* (instead of, in place of) many—the essence of vicarious atonement.
