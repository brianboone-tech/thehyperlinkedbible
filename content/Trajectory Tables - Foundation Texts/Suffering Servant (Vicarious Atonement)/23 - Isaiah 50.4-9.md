### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 50#Isaiah 50 . 4|Isaiah 50:4-9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3925|H3925]] לִמּוּד (*limmud*) - "taught, disciple" - The Servant has the tongue of those taught by God
- [[Lexicon/H4775|H4775]] מָרָה (*marah*) - "rebellious" - The Servant was not rebellious, showing perfect obedience
- [[Lexicon/H1458|H1458]] גַּו (*gav*) - "back" - The Servant gave His back to those who struck Him
- [[Lexicon/H7536|H7536]] רֹק (*roq*) - "spittle, spit" - The Servant endured the shame of being spit upon

**Context**: Isaiah 50:4-9 is the third Servant Song, focusing on the Servant's obedient suffering. Unlike the first two songs, this passage graphically describes physical abuse the Servant willingly endures while maintaining trust in God's vindication.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 6|Psalm 22:6-8]]**: The righteous sufferer mocked and despised prefigures the Servant's shameful treatment
- **[[Readable Bible/25 - Lamentations/Lamentations 3#Lamentations 3 . 30|Lamentations 3:30]]**: "Let him give his cheek to the one who strikes"—parallels the Servant's willing submission to violence
- **[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 1|Isaiah 42:1-4]]**: First Song's gentle Servant now shown enduring violence to accomplish His mission

**Connections**:
- **TO (Earlier OT)**: Psalm 22:6-8 (mocked and despised); Psalm 69:19-20 (reproach and shame); Lamentations 3:30 (giving cheek to striker)
- **FROM OT (Later OT)**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 14|Isaiah 52:14]] (appearance marred); [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 3|Isaiah 53:3]],7 (despised, silent before accusers); [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 13#Zechariah 13 . 7|Zechariah 13:7]] (shepherd struck)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 67|Matthew 26:67]] - spit in face
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 30|Matthew 27:30]] - struck with reed
  - [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 65|Mark 14:65]] - struck and spit upon
  - [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 63|Luke 22:63-65]] - mocked and beaten
**Type Classification**: **Forward-looking** (prophesies future Servant's suffering) | **Direct Type** (divinely revealed pattern of redemptive suffering)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus perfectly fulfills the Servant's obedient suffering. During His passion, soldiers "spit in his face and struck him" (Matt 26:67), "struck his head with a reed" (Matt 27:30), and mocked Him ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 63|Luke 22:63]]). Where the Servant said "I gave my back to those who strike," Jesus willingly submitted to scourging. Where the Servant "hid not my face from disgrace and spitting," Jesus endured the cross "despising the shame" (Heb 12:2). The Servant's confidence—"He who vindicates me is near"—finds fulfillment in Jesus's resurrection vindication. Christ was "not rebellious" even unto death, learning obedience through suffering (Heb 5:8).
