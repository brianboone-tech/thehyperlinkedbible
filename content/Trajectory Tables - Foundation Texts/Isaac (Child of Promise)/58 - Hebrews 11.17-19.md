### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 17|Hebrews 11:17-19]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- προσφέρω (*prospherō*) - "to offer" - To bring as sacrifice
- μονογενής (*monogenēs*) - "only begotten" - Unique, one-of-a-kind son
- λογίζομαι (*logizomai*) - "to consider, reckon" - To reason, calculate
- παραβολή (*parabolē*) - "figure, type" - Symbolic representation

**Context**: Hebrews 11 catalogs Old Testament examples of faith, with Abraham's offering of Isaac as the pinnacle. The author interprets the Akedah as demonstrating resurrection faith: Abraham believed God could raise Isaac from the dead, and symbolically, he did receive Isaac back from death.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 17|Hebrews 11:17-19]] explicitly identifies the Akedah as a 'type' (παραβολή) of Christ's death and resurrection. The author's reasoning: since God promised the covenant would continue through Isaac, and God commanded Isaac's death, Abraham concluded God must raise Isaac from the dead to fulfill the promise. Figuratively (ἐν παραβολῇ), Abraham did receive Isaac back from death when the angel stopped him. This prefigures Christ's actual death and resurrection. Escalation: Isaac's 'resurrection' was figurative/symbolic; Christ's resurrection was literal/historical. Isaac was spared at the last moment; Christ endured death fully. Isaac's restoration pointed forward; Christ's resurrection opens the way for all believers' resurrection. The pattern demonstrates that God's promises cannot fail even if death intervenes - resurrection hope is built into the promise from Genesis 22 onward.
