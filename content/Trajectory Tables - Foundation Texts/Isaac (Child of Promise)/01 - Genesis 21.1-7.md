### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- פָּקַד (*paqad*) - "visited" - Divine attention bringing promised blessing into reality
- צָחַק (*tsachaq*) - "to laugh" - Isaac's name; transformed from Sarah's unbelief (18:12) to joy (21:6)
- מוֹעֵד (*mo'ed*) - "appointed time" - God's sovereign timing in redemptive history
- זֶרַע (*zera*) - "seed/offspring" - The promised line through whom blessing comes

**Context**: Twenty-five years after the initial promise (Genesis 12:1-3), God fulfills His word to Abraham and Sarah. Isaac's birth occurs when both parents are past natural childbearing age (Abraham 100, Sarah 90), demonstrating that God's promises depend on His power, not human ability.

**Christological Connection**: Isaac's miraculous birth from the barren womb prefigures Christ's virgin birth - both demonstrate that salvation comes by God's supernatural intervention, not human ability. The 'appointed time' anticipates [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 4|Galatians 4:4]] ('when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son'). Just as Isaac's birth brought laughter/joy after long waiting, Christ's birth brings 'good news of great joy' ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 10|Luke 2:10]]). Isaac as 'only son' whom Abraham loved ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 2|Genesis 22:2]]) typifies God's only begotten Son. All who believe are 'children of promise' like Isaac ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 28|Galatians 4:28]]), born not by natural descent but by God's supernatural work through faith in Christ.
