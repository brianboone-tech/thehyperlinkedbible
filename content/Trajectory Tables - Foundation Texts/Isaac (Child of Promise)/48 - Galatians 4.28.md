### [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 28|Galatians 4:28]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- ἐπαγγελία (*epangelia*) - "promise" - God's gracious commitment and pledge
- τέκνον (*teknon*) - "child" - Offspring, descendant
- κατὰ πνεῦμα (*kata pneuma*) - "according to Spirit" - By supernatural power, not natural ability
- ἐλεύθερος (*eleutheros*) - "free" - Not under bondage

**Context**: Paul contrasts two covenants using Sarah/Isaac vs. Hagar/Ishmael as allegory. Isaac, born through promise by God's supernatural power, represents believers born of the Spirit through faith. Ishmael, born through human effort (flesh), represents those seeking righteousness through law-keeping.

**Christological Connection**: Paul's application in [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 28|Galatians 4:28]] identifies all believers as 'children of promise like Isaac.' The Isaac pattern is now universalized through Christ: just as Isaac's birth came through God's supernatural intervention (not Abraham's ability), believers are born again through the Spirit's supernatural work (not human effort). The contrast is deliberate: Ishmael (flesh, human effort, bondage) vs. Isaac (promise, divine power, freedom). This typing shows that salvation has always been by grace through faith, never by works. Christ is the ultimate 'child of promise' (supernaturally conceived, born to save), and union with Christ makes believers 'children of promise' (supernaturally born again, freed from law). The escalation: Isaac was one child of promise; the church is countless children from all nations, all born by the same principle - divine promise received by faith, not human ability. This fulfills [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 18|Genesis 22:18]]: 'in your offspring shall all nations be blessed' - Christ is the offspring, and through Him, all who believe become offspring of promise.
