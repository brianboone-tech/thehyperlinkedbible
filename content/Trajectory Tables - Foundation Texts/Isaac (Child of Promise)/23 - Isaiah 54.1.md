### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- עֲקָרָה (*aqarah*) - "barren" - Unable to conceive; picture of hopeless condition
- פָּצַח (*patsach*) - "break forth" - Explosive joy and singing
- שָׁמֵם (*shamem*) - "desolate" - Abandoned, forsaken, without hope
- רִנָּה (*rinnah*) - "joyful shouting" - Exuberant cry of victory and joy

**Context**: Following the Suffering Servant's atoning work (Isaiah 53), Isaiah 54 declares the restoration and expansion of Zion. The barren woman who had no children will have more children than the married woman, reversing the shame of barrenness and promising explosive growth of God's people.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]] prophetically anticipates the church's supernatural growth following Christ's atoning death (Isaiah 53). Paul explicitly applies this text to the church in [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 27|Galatians 4:27]], showing that believers are children of promise born through the gospel, not law. Just as Sarah's barrenness was reversed by God's promise, the 'barren' church (humanly speaking unable to produce children) bears innumerable offspring through gospel proclamation. The escalation: Isaac was one miraculous child; the church is countless children from every nation. The barren woman's joy prefigures the church's joy at the gathering of the nations into God's family. The pattern from Genesis 21 (miraculous birth from barren womb) culminates in the gospel age when the Spirit gives life to spiritually dead people worldwide.
