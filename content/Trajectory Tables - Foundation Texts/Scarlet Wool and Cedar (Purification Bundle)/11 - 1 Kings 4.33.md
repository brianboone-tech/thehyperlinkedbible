### [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 4#1 Kings 4 . 33|1 Kings 4:33]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H730|H730]] אֶרֶז (*'erez*) - "cedar" - The tall, majestic cedar tree of Lebanon
- [[Lexicon/H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*'ezob*) - "hyssop" - The lowly plant growing from walls
- [[Lexicon/H3318|H3318]] יָצָא (*yatsa'*) - "to go out, come forth" - Used here of plants growing from walls

**Context**: This verse describes Solomon's God-given wisdom, manifested in his comprehensive knowledge of creation. "He spoke of trees, from the cedar that is in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall. He spoke also of beasts, and of birds, and of reptiles, and of fish" (1 Kings 4:33). Solomon's wisdom spanned all categories of created things, from greatest to least. The cedar-to-hyssop range explicitly represents the full spectrum of the vegetable kingdom.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
This verse provides the interpretive key for understanding cedar and hyssop in the purification rituals. Scripture itself defines these plants as representing opposite extremes—highest and lowest, greatest and least. When Leviticus 14:4-6 and Numbers 19:6 prescribe both cedar and hyssop together, the symbolism is deliberate: comprehensive purification requiring the full range from majesty to humility.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]] - Cedar and hyssop used together in purification bundle
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]] - Same pair used in red heifer ritual
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-8]] - Christ's descent from divine glory to human humiliation fulfills the cedar-to-hyssop pattern

**Type Classification**: Providential, Backward-Looking

This is providential (not directly commanded as type) and backward-looking (recognized as typological only from NT perspective). Solomon's wisdom encompassing cedar to hyssop was historical fact; its typological significance became clear when Christ fulfilled the pattern.

**Christological Connection**:

First Kings 4:33 provides the biblical foundation for seeing cedar and hyssop as representing opposite extremes. This interpretive key unlocks the purification bundle's profound Christological significance.

Cedar = Christ's divine nature, majesty, and glory. The cedar of Lebanon was renowned for height, strength, durability, and beauty. It was used in temple construction ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 6#1 Kings 6 . 9|1 Kings 6:9-18]]), representing permanence and glory. Christ in His divine nature is eternal, incorruptible, majestic—"the radiance of God's glory and the exact representation of his being" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]).

Hyssop = Christ's human nature, humility, and lowliness. The hyssop was a small, insignificant plant growing from cracks in walls. Christ in His human nature was "despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 3|Isaiah 53:3]]). He had "no beauty that we should desire him."

The purification bundle bound these extremes together with scarlet thread—Christ's blood. The incarnation accomplished what the symbol represented: deity descending to humanity, glory to shame, heaven to earth. Christ "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-7]])—the cedar descending to hyssop level.

The cedar-hyssop range taught that true purification required spanning the full distance from God to man. No ceremony using only one or the other could suffice. Christ alone bridges this chasm, being truly God (cedar's glory) and truly man (hyssop's humility), applying His own blood (scarlet) for our cleansing.
