### [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-8]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3444|G3444]] μορφή (*morphe*) - "form, nature" - Essential nature or character
- [[Lexicon/G2316|G2316]] θεός (*theos*) - "God" - The divine being
- [[Lexicon/G2758|G2758]] κενόω (*kenoo*) - "to empty" - To make void, to deprive of power
- [[Lexicon/G5013|G5013]] ταπεινόω (*tapeinoo*) - "to humble, make low" - To bring down from a high position
- [[Lexicon/G1401|G1401]] δοῦλος (*doulos*) - "servant, slave" - One in bondage or service
- [[Lexicon/G4716|G4716]] σταυρός (*stauros*) - "cross" - The instrument of crucifixion, ultimate shame

**Context**: Philippians 2:5-11 is the great Christological hymn describing Christ's humiliation and exaltation. Paul exhorts believers to imitate Christ's humility (v. 5), then describes His descent: "Though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" (vv. 6-8). This is followed by His exaltation (vv. 9-11).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
The cedar-to-hyssop trajectory established in 1 Kings 4:33 finds ultimate fulfillment here. Solomon spoke "from the cedar that is in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall"—highest to lowest. Christ's incarnation traverses this distance: from "equality with God" (cedar's supreme height) to "death on a cross" (hyssop's lowest humiliation).

**Type Classification**: Providential, Backward-Looking Recognition

The purification bundle's symbolism is providentially arranged (God designed cedar and hyssop with these properties) and recognized as typological from the New Testament perspective. Christ's incarnation reveals what the bundle symbolized.

**Christological Connection**:

[[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-8]] is the supreme New Testament commentary on the purification bundle's symbolism. The passage describes Christ's trajectory from supreme height (cedar) to ultimate depth (hyssop):

**Cedar = "Form of God" (v. 6)**
Christ possessed the essential nature (μορφή) of deity. He enjoyed "equality with God"—the cedar's supreme majesty. This was not grasping or usurpation but intrinsic reality. The cedar of Lebanon, tallest and most glorious of trees, symbolized His divine nature.

**Descent = "Emptied Himself" (v. 7)**
Christ "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself." This self-emptying (κένωσις) describes the incarnation—not abandoning deity but veiling glory, adding humanity. The cedar descends toward hyssop level.

**Hyssop = "Form of a Servant" (v. 7)**
Christ took "the form of a servant" (δούλου μορφήν). The same word (μορφή) used for divine nature describes His human nature—truly God, truly man. The hyssop, lowest of plants growing from wall cracks, symbolized His humble human state: "born in the likeness of men."

**Ultimate Hyssop = "Death on a Cross" (v. 8)**
The progression doesn't stop at incarnation but descends to "death, even death on a cross." Crucifixion was the most shameful, cursed death (Deuteronomy 21:23; Galatians 3:13). Christ reached absolute hyssop-level—"a worm and not a man" (Psalm 22:6), despised and rejected (Isaiah 53:3).

**Scarlet = Blood and Royalty**
Though humiliated, Christ remained royal. The scarlet wool represented both blood sacrifice and regal dignity. He was crowned (in mockery) with thorns, robed in scarlet (Matthew 27:28), crucified as "King of the Jews" (Matthew 27:37). His blood accomplished what scarlet symbolized—cleansing and coronation.

The purification bundle required all three elements together—cedar, hyssop, scarlet bound as one. Christ unified these in His person: divine nature (cedar), human nature (hyssop), sacrificial blood (scarlet). What ceremonial symbols separated, incarnation joined. The bundle's composite nature pointed to Christ's theanthropic person—God and man in one, accomplishing purification through blood.
