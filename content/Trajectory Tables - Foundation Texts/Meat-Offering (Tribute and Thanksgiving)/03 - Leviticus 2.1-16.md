### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 2#Leviticus 2 . 1|Leviticus 2:1-16]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4503-4504#H4503|מִנְחָה]] (*minḥāh*) - "grain offering, tribute, gift" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H5560-5561#H5560|סֹלֶת]] (*sōlet*) - "fine flour" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H8081-8082#H8081|שֶׁמֶן]] (*šemen*) - "oil" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H3828-3829#H3828|לְבֹנָה]] (*lᵉbōnāh*) - "frankincense" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H0234-0235#H0234|אַזְכָּרָה]] (*'azkārāh*) - "memorial portion" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H4417-4418#H4417|מֶלַח]] (*melaḥ*) - "salt" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H7603-7604#H7603|שְׂאֹר]] (*śᵉ'ōr*) - "leaven, yeast" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H1706-1707#H1706|דְּבַשׁ]] (*dᵉbaš*) - "honey" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H7381-7382#H7381|רֵיחַ]] (*rêaḥ*) - "aroma, fragrance" (noun)
- [[Lexicon/H5207-5208#H5207|נִיחֹחַ]] (*nîḥōaḥ*) - "pleasing, soothing" (adjective)

**Context**: Leviticus 2:1-16 prescribes the meat-offering (*minḥāh*), also called grain offering—the only bloodless Levitical sacrifice. It consists of fine flour (*sōlet*) mixed with oil (*šemen*) and frankincense (*lᵉbōnāh*), with salt (*melaḥ*) added but no leaven (*śᵉ'ōr*) or honey (*dᵉbaš*). The offering could be presented raw (vv. 1-3), baked in an oven (v. 4), cooked on a griddle (v. 5), or fried in a pan (v. 7). A memorial portion (*'azkārāh*) was burned on the altar as "a food offering with a pleasing aroma to the LORD" (*'iššeh rêaḥ nîḥōaḥ laYHWH*, v. 2); the remainder went to the priests (v. 3). This bloodless offering represented consecrated human life and labor offered to God—always accompanying blood sacrifices, never standing alone.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-5]] (Cain's grain offering rejected, Abel's blood offering accepted), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 40|Exodus 29:40-41]] (grain offering accompanying burnt offering), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 14#Genesis 14 . 18|Genesis 14:18]] (Melchizedek brings bread and wine)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 15#Numbers 15 . 4|Numbers 15:4-9]] (grain offering quantities specified with burnt/peace offerings), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 20|Isaiah 66:20]] (bring all your brothers... as a grain offering), [[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 1#Malachi 1 . 11|Malachi 1:11]] (in every place a pure offering will be brought to my name)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 5|Hebrews 10:5-10]] (a body you have prepared for me... offering of the body of Jesus Christ), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]] (a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 1|Romans 12:1]] (present your bodies as a living sacrifice)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 2#Leviticus 2 . 1|Leviticus 2:1-16]]'s meat-offering (*minḥāh*) prefigures Christ's perfect humanity offered to God and believers' consecrated worship through Him. The offering's ingredients symbolically represent Christ: **Fine flour** (*sōlet*)—uniformly ground grain without coarseness represents Christ's pure, perfect humanity. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 2|Isaiah 53:2]] describes Him: "he had no form or majesty that we should look at him, and no beauty that we should desire him"—His humanity appeared ordinary externally but was internally flawless. The fine flour's uniform consistency pictures sinless perfection throughout His entire life. **Oil** (*šemen*)—represents the Holy Spirit's anointing. [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18]] records Jesus reading [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 61#Isaiah 61 . 1|Isaiah 61:1]]: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me." [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]] confirms: "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power." Christ's entire ministry flowed from Spirit-anointing, fulfilling the oil mixed with grain. **Frankincense** (*lᵉbōnāh*)—aromatic resin ascending as smoke represents prayers and worship rising to God. [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]] applies this directly: "Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God (*prosphoran kai thysian tō theō eis osmēn euōdias*)." His self-giving love created "pleasing aroma" (*rêaḥ nîḥōaḥ*) that satisfied the Father perfectly. **Salt** (*melaḥ*)—verse 13 commands: "You shall season all your grain offerings with salt. You shall not let the salt of the covenant with your God be missing from your grain offering." Salt represents covenant permanence and preservation. Christ perfectly fulfilled every covenant requirement, remaining faithful unto death ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 8|Philippians 2:8]]). His covenant loyalty never wavered. **No leaven** (*śᵉ'ōr*)—Verse 11 prohibits: "you shall not burn any leaven." Leaven throughout Scripture represents sin, corruption, malice, evil ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 6|1 Corinthians 5:6-8]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 6|Matthew 16:6]]; [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 9|Galatians 5:9]]). Christ alone lived without sin—"He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 22|1 Peter 2:22]]); "in him there is no sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 5|1 John 3:5]]). The unleavened grain offering prefigured His sinless humanity. **No honey** (*dᵉbaš*)—Verse 11 also excludes honey, representing natural sweetness, human effort, fleshly appeal. Christ's obedience wasn't natural human goodness but supernatural Spirit-empowered righteousness. He didn't rely on natural ability but total dependence on the Father ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 19|John 5:19]], 30). **[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 5|Hebrews 10:5-10]] explicitly connects grain offering to incarnation**: "When Christ came into the world, he said, 'Sacrifices and offerings you have not desired, but a body (*sōma*) you have prepared for me'" (v. 5), quoting [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 40#Psalm 40 . 6|Psalm 40:6]]. The body prepared = the fine flour of perfect humanity. Verse 10 concludes: "we have been sanctified through the offering of the body (*tou sōmatos*) of Jesus Christ once for all." Christ's human body—pure (fine flour), anointed (oil), fragrant (frankincense), faithful (salt), sinless (no leaven), supernatural (no honey)—was offered to God as the ultimate grain offering. **Application to believers**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 1|Romans 12:1]] extends the typology: "present your bodies (*parastēsai ta sōmata*) as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship (*logikēn latreian*)." Our consecrated lives become grain offerings—but only acceptable through Christ's mediation. The trajectory shows: Leviticus 2 prescribes grain offering (fine flour, oil, frankincense, salt; no leaven, no honey) → always accompanies blood sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 15#Numbers 15 . 4|Numbers 15:4-9]])—dedication follows atonement → [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 20|Isaiah 66:20]] prophesies Gentiles brought "as a grain offering" → Christ embodies perfect grain offering ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 5|Hebrews 10:5-10]])—His body prepared, offered → His sacrifice creates "fragrant offering" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]) → believers offer bodies as living sacrifices ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 12#Romans 12 . 1|Romans 12:1]]) → financial generosity described as "fragrant offering" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 4#Philippians 4 . 18|Philippians 4:18]]) → spiritual sacrifices offered "through Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 5|1 Peter 2:5]]). What Leviticus 2 symbolized through grain, oil, and incense, Christ fulfilled through perfect incarnate life; what believers now offer in worship—bodies, praise, generosity—gains acceptance solely through Christ's mediating priesthood, becoming fragrant offerings pleasing to God.
