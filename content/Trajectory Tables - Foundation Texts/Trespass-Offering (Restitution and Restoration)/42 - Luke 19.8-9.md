### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 19#Luke 19 . 8|Luke 19:8-9]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5001-5100#G5224|ὑπάρχοντα]] (*hyparchonta*) - "possessions, goods, property"
- [[Lexicon/G4901-5000#G4800|συκοφαντέω]] (*sykophanteō*) - "to defraud, extort, accuse falsely"
- [[Lexicon/G0501-0600#G0591|ἀποδίδωμι]] (*apodidōmi*) - "to give back, repay, restore, recompense"
- [[Lexicon/G5001-5100#G5073|τετραπλοῦς]] (*tetraplous*) - "fourfold, four times as much"
- [[Lexicon/G4701-4800#G4991|σωτηρία]] (*sōtēria*) - "salvation, deliverance"

**Context**: Luke 19:8-9 records Zacchaeus's response to encountering Jesus—immediate, voluntary, and extravagant restitution exceeding the law's requirement. As a chief tax collector, Zacchaeus had enriched himself through a system notorious for extortion and fraud. His declaration demonstrates understanding of the trespass-offering principle: acknowledgment of guilt, restitution to those wronged, and reliance on God's provision of atonement. Jesus' response—"Today salvation has come to this house"—validates that genuine repentance produces visible fruit in making wrongs right. The passage illustrates the gospel application of Leviticus 6:1-7: sins against neighbor require horizontal restitution before vertical atonement can be effectually applied to the conscience.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 6#Leviticus 6 . 4|Leviticus 6:4-5]] (restore what was taken by extortion plus one-fifth), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 22#Exodus 22 . 1|Exodus 22:1]] (fourfold restitution for stolen sheep), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 5#Leviticus 5 . 5|Leviticus 5:5]] (confession of sin)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 12#2 Samuel 12 . 6|2 Samuel 12:6]] (Nathan's parable: restore fourfold), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 5#Numbers 5 . 7|Numbers 5:7]] (confess sin and make restitution)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 8|Matthew 3:8]] (produce fruit in keeping with repentance), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 26#Acts 26 . 20|Acts 26:20]] (prove repentance by deeds), [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 2#James 2 . 17|James 2:17]] (faith without works is dead)

**Christological Connection**: Zacchaeus's fourfold restitution points to Christ's infinite restitution as the ultimate trespass-offering. Where the Levitical law required restitution plus 20% and Zacchaeus offered 400%, Christ provides limitless satisfaction for debt humanity could never repay. The tax collector's extortion created horizontal debt (to those defrauded) and vertical debt (to God whose justice was violated). Christ's sacrifice addresses both dimensions: satisfying divine justice and providing the basis for reconciliation between humans. Zacchaeus couldn't make full restitution—he likely defrauded more people than he could identify or repay. Yet Jesus declares, "Today salvation has come." This demonstrates the gospel principle: Christ's perfect restitution covers what our imperfect efforts cannot. Where Zacchaeus's restitution evidences genuine repentance, Christ's restitution accomplishes complete atonement. The trajectory extends from [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 6#Leviticus 6 . 4|Leviticus 6:4-5]] (restore what was taken plus 20%) through Zacchaeus (restore fourfold) to Christ (restore infinitely). The trespass-offering's dual requirement—horizontal restitution plus vertical sacrifice—finds perfect fulfillment in Christ who makes full satisfaction to God's justice and provides believers with transformed hearts that desire to make wrongs right. "The grace of our Lord overflowed" ([[Readable Bible/54 - 1 Timothy/1 Timothy 1#1 Timothy 1 . 14|1 Timothy 1:14]]) surpasses all required restitution, transforming extortioners into restorers, thieves into givers, trespassers into witnesses of transforming grace.
