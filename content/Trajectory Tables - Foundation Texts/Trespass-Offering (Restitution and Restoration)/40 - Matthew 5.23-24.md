### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 23|Matthew 5:23-24]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4374-4375#G4374|προσφέρῃς]] (*prosphērēs*) - "you are offering, you are bringing" (present subjunctive)
- [[Lexicon/G1435-1436#G1435|δῶρόν]] (*dōron*) - "gift, offering, sacrifice"
- [[Lexicon/G2379-2380#G2379|θυσιαστήριον]] (*thysiastērion*) - "altar, place of sacrifice"
- [[Lexicon/G3415-3416#G3415|μνησθῇς]] (*mnēsthēs*) - "you remember" (aorist passive subjunctive)
- [[Lexicon/G0080-0081#G0080|ἀδελφός]] (*adelphos*) - "brother" (fellow believer)
- [[Lexicon/G1259-1260#G1259|διαλλάγηθι]] (*diallagēthi*) - "be reconciled" (aorist passive imperative)
- [[Lexicon/G5217-5218#G5217|ὕπαγε]] (*hypage*) - "go" (present imperative: go now)

**Context**: Matthew 5:23-24 appears in the Sermon on the Mount's exposition on righteousness exceeding the scribes and Pharisees (5:20). After addressing anger and reconciliation (5:21-22), Jesus provides shocking application: even in the middle of offering sacrifice at the altar, if you remember a brother has something against you, leave the gift there and first be reconciled. The principle is revolutionary—worship without reconciliation is unacceptable to God. This fulfills the trespass-offering's pattern from Leviticus 6:1-7 where restitution to the wronged neighbor must precede sacrifice to God. Jesus radicalizes this: even the memory of unresolved conflict requires immediate action. True worship demands horizontal peace as prerequisite for vertical access.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 23|Matthew 5:23-24]]'s command to be reconciled before offering fulfills the trespass-offering's pattern while pointing to Christ's reconciling work. [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 6#Leviticus 6 . 5|Leviticus 6:5-6]] prescribed restitution to neighbor before guilt-offering to God; Jesus insists on reconciliation even during worship. This shows that Christ's trespass-offering produces reconciling community—having received vertical reconciliation ("God... through Christ reconciled us to himself," [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 18|2 Corinthians 5:18]]), believers pursue horizontal reconciliation ("gave us the ministry of reconciliation," [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 18|2 Corinthians 5:18]]). Zacchaeus demonstrates gospel transformation through trespass-offering ethics: "If I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 19#Luke 19 . 8|Luke 19:8]])—exceeding the 20% penalty, showing genuine repentance produces generous restitution. The sequence Jesus prescribes—remember, leave altar, be reconciled, then offer—shows that Christ's work doesn't bypass ethics but fulfills them. Where Levitical guilt-offerings addressed specific past debts, Christ's atonement creates ongoing reconciliation ministry. Paul's instruction to Philemon—"If [Onesimus] has wronged you at all, or owes you anything, charge that to my account" (Philemon 18)—embodies Christ's substitutionary work: bearing another's debt to enable reconciliation. The trajectory extends from Leviticus 6's prescription (restitution before sacrifice) through Jesus' application (reconciliation during worship) to the church's practice ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 18|2 Corinthians 5:18-20]]: ambassadors of reconciliation). Christ's trespass-offering enables both vertical peace with God and horizontal peace with neighbors.
