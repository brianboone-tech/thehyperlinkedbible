### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 15|Exodus 12:15-20]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- מַצָּה (maṣṣâ) - unleavened bread
- שְׂאֹר (śəʾōr) - leaven, yeast
- חָמֵץ (ḥāmēṣ) - what is leavened
- כָּרַת (kāraṯ) - to cut off, destroy
- מִקְרָא־קֹדֶשׁ (miqrāʾ-qōḏeš) - holy convocation

**Context**: The night of the Exodus from Egypt. God institutes the Passover lamb and the Feast of Unleavened Bread as perpetual ordinances. The unleavened bread commemorates Israel's hasty departure—no time for dough to rise (Exodus 12:39). But the symbolism goes deeper: leaven represents corruption that must be purged.

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 19#Genesis 19 . 3|Genesis 19:3]] - Lot baked unleavened bread for the angels—hospitality in haste, foreshadowing urgency of redemption
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 23#Leviticus 23 . 6|Leviticus 23:6-8]] - feast made perpetual statute
  - [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 16#Deuteronomy 16 . 3|Deuteronomy 16:3-4]] - unleavened bread called "bread of affliction"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 6|1 Corinthians 5:6-8]] - Paul applies the feast to the church: "Cleanse out the old leaven"
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 6|Matthew 16:6-12]] - Jesus warns of the leaven of false teaching
**Christological Connection**: The Feast of Unleavened Bread points to the necessity of purity after redemption. Christ, our Passover lamb, was sacrificed ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]). Therefore, we must "celebrate the festival, not with the old leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 8|1 Corinthians 5:8]]). The seven-day purge of leaven from Israelite homes prefigures the church's perpetual purging of sin. What was temporal (one week annually) becomes permanent (continuous sanctification). The feast teaches that justification leads to sanctification—we are saved not only from sin's penalty but also from sin's power and pollution.

**Application**: Have you purged the leaven of sin from your life? Just as Israel could not celebrate Passover while retaining leaven, we cannot claim salvation while clinging to sin. Search your heart as Israel searched their homes. Confess and forsake hidden sin—not just obvious transgressions but also the leaven of hypocrisy, malice, and false teaching. Live in the sincerity and truth that befits those redeemed by the blood of the Lamb.
