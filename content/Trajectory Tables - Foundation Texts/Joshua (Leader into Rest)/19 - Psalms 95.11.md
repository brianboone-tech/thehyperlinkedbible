### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 11|Psalm 95:11]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- **קָצַף** (qatsaph) - "Wrath/anger" - divine judgment against rebellion, oath sworn in anger
- **מְנוּחָה** (menuchah) - "Rest" - same word as Joshua's rest, now forfeited through unbelief
- **בָּא** (bo) - "Enter" - entering into God's rest, covenant privilege, access to divine presence

**Context**: Psalm 95 calls Israel to worship, warning against hardening hearts as the wilderness generation did at Meribah (Exodus 17:1-7) and Massah (testing the LORD). Because of unbelief, God swore in His wrath, "They shall not enter my rest." This psalm was written centuries after Joshua, proving that even after entering Canaan, Israel still needed to enter God's rest.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 20|Numbers 14:20-23]] (wilderness generation forbidden to enter the land), [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 1#Deuteronomy 1 . 34|Deuteronomy 1:34-35]] (the LORD was angry, swore none of this evil generation shall see the good land), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 7|Hebrews 3:7-11]] (quotes Psalm 95, applies to Christian warning)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 21#Joshua 21 . 44|Joshua 21:44]] (the LORD gave them rest on every side), [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 22#Joshua 22 . 4|Joshua 22:4]] (the LORD has given rest to your brothers), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 132#Psalm 132 . 8|Psalm 132:8]], 14 (Arise, O LORD, to Your resting place)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 7|Hebrews 3:7-4]]:11 (extended exposition: a rest remains for God's people, entered by faith), [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28-29]] (Jesus: Come to me and I will give you rest)

**Christological Connection**: Psalm 95 reveals that Joshua's rest was incomplete, necessitating the greater Joshua—Jesus Christ. The progression: (1) The wilderness generation forfeited rest through unbelief (Numbers 14); (2) Joshua led the next generation into the land, providing geographical rest ([[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 21#Joshua 21 . 44|Joshua 21:44]]); (3) Yet Psalm 95 warns future generations they can still miss "my rest," proving Joshua's rest was not final; (4) Hebrews interprets: "If Joshua had given them rest, God would not speak of another day" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8]]); (5) Conclusion: "There remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9]]), entered through faith in Jesus. Christ provides the rest Joshua could only foreshadow: rest from works-righteousness ("whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works," [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 10|Hebrews 4:10]]), rest of conscience ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 22|Hebrews 10:22]]), and eschatological rest ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]]). Jesus invites: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28]])—the rest unavailable through Joshua, unavailable through law-keeping, available only through trusting Christ's finished work. The warning of Psalm 95 remains: "Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 15|Hebrews 3:15]]). Salvation-rest is offered; it must be received by faith. Where Joshua gave temporal rest in the land, Jesus gives eternal rest in God's presence.
