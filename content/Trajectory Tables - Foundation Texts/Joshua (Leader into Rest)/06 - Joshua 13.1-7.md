### [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 13#Joshua 13 . 1|Joshua 13:1-7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- **זָקַנְתָּ** (zakanta) - "You are old, advanced in years" - Joshua's limitations, unfinished task
- **אֶרֶץ רַבָּה מְאֹד** (eretz rabbah me'od) - "Very much land" - still remains to be possessed despite conquest
- **שְׁאָר** (she'ar) - "Remnant/remainder" - incomplete conquest, enemies left in the land

**Context**: After summarizing Israel's successful conquest (Joshua 21:43-45), God informs Joshua that despite these victories, "very much land remains to be possessed." Joshua is old, will not complete the conquest, yet must divide the land among the tribes trusting that each will fully possess their allotment. This juxtaposition—success yet incompleteness—sets up the tension in Judges.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 33#Numbers 33 . 50|Numbers 33:50-56]] (God commands total conquest, warns against leaving Canaanites), [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 1|Deuteronomy 7:1-5]] (do not make covenant with them, do not intermarry, utterly destroy them)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 19|Judges 1:19-36]] (litany of failures: could not drive out, did not drive out), [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 1|Judges 2:1-3]] (angel rebukes Israel for disobedience, enemies will be thorns in your sides), [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 3#Judges 3 . 1|Judges 3:1-6]] (nations left to test Israel, Israel intermarried with them)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8-9]] (if Joshua had given them rest, there would not be talk of another day), [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 12|Philippians 3:12]] (not that I have already obtained this or am already perfect, but I press on)

**Christological Connection**: Joshua's incomplete conquest reveals the need for the greater Joshua—Jesus Christ—who alone achieves total victory. The pattern: Joshua began conquest but died with enemies remaining; Jesus completed conquest by destroying the ultimate enemies through His death and resurrection. The escalation: Joshua's enemies were Canaanite nations (physical); Christ's enemies are sin, death, and Satan (spiritual and cosmic). Joshua conquered land for earthly inheritance; Christ conquered death for heavenly inheritance. Joshua's limitations (old age, death) prevented completion; Christ's perfection (eternally alive, "the same yesterday, today, and forever," [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 8|Hebrews 13:8]]) guarantees complete and lasting victory. Hebrews explicitly contrasts Joshua's incomplete rest with Christ's complete rest: "If Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8]]). But there remains a Sabbath-rest because Christ has done what Joshua could not—fully and finally defeated every enemy. The incomplete conquest drives us forward to Christ, in whom "all the promises of God find their Yes" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 1#2 Corinthians 1 . 20|2 Corinthians 1:20]]). Where Joshua's conquest was penultimate, Christ's is ultimate. What Joshua inaugurated, Jesus consummates.
