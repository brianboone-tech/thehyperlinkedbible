**Context**: After three-and-a-half years of drought, Elijah confronts 450 prophets of Baal and 400 prophets of Asherah at Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:19-40). The contest determines: "The God who answers by fire, he is God" (v. 24). Baal's prophets cry out from morning until evening—cutting themselves, prophesying frantically—but "there was no voice, no one answered, no one paid attention" (v. 29). Then Elijah repairs Yahweh's broken altar with twelve stones (representing twelve tribes), digs trench, arranges wood and sacrifice, and drenches everything with water three times. At the time of evening sacrifice, Elijah prays briefly: "O LORD, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, let it be known this day that you are God in Israel, and that I am your servant, and that I have done all these things at your word. Answer me, O LORD, answer me, that this people may know that you, O LORD, are God, and that you have turned their hearts back" (vv. 36-37). Immediately fire falls from heaven, consuming sacrifice, wood, stones, dust, and water. The people fall on their faces and confess: "The LORD, he is God; the LORD, he is God!" This demonstrates Yahweh's absolute supremacy over Baal and calls Israel back to covenant faithfulness.

**Christological Connection**: First Kings 18:36-39 records fire from heaven consuming Elijah's sacrifice, vindicating Yahweh against Baal and turning Israel's hearts back to covenant faithfulness—prefiguring Christ as ultimate vindication of God's glory and mediator of covenant renewal. Elijah's prayer appeals to "God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel," invoking patriarchal promises fulfilled ultimately in Christ ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 16|Galatians 3:16]]). Elijah intercedes: "let it be known this day that you are God in Israel"—prefiguring Christ's mission to reveal Father's glory ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 4|John 17:4]], 6). The rebuilt twelve-stone altar symbolizes covenant unity of all Israel, pointing to Christ gathering scattered people into one ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 52|John 11:52]]; [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-16]]). Fire consuming sacrifice demonstrates divine acceptance, foreshadowing God's acceptance of Christ's sacrifice—vindicated by resurrection ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 4|Romans 1:4]]). Where fire consumed Elijah's sacrifice publicly, Christ's resurrection publicly vindicated His sacrifice. Elijah prays "that this people may know that you, O LORD, are God, and that you have turned their hearts back"—attributing repentance to God's work, prefiguring new covenant promise: "I will give you a new heart" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26]]). Where Elijah mediated temporary covenant renewal, Christ mediates eternal covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 15|Hebrews 9:15]]). The people's confession "Yahweh, he is God!" prefigures universal confession: "every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 11|Philippians 2:11]]). Fire from heaven symbolizes God's holy presence accepting sacrifice—pointing to Spirit's descent at Pentecost ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 3|Acts 2:3]]), marking acceptance of Christ's once-for-all offering. Where Baal's prophets' cries went unanswered, Christ's prayer is always heard ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 42|John 11:42]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 7|Hebrews 5:7]]). The comprehensive consumption (sacrifice, wood, stones, dust, water) demonstrates total divine power—Christ's sacrifice totally satisfied divine justice, consuming sin's penalty completely. [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 9#Luke 9 . 54|Luke 9:54-55]] shows Christ's restraint—when disciples asked to call fire on Samaritans "as Elijah did," Jesus rebuked them. This reveals Christ's superiority: Elijah called fire in judgment during old covenant, Christ withholds consuming fire during gospel age to offer mercy. Yet [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 29|Hebrews 12:29]] warns "our God is a consuming fire"—Christ will return in flaming fire executing final judgment ([[Readable Bible/53 - 2 Thessalonians/2 Thessalonians 1#2 Thessalonians 1 . 7|2 Thessalonians 1:7-8]]). [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 5|Revelation 11:5]] depicts two witnesses with Elijah-like power—fire from mouth consuming enemies—eschatological prophets bearing Elijah's authority in last days. The trajectory shows: Fire vindicates Elijah's sacrifice and turns Israel back (type, temporal, national) → Christ's resurrection vindicates His sacrifice and initiates new covenant (antitype, eternal, universal). Where Elijah confronted false prophets, Christ confronted religious hypocrisy. Where fire consumed one sacrifice on Carmel, Christ's sacrifice avails for all sins forever. Where Elijah mediated temporary covenant renewal, Christ mediates permanent covenant. Where fire demonstrated Yahweh > Baal, resurrection demonstrates Christ > death. Where twelve stones represented Israel's tribes, Christ gathers one new humanity from every nation. The pattern reveals Christ as greater than Elijah: both mediators calling people back to God, both demonstrating divine power, both vindicating God's truth against falsehood—but Christ alone is God's final Word ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 1|Hebrews 1:1-2]]), whose sacrifice needs no fire to consume it because resurrection fire vindicated it eternally, establishing new covenant in His blood for all who believe.
