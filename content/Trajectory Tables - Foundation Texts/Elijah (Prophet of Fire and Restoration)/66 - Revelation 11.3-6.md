**Context**: Revelation 11:1-14 describes two witnesses who prophesy 1,260 days (three-and-a-half years—same duration as Elijah's drought, Luke 4:25; James 5:17) during tribulation period. Verses 3-6 detail their ministry and powers: clothed in sackcloth (prophetic mourning), identified as "two olive trees and two lampstands" (echoing Zechariah 4:3, 11-14—Joshua and Zerubbabel), standing before Lord of earth. They possess Elijah-like and Moses-like powers: (1) Fire from mouth consuming enemies (Elijah called fire, 2 Kings 1:10-12); (2) Authority to shut sky so no rain falls during prophecy (Elijah shut heaven three-and-a-half years, 1 Kings 17:1; James 5:17); (3) Authority to turn waters to blood and strike earth with plagues (Moses turned Nile to blood, Exodus 7:17-21). After completing testimony, they're killed, bodies lie in Jerusalem three-and-a-half days, then God resurrects and raptures them (vv. 7-12). The two witnesses represent eschatological prophets bearing Moses-Elijah authority, confronting final apostasy before Christ's return—Malachi 4:5-6 pattern recurring at end of age.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 11#Revelation 11 . 3|Revelation 11:3-6]] describes two witnesses prophesying with Moses-Elijah authority during final tribulation, pointing to Christ as ultimate witness and Lord who commissions, empowers, vindicates, and returns to consummate kingdom. The witnesses are called "MY witnesses" (*martyrsin mou*, v. 3)—Christ (or Father) commissions them, echoing [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 8|Acts 1:8]]: "you will be my witnesses." Their 1,260-day ministry (three-and-a-half years) mirrors Elijah's drought duration ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 25|Luke 4:25]]; [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 5#James 5 . 17|James 5:17]]), creating typological parallel: Elijah's ministry → John the Baptist's preparatory ministry → Two witnesses' eschatological ministry. Where Elijah confronted Ahab's apostasy, John confronted religious hypocrisy, the witnesses confront final antichrist system (the "beast," v. 7). Their powers explicitly evoke Elijah and Moses: (1) "Authority to shut the sky, that no rain may fall during the days of their prophesying" (*exousian kleisai ton ouranon hina mē hyetos brechē*, v. 6)—Elijah shut heaven three-and-a-half years ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 17#1 Kings 17 . 1|1 Kings 17:1]]; [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 5#James 5 . 17|James 5:17]]); (2) "Fire proceeds from their mouth and consumes their foes" (v. 5)—Elijah called fire from heaven ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 1#2 Kings 1 . 10|2 Kings 1:10-12]]); (3) "Authority over the waters to turn them into blood and to strike the earth with every plague" (v. 6)—Moses turned Nile to blood and executed ten plagues (Exodus 7-12). This Moses-Elijah combination represents Law and Prophets—complete OT witness—testifying to Christ. At Transfiguration, Moses and Elijah appeared with Christ discussing His "exodus" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 9#Luke 9 . 30|Luke 9:30-31]]), then disappeared: "they saw no one but Jesus only" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 17#Matthew 17 . 8|Matthew 17:8]]). Similarly, the two witnesses testify then are taken to heaven (v. 12), culminating in seventh trumpet: "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (v. 15). The witnesses wear sackcloth (v. 3)—prophetic mourning garb—calling for repentance before final judgment, fulfilling [[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 4#Malachi 4 . 5|Malachi 4:5-6]]: "I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the LORD comes. And he will turn the hearts of fathers to children... lest I come and strike the land with a decree of utter destruction." Where Malachi promised Elijah would come before Day of LORD, Revelation shows Elijah-pattern (two witnesses with Elijah-authority) immediately before Christ's return. Their identification as "two olive trees and two lampstands standing before the Lord of the earth" (v. 4) quotes [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 4#Zechariah 4 . 3|Zechariah 4:3]], 11-14—olive trees supplying oil to lampstand, representing Spirit-empowered witness. Christ earlier identified churches as lampstands ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 20|Revelation 1:20]]); here individual witnesses are lampstands, showing faithful testimony maintained until end. The witnesses' death by beast (v. 7), three-and-a-half-day public display (v. 9, 11), resurrection, and ascension "in the cloud" (v. 11-12) mirrors Christ's pattern: crucifixion, burial, resurrection after three days, ascension in cloud ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 9|Acts 1:9]]). This validates their testimony through martyrdom then vindication, just as Christ's resurrection vindicated His claims. The trajectory shows escalation: Elijah shut heaven, called fire (type, temporal, national judgment on Israel) → John the Baptist came in Elijah's spirit (prophetic fulfillment, preparatory, calling Israel to repent before Messiah) → Two witnesses prophesy with Elijah-Moses power (eschatological fulfillment, universal, final call to repentance before Christ's return). Where Elijah's ministry prepared for covenant renewal at Carmel, witnesses' ministry prepares for eternal kingdom: "the seventh angel blew his trumpet, and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, 'The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever'" (v. 15). The Christological point: Christ commissions witnesses, empowers them with Spirit (oil from olive trees to lampstands), protects them until testimony complete (v. 7—"when they have finished their testimony"), resurrects and raptures them, then returns as conquering King. Where Moses and Elijah appeared at Transfiguration then vanished (showing Christ's superiority), the two witnesses testify then ascend (showing Christ's vindication of faithful martyrs). Where Malachi ended OT with Elijah-promise, Revelation consummates NT with Elijah-pattern: witnesses call final generation to repent, suffer martyrdom, are resurrected-glorified, and Christ establishes eternal kingdom. The ultimate witness is Christ Himself—the "faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of kings on earth" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 5|Revelation 1:5]])—whose death and resurrection pattern is recapitulated in His witnesses, whose Spirit empowers their testimony, whose authority they bear, and whose return vindicates all who suffered for His name: "Blessed and holy is the one who shares in the first resurrection! Over such the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ and will reign with him for a thousand years" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 6|Revelation 20:6]]).
