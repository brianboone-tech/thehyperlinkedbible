**Context**: Six days after Peter's confession that Jesus is Messiah and Jesus' first passion prediction (Matthew 16:13-23), Jesus takes Peter, James, and John to a high mountain privately. There He is transfigured (*metamorphoō*—same root as "metamorphosis"): His face shone like sun, garments became white as light. Moses and Elijah appeared, conversing with Him about His coming "departure" (*exodos*—Luke 9:31). Peter impulsively suggests building three tents (Matthew 17:4). Then bright cloud overshadowed them and Father's voice declared: "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him." When disciples looked up, "they saw no one but Jesus only" (v. 8). The Transfiguration reveals Jesus' divine glory—preview of resurrection/ascension glory. The appearance of Moses (representing Law, though he died) and Elijah (representing Prophets, translated to heaven) testifies that all Scripture points to Christ. Father's voice echoes Jesus' baptism (Matthew 3:17) but adds command: "listen to him"—showing Christ's authority supersedes Moses and Elijah. The disciples' terror and Jesus' reassuring touch (vv. 6-7) emphasize both divine majesty and gracious accessibility.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 17#Matthew 17 . 1|Matthew 17:1-5]] records Jesus' transfiguration—revealing His divine glory and superiority over Moses and Elijah, confirming Him as beloved Son to whom we must listen. The transformation (*metemorphōthē*—metamorphosis) unveils Christ's intrinsic glory: "his face shone like the sun, and his garments became white as light" (v. 2). Unlike Moses whose face merely reflected glory after meeting God ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 34#Exodus 34 . 29|Exodus 34:29-35]]), Christ's glory radiates from within—He possesses divine nature eternally ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 1|John 1:1]], 14; [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 15|Colossians 1:15]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]). This previews resurrection glory when Christ rose in power ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 4|Romans 1:4]]) and ascended to Father's right hand ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 20|Ephesians 1:20]]). The appearance of Moses and Elijah testifies that all Scripture points to Christ. Moses represents Law, Elijah represents Prophets—their presence shows "all the Prophets and the Law prophesied until John" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 13|Matthew 11:13]]), culminating in Christ who fulfills both. [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 9#Luke 9 . 31|Luke 9:31]] specifies they discussed Jesus' coming "departure" (*exodos*)—His death, resurrection, ascension—showing OT witnesses understood Christ's redemptive mission. Where Moses led first exodus from Egypt, Christ accomplishes ultimate exodus from sin and death. Where Elijah prepared for temporal covenant renewal at Carmel, Christ establishes eternal new covenant in His blood. Peter's suggestion to build three tents (v. 4)—equalizing Moses, Elijah, and Jesus—is immediately corrected by Father's voice: "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him" (v. 5). The command "listen to HIM" (*akouete autou*) echoes [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 18#Deuteronomy 18 . 15|Deuteronomy 18:15]]—"The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you... It is to him you shall listen." Moses himself prophesied that greater prophet would come; now Father commands obedience to that prophet—Jesus. The phrase "they saw no one but Jesus only" (v. 8) is theologically loaded: Moses and Elijah vanish because their witness is complete; Christ alone remains because He alone is eternal, sufficient, supreme. The trajectory shows escalation: Moses' face reflected glory temporarily → Christ's face shines with intrinsic glory eternally. Moses gave Law → Christ fulfills Law. Elijah prepared for covenant renewal → Christ IS the covenant. Moses died and was buried ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 34#Deuteronomy 34 . 5|Deuteronomy 34:5-6]]) → Elijah was translated to heaven ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 2#2 Kings 2 . 11|2 Kings 2:11]]) → Christ died, rose, and ascended in triumph. Moses and Elijah appeared at Transfiguration as witnesses → Christ appeared in resurrection as victor. The Shekinah cloud overshadowing them (v. 5) recalls [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 40#Exodus 40 . 34|Exodus 40:34-35]] (cloud filling tabernacle) and [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]] (cloud filling temple)—divine presence now manifested in Christ, the true temple ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]). Father's declaration "This is my beloved Son" echoes [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 2#Psalm 2 . 7|Psalm 2:7]] (Davidic king) and [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 1|Isaiah 42:1]] (Suffering Servant), identifying Jesus as both conquering King and atoning Sacrifice. The phrase "in whom I am well pleased" (*en hō eudokēsa*) expresses complete approval—echoing baptism ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 17|Matthew 3:17]]) but now before witnesses representing all OT Scripture. The Transfiguration occurred six days after Peter's confession that Jesus is "the Christ, the Son of the living God" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 16|Matthew 16:16]]) and Jesus' first passion prediction (16:21). Peter had objected to suffering Messiah (16:22); the Transfiguration confirms: glory follows suffering, resurrection vindicates crucifixion, divine Son will triumph despite apparent defeat. Second Peter 1:16-18 cites Transfiguration as eyewitness proof: "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty... we heard this voice borne from heaven... when we were with him on the holy mountain." The Christological point: Jesus is not merely great teacher or prophet but divine Son possessing intrinsic glory, superior to Moses and Elijah, fulfilling all Scripture, deserving exclusive obedience. Where Moses mediated old covenant, Christ mediates better covenant ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 6|Hebrews 8:6]]). Where Elijah called fire from heaven at Carmel, Christ will return "in flaming fire" executing judgment ([[Readable Bible/53 - 2 Thessalonians/2 Thessalonians 1#2 Thessalonians 1 . 7|2 Thessalonians 1:7-8]]). Where Moses' glory faded, Christ's glory increases forever. Where Elijah was taken to heaven, Christ ascended after conquering death. Where Moses and Elijah appeared then vanished, Christ remains eternally—"Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 8|Hebrews 13:8]]). The Transfiguration reveals what faith sees now and sight will see then: Christ in glory, radiant as sun, dwelling among us ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]], 23), worthy of all worship, to whom alone we listen, in whom alone we trust, by whom alone we are saved—God's beloved Son, with whom He is well pleased, the Alpha and Omega, the Beginning and the End, the one Moses and Elijah testified about but who infinitely surpasses their greatest glories combined.
