### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 32|Luke 2:32]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5457|G5457]] φῶς (phos) - light
- [[Lexicon/G602|G602]] ἀποκάλυψις (apokalypsis) - revelation, unveiling, disclosure
- [[Lexicon/G1484|G1484]] ἔθνος (ethnos) - nation, Gentiles (plural ἔθνη, ethne)
- [[Lexicon/G1391|G1391]] δόξα (doxa) - glory, splendor, honor

**Context**:
Luke 2:22-38 narrates the presentation of the infant Jesus at the temple, forty days after his birth (per Leviticus 12:2-8). Simeon, a righteous and devout man awaiting "the consolation of Israel" (v. 25), is led by the Holy Spirit to the temple. Upon seeing Jesus, he takes the child in his arms and praises God with the Nunc Dimittis (vv. 29-32), declaring that he has seen God's salvation prepared "in the presence of all peoples" (v. 31)—"a light for revelation to the Gentiles, and for glory to your people Israel" (v. 32). This song interprets Jesus's identity and mission through the lens of Isaiah's Servant Songs.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]] promised all families blessed in Abraham.
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]] commissioned the Servant as "light for the nations" (אוֹר גּוֹיִם, or goyim; LXX φῶς ἐθνῶν, phos ethnon).
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|Isaiah 49:6]] escalates: "I will make you as a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth" (LXX φῶς ἐθνῶν, phos ethnon).
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 46#Isaiah 46 . 13|Isaiah 46:13]] promises: "I will put salvation in Zion, for Israel my glory."
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 1|Isaiah 60:1-3]]: "Arise, shine, for your light has come... And nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising."
- Simeon draws on this Isaianic trajectory, recognizing Jesus as the Servant who will bring light to Gentiles and glory to Israel.

**Type Classification**:
- **Forward-Looking Type**: Isaiah's prophecies explicitly anticipated the Servant's mission to bring light to nations. Simeon recognizes this expectation fulfilled in Jesus.
- **Direct Type**: God directly commissioned the Servant to this role (Isaiah 42:6; 49:6), and Jesus fulfills it.

**Christological Connection**:
Simeon's declaration establishes Jesus's identity as the Isaianic Servant. Jesus later affirms this: "I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]]). John's prologue echoes Simeon: "The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world. He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 9|John 1:9-10]]). The light metaphor explains Jesus's saving work: he illuminates truth ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 4|John 1:4-5]], "the light shines in the darkness"), exposes sin ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 19|John 3:19-20]], "people loved the darkness rather than the light"), and provides guidance ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 46|John 12:46]], "I have come into the world as light, so that whoever believes in me may not remain in darkness"). The Great Commission implements the light-bearing mission: disciples are sent to "all nations" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]]) as Jesus was sent to be "light to the nations." The trajectory: Isaiah prophesies Servant as light → Simeon recognizes Jesus as that light → Jesus declares "I am the light" → Jesus commissions disciples to extend the light → Paul preaches to Gentiles as "light to nations" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 47|Acts 13:47]]) → [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 23|Revelation 21:23-24]] consummates ("the Lamb is its lamp... the nations will walk by its light"). Christ is the light that penetrates Gentile darkness, and his gospel creates a people who shine with his reflected glory ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 14|Matthew 5:14-16]]; [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 15|Philippians 2:15]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).
