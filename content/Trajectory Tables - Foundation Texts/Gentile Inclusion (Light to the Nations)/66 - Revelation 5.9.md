**Context**:
Revelation 5 depicts the heavenly throne room where a scroll sealed with seven seals cannot be opened by anyone. John weeps until the Lion of Judah, the Root of David, appears—yet he sees not a lion but "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (v. 6). When the Lamb takes the scroll, the four living creatures and twenty-four elders fall down and sing a new song (vv. 9-10), declaring the Lamb worthy because by his blood he ransomed people from every tribe, language, people, and nation.

**Type Classification**:
- **Forward-Looking Type**: Every OT text in the Gentile Inclusion trajectory anticipated this moment—universal worship of the Lamb from all nations.
- **Providential Type**: God sovereignly orchestrated redemptive history from Abraham to Revelation to create a multinational redeemed people.

**Christological Connection**:
The Lamb's identity synthesizes OT messianic expectations:
- **Lion of Judah** ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 9|Genesis 49:9-10]]): Royal authority from Judah's line.
- **Root of David** ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 1|Isaiah 11:1]], 10): Davidic Messiah who is both descendant and source ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 41|Matthew 22:41-46]]).
- **Lamb slain** ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]): Suffering Servant bearing sin.
- **Worthy to open scroll** ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 5|Revelation 5:5]], 9): Universal authority through sacrificial death.

The song declares the Lamb "worthy" (ἄξιος, axios) because of his atoning work. His worthiness is not intrinsic divinity alone (though he shares the throne with God, v. 13) but earned through obedience unto death ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 8|Philippians 2:8-11]]). The cross is the basis for universal dominion.

The trajectory reaches consummation: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]] ("all families blessed") → [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 27|Psalm 22:27]] ("all families worship") → [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]]; 49:6 ("light to nations") → [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 32|Luke 2:32]] (Simeon recognizes Jesus as that light) → [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 9|Romans 15:9-12]] (Paul proves from Scripture) → [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 3#Ephesians 3 . 6|Ephesians 3:6]] (mystery revealed: Gentiles are fellow heirs) → [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 9|Revelation 5:9]] (multinational multitude ransomed by Lamb's blood). The four-fold description ("tribe, language, people, nation") appears again in [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]; 13:7; 14:6, emphasizing the theme's centrality. What was promise to Abraham becomes reality in the Lamb's redemptive work. The slain Lamb who appeared weak (crucified) is revealed as strong (victorious); the suffering Servant who seemed defeated (died) is enthroned as King. The song's refrain—"Worthy are you... for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation"—is the theological and doxological climax of the entire Gentile Inclusion trajectory. The cross accomplishes what the prophets anticipated, creating an eternal multinational family united in worship of the Lamb who was slain.
