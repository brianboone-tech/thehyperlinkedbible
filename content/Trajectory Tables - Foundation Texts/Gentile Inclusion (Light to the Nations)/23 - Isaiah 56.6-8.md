**Context**:
Isaiah 56 opens the final section of Isaiah (chapters 56-66), addressing the post-exilic community. It begins by affirming that God's salvation extends to foreigners and eunuchs—two groups typically excluded from full covenant participation (Deuteronomy 23:1-8). Verses 6-8 promise that Gentile proselytes who embrace covenant obligations will be welcomed into God's house, their worship accepted, and their inclusion part of God's gathering of all peoples.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 48|Exodus 12:48-49]] allowed foreigners to keep Passover if circumcised, showing conditional inclusion.
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 23#Deuteronomy 23 . 1|Deuteronomy 23:1-8]] restricted eunuchs, Ammonites, and Moabites from the assembly—yet Ruth the Moabite became David's great-grandmother.
- [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 41|1 Kings 8:41-43]] (Solomon's temple dedication): "Likewise, when a foreigner, who is not of your people Israel, comes from a far country for your name's sake... when he comes and prays toward this house, hear in heaven your dwelling place and do according to all for which the foreigner calls to you."
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 87#Psalm 87 . 4|Psalm 87:4-6]] envisions Gentile nations registered as "born in Zion."
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 2#Isaiah 2 . 2|Isaiah 2:2-4]] prophesied nations streaming to Zion to learn Torah.
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 19#Isaiah 19 . 23|Isaiah 19:23-25]] promises Egypt and Assyria will worship alongside Israel: "In that day Israel will be the third with Egypt and Assyria, a blessing in the midst of the earth, whom the LORD of hosts has blessed, saying, 'Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel my inheritance.'"
- The trajectory moves from restricted access → conditional inclusion → prophetic vision of full welcome.

**Type Classification**:
- **Forward-Looking Type**: The text explicitly anticipates future inclusion ("I will bring them... I will gather yet others"). This is expectational prophecy.
- **Providential Type**: God sovereignly reveals his intention to gather all peoples, preparing for the multinational Church.

**Christological Connection**:
Jesus's temple cleansing ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 11#Mark 11 . 15|Mark 11:15-17]]) demonstrates his commitment to fulfilling [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 56#Isaiah 56 . 7|Isaiah 56:7]]. By driving out money changers from the Court of the Gentiles, he restores the space for Gentile worship and rebukes Israel for obstructing it. His death tears the temple veil ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 15#Mark 15 . 38|Mark 15:38]]), opening full access to God for all who believe. [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 13|Ephesians 2:13-18]] explains: "But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ... For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father." The "house of prayer for all peoples" is realized in the Church, built on Christ the cornerstone ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 19|Ephesians 2:19-22]]). [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 8#Acts 8 . 26|Acts 8:26-40]] (Ethiopian eunuch's conversion) illustrates the literal fulfillment—a eunuch (excluded in [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 23#Deuteronomy 23 . 1|Deuteronomy 23:1]]) and a foreigner (from Ethiopia) reads Isaiah (specifically chapter 53!), believes in Jesus, and is baptized. Philip's explanation of Isaiah 53 leads to the eunuch's inclusion, demonstrating that what [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 56#Isaiah 56 . 6|Isaiah 56:6-8]] promised, Christ accomplished. The trajectory: Isaiah promises foreigners and eunuchs welcome → Jesus cleanses temple to facilitate Gentile access → Jesus's death opens access → Ethiopian eunuch (both foreigner and eunuch) is saved → Church becomes multinational house of prayer → New Jerusalem receives nations ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 24|Revelation 21:24-26]]). Christ transforms prophetic promise into eschatological reality, gathering "others" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 56#Isaiah 56 . 8|Isaiah 56:8]]) from every tribe and tongue ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]).
