**Context**:
Isaiah 42:1-9 is the first of the four Servant Songs (42:1-9; 49:1-13; 50:4-11; 52:13-53:12). The Servant is endowed with God's Spirit (v. 1), commissioned to bring justice to the nations (v. 1), gentle in ministry (vv. 2-3), persistent until justice is established globally (v. 4), and given as both "covenant for the people" and "light for the nations" (v. 6). This dual role—benefiting Israel and the Gentiles—anticipates the Messiah's work to reconcile both into one body (Ephesians 2:14-16).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]] promised blessing to all families through Abraham's seed.
- Isaiah develops "light" imagery: 9:2 (light dawning in darkness, quoted in [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 4#Matthew 4 . 16|Matthew 4:16]]), 42:6 (light to nations), 49:6 (light to ends of earth), 60:1-3 (arise, shine, nations come to your light).
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 3|Isaiah 60:3]]: "And nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising."
- The Servant is identified with Israel corporately ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 3|Isaiah 49:3]], "You are my servant, Israel") yet distinguished from Israel as one who brings Israel back to God ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 5|Isaiah 49:5-6]]). This corporate-individual fluidity resolves in Messiah, the true Israel who accomplishes what national Israel failed to do.

**Type Classification**:
- **Forward-Looking Type**: The text explicitly anticipates the Servant's future mission to bring light to nations. This is expectational prophecy.
- **Direct Type**: God directly commands and commissions the Servant to this role (v. 6, "I will give you").

**Christological Connection**:
Jesus explicitly identifies himself as the light: "I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]]). John's prologue declares, "The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 9|John 1:9]]). Simeon's recognition of the infant Jesus as "light for revelation to Gentiles" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 32|Luke 2:32]]) connects Jesus's identity to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]] and 49:6. Paul interprets his apostolic mission as continuation of the Servant's light-bearing role ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 47|Acts 13:47]]), showing that Jesus (the Servant) accomplishes the mission and the Church (sent by Jesus) extends it. The trajectory: Servant called to be light ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 42#Isaiah 42 . 6|Isaiah 42:6]]) → Jesus is the light ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]]) → believers shine as lights ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 14|Matthew 5:14-16]]; [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 15|Philippians 2:15]]) → nations come to the light ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 24|Revelation 21:24]]). Christ's incarnation, ministry, death, and resurrection are the light that penetrates Gentile darkness. His gospel creates a people who "proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]). What Isaiah prophesied, Jesus accomplished, and the Church proclaims to the ends of the earth.
