### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 4|Genesis 45:4-8]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7971-7972#H7971|שָׁלַח]] (*šālaḥ*) - "to send, to commission, to dispatch"
- [[Lexicon/H2421-2422#H2421|חָיָה]] (*ḥāyâ*) - "to preserve life, to keep alive, to sustain"
- [[Lexicon/H6440-6441#H6440|לְפָנַי]] (*lᵉp̄ānay*) - "before me, ahead of me"
- [[Lexicon/H7760-7761#H7760|שׂוּם]] (*śûm*) - "to place, to appoint, to make"
- [[Lexicon/H4910-4911#H4910|מֹשֵׁל]] (*mōšēl*) - "ruler, governor, one who has dominion"

**Context**: After revealing his identity to his terrified brothers, Joseph urgently calls them to come close. He reinterprets their betrayal through the lens of divine sovereignty: though they sold him into Egypt, 'God sent me before you to preserve life' (v. 5). Three times Joseph emphasizes God's agency—'God sent me' (vv. 5, 7, 8)—transforming their evil into God's redemptive purpose. Their crime becomes the means of worldwide salvation from famine.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 28|Genesis 37:28]] (you sold me here - now reinterpreted), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 50#Genesis 50 . 20|Genesis 50:20]] (you meant evil, God meant it for good)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 105#Psalm 105 . 16|Psalm 105:16-17]] (He summoned a famine... He had sent a man ahead of them), [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 16#Proverbs 16 . 9|Proverbs 16:9]] (The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 23|Acts 2:23]] (delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 27|Acts 4:27-28]] (to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 28|Romans 8:28]] (God works all things together for good)

**Christological Connection**: Joseph's reinterpretation of his brothers' betrayal as God's redemptive sending provides the OT's clearest type of how the cross accomplishes salvation through human evil. Joseph's triple declaration 'God sent me' (vv. 5, 7, 8) prefigures Christ as the Father's 'sent one'—the verb *šālaḥ* (send) connects to Jesus being 'sent' by the Father throughout John's Gospel ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 17|John 3:17]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 36|5:36]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 57|6:57]]). Joseph's purpose 'to preserve life' (*lᵉhaḥăyôṯ*, v. 5) parallels Christ's mission: 'I came that they may have life and have it abundantly' ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 10|John 10:10]]). The stunning theological move—'it was not you who sent me here, but God' (v. 8)—mirrors Peter's Pentecost sermon interpreting the cross: Jesus was 'delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God' yet 'crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 23|Acts 2:23]]). Both truths hold simultaneously: the brothers genuinely sold Joseph / the Jews genuinely crucified Christ; yet God genuinely orchestrated both for salvation purposes. Joseph preserving 'a remnant on earth' (v. 7) and keeping many 'alive by a great deliverance' foreshadows Christ saving 'his people from their sins' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 21|Matthew 1:21]]) and bringing 'many sons to glory' ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]). The early church applies Joseph's principle when praying about Christ's crucifixion: Herod, Pilate, Gentiles, and Jews 'gathered together... to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 27|Acts 4:27-28]])—human conspiracy accomplishing divine decree. Joseph's grace toward his guilty brothers—'do not be distressed or angry with yourselves' (v. 5)—prefigures Christ's prayer from the cross: 'Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do' ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 23#Luke 23 . 34|Luke 23:34]]). Most profoundly, Joseph articulates the cross's paradox: supreme human evil achieving supreme divine good. The brothers intended his death; God intended the world's life. The Sanhedrin intended Christ's death; God intended humanity's life. Paul summarizes: 'None of the rulers of this age understood this, for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory' ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 2#1 Corinthians 2 . 8|1 Corinthians 2:8]])—exactly Joseph's point about his brothers. God transforms the worst that humans can do into the best that God accomplishes, making Joseph's story the Old Testament's supreme foreshadowing of Calvary's mystery.
