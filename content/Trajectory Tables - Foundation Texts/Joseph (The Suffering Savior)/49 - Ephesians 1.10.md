### [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 10|Ephesians 1:10]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3622#G3622|οἰκονομία]] (*oikonomia*) - "stewardship, administration, plan, dispensation"
- [[Lexicon/G4138#G4138|πλήρωμα τῶν καιρῶν]] (*plērōma tōn kairōn*) - "fullness of the times, completion of the ages"
- [[Lexicon/G346#G346|ἀνακεφαλαιώσασθαι]] (*anakephalaiosasthai*) - "to sum up, to unite, to bring under one head"
- [[Lexicon/G3956#G3956|τὰ πάντα]] (*ta panta*) - "all things, the universe, everything"
- [[Lexicon/G2032#G2032|ἐπουράνιος]] (*epouranios*) - "heavenly, celestial"
- [[Lexicon/G1919#G1919|ἐπίγειος]] (*epigeios*) - "earthly, terrestrial"

**Context**: Paul's opening benediction in Ephesians praises God for blessing believers 'with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places' (v. 3). Verses 3-14 form a single Greek sentence celebrating God's eternal plan of redemption. Verse 10 identifies the telos (goal) of this plan: 'as a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth.'

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]] (In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 7|Genesis 45:7]] (God sent me before you to preserve life)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 46#Isaiah 46 . 10|Isaiah 46:10]] (declaring the end from the beginning), [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 2#Daniel 2 . 44|Daniel 2:44]] (God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 16|Colossians 1:16-20]] (all things created through him and for him, to reconcile to himself all things), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 28|1 Corinthians 15:28]] (that God may be all in all), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]] (Behold, I am making all things new)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's vision of cosmic reconciliation—'to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth'—finds its OT foreshadowing in Joseph's reconciliation ministry. Joseph reunited his scattered family, bringing brothers who intended evil into reconciliation through the very one they betrayed. His brothers came from Canaan (representing one realm), while Joseph ruled Egypt (another realm), and his administration brought both together in peace. Similarly, Christ unites heaven and earth, reconciling 'things in heaven and things on earth' under his headship. Joseph's declaration 'God sent me before you to preserve a remnant on earth, and to keep alive for you many survivors' ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 7|Genesis 45:7]]) prefigures God's cosmic plan 'to unite all things in him.' The pattern is identical: (1) one person suffers, (2) through suffering gains universal authority, (3) uses authority to reconcile enemies, (4) brings many into blessing through his exaltation. Joseph's reconciliation of his eleven brothers typologically represents Christ reconciling scattered humanity—'he came and preached peace to you who were far off and peace to those who were near' ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 17|Ephesians 2:17]]). The 'fullness of time' (*plērōma tōn kairōn*) when God unites all things parallels the appointed moment when Joseph's brothers stood before him—'God sent me before you' ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 5|Genesis 45:5]]), timing ordained from the beginning. Joseph's administration during famine demonstrates what Paul describes: all people—Egyptians and foreigners, family and strangers—coming under one head's benevolent rule, receiving provision from his hand. The scope expands from Joseph's reconciliation (twelve tribes) to Christ's reconciliation (cosmic totality), yet the principle remains: God works through one man's suffering and exaltation to unite many. Joseph told his brothers, 'it was not you who sent me here, but God' ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 8|Genesis 45:8]]), revealing divine purposes behind human actions—exactly what Paul celebrates in Ephesians: God's 'purpose, which he set forth in Christ as a plan for the fullness of time' (v. 9-10). The brothers' evil intentions and God's good purposes coexisted in the same events, accomplishing reconciliation. Similarly, the cross—supreme human evil—becomes God's means of cosmic reconciliation: 'through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross' ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 20|Colossians 1:20]]). Joseph's tears of joy at reunion with brothers ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 45#Genesis 45 . 14|Genesis 45:14-15]]) foreshadow the eschatological day when Christ presents his reconciled people to the Father and 'God will wipe away every tear from their eyes' ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 4|Revelation 21:4]]). The trajectory from Joseph's administration (preserving physical life during famine) to Christ's headship (granting eternal life to all creation) shows progressive revelation's movement from type to antitype, shadow to substance, temporal to eternal. Yet both demonstrate God's consistent method: working through one man's suffering to unite many under blessing, transforming intended evil into accomplished good, bringing scattered rebels into reconciled family. The 'fullness of time' awaits when every knee bows to Christ ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 10|Philippians 2:10]]), just as Joseph's brothers finally bowed before the one they sold—divine purposes fulfilled, reconciliation achieved, all things united under one head.
