### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 41#Genesis 41 . 55|Genesis 41:55-57]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7458-7459#H7458|רָעָב]] (*rā'āḇ*) - "famine, hunger, scarcity"
- [[Lexicon/H6817-6818#H6817|צָעַק]] (*ṣā'aq*) - "to cry out, to call for help"
- [[Lexicon/H6605-6606#H6605|פָּתַח]] (*pāṯaḥ*) - "to open, to release, to make available"
- [[Lexicon/H7666-7667#H7666|שָׁבַר]] (*šāḇar*) - "to buy grain, to purchase food"
- [[Lexicon/H0776-0777#H0776|אֶרֶץ]] (*'ereṣ*) - "earth, land, world"

**Context**: As the seven-year famine intensifies, all Egypt cries to Pharaoh for bread. Pharaoh directs them to Joseph: 'Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do' (v. 55). Joseph opens all the storehouses and sells grain to Egyptians. The famine's worldwide extent brings 'all the earth' to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, establishing his role as savior beyond Israel or Egypt to the entire known world.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 41#Genesis 41 . 29|Genesis 41:29-30]] (Joseph's prophecy of seven years of famine), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 41#Genesis 41 . 48|Genesis 41:48-49]] (Joseph storing grain in the seven years of plenty)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 42#Genesis 42 . 5|Genesis 42:5-6]] (Joseph's brothers come to Egypt for grain), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 105#Psalm 105 . 16|Psalm 105:16-17]] (When he summoned a famine... he had sent a man ahead of them)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 35|John 6:35]] (I am the bread of life), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 14|John 4:14]] (whoever drinks of the water I give will never thirst), [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 4#1 John 4 . 14|1 John 4:14]] (the Father has sent his Son to be the Savior of the world)

**Christological Connection**: Joseph as the world's only source of grain during famine provides one of Scripture's clearest types of Christ as humanity's only Savior. Pharaoh's command 'Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do' (v. 55) prefigures the Father's declaration: 'This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 17#Matthew 17 . 5|Matthew 17:5]]). Just as starving Egyptians must go to Joseph or perish, spiritually dead sinners must come to Christ for life—'there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 12|Acts 4:12]]). The universal scope—'all the earth came to Egypt to Joseph to buy grain' (v. 57)—anticipates Christ's worldwide mission. John declares God 'sent his Son to be the Savior of the world' ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 4#1 John 4 . 14|1 John 4:14]]), not merely of Israel. Jesus's promise 'whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst' ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 35|John 6:35]]) directly parallels Joseph satisfying physical hunger with grain. The opened storehouses (v. 56) represent Christ's abundant provision—'from his fullness we have all received, grace upon grace' ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 16|John 1:16]]). Joseph prepared during seven years of plenty to sustain through seven years of famine; Christ's incarnation and atoning work provide eternal sustenance for all who come. The commercial transaction—people 'bought' (*šāḇar*) grain from Joseph—reminds that salvation, though freely offered, cost everything. Joseph eventually owned all Egypt through grain sales ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 47#Genesis 47 . 13|Genesis 47:13-26]]); Christ 'purchased' his church 'with his own blood' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 20#Acts 20 . 28|Acts 20:28]]). Most significantly, Joseph's brothers—who sold him into slavery—must come to him for bread, discovering their salvation depends on the one they betrayed. Similarly, those who 'crucified the Lord of glory' ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 2#1 Corinthians 2 . 8|1 Corinthians 2:8]]) must come to him for life, receiving grace from the one they rejected. The worldwide famine represents universal spiritual death; Joseph/Christ provides the only remedy. As all nations came to Egypt, so 'people will come from east and west, and from north and south, and recline at table in the kingdom of God' ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 13#Luke 13 . 29|Luke 13:29]]). Joseph's exaltation positioned him to save the world; Christ's exaltation—'lifted up from the earth'—draws 'all people' to himself ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 32|John 12:32]]).
