### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 18|Genesis 37:18-28]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5230-5231#H5230|נָכַל]] (*nāḵal*) - "to act deceitfully, to conspire"
- [[Lexicon/H0953-0954#H0953|בּוֹר]] (*bôr*) - "pit, cistern, dungeon"
- [[Lexicon/H4376-4377#H4376|מָכַר]] (*māḵar*) - "to sell, to hand over"
- [[Lexicon/H3701-3702#H3701|כֶּסֶף]] (*kesep̄*) - "silver, money"
- [[Lexicon/H3382-3383#H3382|יִשְׁמְעֵאלִים]] (*Yišmᵉ'ēlîm*) - "Ishmaelites, desert traders"

**Context**: Joseph's brothers see him approaching from afar and immediately conspire to kill him. Reuben persuades them to cast him into a pit instead, intending to rescue him later. While eating bread, they see a caravan of Ishmaelite traders and Judah proposes selling Joseph rather than killing him. They sell him for twenty pieces of silver, and he is taken to Egypt.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 8|Genesis 4:8]] (Cain killed Abel - fratricidal pattern), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 29|Genesis 25:29-34]] (Esau sells birthright - brothers' rivalry)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 42#Genesis 42 . 21|Genesis 42:21-22]] (brothers remember their guilt), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 105#Psalm 105 . 17|Psalm 105:17-18]] (He sent a man before them, Joseph sold as a slave)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 11|John 1:11]] (He came to his own, his own did not receive him), [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 14|Matthew 26:14-15]] (Judas sold Jesus for thirty pieces of silver), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 9|Acts 7:9]] (patriarchs were jealous of Joseph and sold him into Egypt)

**Christological Connection**: Joseph's rejection by his brothers provides one of Scripture's clearest types of Christ's rejection by Israel. The brothers' conspiracy 'from afar' (v. 18) parallels the Sanhedrin's plot against Jesus ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 3|Matthew 26:3-4]]). The mocking title 'master of dreams' corresponds to the crown of thorns and 'Hail, King of the Jews!' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 29|Matthew 27:29]])—both mock the very authority that will prove true. The stripping of Joseph's robe (v. 23) prefigures soldiers stripping Jesus and dividing his garments ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 23|John 19:23-24]]). Joseph cast into the pit foreshadows Christ's descent into death—both 'descend' before 'ascending' to salvation's accomplishment. The twenty pieces of silver anticipate Judas's thirty pieces ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 15|Matthew 26:15]]), both betrayals by those who should have been brothers. Judah's role in proposing the sale parallels the tribe of Judah (Jewish leaders) delivering Christ to Gentiles (Romans). The brothers 'sat down to eat' while Joseph suffered, just as Jesus's enemies mocked him on the cross while he bore their sins ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 39|Matthew 27:39-44]]). Yet the greatest parallel emerges later: as Joseph declares 'You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive' ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 50#Genesis 50 . 20|Genesis 50:20]]), so the cross—supreme human evil—accomplishes divine salvation. Peter proclaims the same paradox: though Jesus was 'delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God,' he was crucified 'by the hands of lawless men' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 23|Acts 2:23]]). Stephen, the first martyr, makes the connection explicit: 'The patriarchs, jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt, but God was with him' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 9|Acts 7:9]])—divine presence sustains the rejected one, transforming his suffering into the world's salvation.
