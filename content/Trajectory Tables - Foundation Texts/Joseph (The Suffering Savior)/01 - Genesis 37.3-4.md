### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 3|Genesis 37:3-4]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H0157-0158#H0157|אָהַב]] (*'āhab*) - "to love, to have affection for"
- [[Lexicon/H3801-3802#H3801|כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים]] (*kᵉtōnet passîm*) - "robe of many colors, ornamental tunic"
- [[Lexicon/H8130-8131#H8130|שָׂנֵא]] (*śānē'*) - "to hate, to be hostile toward"

**Context**: Jacob's preferential love for Joseph, expressed through the gift of an ornamental robe, provokes his brothers' intense hatred and inability to speak peacefully with him. This favoritism sets in motion the events that lead to Joseph's suffering and ultimate exaltation.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 28|Genesis 25:28]] (Isaac loved Esau, Rebekah loved Jacob - pattern of parental favoritism)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 18|Genesis 37:18-28]] (brothers' hatred leads to selling Joseph), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 44#Genesis 44 . 20|Genesis 44:20]] (Joseph remembered as the child of Jacob's old age)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 17|Matthew 3:17]] (This is my beloved Son), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 11|John 1:11]] (He came to his own, his own did not receive him)

**Christological Connection**: Jacob's love for Joseph—excessive, visible, provocative—prefigures the Father's love for Christ proclaimed at his baptism: 'This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 17|Matthew 3:17]]). The ornamental robe marking Joseph as heir corresponds to Christ's unique sonship and right to inherit all things ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 2|Hebrews 1:2]]). Joseph's brothers' hatred when they 'saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers' (v. 4) foreshadows how Christ's unique relationship with the Father provoked murderous envy: 'He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him' ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 11|John 1:11]]). The inability to 'speak peacefully' escalates to conspiracy and violence, just as religious leaders 'sought to destroy him' ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 11#Mark 11 . 18|Mark 11:18]]) because of his claims to divine sonship. The pattern is identical: special love from the father → visible tokens of favor → brothers' hatred → plot to kill → suffering leading to exaltation. As Joseph's robe was stripped from him ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 23|Genesis 37:23]]), Christ's garments were taken at crucifixion ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 23|John 19:23-24]]). Yet the Father's love, though it provokes human opposition, ultimately accomplishes salvation for the very brothers who rejected the beloved son—Joseph saved his brothers from famine, Christ saves his people from sin.
