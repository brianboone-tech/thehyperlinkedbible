### [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 1|Ruth 4:1-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1350|H1350]] גֹּאֵל (*goel*) - "kinsman-redeemer"
- [[Lexicon/H7069|H7069]] קָנָה (*qanah*) - "to buy, acquire, redeem"
- [[Lexicon/H6965|H6965]] קוּם (*qum*) - "to raise up, establish"
- [[Lexicon/H8034|H8034]] שֵׁם (*shem*) - "name"
- [[Lexicon/H5159|H5159]] נַחֲלָה (*nachalah*) - "inheritance, heritage"
- [[Lexicon/H7843|H7843]] שָׁחַת (*shachat*) - "to destroy, ruin"
- [[Lexicon/H8179|H8179]] שַׁעַר (*shaʿar*) - "gate" (place of legal proceedings)

**Context**: Boaz goes to the city gate—the place of legal transactions and judgments—to settle Ruth's redemption. A nearer kinsman has first right (גְּאֻלָּה). Boaz presents the case: Naomi is selling Elimelech's land. The nearer kinsman agrees to redeem it. Then Boaz adds the crucial condition: "On the day you buy the land from Naomi, you must also acquire Ruth the Moabitess, the widow of the deceased, to raise up the name of the deceased on his inheritance" (v. 5). The nearer kinsman refuses: "I cannot redeem it myself, or I would jeopardize my own inheritance. Take my right of redemption, because I cannot redeem it" (v. 6).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The nearer kinsman's refusal contrasts with Judah's acknowledgment of Tamar (Gen 38:26) and anticipates the law's inability to save (Rom 8:3).
- "I would jeopardize my own inheritance"—the cost of redeeming Ruth (Moabitess, potential legal complications) exceeds his willingness. The law can identify what should be done but cannot produce willingness.
- The gate proceedings fulfill [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 25#Deuteronomy 25 . 7|Deuteronomy 25:7]]: "If the man does not want to take his brother's widow, she shall go to the elders at the gate."

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Leviticus 25:25 (nearest kinsman redeems), Deuteronomy 25:7-10 (gate proceedings for levirate refusal), Genesis 38:26 (Judah acknowledges Tamar)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 32#Jeremiah 32 . 10|Jeremiah 32:10-15]] (deed of purchase with witnesses)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 3|Romans 8:3-4]] (what the law was powerless to do, God did), [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]] (Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law)

**Type Classification**: Providential (narratively arranged contrast); Forward-Looking (the unable/unwilling kinsman anticipates the law's limitations, the willing Boaz anticipates Christ).

**Christological Connection**: The nearer kinsman's refusal illuminates Christ's willingness. (1) **The Law Cannot Redeem**: The nearer kinsman had legal right but refused—"I cannot redeem it." [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 3|Romans 8:3]]: "What the law was powerless to do...God did by sending His own Son." (2) **Self-Preservation**: The kinsman feared ruining his inheritance; Christ "did not consider equality with God something to be grasped" (Phil 2:6). (3) **Unwillingness vs. Willingness**: "I cannot" versus Boaz's "I will." Christ: "I lay down My life...No one takes it from Me, but I lay it down of My own accord" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 17|John 10:17-18]]). (4) **Anonymous vs. Named**: The refusing kinsman is "so-and-so"; Christ has "the name above every name" (Phil 2:9). (5) **Cost Counted**: The kinsman calculated cost and declined; Christ "for the joy set before Him endured the cross" (Heb 12:2). The contrast reveals that redemption requires not just qualification (kinship, ability) but willingness—and Christ was infinitely willing.
