### [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 2#Ruth 2 . 1|Ruth 2:1, 20]]; [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 9|Ruth 3:9, 12]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1350|H1350]] גֹּאֵל (*goel*) - "kinsman-redeemer, redeemer"
- [[Lexicon/H4129|H4129]] מוֹדַע (*modaʿ*) - "kinsman, relative"
- [[Lexicon/H376|H376]] אִישׁ גִּבּוֹר חַיִל (*ʾish gibbor chayil*) - "mighty man of wealth/valor"
- [[Lexicon/H2617|H2617]] חֶסֶד (*chesed*) - "steadfast love, kindness, loyalty"
- [[Lexicon/H3671|H3671]] כָּנָף (*kanaph*) - "wing, corner of garment"
- [[Lexicon/H6566|H6566]] פָּרַשׂ (*paras*) - "to spread out"

**Context**: Ruth 2:1 introduces Boaz: "a prominent man of noble character from the clan of Elimelech." After Ruth gleans in his field and returns to Naomi, her mother-in-law exclaims: "He is one of our kinsman-redeemers" (גֹּאֵל, 2:20). In chapter 3, Ruth approaches Boaz at the threshing floor with a direct appeal: "Spread the corner of your garment over me, for you are a kinsman-redeemer" (3:9). Boaz confirms: "Yes, it is true that I am a kinsman-redeemer" (3:12). The word גֹּאֵל appears repeatedly (2:20; 3:9, 12, 13; 4:1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 14), marking Boaz's identity as the redemption figure.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The identification of Boaz as גֹּאֵל connects to Leviticus 25's redemption laws. The narrative embodies the legal principle.
- Ruth's appeal to "spread your wing" (כָּנָף) echoes Boaz's earlier blessing: "under whose wings you have taken refuge" (2:12). Ruth asks Boaz to be the human agent of God's protective provision.
- The vocabulary of refuge and covering connects to God's protective "wings" throughout the Psalms (17:8; 36:7; 57:1; 61:4; 63:7; 91:4).

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Leviticus 25:25, 47-49 (goel laws), Ruth 2:12 (under God's wings), Psalm 91:4 (under His wings you will find refuge)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 16#Ezekiel 16 . 8|Ezekiel 16:8]] (God spreads His garment over Israel in marriage covenant)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5]] (Boaz in genealogy), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 11|Hebrews 2:11-17]] (Christ not ashamed to call us brothers)

**Type Classification**: Providential (narrative arranged to reveal redemption); Forward-Looking (goel terminology anticipates messianic redemption).

**Christological Connection**: Boaz's identification as גֹּאֵל directly prefigures Christ. (1) **Wealthy and Able**: Boaz is אִישׁ גִּבּוֹר חַיִל ("man of wealth/valor"); Christ possesses infinite worth: "in Him all the fullness of Deity dwells bodily" (Col 2:9). (2) **Kinsman by Clan**: Boaz is "from the clan of Elimelech"; Christ entered our clan: "Both the One who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are of the same family" (Heb 2:11). (3) **Protective Wings**: Ruth sought refuge under Boaz's garment; believers find refuge under Christ: "How often I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings" (Matt 23:37). (4) **Acknowledged Redeemer**: Boaz confirms "I am a kinsman-redeemer"; Christ identifies Himself as Savior: "I am the way, the truth, and the life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]]). (5) **Willing to Redeem**: Boaz says "I will" ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 3#Ruth 3 . 13|Ruth 3:13]]); Christ says "I will" to cleanse the leper, to give rest, to raise up on the last day.
