### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 18#Numbers 18 . 1|Numbers 18:1-7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5301-5400#H5375|תִּשְׂאוּ]] (*tiśʾû*) - "you shall bear, carry"
- [[Lexicon/H5701-5800#H5771|עֲוֺן]] (*ʿăwōn*) - "iniquity, guilt"
- [[Lexicon/H4501-4600#H4720|הַמִּקְדָּשׁ]] (*hammiqdāš*) - "the sanctuary, holy place"
- [[Lexicon/H3501-3600#H3547|כְּהֻנָּה]] (*kəhunnâ*) - "priesthood"
- [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8334|וַעֲבַדְתֶּם]] (*waʿăbadtem*) - "and you shall serve, minister"
- [[Lexicon/H4601-4700#H4976|מַתָּנָה]] (*mattānâ*) - "gift, grant"

**Context**: After Korah's rebellion (Numbers 16) and Aaron's budding rod confirming his priesthood (Numbers 17), God assigns comprehensive priestly responsibilities to Aaron and his sons. They bear the iniquity of the sanctuary and priesthood, guard the holy places, and serve at the altar and within the veil. The priesthood is described as a "gift" from God, emphasizing divine appointment rather than human achievement.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 18#Numbers 18 . 1|Numbers 18:1-7]]'s comprehensive priestly duties prefigure Christ's superior mediatorial work. As Aaron and his sons bore "iniquity connected with the sanctuary" (v. 1), mediating between holy God and sinful Israel, Christ bore humanity's iniquity completely: "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]]). Aaron's sons bore sanctuary guilt as servants maintaining covenant; Christ bore human guilt as substitute establishing new covenant. The Aaronic priesthood required multiple priests serving in rotation—continual ministry because no single priest sufficed. Christ's priesthood requires no successors: "he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 24|Hebrews 7:24]]). Aaron's priests ministered "regularly" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 6|Hebrews 9:6]]), entering the holy places daily; Christ entered "once for all into the holy places" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]), accomplishing eternal redemption. The assignment to "guard your priesthood for all that concerns the altar and that is within the veil" (v. 7) encompassed comprehensive ministry—from bronze altar (sacrifice for sin) to golden altar (intercession) to ark (God's presence). Christ's priesthood encompasses all: He is both sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]) and intercessor ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]), providing access to God's very throne ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 16|Hebrews 4:16]]). The designation of priesthood as "gift" (*mattānâ*, v. 7) is fulfilled in Christ who was "appointed by him who said to him, 'You are my Son'" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 5|Hebrews 5:5]])—divine appointment, not human presumption. The warning that "any outsider who comes near shall be put to death" (v. 7) protected both God's holiness and Israel's safety under the old covenant; Christ's priesthood opens access to all believers, making them "a royal priesthood" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]) who "have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19]]). The trajectory moves from **exclusive, mortal, standing priests bearing iniquity continually** to **the inclusive, eternal, seated Priest who bore iniquity completely**, perfecting forever those who draw near through Him.
