### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 38|Exodus 29:38-42]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3601-3700#H3532|כְּבָשִׂים]] (*kəbāśîm*) - "lambs"
- [[Lexicon/H8501-8600#H8549|תְּמִימִם]] (*təmîmim*) - "without blemish, perfect"
- [[Lexicon/H8601-8700#H8548|תָּמִיד]] (*tāmîd*) - "continually, regularly, perpetually"
- [[Lexicon/H5901-6000#H5930|עֹלָה]] (*ʿōlâ*) - "burnt offering, whole offering"
- [[Lexicon/H1101-1200#H1242|בַּבֹּקֶר]] (*babbōqer*) - "in the morning"
- [[Lexicon/H6101-6200#H6153|בֵּין הָעַרְבָּיִם]] (*bên hāʿarbayim*) - "between the evenings, at twilight"

**Context**: God prescribes the continual burnt offering to be offered twice daily—morning and evening—at the entrance to the tent of meeting. This establishes Israel's daily rhythm of worship and atonement, maintaining the covenant relationship through perpetual sacrifice. The regular offerings frame each day with recognition of human sinfulness and dependence on God's provision of atonement.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]] (God dwelling among Israel), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 6#Leviticus 6 . 13|Leviticus 6:13]] (fire never goes out), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 28#Numbers 28 . 3|Numbers 28:3-8]] (expansion of daily offering regulations)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 29|1 Kings 18:29]] (Elijah's sacrifice at time of evening offering), [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 16#2 Kings 16 . 15|2 Kings 16:15]] (Ahaz's altar for morning and evening offerings), [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 13#2 Chronicles 13 . 11|2 Chronicles 13:11]] (Abijah's priests burn offerings morning and evening)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11-12]] (priests standing daily vs. Christ sitting after one sacrifice), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 27|Hebrews 7:27]] (Christ offered once), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]] (Lamb of God)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 38|Exodus 29:38-42]]'s continual burnt offering prefigures Christ's once-for-all sacrifice while highlighting the inadequacy of the old covenant system. The *tāmîd* offerings required two lambs daily—730 lambs annually, 7,300 per decade, tens of thousands across centuries—yet could never truly "take away sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 4|Hebrews 10:4]]). The sheer volume of repeated sacrifices demonstrates covenant maintenance without covenant perfection. Christ fulfills what the morning and evening lambs prefigured: He is "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]])—not merely covering sin daily but removing it permanently. The contrast is stark: Levitical priests "stand daily offering sacrifices" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]]), their standing posture signaling unfinished work; Christ "offered for all time a single sacrifice" then "sat down at the right hand of God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]), His seated posture proving completed redemption. The daily offerings pointed forward to Christ through multiple correspondences: (1) **Substitutionary**—lambs died in place of sinners, as Christ "bore our sins in his body" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]]); (2) **Total consecration**—burnt offerings were wholly consumed, as Christ gave Himself completely ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]); (3) **Perfect sacrifice**—lambs were unblemished (*təmîmim*), as Christ was "without blemish" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]); (4) **Covenantal access**—offerings enabled God's presence, as Christ's blood grants "confidence to enter the holy places" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19]]). Yet Christ's sacrifice infinitely surpasses the daily offerings through escalation: what was repeated is now once-for-all; what was provisional is now permanent; what maintained covenant is now perfects it; what covered sins now removes them. The trajectory moves from **continual insufficiency** to **completed sufficiency**, from priests who stand because their work never ends to the Priest who sits because His work is finished forever.
