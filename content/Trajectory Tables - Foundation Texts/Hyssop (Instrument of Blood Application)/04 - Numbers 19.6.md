### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H201-300#H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*ʾêzôb*) - "hyssop, a plant used for medicinal and religious purposes"
- [[Lexicon/H6701-6800#H6718|H6718]] אֶרֶז (*ʾerez*) - "cedar"
- [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8144|H8144]] תּוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי (*tôlaʿat šānî*) - "scarlet wool, crimson thread"
- [[Lexicon/H6501-6600#H6510|H6510]] פָּרָה (*pārâ*) - "heifer, young cow"
- [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8313|H8313]] שָׂרַף (*śārap*) - "to burn"

**Context**: Numbers 19 prescribes the red heifer ritual for purification from corpse-defilement—the most severe uncleanness. A perfect red heifer without blemish, never yoked, was slaughtered outside the camp and completely burned (vv. 2-5). During the burning, "the priest is to take cedar wood, hyssop, and scarlet wool and throw them onto the burning heifer" (v. 6). The ashes, mixed with living water, created purification water sprinkled with hyssop on anyone defiled by touching a dead body (v. 18). Contact with death rendered one unclean seven days (v. 11); sprinkling on the third and seventh days cleansed (v. 12, 19). This ritual powerfully symbolized purification from death itself—sin's ultimate consequence.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]]** - Hyssop first applied blood to deliver from death's angel at Passover. Now it purifies from death's defilement, expanding the typology: hyssop consistently mediates cleansing from death in various forms.
- **[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]]** - The same triad (cedar, hyssop, scarlet) appeared in leprosy cleansing. This repetition establishes a pattern: these three elements together represent comprehensive ceremonial purification.
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]]** - David's prayer "Purge me with hyssop" demonstrates understanding that physical cleansing pointed to spiritual reality. He sought purification from sin's death-producing defilement, not merely ceremonial uncleanness.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]] (hyssop delivering from death at Passover), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]] (hyssop in leprosy cleansing), [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 19|Genesis 3:19]] ("dust you are, and to dust you shall return"—death entered through sin)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] (spiritual cleansing through hyssop-typology), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25]] ("I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean")
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13-14]] (red heifer ashes compared to Christ's blood), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 19|Hebrews 9:19]] (Moses' sprinkling with hyssop), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 29|John 19:29]] (hyssop at Christ's death), [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 5#1 John 5 . 6|1 John 5:6]] (Christ came by water and blood)

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (divinely commanded ceremonial law) and **Forward-Looking** (Hebrews 9:13-14 explicitly interprets the red heifer as expectational type pointing to Christ's superior cleansing)

**Christological Connection**: Hyssop thrown onto the burning red heifer outside the camp prefigures Christ's sacrifice outside Jerusalem's gate ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]). The red heifer's ashes, mixed with living water and sprinkled with hyssop, cleansed from death's defilement; Christ's blood and the Spirit's water cleanse from sin's deadly corruption. Cedar represents His divinity (incorruptible), hyssop His humanity (humble), scarlet His royal blood. What required repeated applications in the old covenant (third and seventh days), Christ accomplished "once for all" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 10|Hebrews 10:10]]). The ritual's paradox—cleansers becoming unclean—highlighted need for a sinless mediator who could purify without contamination. Christ fulfilled that need: "He offered Himself without spot to God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]), purging consciences from dead works to serve the living God. Hyssop, present from Passover through red heifer to Calvary, consistently witnesses the blood-application bringing deliverance from death.
