### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 22|Hebrews 10:22]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4334|G4334]] προσέρχομαι (*proserchomai*) - "to draw near, approach"
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2588|G2588]] καρδία (*kardia*) - "heart"
- [[Lexicon/G4401-4500#G4472|G4472]] ῥαντίζω (*rhantizō*) - "to sprinkle"
- [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4190|G4190]] πονηρός (*ponēros*) - "evil, wicked"
- [[Lexicon/G4801-4900#G4893|G4893]] συνείδησις (*syneidēsis*) - "conscience"
- [[Lexicon/G3001-3100#G3068|G3068]] λούω (*louō*) - "to wash, bathe"

**Context**: Hebrews 10:19-25 forms the climactic exhortation following the theological exposition of Christ's superior priesthood and sacrifice (chs. 7-10). Having demonstrated Christ's once-for-all sacrifice (10:10-18), the author issues threefold call: "Let us draw near" (v. 22), "Let us hold fast" (v. 23), "Let us consider one another" (v. 24). Verse 22 grounds confident approach to God in Christ's work: "Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water." The language echoes priestly preparation (Exodus 29:4, 40:12) and purification rituals involving hyssop-sprinkling, now fulfilled spiritually.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 4|Exodus 29:4]]** - Aaron and his sons were washed with water before consecration to priesthood, establishing that cleansing precedes access to God's presence.
- **[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 6|Leviticus 14:6-7]]** - The healed leper received sevenfold sprinkling with hyssop dipped in blood and water, then washed his body—both external cleansing and ceremonial purification required.
- **[[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 18|Numbers 19:18-19]]** - One ceremonially clean sprinkled the defiled with hyssop dipped in purification water on the third and seventh days, then the defiled person washed clothes and bathed—sprinkling preceded washing.
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]]** - David prayed "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow," pairing hyssop-sprinkling with washing for comprehensive cleansing.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 4|Exodus 29:4]] (priestly washing), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 7|Leviticus 14:7]] (sprinkling and washing for lepers), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 18|Numbers 19:18-19]] (death-defilement purification), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] (spiritual cleansing prayer), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25]] (God's promise to sprinkle clean water)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 2|1 Peter 1:2]] (elect "for sprinkling by Jesus Christ's blood"), [[Readable Bible/56 - Titus/Titus 3#Titus 3 . 5|Titus 3:5]] (washing of regeneration), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 26|Ephesians 5:26]] (church cleansed by washing of water with the word)

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (ceremonial sprinkling was divinely commanded) and **Fulfilled** (apostolic application to believers' spiritual experience through Christ's blood)

**Christological Connection**: Hyssop sprinkled blood and water for ceremonial cleansing; Christ's blood sprinkles the conscience for spiritual purification. The trajectory reaches consummation: where Leviticus required external sprinkling through hyssop followed by washing, Hebrews declares internal sprinkling through Christ's blood grants perpetual access. David prayed "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]])—Hebrews announces this prayer answered: hearts sprinkled, bodies washed, consciences purged. The humble hyssop plant applied blood to unclean Israelites, enabling temporary approach to the earthly tabernacle; Christ's blood sprinkles believing hearts, enabling permanent approach to God's heavenly throne. No more repeated ceremonies, no more external rituals insufficient for conscience—Christ's once-for-all sacrifice accomplishes complete, permanent, internal cleansing. Believers draw near "with true heart in full assurance of faith," confidence rooted not in ceremonial performance but Christ's finished work. The exhortation assumes realized cleansing: hearts already sprinkled, bodies already washed—therefore approach boldly. Hyssop's shadow gives way to Christ's substance. The instrument of ceremonial purification witnesses to the High Priest who purifies eternally.
