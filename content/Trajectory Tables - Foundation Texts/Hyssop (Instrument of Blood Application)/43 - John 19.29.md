### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 29|John 19:29]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5301-5400#G5301|G5301]] ὕσσωπος (*hyssōpos*) - "hyssop" (from Hebrew [[Lexicon/H201-300#H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב)
- [[Lexicon/G3601-3700#G3690|G3690]] ὄξος (*oxos*) - "sour wine, vinegar"
- [[Lexicon/G4601-4700#G4699|G4699]] σπόγγος (*spoggos*) - "sponge"
- [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4060|G4060]] περιτίθημι (*peritithēmi*) - "to put around, place on"

**Context**: John's crucifixion narrative emphasizes fulfillment of Scripture and theological symbolism. After Jesus said "I thirst" (fulfilling Psalm 69:21), "A jar of sour wine was sitting there. So they soaked a sponge in the wine, put it on a stalk of hyssop, and lifted it to His mouth" (John 19:29). John alone among the Gospel writers mentions hyssop—Matthew and Mark say "reed" (κάλαμος, *kalamos*), but John specifies ὕσσωπος (*hyssōpos*). This is deliberate theological detail, not botanical confusion. John earlier identified Jesus as "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29) and carefully times the crucifixion to coincide with Passover lamb slaughter (19:14). Hyssop's appearance climaxes the typological pattern.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]]** - Hyssop applied Passover lamb's blood to doorposts, protecting from death's angel. Now Christ, the true Passover Lamb, dies with hyssop present—the instrument witnessing the reality.
- **[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]]** - Hyssop with blood and water cleansed lepers. Moments later, blood and water flow from Christ's side ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|John 19:34]]), fulfilling what the ritual prefigured.
- **[[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]]** - Hyssop thrown on the burning red heifer created ashes for purification from death-defilement. Christ died "outside the gate" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]) like the red heifer "outside the camp," purifying from sin's deadly contamination.
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]]** - David prayed "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean." At Calvary, the cleansing David sought is accomplished—not through ceremonial hyssop but through Christ's blood.

- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]] (hyssop at Passover), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 6|Leviticus 14:6]] (hyssop applying blood and water), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]] (hyssop in red heifer purification), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] (hyssop-cleansing prayer), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 69#Psalm 69 . 21|Psalm 69:21]] (prophecy of sour wine)

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (Passover was divinely commanded with hyssop specified) and **Fulfilled** (John's inclusion demonstrates deliberate typological fulfillment recognized by apostolic witness)

**Christological Connection**: Hyssop appeared at the first Passover, applying blood that saved from death. At Calvary, hyssop appeared as the true Passover Lamb died, shedding blood that saves from sin. The connection is inescapable: John carefully times Jesus' death to Passover (19:14), identifies Him as the Lamb (1:29), preserves the detail that no bones were broken (19:36, fulfilling Passover law), and records hyssop's presence (19:29)—all proclaiming fulfillment. Where Exodus 12 commanded "Take hyssop, dip it in the blood... and apply it to the doorframe," Christ's blood, witnessed by hyssop, is applied to sinners' hearts through faith. The humble plant used for millennia to apply cleansing blood witnessed the final, perfect sacrifice: "The blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]). Hyssop's last biblical appearance at the cross declares: the shadow has given way to substance, the type to antitype, the promise to fulfillment. Christ our Passover has been sacrificed.
