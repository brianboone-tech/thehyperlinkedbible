### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H201-300#H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*ʾêzôb*) - "hyssop, a plant used for medicinal and religious purposes"
- [[Lexicon/H1-100#H92|H92]] אֲגֻדָּה (*ʾăguddâ*) - "bunch, bundle" (cluster of hyssop)
- [[Lexicon/H1801-1900#H1818|H1818]] דָּם (*dām*) - "blood"
- [[Lexicon/H5401-5500#H5414|H5414]] נָתַן (*nātan*) - "to give, put, set" (brush/apply)

**Context**: On the night of the tenth plague—death of the firstborn—God commanded each Israelite household to slaughter a Passover lamb and apply its blood to the doorframe using a cluster of hyssop. This blood would mark them for deliverance: "When the LORD sees the blood on the top and sides of the doorframe, He will pass over that doorway, and He will not permit the destroyer to enter your houses and strike you down" (Exodus 12:23). The command was specific: take hyssop, dip it in the lamb's blood collected in a basin, and brush it on the lintel and doorposts. No one was to leave their house until morning—safety came only within the blood-marked dwelling.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]], 18** - Hyssop reappears in the red heifer ritual, thrown into the burning heifer (19:6) and used to sprinkle purification water on the unclean (19:18). This connects hyssop with purification from death-defilement, expanding its typological significance beyond Passover.
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]]** - David's prayer "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean" demonstrates that later OT authors understood hyssop's ceremonial use pointed to spiritual cleansing. David looked beyond the physical ritual to the reality it prefigured.
- **[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]]** - Hyssop appears in the leprosy cleansing ritual with cedar wood, scarlet yarn, blood, and living water, establishing a consistent pattern: hyssop mediates cleansing through blood application.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 21|Genesis 3:21]] - God clothed Adam and Eve with animal skins, requiring the first sacrifice and foreshadowing blood-covering for sin
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]] (leprosy cleansing with hyssop), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]], 18 (red heifer purification with hyssop), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] (spiritual cleansing through hyssop-typology)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 29|John 19:29]] (hyssop at Christ's crucifixion), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 19|Hebrews 9:19]] (Moses' covenant-sprinkling with hyssop), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]] ("Christ our Passover has been sacrificed")

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (divinely commanded ceremonial act) and **Forward-Looking** (Psalm 51:7 shows expectational understanding; John 19:29 demonstrates deliberate fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: Hyssop applied the Passover lamb's blood that saved from death's angel; at Calvary, hyssop appeared as the true Lamb's blood was shed to save from sin's judgment. The instrument of ceremonial deliverance witnessed the reality it prefigured. Christ's death fulfilled what Exodus 12 typified: protection through substitutionary blood, applied personally, delivering from divine wrath. The humble hyssop plant, used repeatedly for cleansing throughout Israel's history, points to the humble means by which God accomplished salvation—through faith in the crucified Messiah, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]).
