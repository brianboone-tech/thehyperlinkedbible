### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H201-300#H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*ʾêzôb*) - "hyssop, a plant used for medicinal and religious purposes"
- [[Lexicon/H6701-6800#H6761|H6761]] צָרַעַת (*ṣāraʿat*) - "leprosy, skin disease"
- [[Lexicon/H2801-2900#H2891|H2891]] טָהֵר (*ṭāhēr*) - "to be clean, to cleanse, to purify"
- [[Lexicon/H6701-6800#H6718|H6718]] אֶרֶז (*ʾerez*) - "cedar"
- [[Lexicon/H8301-8400#H8144|H8144]] שָׁנִי (*šānî*) - "scarlet"

**Context**: Leviticus 14 prescribes the ritual for cleansing a person healed from leprosy (ṣāraʿat). Significantly, the ceremony assumes healing has already occurred—the priest examines "outside the camp" and confirms the leprosy is gone (v. 3). The ritual doesn't heal; it certifies healing and restores to community. The priest commands four elements: two live clean birds, cedar wood, scarlet yarn, and hyssop (v. 4). One bird is slaughtered over fresh water in an earthen vessel; the living bird, along with cedar, scarlet, and hyssop, is dipped in the blood mixed with water (v. 6), then sprinkled seven times on the former leper before being released into the open field (v. 7). This complex ritual powerfully dramatizes cleansing, substitution (slain bird), and freedom (released bird).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]]** - Hyssop first appeared applying Passover lamb's blood for deliverance from death. Now it applies blood for cleansing from leprosy—sin's physical picture. The consistent pattern: hyssop mediates blood-application for salvation/restoration.
- **[[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]]** - Hyssop again appears with cedar and scarlet in the red heifer ritual for purification from death-defilement. These three elements (cedar, hyssop, scarlet) form a recurring triad in cleansing rituals, establishing typological consistency.
- **[[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 5#2 Kings 5 . 1|2 Kings 5:1-14]]** - Naaman's leprosy cleansing through washing in Jordan seven times (matching the sevenfold sprinkling in [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 7|Leviticus 14:7]]) demonstrates that healing leprosy was equivalent to raising the dead—a miracle only God could perform ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 5#2 Kings 5 . 7|2 Kings 5:7]]).

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]] (hyssop applying Passover blood), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 18|Exodus 30:18-21]] (bronze laver for cleansing before service)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]], 18 (hyssop in red heifer purification), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]] (David's prayer for hyssop-cleansing), [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 5#2 Kings 5 . 14|2 Kings 5:14]] (Naaman cleansed from leprosy)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 1#Mark 1 . 44|Mark 1:44]] (Jesus commands cleansed leper to fulfill Leviticus 14), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13-14]] (comparing ceremonial cleansing to Christ's blood), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|John 19:34]] (blood and water from Christ's side)

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (divinely instituted ceremonial law) and **Forward-Looking** (the slain/released birds, blood-water mixture, and sevenfold sprinkling all point expectationally toward fuller cleansing)

**Christological Connection**: Hyssop applied blood mixed with living water to cleanse from leprosy—the visible picture of sin. At the cross, hyssop appeared as blood and water flowed from Christ's pierced side ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 29|John 19:29]], 34), accomplishing the spiritual reality the ceremony foreshadowed. The slain bird represents Christ's death; the released bird, His resurrection setting captives free. Cedar's nobility, hyssop's humility, and scarlet's royalty all point to Christ—the King who humbled Himself, shedding royal blood to cleanse from sin's leprosy. What Leviticus 14 accomplished ceremonially and temporarily, Christ's sacrifice accomplishes spiritually and eternally. The healed leper brought to the priest finds its antitype in sinners brought to Christ, cleansed by His blood, restored to God's community.
