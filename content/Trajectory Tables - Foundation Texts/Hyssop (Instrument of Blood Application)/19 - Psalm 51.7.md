### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 7|Psalm 51:7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2401-2500#H2398|H2398]] חָטָא (*ḥāṭāʾ*) - "to sin, to miss the mark"
- [[Lexicon/H201-300#H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*ʾêzôb*) - "hyssop"
- [[Lexicon/H2801-2900#H2891|H2891]] טָהֵר (*ṭāhēr*) - "to be clean, to cleanse, to purify"
- [[Lexicon/H3501-3600#H3526|H3526]] כָּבַס (*kābas*) - "to wash, to cleanse"
- [[Lexicon/H7601-7700#H7950|H7950]] שֶׁלֶג (*šeleg*) - "snow"

**Context**: Psalm 51 is David's penitential prayer after Nathan confronted him regarding adultery with Bathsheba and murder of Uriah (2 Samuel 11-12). The superscription identifies the historical setting; the content reveals a soul crushed by guilt seeking divine mercy. Verse 7 stands at the psalm's center, moving from confession (vv. 3-6) to petition for cleansing (vv. 7-12). David doesn't merely ask forgiveness; he pleads for purification: "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow." This reveals understanding that hyssop's ceremonial use pointed to spiritual reality. David committed sins requiring no ceremonial hyssop-cleansing (adultery and murder had no purification ritual), yet he appeals to hyssop's typological significance.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]]** - David would have known hyssop applied Passover lamb's blood for deliverance from death's angel. His sins deserved death; he seeks cleansing through the reality Passover foreshadowed.
- **[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-7]]** - Hyssop cleansed lepers through blood-sprinkling. David treats his sin as spiritual leprosy requiring divine cleansing, not mere ceremonial washing.
- **[[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]], 18** - Hyssop purified from death-defilement. David's sins produced death (Bathsheba's husband, their first child); he needs purification from sin's death-dealing pollution.
- **[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 32#Psalm 32 . 1|Psalm 32:1-2]]** - David's companion penitential psalm declares: "Blessed is the one whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered"—the language of atonement and blood-covering that hyssop applied.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 22|Exodus 12:22]] (hyssop applying blood at Passover), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 14#Leviticus 14 . 4|Leviticus 14:4-7]] (hyssop cleansing lepers), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 6|Numbers 19:6]], 18 (hyssop purifying from death)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25]] ("I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses"), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 1#Isaiah 1 . 18|Isaiah 1:18]] ("though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow")
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7-9]] (Christ's blood cleanses from all sin), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13-14]] (ceremonial cleansing points to Christ's blood purging the conscience), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]] (robes washed white in the Lamb's blood)

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (David appeals to divinely instituted ceremonial rituals) and **Forward-Looking** (David's expectational faith looks beyond ceremony to spiritual cleansing, fulfilled in Christ)

**Christological Connection**: David prayed "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean"; Christ's blood provides the cleansing David sought. At Calvary, hyssop appeared ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 29|John 19:29]]) as the reality was accomplished—not ceremonial cleansing but spiritual purification "from dead works to serve the living God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]). David's confidence—"I shall be clean," "whiter than snow"—finds fulfillment in those who "have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]]). The paradox David intuited (blood making white) Christ resolves: His sacrificial blood accomplishes what hyssop-ceremonies foreshadowed. Where David could only appeal to the type, believers possess the antitype. Where David prayed in hope, the church testifies in fulfillment: "The blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]). Hyssop applied blood; Christ is the blood applied. David's penitential prayer becomes the believer's confident assurance: cleansing through Christ is complete, permanent, perfect—whiter than snow.
