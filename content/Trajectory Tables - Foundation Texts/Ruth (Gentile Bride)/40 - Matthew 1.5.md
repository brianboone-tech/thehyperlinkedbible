### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4533|G4533]] Σαλμών (*Salmōn*) - "Salmon"
- [[Lexicon/G1080|G1080]] γεννάω (*gennaō*) - "to beget, give birth to"
- [[Lexicon/G1005|G1005]] Βοόζ (*Booz*) - "Boaz"
- [[Lexicon/G4477|G4477]] Ῥαχάβ (*Hrachab*) - "Rahab"
- [[Lexicon/G4503|G4503]] Ῥούθ (*Hrouth*) - "Ruth"
- [[Lexicon/G5601|G5601]] Ὠβήδ (*Ōbēd*) - "Obed"

**Context**: Matthew 1:5 states: "Salmon was the father of Boaz by Rahab, Boaz was the father of Obed by Ruth, Obed was the father of Jesse." Matthew deliberately includes four women in Jesus' genealogy—Tamar (v. 3), Rahab (v. 5a), Ruth (v. 5b), and "Uriah's wife" (v. 6)—all with Gentile connections or irregular circumstances. Ruth the Moabitess appears in the Messiah's direct lineage, fulfilling what her story anticipated.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Matthew follows [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 18|Ruth 4:18-22]]'s genealogy but adds the women's names—highlighting grace operating through unexpected channels.
- By including Rahab (Canaanite harlot), Ruth (Moabitess), and Bathsheba (implicated in David's sin), Matthew demonstrates that the Messiah's lineage includes those who found grace despite legal or moral disqualification.
- The genealogy connects Genesis 38 (Tamar-Judah) through Ruth to David to Christ—a sustained pattern of grace transcending expectation.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Ruth 4:21 (Boaz father of Obed), Genesis 38:29 (Perez born), Joshua 2:1 (Rahab the harlot), 2 Samuel 11 (David and Bathsheba)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 32|Luke 3:32]] (parallel genealogy), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 3|Romans 1:3]] (descended from David), [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 28|Galatians 3:28]] (no Jew or Greek in Christ)
- **PARALLEL**: Luke 3:31-33 (Ruth's line in Luke's genealogy)

**Type Classification**: Direct (apostolic inclusion in messianic genealogy); Fulfillment (OT patterns reach their goal in Christ).

**Christological Connection**: Ruth's presence in Matthew 1 directly connects her to Christ. (1) **Gentile in Genealogy**: A Moabitess is Jesus' ancestress. Christ's lineage already included Gentiles—His mission to "all nations" (Matt 28:19) is embedded in His DNA. (2) **Grace Pattern**: Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, Bathsheba—each found grace despite disqualification. Christ came "to seek and save the lost" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 19#Luke 19 . 10|Luke 19:10]]), not the self-righteous. (3) **Women Highlighted**: Patriarchal genealogies typically named men only. Matthew's emphasis on women anticipates the gospel's radical inclusion—in Christ "there is no male and female" (Gal 3:28). (4) **From Ruth to Christ**: The line runs Boaz→Obed→Jesse→David→...→Jesus. Ruth is not tangential but central—grandmother of the king, ancestress of the King of kings. (5) **Anticipating the Bride**: Ruth the redeemed bride is in the lineage of Christ the Bridegroom. Her story anticipates the church—Gentile converts who become Christ's Bride. The Moabitess in the genealogy demonstrates that the Messiah's people would always include those from "every nation."
