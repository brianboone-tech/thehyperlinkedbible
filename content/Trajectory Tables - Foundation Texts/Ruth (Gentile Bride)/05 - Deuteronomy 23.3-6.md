### [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 23#Deuteronomy 23 . 3|Deuteronomy 23:3-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5984|H5984]] עַמּוֹנִי (*ʿammoni*) - "Ammonite"
- [[Lexicon/H4125|H4125]] מוֹאָבִי (*moʾavi*) - "Moabite"
- [[Lexicon/H6951|H6951]] קָהָל (*qahal*) - "assembly, congregation"
- [[Lexicon/H935|H935]] בּוֹא (*boʾ*) - "to enter, come"
- [[Lexicon/H1875|H1875]] דָּרַשׁ (*darash*) - "to seek, inquire"
- [[Lexicon/H7965|H7965]] שָׁלוֹם (*shalom*) - "peace, welfare"
- [[Lexicon/H2896|H2896]] טוֹבָה (*tovah*) - "good, prosperity"
- [[Lexicon/H5769|H5769]] עוֹלָם (*ʿolam*) - "forever, perpetuity"

**Context**: Deuteronomy 23:3-6 excludes Ammonites and Moabites from Israel's assembly: "No Ammonite or Moabite may enter the assembly of the LORD. Even to the tenth generation, none of them may enter the assembly of the LORD forever" (v. 3). The reason given is their inhospitality during the Exodus and hiring of Balaam (vv. 4-5). Verse 6 commands: "You shall not seek their peace or their prosperity all your days forever." This law creates the theological tension Ruth's story resolves: how can a Moabitess become an ancestress of David?

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The exclusion relates to corporate/political alliance, not individual conversion. Rabbinic interpretation distinguished between "Ammonite/Moabite males" (excluded) and females (not mentioned).
- Ruth's story demonstrates that faith-based incorporation transcends ethnic exclusion. Her confession ([[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 16|Ruth 1:16-17]]) constitutes genuine conversion.
- [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 1|Nehemiah 13:1-3]] applies this law strictly; Ruth's canonical position provides the counterbalance—grace for converts.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Numbers 22-24 (Balaam's hiring), Deuteronomy 2:9, 19 (earlier provision for Moab/Ammon)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 1#Ruth 1 . 16|Ruth 1:16-17]] (Ruth's conversion), [[Readable Bible/08 - Ruth/Ruth 4#Ruth 4 . 17|Ruth 4:17-22]] (Moabitess in David's line), [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 1|Nehemiah 13:1-3]] (application of exclusion)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 34|Acts 10:34-35]] (God shows no partiality), [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 28|Galatians 3:28]] (no Jew or Greek in Christ)

**Type Classification**: Direct (legislated exclusion); Backward-Looking (Ruth's story reveals that the law had limits the original audience couldn't perceive).

**Christological Connection**: This law establishes the background against which Ruth's grace shines. (1) **Excluded by Law**: Moabites were excluded from the assembly; Gentiles were "strangers to the covenants of promise" (Eph 2:12). The exclusion makes inclusion miraculous. (2) **Overcoming Exclusion**: Ruth's faith demonstrates that the law's exclusion had limits. In Christ, "There is neither Jew nor Greek" (Gal 3:28)—all exclusions are overcome. (3) **"Tenth Generation"**: The prohibition extended to the tenth generation; Ruth's descendant David was approximately the third generation (Ruth→Obed→Jesse→David). Grace operates outside legal calculation. (4) **"Forever" Qualified**: Even "forever" exclusions yield to greater purposes. Christ's inclusion of Gentiles fulfills and transcends OT restrictions. (5) **Enemy Becomes Family**: Moab's enmity (hiring Balaam) is overcome when a Moabitess becomes the Messiah's ancestress. Enemies become the Bride.
