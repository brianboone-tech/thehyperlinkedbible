### [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 15#Deuteronomy 15 . 1|Deuteronomy 15:1-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H8058|H8058]] שָׁמַט (*shamat*) - to release, remit
- [[Lexicon/H8059|H8059]] שְׁמִטָּה (*shemittah*) - release, remission (of debt)
- [[Lexicon/H5383|H5383]] נָשָׁה (*nashah*) - to lend, be a creditor
- [[Lexicon/H5378|H5378]] נָשָׁא (*nasha*) - to exact (debt)

**Context**: Deuteronomy 15:1-2: "At the end of every seven years you shall grant a release (שְׁמִטָּה). And this is the manner of the release: every creditor shall release (שָׁמוֹט) what he has lent to his neighbor." Verses 4-6 promise: "There will be no poor among you... if only you will strictly obey... the LORD your God will bless you... and you shall lend to many nations."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Extends sabbatical year from agricultural to economic sphere
- Same Hebrew root (שׁמט) used for land release (Exod 23:11) and debt release
- Connects economic justice to covenant blessing

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 23#Exodus 23 . 10|Exodus 23:10-11]] - Same root word for "release"
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 25#Leviticus 25 . 35|Leviticus 25:35-38]] - Laws about lending to poor
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 5#Nehemiah 5 . 1|Nehemiah 5:1-11]] - Nehemiah enforces debt release
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 10#Nehemiah 10 . 31|Nehemiah 10:31]] - Covenant renewal includes debt release
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 6#Matthew 6 . 12|Matthew 6:12]] - "Forgive us our debts"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 18#Matthew 18 . 21|Matthew 18:21-35]] - Parable of unforgiving servant

**Type Classification**: Direct Type - Divinely commanded institution with economic and spiritual dimensions

**Christological Connection**: The sabbatical year's debt release typifies Christ's forgiveness of our spiritual debt. As Israel was to "release" (שָׁמַט) what was owed, Christ releases believers from the debt of sin. The Lord's Prayer ("forgive us our debts") and the parable of the unforgiving servant (Matt 18) draw on this sabbatical year imagery. Those forgiven much must forgive others - economic mercy typifies gospel mercy.
