**Context:**
The Queen of Sheba, having heard of Solomon's fame "concerning the name of the LORD," travels from southern Arabia (approximately 1,200 miles) to test him with hard questions. She arrives with an enormous caravan bearing spices, gold, and precious stones—diplomatic gifts befitting royal encounter. After Solomon answers all her questions ("there was nothing hidden from the king that he could not explain to her"), she is left breathless: "The half was not told me. Your wisdom and prosperity surpass the report that I heard" (v. 7). She blesses the LORD, Solomon's God, recognizing Him as Israel's true King who set Solomon on the throne "to execute justice and righteousness" (v. 9). This visit of a Gentile queen acknowledging Israel's God and wisdom prefigures the nations streaming to Christ's light, with Jesus declaring: "The queen of the South will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42).

**Ninefold Analysis:**

**OT Context:**
First Kings 10:1-13 stands at the apex of Solomon's glory—international recognition of Israel's God-given wisdom and prosperity. The Queen of Sheba's motivation combines political, economic, and spiritual interests: "She came to test him with hard questions" (v. 1). The Hebrew *ḥîḏôṯ* (riddles, difficult questions) suggests more than casual curiosity—ancient Near Eastern diplomacy employed wisdom contests to assess potential allies' intelligence and capability. Her journey from Sheba (modern Yemen/Ethiopia region) demonstrates remarkable determination—traveling over 1,000 miles through desert terrain with a massive caravan. The gifts she brings—"a very great quantity" of spices, gold, and precious stones—indicate both wealth and diplomatic protocol. Significantly, the text states she came "when she heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD" (v. 1)—her interest isn't merely in Solomon's wisdom but in the God behind it. Solomon's comprehensive answers impress her beyond expectation: "She told him all that was on her heart" (*kol 'ăšer hāyāh 'im-ləḇāḇāh*), and "Solomon answered all her questions; there was nothing hidden from the king that he could not explain to her" (vv. 2-3). The phrase "nothing hidden" (*lō'-hāyāh dāḇār ne'əlām*) emphasizes Solomon's complete mastery. Her response combines four elements: (1) Overwhelmed wonder—"there was no more breath in her" (*wəlō'-hāyāh ḇāh 'ôḏ rûaḥ*, v. 5), literally "no spirit remained"; (2) Confession of understatement—"The half was not told me" (v. 7); (3) Blessing of Solomon's servants and people (v. 8); (4) Most significantly, blessing of the LORD: "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel! Because the LORD loved Israel forever, he has made you king, that you may execute justice and righteousness" (v. 9). This Gentile queen acknowledges Israel's God as the source of Solomon's wisdom and kingdom, recognizing the divine purpose: justice and righteousness. The exchange of gifts (vv. 10-13) seals diplomatic and trade relations, with special note that Solomon gave the queen "all that she desired, whatever she asked besides what was given her by the bounty of King Solomon" (v. 13).

**Jewish Backgrounds:**
Jewish tradition embellished the Queen of Sheba narrative significantly. Targum Sheni (on Esther) expands her visit into elaborate romance, presenting riddles she posed to Solomon (e.g., distinguishing circumcised boys from uncircumcised, male from female, identifying genuine flowers from artificial). Josephus (*Antiquities* 8.165-175) identifies her as "queen of Egypt and Ethiopia" and emphasizes her amazement at Solomon's wisdom and magnificence, though he doesn't embellish with riddles. Ethiopian tradition (recorded in *Kebra Negast*, "Glory of the Kings") claims the Queen of Sheba (named Makeda) bore Solomon a son, Menelik I, who allegedly brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia—though this legend lacks biblical or historical support. Rabbinic interpretation (Midrash Mishle 1:1) saw the Queen of Sheba's visit as fulfillment of Psalm 72:10: "May the kings of Tarshish and of the coastlands render him tribute; may the kings of Sheba and Seba bring gifts!" The Talmud (Bava Batra 15b) debates whether Solomon wrote Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and Song of Solomon before or after the Queen's visit, concluding these works emerged from the wisdom she witnessed. Medieval Jewish commentators like Rashi emphasized that she came specifically because of "the name of the LORD"—not merely Solomon's personal fame but God's reputation. The Zohar (mystical tradition) interpreted her visit as representing the Gentile world's ultimate recognition of divine wisdom, prefiguring messianic age when all nations acknowledge the God of Israel.

**Text Form:**
The narrative employs typical Hebrew storytelling structure: (1) Setting and arrival (vv. 1-2); (2) Interaction and testing (v. 3); (3) Queen's response (vv. 4-9); (4) Exchange of gifts (vv. 10-13); (5) Departure (v. 13). The opening phrase "when the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD" uses *šēma'* (report, fame) which will be contrasted with direct experience: what she heard versus what she saw. The verb "to test" (*lənassōṯô*) from *nāsāh* means to prove, try, examine—used elsewhere for testing faith (Genesis 22:1) or testing God (Exodus 17:2). The description of her caravan uses repetitive abundance language: "very great quantity" (*raḇ mə'ōḏ*), "gold in great quantity" (*zāhāḇ raḇ*), "precious stones" (*'eḇen yəqārāh*). Her reaction employs emphatic negative construction: "there was no (*lō'-hāyāh*) more breath (*rûaḥ*) in her"—complete astonishment. The phrase "the half was not told me" (*lō'-hāyāh ḥăṣî haggəḏāh*) uses *ḥăṣî* (half, middle), suggesting she heard 50% but reality exceeded 100%. Her blessing formula "Blessed be (*bārûḵ*) the LORD" parallels liturgical blessings throughout Scripture. The divine motivation she articulates—"Because (*ya'an*) the LORD loved (*'ahaḇ*) Israel forever (*lə'ōlām*)"—provides theological interpretation of Solomon's reign: not personal achievement but divine gift for Israel's benefit. The concluding note that Solomon gave her "all that she desired, whatever she asked" (*'ēṯ kol-ḥepṣāh 'ăšer šā'ālāh*) demonstrates royal generosity exceeding diplomatic protocol.

**Hermeneutical Use:**
First Kings 10:1-13 functions typologically as the Queen of Sheba's visit establishes the pattern for Gentile acknowledgment of God's chosen king, fulfilled in the wise men's visit to infant Jesus (Matthew 2:1-11) and consummated when "the nations will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it" (Revelation 21:24). Jesus explicitly identifies this connection: "The queen of the South will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42). The typological movement proceeds: (1) **Geographic pattern**—Queen from distant land (Sheba) → Wise men from the East → All nations to new Jerusalem; (2) **Recognition pattern**—She hears report, comes, sees, confesses → Magi follow star, worship, present gifts → Every knee bows (Philippians 2:10); (3) **Wisdom pattern**—Solomon answers all questions → Christ "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3) → Lamb opens sealed scroll (Revelation 5); (4) **Gift pattern**—Gold, spices, precious stones → Gold, frankincense, myrrh → Nations bring glory and honor (Revelation 21:26); (5) **Blessing pattern**—Queen blesses the LORD → Magi worship Christ → Universal worship (Revelation 5:13). The phrase "the half was not told me" finds fulfillment in 1 Corinthians 2:9: "What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the heart of man imagined, what God has prepared for those who love him." Paul applies Isaiah 64:4 to gospel revelation, suggesting that even Scripture's testimony falls short of Christ's actual glory. The Queen's breathlessness prefigures worship response to Christ: "At the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth" (Philippians 2:10).

**Theological Use:**
This passage establishes foundational truths about God's universal purposes and the magnetic power of divine wisdom: **(1) God's wisdom attracts all peoples**—The Queen's long journey demonstrates wisdom's universal appeal. Christ declares: "And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself" (John 12:32). Divine wisdom magnetically draws truth-seekers from every nation. **(2) Report creates hunger; encounter creates worship**—The Queen heard secondhand testimony, but personal experience transformed curiosity into confession: "Blessed be the LORD your God." Similarly, "Faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ" (Romans 10:17), but maturity comes through personal encounter. **(3) Gentile inclusion was God's plan from the beginning**—Abraham's covenant promised "all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). The Queen of Sheba's visit demonstrates this promise advancing. Paul reveals the mystery: "The Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel" (Ephesians 3:6). **(4) God's king exists for justice and righteousness**—The Queen rightly identifies the purpose: God set Solomon on the throne "that you may execute justice and righteousness" (v. 9). This anticipates Christ's kingdom: "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever, the scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your kingdom" (Hebrews 1:8). **(5) Reality exceeds testimony**—"The half was not told me" (v. 7) applies infinitely more to Christ: "No eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the heart of man imagined, what God has prepared for those who love him" (1 Corinthians 2:9). Even Scripture's testimony—though true—cannot fully convey Christ's glory experienced personally. **(6) Wisdom serves the glory of God, not self**—The Queen came "concerning the name of the LORD" (v. 1). Solomon's wisdom didn't draw attention to himself but to God—exactly Christ's pattern: "I do not seek my own glory" (John 8:50). **(7) Royal generosity exceeds expectation**—Solomon gave "all that she desired, whatever she asked" (v. 13), prefiguring Christ's extravagant grace: "He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all, how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?" (Romans 8:32).

**Rhetorical Use:**
The passage's rhetorical power lies in the journey-encounter-response structure that invites readers to identify with the Queen. Her progression mirrors the spiritual journey: (1) **Hearing**—"she heard of the fame of Solomon" (v. 1); (2) **Seeking**—"she came to test him" (v. 1); (3) **Questioning**—"she told him all that was on her heart" (v. 2); (4) **Receiving**—"Solomon answered all her questions" (v. 3); (5) **Experiencing**—"when the queen of Sheba had seen all the wisdom of Solomon, the house that he had built..." (v. 4); (6) **Overwhelmed**—"there was no more breath in her" (v. 5); (7) **Confessing**—"The half was not told me" (v. 7); (8) **Blessing**—"Blessed be the LORD your God" (v. 9); (9) **Receiving gifts**—"King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all that she desired" (v. 13). This nine-stage pattern models spiritual pilgrimage from hearing to worship. Rhetorically, the text creates enormous contrast between secondhand report and firsthand experience—what she heard versus what she saw. The phrase "the half was not told me" functions as a rhetorical climax, expressing the inadequacy of words to convey reality. For Christian readers, this invites application: Have we merely heard about Christ, or have we encountered Him personally? The Queen's breathless response models appropriate worship: intellect, emotion, and will fully engaged. Her blessing of the LORD provides liturgical model—acknowledging God as source of all good gifts. The extravagant gift exchange demonstrates covenant-making through reciprocal generosity, pointing to Christ's lavish grace toward believers.

**Christological Connection:**
First Kings 10:1-13 establishes the Queen of Sheba's visit as a prophetic preview of Christ drawing all nations to Himself. Jesus explicitly claims: "Something greater than Solomon is here" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 42|Matthew 12:42]]), making the typological connection direct. **Where the Queen traveled 1,200 miles to hear Solomon's wisdom, the wise men followed a star to worship newborn Christ** ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 2#Matthew 2 . 1|Matthew 2:1-11]]). Both parties came from distant lands bearing precious gifts; both recognized divine revelation; both returned transformed. **Where Solomon answered the Queen's questions, Christ possesses "all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" hidden in Him** ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 3|Colossians 2:3]]). No question baffles Christ: "In him are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." **Where the Queen confessed "the half was not told me," believers discover Christ's glory infinitely exceeds Scripture's testimony**. Paul prays that believers "may have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 3#Ephesians 3 . 18|Ephesians 3:18-19]])—reality exceeds description. **Where the Queen blessed the LORD for setting Solomon on Israel's throne, angels announce Christ's birth**: "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among those with whom he is pleased!" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 2#Luke 2 . 14|Luke 2:14]]). **Where Solomon's wisdom drew one foreign queen, Christ draws all nations**: "The kings of the earth will bring their glory into it [the New Jerusalem]" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 24|Revelation 21:24]]). Isaiah prophesied: "Nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 3|Isaiah 60:3]])—fulfilled partially in Solomon, completely in Christ. **Where Solomon gave the Queen "all that she desired," Christ gives believers everything**: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 3|Ephesians 1:3]]). **Where the Queen returned to her land, those who encounter Christ are sent out**: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]]). The trajectory is clear: Solomon's wisdom attracted one Gentile queen; Christ's wisdom reconciles Jew and Gentile, creating "one new man in place of the two, so making peace" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 15|Ephesians 2:15]]). The Queen's confession "blessed be the LORD your God" becomes the universal acclamation: "At the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 10|Philippians 2:10-11]]). What Solomon's wisdom began—drawing Gentiles to acknowledge Israel's God—Christ's greater wisdom completes, as all nations stream to worship Him: "After this I looked, and behold, a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, clothed in white robes, with palm branches in their hands, and crying out with a loud voice, 'Salvation belongs to our God who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!'" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9-10]]). Solomon's glory was true but temporary; Christ's glory is eternal and all-surpassing.
