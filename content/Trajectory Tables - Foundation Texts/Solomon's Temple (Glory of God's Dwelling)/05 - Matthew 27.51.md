### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2601-2700#G2665|καταπέτασμα]] (*katapetasma*) - "veil, curtain"
- [[Lexicon/G3401-3500#G3485|ναός]] (*naos*) - "temple, sanctuary (inner temple)"
- [[Lexicon/G4401-4500#G4977|ἐσχίσθη]] (*eschisthē*) - "was torn, split, rent"
- [[Lexicon/G1401-1500#G1417|δύο]] (*dyo*) - "two"
- [[Lexicon/G0401-0500#G0509|ἄνωθεν]] (*anōthen*) - "from above, from top"
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2736|κάτω]] (*katō*) - "below, to bottom"

**Context**: At the moment of Christ's death, God Himself tears the temple veil from top to bottom, dramatically opening access to the Most Holy Place. What had been restricted to one man (high priest), one day (Day of Atonement), one time per year, is now thrown open through Christ's sacrifice. This divine act declares the old covenant finished and the new covenant inaugurated.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31-33]] (veil separates Holy Place from Most Holy)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2, 12-15]] (Day of Atonement access through veil), [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 6|1 Kings 8:6-8]] (ark behind veil)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 8|Hebrews 9:8]] (way not yet opened), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-20]] (new and living way through veil/Christ's flesh), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14]] (dividing wall broken down)

**Christological Connection**: The veil's tearing at Christ's death reveals Him as the ultimate temple and the ultimate sacrifice. Solomon's temple housed the ark behind the veil, accessible only through repeated animal sacrifices. Christ's death tears the veil because He is both the true temple ("destroy this temple... the temple of his body," [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19, 21]]) and the final sacrifice that "put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]). Hebrews interprets the torn veil as "his flesh"—the veil barring access is removed by Christ's body being torn on the cross ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]). Where the high priest entered yearly "not without blood" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 7|Hebrews 9:7]]), Christ entered "once for all... by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]). The cherubim woven on the veil, barring entrance like Eden's guardians, are defeated by the last Adam who opens paradise again ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 23#Luke 23 . 43|Luke 23:43]]). Paul declares that Christ "has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility... that he might create in himself one new man" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-15]]), universalizing the temple's restricted access to all believers—Jew and Gentile. What Solomon's temple veil restricted (one man, one day, one place), Christ's torn flesh opens (all believers, all times, all places). The writer of Hebrews urges: "Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 16|Hebrews 4:16]])—confidence unthinkable before the veil was torn. The temple's destruction in AD 70, forty years later, confirmed physically what happened spiritually in AD 30—the old system was finished, fulfilled, and surpassed in Christ. The trajectory culminates in Revelation where there is "no temple" in the new creation ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22|Revelation 21:22]]) because unmediated access to God's presence is the eternal reality. [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]] marks the decisive moment—the veil torn, the way opened, access granted forever through Christ's sacrifice.
