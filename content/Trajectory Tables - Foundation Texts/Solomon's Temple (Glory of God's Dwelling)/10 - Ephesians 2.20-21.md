**Context**: Paul describes the church as God's temple under construction, built on the foundation of apostles and prophets with Christ Jesus as the cornerstone. This living temple, composed of believers from all nations, grows into a holy dwelling place for God by the Spirit. What Solomon's temple represented statically, the church embodies dynamically—God's glory dwelling among His people.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 20|Ephesians 2:20-21]] shows how Christ fulfills and surpasses Solomon's temple in every dimension. Where Solomon's temple had a cornerstone orienting the structure, Christ IS the cornerstone rejected by builders ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 118#Psalms 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]]) yet chosen by God as precious ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 28#Isaiah 28 . 16|Isaiah 28:16]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 6|1 Peter 2:6-7]]). Where Solomon's temple took seven years to build, Christ's temple—the church—has been under construction for two millennia and continues growing. Where Solomon used gold, cedar, and stone, Christ uses redeemed people: "you yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 5|1 Peter 2:5]]). Where Solomon's temple was restricted to Israel, Christ's temple incorporates all nations—Paul emphasizes that Gentiles, formerly "alienated from the commonwealth of Israel" (v. 12), are now "fellow citizens... members of the household of God" (v. 19). Where the glory-cloud filled Solomon's temple once ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 10|1 Kings 8:10-11]]), the Spirit perpetually indwells Christ's temple—"you are being built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (v. 22). Where Solomon's temple stood in one location, Christ's temple exists globally wherever believers gather. Paul declares "you are God's temple and God's Spirit dwells in you" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16|1 Corinthians 3:16]])—corporately, not merely individually. The "foundation of the apostles and prophets" parallels the ark containing God's word in Solomon's temple—authoritative revelation grounds the structure. But where Solomon's temple was destroyed twice (586 BC, AD 70), Christ promises: "I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 16#Matthew 16 . 18|Matthew 16:18]])—an indestructible temple. The trajectory culminates in Revelation where John sees no physical temple because "the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its temple" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 22|Revelation 21:22]])—but until then, the church functions as God's earthly temple, displaying His glory to the world. What Solomon's temple accomplished locally and temporarily, Christ's living temple accomplishes globally and eternally—a holy dwelling place where God's presence fills redeemed people from every nation, growing toward completion when Christ returns and presents His church "in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that she might be holy and without blemish" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 27|Ephesians 5:27]]).
