### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 14|John 7:14-15]]

**Context**: During the Feast of Tabernacles (Sukkot), commemorating Israel's wilderness wandering and God's provision, Jesus goes up to the temple and teaches. The Jews marvel at His scriptural knowledge despite His lack of formal rabbinic training. This scene demonstrates Christ's authority over the temple and positions Him as the true teacher of God's word, surpassing the scribes and Pharisees.

**Christological Connection**: Jesus teaching in the temple fulfills Haggai's prophecy that the second temple would possess greater glory than Solomon's ([[Readable Bible/37 - Haggai/Haggai 2#Haggai 2 . 9|Haggai 2:9]]). Where Solomon's temple housed the law written on stone tablets in the ark, Christ embodies the law perfectly, teaching with authority that surpasses Moses ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 21|Matthew 5:21-48]]). The temple's purpose was to be "a house of prayer for all nations" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 56#Isaiah 56 . 7|Isaiah 56:7]]), yet Jesus is the true mediator through whom all nations access God. Solomon's wisdom attracted the Queen of Sheba ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 10#1 Kings 10 . 1|1 Kings 10:1-9]]), but Jesus declares "something greater than Solomon is here" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 42|Matthew 12:42]])—He is the wisdom of God incarnate ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 24|1 Corinthians 1:24]]). The Jews' question—"How has this man learned without having studied?"—reveals divine irony: the one they question is the Logos, the eternal Word through whom all things were made ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 3|John 1:3]]), now teaching mortals in their temple. Jesus later explains: "It is written in the Prophets, 'And they will all be taught by God'" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 45|John 6:45]]), identifying Himself as that divine Teacher. Where the temple was the place of divine instruction through priests and Levites, Christ is the ultimate Prophet who speaks God's words directly. Paul declares Christ as "our wisdom from God" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 30|1 Corinthians 1:30]]), and that "in him are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 3|Colossians 2:3]]). The temple's function as teaching center finds fulfillment in Christ who commands His apostles to "make disciples of all nations... teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19-20]]). The church continues this teaching ministry, being "built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the cornerstone" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 20|Ephesians 2:20]]). Where Israel gathered at the temple three times yearly for feasts and instruction, the church gathers continuously around Christ's word, the living temple from whom wisdom flows. The scene in [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 14|John 7:14-15]] demonstrates the transition from physical temple to personal Teacher—from structure to Savior, from building to Body, from Jerusalem's courts to worldwide proclamation of divine wisdom in Christ.
