### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 11|Revelation 21:11, 23]]

**Context**: John's vision of the New Jerusalem reveals the ultimate fulfillment of God's dwelling-with-humanity. The city possesses "the glory of God" with radiance like precious jasper (v. 11), and requires no sun or moon because "the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb" (v. 23). What Solomon's temple foreshadowed temporarily—God's glorious presence illuminating His people—becomes eternal reality in the new creation.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 11|Revelation 21:11]], 23 reveals Christ as the ultimate fulfillment of Solomon's temple glory. Where the cloud filled Solomon's temple, the Lamb illuminates the new Jerusalem. Jesus declared "I am the light of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]]), and now in consummation, He IS the city's lamp (λύχνος). Where Solomon's temple required seven-branched lampstand ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 31|Exodus 25:31]]), the new temple needs no artificial light—the Lamb Himself radiates divine glory. John's prologue announced "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]])—that glory, glimpsed in incarnation, now floods the eternal city. Paul wrote that "the god of this world has blinded the minds of unbelievers, to keep them from seeing the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 4|2 Corinthians 4:4]])—in new creation, nothing obscures that glory. Where Moses couldn't look upon God's glory without dying ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]]), redeemed humanity "will see his face" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]]) because the Lamb's blood has made them holy. The transfiguration previewed this glory—Jesus's "face shone like the sun" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 17#Matthew 17 . 2|Matthew 17:2]]), and Peter testifies "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty" ([[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 1#2 Peter 1 . 16|2 Peter 1:16]]). But what was temporary on the mount becomes eternal in the city. The pattern moves: glory filled tabernacle (Exodus 40) → glory filled temple (1 Kings 8) → glory departed (Ezekiel 10) → glory returned in Christ (John 1) → glory fills church (Ephesians 2) → glory illuminates new creation (Revelation 21). What Solomon's temple achieved partially—manifesting God's presence through gold-reflected glory—Christ accomplishes fully—He IS the glory, and His presence transforms the entire new creation into God's temple. Isaiah's prophecy is fulfilled: "The LORD will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 60#Isaiah 60 . 19|Isaiah 60:19]]), and "they shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 9|Isaiah 11:9]]). The trajectory that began with "let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]) consummates in "the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]). Solomon's temple was glorious preview; Christ the Lamb is eternal reality—the Light that never dims, the Glory that never departs, the Temple where God and humanity dwell together forever.
