### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 20|Genesis 8:20-21]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4101-4200#H4196|מִזְבֵּחַ]] (*mizbēaḥ*) - "altar" (from root meaning "to slaughter")
- [[Lexicon/H5701-5800#H5927|וַיַּעַל]] (*wayyaʿal*) - "and he offered up" (Hiphil, causative—made to ascend)
- [[Lexicon/H5701-5800#H5930|עֹלֹת]] (*ʿōlōt*) - "burnt offerings" (plural, from root "to ascend")
- [[Lexicon/H2801-2900#H2889|הַטְּהוֹרָה]] (*haṭṭəhôrâ*) - "the clean" (animals and birds)
- [[Lexicon/H7401-7500#H7381|וַיָּרַח]] (*wayyāraḥ*) - "and he smelled" (Hiphil)
- [[Lexicon/H7401-7500#H7381|רֵיחַ]] (*rêaḥ*) - "odor, scent"
- [[Lexicon/H5101-5200#H5207|נִיחֹחַ]] (*nîḥōaḥ*) - "soothing, pleasing" (sweet savor)
- [[Lexicon/H7001-7100#H7043|אֲקַלֵּל]] (*ʾăqallēl*) - "I will curse" (Piel, with negative—"not curse again")

**Context**: After the flood's judgment, Noah's burnt offering becomes the first explicit burnt offering in Scripture, described as "a sweet savor" (re'ach nichoach) to the LORD. This watershed moment establishes the burnt offering pattern: total consecration of clean animals, complete consumption on the altar, ascending smoke pleasing to God. The LORD's response—promising never again to curse the ground—reveals the burnt offering's efficacy in securing divine favor after judgment. Noah's unreserved offering of clean animals signals complete surrender to God in the post-flood world.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 4|Genesis 4:4]] (Abel's offering from flock, accepted by LORD)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 1#Leviticus 1 . 9|Leviticus 1:9]] (burnt offering as sweet savor), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 18|Exodus 29:18]] (ram as burnt offering, sweet savor to LORD)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]] (Christ gave himself as offering and sacrifice, sweet-smelling aroma), [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 4#Philippians 4 . 18|Philippians 4:18]] (gifts as sweet-smelling aroma, acceptable sacrifice)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 20|Genesis 8:20-21]]'s burnt offering prefigures Christ's total consecration as "sweet-smelling aroma" to the Father. Noah offered clean animals completely—nothing withheld, everything consumed—as expression of total devotion after experiencing God's judgment against sin. The smoke ascending to heaven depicted complete surrender pleasing to God. This "sweet savor" (re'ach nichoach) becomes the controlling metaphor throughout Leviticus for burnt offerings ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 1#Leviticus 1 . 9|Leviticus 1:9]], 13, 17). [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]] applies this exact language to Christ: "gave Himself for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling aroma." Christ's life and death were continuous burnt offering—unreserved consecration to the Father's will. Where Noah offered select animals, Christ offered Himself entirely. The burnt offering's complete consumption by fire prefigures Christ's complete suffering under divine wrath—His body, soul, spirit wholly given. God's favorable response to Noah's offering ("I will never again curse") foreshadows the Father's acceptance of Christ's sacrifice, securing eternal covenant blessings. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 32|Romans 8:32]] echoes this: "He who did not spare His own Son, but delivered Him up for us all, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things?" Christ's burnt offering secures all covenant promises. The pattern established in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 20|Genesis 8:20-21]]—judgment, salvation, total consecration, divine acceptance, covenant promise—finds ultimate fulfillment at Calvary, where Christ's complete self-offering satisfies justice and secures grace.
