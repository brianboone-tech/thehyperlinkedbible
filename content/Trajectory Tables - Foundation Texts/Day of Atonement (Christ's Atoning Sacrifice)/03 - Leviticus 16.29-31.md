### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 29|Leviticus 16:29-31]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6031|תְּעַנּוּ]] (*təʿannû*) - "you shall afflict, humble"
- [[Lexicon/H5315|נַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם]] (*napšōtêḵem*) - "yourselves, your souls"
- [[Lexicon/H2708|חֻקַּת עוֹלָם]] (*ḥuqqat ʿôlām*) - "statute forever, perpetual ordinance"
- [[Lexicon/H7676|שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן]] (*šabbat šabbātôn*) - "Sabbath of solemn rest"
- [[Lexicon/H4399|מְלָאכָה]] (*məlāḵāh*) - "work"
- [[Lexicon/H6213|תַעֲשׂוּ]] (*taʿăśû*) - "you shall do, perform"

**Context**: Leviticus 16:29-31 commands Israel to observe the Day of Atonement annually on the tenth day of the seventh month by afflicting themselves (fasting), doing no work, and resting solemnly. This "Sabbath of solemn rest" exceeded weekly Sabbath's solemnity—complete cessation from work, total focus on atonement. The self-affliction expressed repentance and identification with the sacrifices' seriousness. This external affliction foreshadowed NT believers' internal mortification of sin, enabled by Christ's completed atonement and empowered by the Spirit.

- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 1|Hebrews 12:1-2]] (lay aside every weight and sin), [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 13|Romans 8:13]] (put to death deeds of body), [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 5|Colossians 3:5]] (put to death what is earthly)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 29|Leviticus 16:29-31]]'s command to "afflict yourselves" finds fulfillment in believers' Spirit-empowered mortification of sin. The external fasting typified internal heart-work. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 1|Hebrews 12:1-2]] commands: "let us lay aside every weight, and sin which clings so closely, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us, looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who... endured the cross." Christ endured ultimate affliction—bearing God's wrath—so believers could experience true rest. The "Sabbath of solemn rest" (v. 31) foreshadows rest found in Christ. [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 11#Matthew 11 . 28|Matthew 11:28]] invites: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 9|Hebrews 4:9-10]] declares: "So then, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God, for whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his." The work cessation (v. 29) pictures ceasing from self-righteousness—[[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 8|Ephesians 2:8-9]] states: "by grace you have been saved through faith... not a result of works, so that no one may boast." [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 13|Romans 8:13]] transforms affliction: "if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live"—mortification powered by Spirit, not mere self-discipline. [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 5|Colossians 3:5]] commands: "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you." The trajectory shows: Israel afflicts flesh through fasting (external) → Christ accomplishes atonement through death (substitutionary) → believers mortify sin through Spirit (internal) → eternal rest in new creation (consummation).
