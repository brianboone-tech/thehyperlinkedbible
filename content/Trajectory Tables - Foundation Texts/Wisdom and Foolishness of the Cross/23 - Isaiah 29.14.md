### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 29#Isaiah 29 . 14|Isaiah 29:14]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2451|H2451]] חָכְמָה (chokmah) - wisdom
- [[Lexicon/H2450|H2450]] חָכָם (chakam) - wise
- [[Lexicon/H998|H998]] בִּינָה (binah) - understanding, intelligence
- [[Lexicon/H6381|H6381]] פָּלָא (pala) - to be wonderful, marvelous; to confound

**Context**:
Isaiah 29:14 is part of a prophetic judgment against Jerusalem for religious formalism. The people "draw near to Me with their mouths and honor Me with their lips, but their hearts are far from Me" (v. 13). God declares: "Therefore I will again confound these people with wonder upon wonder. The wisdom of the wise will vanish, and the intelligence of the intelligent will be hidden." This verse becomes foundational for Paul's theology of the cross.

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking Direct (prophetic word with eschatological fulfillment in the cross event)

**Christological Connection**:
[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 29#Isaiah 29 . 14|Isaiah 29:14]] prophetically announces the pattern that culminates at Calvary. Paul applies this verse directly to the cross in [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 19|1 Corinthians 1:19]], showing that the crucifixion is God's ultimate "wonder upon wonder" that confounds all human wisdom. The religious leaders who thought they were wise in crucifying Jesus actually fulfilled God's hidden wisdom. The cross appears as foolishness but is revealed as God's supreme wisdom—destroying the wisdom of the wise while accomplishing redemption. What Isaiah prophesied as judgment on Jerusalem's formalism finds ultimate expression in God's reversal at the cross.
