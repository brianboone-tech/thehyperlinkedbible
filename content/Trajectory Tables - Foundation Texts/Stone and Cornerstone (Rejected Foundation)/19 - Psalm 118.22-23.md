**Context**: Psalm 118 is a thanksgiving hymn celebrating God's deliverance and steadfast love. It was likely used in temple liturgy, possibly at the Feast of Tabernacles or a royal coronation/victory celebration. The psalm moves from thanksgiving for deliverance (vv. 1-18), through a procession to the temple gates (vv. 19-27), to final praise (vv. 28-29). Verses 22-23 form the theological climax: the one who was rejected has become the cornerstone. The passive voice ("has become") indicates God's sovereign reversal—what seems like defeat is actually divine victory.

**Type Classification**: **Forward-looking** (contains expectational elements pointing to future vindication of God's chosen) and **Direct** (embedded in Israel's worship as divinely inspired prophecy)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ is the ultimate fulfillment of the rejected stone that becomes the cornerstone. Religious leaders (the "builders") rejected Him, culminating in His crucifixion. But God reversed their verdict through resurrection and exaltation, making Christ the cornerstone of the new temple—the church. Jesus explicitly claimed this psalm ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 42|Matthew 21:42-44]]), and the apostles proclaimed it as central to the gospel ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 11|Acts 4:11-12]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 6|1 Peter 2:6-7]]). The pattern of rejection-then-exaltation defines Christ's path to glory: despised and rejected by men ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 3|Isaiah 53:3]]), yet exalted to the highest place ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9-11]]). Those who stumble over this stone will be broken, but those who build on it will never be shaken ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 44|Matthew 21:44]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 6|1 Peter 2:6]]). This is "marvelous in our eyes"—the wisdom of God that confounds the wise.
