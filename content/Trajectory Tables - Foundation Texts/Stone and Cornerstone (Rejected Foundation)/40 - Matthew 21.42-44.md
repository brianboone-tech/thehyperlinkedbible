**Context**: Matthew 21:33-46 records Jesus' Parable of the Tenants, told to the chief priests and Pharisees in the temple courts during Passion Week. The parable depicts Israel's leaders as wicked tenants who reject God's servants (the prophets) and finally kill the son (Jesus). After the parable, Jesus applies Psalm 118:22 directly to Himself—He is the stone the builders rejected that has become the cornerstone. He then adds a warning not found in Psalm 118 but alluding to Isaiah 8:14 and Daniel 2:34-35: whoever falls on this stone will be broken, and whoever it falls on will be crushed. The religious leaders understand He is speaking about them and want to arrest Him, but they fear the crowd.

**Type Classification**: **Direct Fulfillment** (Jesus explicitly claims the OT stone prophecies as referring to Himself)

**Christological Connection**: This passage is Jesus' own explicit claim to be the Stone of Old Testament prophecy. He identifies Himself as:
1. The Rejected Stone ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]]) - despised by religious leaders, crucified
2. The Cornerstone ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]]) - exalted by God through resurrection, made the foundation of God's new temple
3. The Stone of Stumbling ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 8#Isaiah 8 . 14|Isaiah 8:14]]) - causing offense to those who reject Him
4. The Crushing Stone ([[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 2#Daniel 2 . 34|Daniel 2:34-35]]) - bringing judgment on all who oppose God's kingdom

Jesus presents a stark either/or: encounter with the Stone is inevitable, and it results in either salvation or judgment. Those who "fall on this stone" (an idiom for stumbling over it, taking offense at it) will be "broken"—a picture of brokenness that can lead to repentance and restoration. Those who refuse to repent and on whom "it falls" will be utterly crushed—eschatological judgment. Paul echoes this in [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 9#Romans 9 . 32|Romans 9:32-33]] and [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 23|1 Corinthians 1:23]] (Christ the stumbling block). Peter expounds it in [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 6|1 Peter 2:6-8]] (precious to believers, stone of stumbling to disobedient). The irony is profound: the very Stone God appointed for salvation becomes the means of judgment for those who reject it. The "builders" (religious experts who should have recognized God's Messiah) are the very ones who reject Him, fulfilling the pattern of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]]. This is "marvelous in our eyes"—God's wisdom confounding human wisdom, divine reversal making the rejected One the exalted cornerstone of a new creation.
