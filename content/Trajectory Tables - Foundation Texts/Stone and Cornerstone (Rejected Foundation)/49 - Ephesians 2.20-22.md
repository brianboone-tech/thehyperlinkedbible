**Context**: Ephesians 2:11-22 presents Paul's theology of Jew-Gentile reconciliation through Christ. Formerly, Gentiles were "far off," separated from Israel's covenants, without hope and without God (vv. 11-12). But Christ's death broke down the dividing wall, creating one new humanity, reconciling both Jew and Gentile to God in one body through the cross (vv. 13-18). Verses 19-22 develop the corporate identity of this reconciled community using three images: (1) fellow citizens in God's kingdom (v. 19), (2) members of God's household (v. 19), and (3) a holy temple built on the foundation of apostles and prophets, with Christ as the cornerstone (vv. 20-22). This is Paul's most extended architectural metaphor for the church.

**Type Classification**: **Direct Fulfillment** (apostolic interpretation of OT stone and temple prophecies fulfilled in Christ and the church)

**Christological Connection**: Paul identifies Christ Jesus as the **cornerstone** (akrogōniaios) of God's spiritual temple, the church. In ancient construction, the cornerstone served multiple critical functions:

1. **Determines alignment** - The cornerstone set the angle and orientation for the entire building. All subsequent stones were aligned to it. Christ is the standard of truth, holiness, and doctrine; the church must align to Him.

2. **Bears structural load** - The cornerstone supported enormous weight, especially at the corner where two walls met. Christ bears the weight of the church, sustaining it by His power ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 17|Colossians 1:17]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]).

3. **Joins two walls** - In Paul's context, the cornerstone joins Jew and Gentile into one structure. Christ is the mediator who reconciles both to God and to each other ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-16]]).

4. **Most precious stone** - Cornerstones were often the largest, most costly, and most carefully selected stones. Christ is precious beyond measure—chosen by God, tested and proven ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 28#Isaiah 28 . 16|Isaiah 28:16]]; [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 4|1 Peter 2:4]], 6).

Paul's imagery fulfills multiple OT types:

- **Temple typology** - Solomon's temple pointed forward to Christ's body ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]) and the church as God's dwelling ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16|1 Corinthians 3:16]]; 6:19). The glory that filled Solomon's temple now fills the church through the Holy Spirit ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 22|Ephesians 2:22]]).

- **Stone typology** - Christ is the Stone prophesied in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 24|Genesis 49:24]] (Stone of Israel), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22]] (rejected cornerstone), and [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 28#Isaiah 28 . 16|Isaiah 28:16]] (precious foundation stone).

- **Davidic kingdom** - God promised to build David's son a house and establish his throne forever ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]]). Christ, the Son of David, is the builder of God's eternal temple-kingdom, and He Himself is the cornerstone.

The foundation of "apostles and prophets" (v. 20) refers to the apostolic witness and prophetic writings of the NT ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 3#Ephesians 3 . 5|Ephesians 3:5]]), or possibly OT prophets and NT apostles together. Either way, Christ is the cornerstone—more fundamental even than the apostles. They are the foundation in the sense of being the first layer of stones built on Christ, but He is the cornerstone that holds everything together.

The church is a "growing" temple (v. 21)—organic, living, expanding as more believers are added. This echoes Daniel 2's stone that becomes a mountain filling the earth and Jesus' parables of the kingdom growing like a mustard seed ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 13#Matthew 13 . 31|Matthew 13:31-32]]). The church is not yet complete; it is "being built together" (v. 22), pointing to eschatological consummation when the New Jerusalem descends as a completed temple-city, the bride adorned for her husband ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 2|Revelation 21:2-3]]).

God dwells in this temple "by the Spirit" (v. 22), fulfilling Ezekiel's vision of God's glory returning to the temple ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 43#Ezekiel 43 . 1|Ezekiel 43:1-5]]) and Jesus' promise of the Spirit's indwelling ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 16|John 14:16-17]], 23). The church is the true temple, the place where heaven and earth meet, where God dwells among His people—and Christ the cornerstone makes it all possible.
