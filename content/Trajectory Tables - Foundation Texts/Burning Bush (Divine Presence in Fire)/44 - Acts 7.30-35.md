### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 30|Acts 7:30-35]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G32|G32]] ἄγγελος (angelos) - "angel" - appeared in the flames
- [[Lexicon/G5395|G5395]] φλόξ (phlox) - "flame" - of fire in the bush
- [[Lexicon/G942|G942]] βάτος (batos) - "bush/thornbush" - the burning bush
- [[Lexicon/G40|G40]] ἅγιος (hagios) - "holy" - the ground was holy
- [[Lexicon/G3086|G3086]] λυτρωτής (lytrōtēs) - "redeemer/deliverer" - God sent Moses as
- [[Lexicon/G758|G758]] ἄρχων (archōn) - "ruler" - Moses as ruler and redeemer

**Context**: Stephen, on trial before the Sanhedrin, recounts Israel's history. He describes Moses' encounter with the burning bush to make a theological point: God is not confined to the temple. He appeared in a bush, in the wilderness, to a fugitive shepherd. The One who dwelt in the burning bush commissioned Israel's deliverance then—and now works through Jesus, whom they rejected as they rejected Moses.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 1|Exodus 3:1-6]] - Stephen quotes the original burning bush narrative
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 33#Deuteronomy 33 . 16|Deuteronomy 33:16]] - "Him who dwelt in the burning bush" blessed Joseph
- Stephen connects the bush to Moses' role as "ruler and redeemer" (7:35)

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 1|Exodus 3:1-6]] - Stephen directly recounts and applies the burning bush theophany
- **FROM OT**: The entire Moses narrative leads to this: rejected deliverer sent by God
- **FROM NT**: Stephen's point: as Israel rejected Moses at first, so they rejected Jesus

**Type Classification**: NT Exposition applying the burning bush pattern to Christ and His Church

**Christological Connection**: Stephen presents Moses as a type of Christ: rejected by his brothers, sent by God as deliverer, vindicated as ruler. The One who appeared in the burning bush to commission Moses is the One who now works through Jesus. Stephen's martyrdom demonstrates the ongoing reality: the Church burns under persecution (Stephen stoned) but is not consumed (the gospel spreads). The presence that dwelt in the bush now dwells in believers, transforming fire from destroyer to refiner.
