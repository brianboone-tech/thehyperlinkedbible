### [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 3#Daniel 3 . 19|Daniel 3:19-27]]

**Hebrew/Aramaic Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H861|H861]] אַתּוּן (ʾattûn) - "furnace" - the instrument of execution
- [[Lexicon/H5135|H5135]] נוּר (nûr) - "fire" - the element within
- [[Lexicon/H7655|H7655]] שִׁבְעָה (šiḇʿâ) - "seven" - times hotter than usual
- [[Lexicon/H8271|H8271]] שְׁלֹשָׁה (šelōšâ) - "three" - the faithful men
- [[Lexicon/H426|H426]] אֱלָהּ (ʾĕlāh) - "god" - the fourth figure "like a son of the gods"
- [[Lexicon/H7313|H7313]] שָׁלְטָן (šolṭān) - "power" - fire had no power over their bodies

**Context**: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refuse to worship Nebuchadnezzar's golden image. The enraged king heats the furnace seven times hotter and casts them in bound. But the king sees four figures walking freely in the fire—the fourth "like a son of the gods." When they emerge, "the fire had no power over their bodies; their hair was not singed, their robes were not scorched, and there was no smell of fire on them" (3:27).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 2|Exodus 3:2]] - The burning bush burned but was not consumed; here three men are not consumed
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 43#Isaiah 43 . 2|Isaiah 43:2]] - "When you walk through the fire, you will not be burned" is literally fulfilled
- [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 3#Daniel 3 . 28|Daniel 3:28]] - Nebuchadnezzar acknowledges "they trusted in him and defied the king's command"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 3#Exodus 3 . 2|Exodus 3:2]] - The burning bush principle applied to persons rather than a shrub
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 43#Isaiah 43 . 2|Isaiah 43:2]]'s promise is demonstrated in the furnace
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 34|Hebrews 11:34]] - Faith heroes "quenched the fury of the flames"

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged), Backward-Looking - Recognized as exemplifying the burning bush pattern of divine preservation in fire

**Christological Connection**: The fourth figure "like a son of the gods" is widely understood as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ. He stood with His servants in the fire—not removing them from it but preserving them in it. This is Christ's pattern throughout redemptive history: He does not always rescue believers from trial but promises His presence in it. The One who walked in the furnace with the three later walked the road to Calvary alone, bearing the full fury of divine fire so that His people might forever be preserved.
