### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 5#John 5 . 45|John 5:45-47]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2723|G2723]] κατηγορέω (*katēgoreō*) - to accuse
- [[Lexicon/G4100|G4100]] πιστεύω (*pisteuō*) - to believe
- [[Lexicon/G1125|G1125]] γράφω (*graphō*) - to write

**Context**: "Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who accuses you: Moses, on whom you have set your hope. For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me. But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words?" Jesus inverts Jewish expectation: Moses, their hero and hope, becomes their accuser because his writings testify to Christ.

**Type Classification**: Prophetic Anticipation - Forward-looking (Moses's writings anticipate Christ)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus makes a stunning claim: Moses wrote about Him. This is not isolated prophecy but comprehensive testimony. Moses recorded the seed promise ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]]), the blessing through Abraham's seed ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]; 22:18), Shiloh from Judah ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 10|Genesis 49:10]]), the Passover lamb (Exodus 12), the bronze serpent (Numbers 21), the Prophet like Moses ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 18#Deuteronomy 18 . 15|Deuteronomy 18:15]]), and the entire sacrificial system pointing to atonement. To believe Moses properly is to believe in Christ; to reject Christ is to reject Moses. The irony is devastating: those who pride themselves on devotion to Moses actually disbelieve him. The Torah they study daily condemns them because they miss its central subject. Moses becomes their accuser (κατηγορέω) precisely because they claim him as their hope (ἐλπίζω). Christ does not need to accuse—Moses does.
