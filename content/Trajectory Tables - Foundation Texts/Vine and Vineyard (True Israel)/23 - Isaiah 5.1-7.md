### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 5#Isaiah 5 . 1|Isaiah 5:1-7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- כֶּרֶם (kerem, H3754) - vineyard
- שֹׂרֵקִים (soreqim, H8321) - choice vines
- מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat, H4941) - justice
- מִשְׂפָּח (mispach, H4939) - bloodshed (wordplay)
- צְדָקָה (tsedaqah, H6666) - righteousness
- צְעָקָה (tse'aqah, H6818) - cry of distress (wordplay)

**Context**: Isaiah's "Song of the Vineyard" appears early in his ministry (c. 740-700 BC) as a parable of judgment against Judah and Jerusalem. The vineyard represents Israel/Judah as God's covenant people.

- Builds on [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 80#Psalm 80 . 8|Psalm 80:8-16]] (vine brought from Egypt)

- Directly quoted by Jesus in [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 33|Matthew 21:33-44]], [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 12#Mark 12 . 1|Mark 12:1-12]], [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 20#Luke 20 . 9|Luke 20:9-19]]

**Type Classification**: Providential, Forward-looking (v. 7 explicitly interprets the parable; the judgment implies a future restoration)

**Christological Connection**: God asks, "What more could I have done for My vineyard?" (v. 4). The answer comes in John 15—God plants His own Son as the True Vine. Where Israel produced "sour grapes" (beushim), Christ bears perfect fruit. The judgment pronounced here ("I will make it a wasteland," v. 6) falls on unbelieving Israel in AD 70 and ultimately on all who reject the True Vine. But those grafted into Christ become part of the pleasant vineyard of [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 27#Isaiah 27 . 2|Isaiah 27:2-6]].
