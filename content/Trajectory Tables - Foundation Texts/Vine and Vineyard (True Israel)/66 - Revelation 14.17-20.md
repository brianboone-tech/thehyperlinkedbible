### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 17|Revelation 14:17-20]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἄμπελος τῆς γῆς (ampelos tēs gēs, G288 + G1093) - vine of the earth
- βότρυς (botrys, G1009) - cluster of grapes
- ληνός (lēnos, G3025) - winepress
- θυμός τοῦ θεοῦ (thymos tou theou, G2372 + G2316) - wrath of God
- αἷμα (haima, G129) - blood

**Context**: Revelation 14 presents three angels proclaiming eternal gospel (vv. 6-7), Babylon's fall (v. 8), and judgment on beast-worshipers (vv. 9-12), followed by the blessed dead (v. 13) and two harvests: grain (vv. 14-16) and grapes (vv. 17-20). The grape harvest depicts final judgment.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Fulfills [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 5#Isaiah 5 . 1|Isaiah 5:1-7]] (vineyard judgment) eschatologically
- Echoes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 1|Isaiah 63:1-6]] (God treading the winepress in judgment)
- Culminates [[Readable Bible/29 - Joel/Joel 3#Joel 3 . 12|Joel 3:12-14]] (harvest valley of decision)

**Type Classification**: Providential, Forward-looking (OT vineyard judgments anticipated this eschatological consummation)

**Christological Connection**: The "vine of the earth" contrasts with Christ the True Vine ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 1|John 15:1]]). Those who refuse to abide in Jesus remain part of the earthly, fruitless vine destined for God's winepress of wrath. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 19#Revelation 19 . 15|Revelation 19:15]] reveals that Christ Himself "treads the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty"—the Judge is the rejected Vine. The two harvests (grain in vv. 14-16, grapes in vv. 17-20) represent two humanities: those united to Christ bearing fruit unto eternal life, and those who remain in Adam bearing fruit unto wrath. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 27#Isaiah 27 . 2|Isaiah 27:2-6]] promised a "pleasant vineyard" that fills the earth with fruit—that is the church, those grafted into the True Vine. The "vine of the earth" is all who reject Christ and remain fruitless, facing eschatological judgment.
