### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 4|John 15:4-5]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- μένω (menō, G3306) - to abide, remain, continue, dwell (appears 4x in vv. 4-5)
- καρπός (karpos, G2590) - fruit
- χωρίς (chōris, G5565) - apart from, without, separate from
- οὐδέν (ouden, G3762) - nothing

**Context**: Central imperative of the vine discourse. After establishing that Jesus is the True Vine (v. 1), the Father is the vinedresser (v. 1), and branches are pruned for fruitfulness (v. 2), Jesus commands disciples to "abide in Me." This mutual indwelling (v. 4: "I in you") is the key to Christian life and fruitfulness.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Fulfills what Israel failed to do—remain faithful to covenant relationship
- Answers the lament of Psalm 80 (restore us) with the way of restoration (abide in Messiah)
- Overcomes the degeneration of [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 2#Jeremiah 2 . 21|Jeremiah 2:21]] through vital union with True Vine

**Type Classification**: Direct, Forward-looking (applies OT vineyard theology directly to believers' relationship with Christ)

**Christological Connection**: "Apart from Me you can do nothing" (v. 5) is the most radical statement of human dependence on Christ in Scripture. Branches have no inherent life—they derive everything from the vine. This applies the lesson of Ezekiel 15 (vine wood worthless except for fruit-bearing) to Christian spirituality. Fruitfulness is not achieved through self-effort, religious performance, or moral striving, but through organic connection to Jesus. "Abiding" is not passive mysticism but active faith, obedience (v. 10), and love (vv. 9, 12). The imperatives are demanding, but the promise is glorious: "whoever abides in Me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit."
