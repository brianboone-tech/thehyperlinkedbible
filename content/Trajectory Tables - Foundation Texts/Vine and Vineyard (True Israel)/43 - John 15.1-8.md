### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 1|John 15:1-8]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἄμπελος ἀληθινή (ampelos alēthinē, G288 + G228) - TRUE vine (genuine, ideal, real)
- γεωργός (geōrgos, G1092) - vinedresser, farmer
- κλῆμα (klēma, G2814) - branch
- μένω (menō, G3306) - to abide, remain, dwell (11x in vv. 1-11)
- καρπός (karpos, G2590) - fruit (8x in vv. 1-16)
- καθαίρω (kathairō, G2508) - to prune, cleanse

**Context**: Upper Room Discourse on the night before Jesus' crucifixion. Follows washing disciples' feet (ch. 13), promising the Holy Spirit (ch. 14), and precedes teaching on persecution (15:18-16:4) and the Spirit's work (16:5-15). The vine metaphor grounds Christian life in union with Christ.

**Type Classification**: Providential, Forward-looking (OT vineyard texts anticipated this fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus declares "I am the TRUE vine"—the authentic, ideal vine that all OT vines foreshadowed. Where Israel failed as God's vineyard (Isa 5), Jesus succeeds perfectly. The Father is the vinedresser who prunes branches to increase fruitfulness, explaining redemptive suffering. Abiding in Christ is not mystical passivity but vital, organic connection—like a branch deriving life from the vine. Apart from Jesus, branches can do nothing (v. 5); fruitless branches are removed and burned (v. 6), echoing Ezekiel 15. Genuine disciples bear "much fruit" (v. 8), glorifying the Father. This grounds all Christian spirituality in union with Christ—not self-effort but life-flow from the True Vine.
