### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 33|Matthew 21:33-44]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἄμπελος (ampelos, G288) - vine
- ἀμπελών (ampelōn, G290) - vineyard
- γεωργός (geōrgos, G1092) - farmer, tenant
- καρπός (karpos, G2590) - fruit
- λίθος (lithos, G3037) - stone (v. 42, rejected stone becomes cornerstone)

**Context**: Jesus confronts the chief priests and Pharisees in the temple courts during Passion Week. This parable follows the parable of the two sons (21:28-32) and precedes the wedding feast parable (22:1-14). All three indict Israel's leaders for rejecting God's messengers.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Directly quotes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 5#Isaiah 5 . 1|Isaiah 5:1-2]] (opening description of vineyard)
- Alludes to [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 118#Psalm 118 . 22|Psalm 118:22-23]] (rejected stone becomes cornerstone, v. 42)
- Echoes [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 80#Psalm 80 . 8|Psalm 80:8-16]] (vineyard brought from Egypt)

**Type Classification**: Direct, Forward-looking (Isaiah 5 contained forward-looking elements; Jesus makes explicit messianic application)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus is the beloved Son whom the tenants kill to seize the inheritance (vv. 37-39). His death fulfills Isaiah's vineyard judgment but also inaugurates the restoration—He becomes the cornerstone of God's new temple (v. 42). The kingdom is given to "a nation producing its fruit" (v. 43)—the church, comprised of Jews and Gentiles united to Christ the True Vine. The fruitfulness God sought in Isaiah 5 is finally achieved in those who abide in Jesus ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 5|John 15:5]]).
