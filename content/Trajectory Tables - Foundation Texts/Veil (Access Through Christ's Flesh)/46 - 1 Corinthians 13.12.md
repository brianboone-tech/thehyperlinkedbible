### [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 13#1 Corinthians 13 . 12|1 Corinthians 13:12]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0991-0992#G0991|βλέπομεν]] (*blepomen*) - "we see, perceive"
- [[Lexicon/G2072-2073#G2072|ἐσόπτρου]] (*esoptrou*) - "mirror"
- [[Lexicon/G0135-0136#G0135|αἰνίγματι]] (*ainigmati*) - "riddle, enigma, dimly"
- [[Lexicon/G4383-4384#G4383|πρόσωπον]] (*prosōpon*) - "face"
- [[Lexicon/G1921-1922#G1921|ἐπιγνώσομαι]] (*epignōsomai*) - "I will know fully, recognize completely"
- [[Lexicon/G1921-1922#G1921|ἐπεγνώσθην]] (*epegnōsthēn*) - "I was known fully"

**Context**: First Corinthians 13, Paul's great hymn to love, concludes by contrasting present partial knowledge with future perfect knowledge. Verses 9-12 emphasize that current revelation, prophecy, and knowledge are incomplete ("in part"), but "when the perfect comes, the partial will pass away" (v. 10). Verse 12 illustrates this contrast: now we see dimly in a mirror, but then face to face; now I know partially, but then I will know fully even as I am fully known.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]] (no one can see God's face and live), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 12#Numbers 12 . 8|Numbers 12:8]] (with Moses I speak face to face)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 19#Job 19 . 26|Job 19:26-27]] (in my flesh I shall see God), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 17#Psalms 17 . 15|Psalm 17:15]] (when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 2|1 John 3:2]] (we shall see him as he is), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]] (they will see his face)

**Christological Connection**: First Corinthians 13:12 points to the eschatological removal of all barriers between believers and God, culminating what Christ's death inaugurated when the temple veil was torn. Under the old covenant, direct vision of God was forbidden—"you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]]). The veil separated sinful humanity from God's holy presence. Even the high priest entered behind the veil with protective incense lest he see God and die ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 13|Leviticus 16:13]]). Now, "we see in a mirror dimly"—revelation through Christ is true but mediated, clear but incomplete. But Christ's work guarantees eschatological consummation: "then face to face" (*prosōpon pros prosōpon*). What was death-threatening under the old covenant becomes the believer's supreme joy in the new creation. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]] promises: "They will see his face"—the very thing [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]] forbade is now the redeemed's eternal privilege. The veil that barred Moses from seeing God's face is removed through Christ's sacrifice, progressively now ("we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus," [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19]]) and completely then ("we shall see him as he is," [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 2|1 John 3:2]]). The promise "I will know fully, even as I have been fully known" anticipates perfect, unhindered communion with God—no veil separating, no dimness obscuring, no barrier remaining. Christ's torn flesh (the torn veil, [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]) opened access to God's presence that will reach perfect fulfillment when believers stand in God's immediate presence, seeing Him face to face in the new creation.
