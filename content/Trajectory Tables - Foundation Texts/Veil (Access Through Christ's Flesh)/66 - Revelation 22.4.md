### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3708-3709#G3708|ὄψονται]] (*opsontai*) - "they will see, behold"
- [[Lexicon/G4383-4384#G4383|πρόσωπον]] (*prosōpon*) - "face"
- [[Lexicon/G3686-3687#G3686|ὄνομα]] (*onoma*) - "name"
- [[Lexicon/G3359-3360#G3359|μέτωπον]] (*metōpon*) - "forehead"

**Context**: Revelation 22:1-5 describes the new creation's eternal state—the tree of life restored, the curse removed, God's throne established, and His servants worshiping Him. Verse 4 presents the climax of redemptive history: "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads." This fulfills what the entire biblical narrative has been moving toward—unhindered, face-to-face access to God's presence, the veil removed forever, the barrier sin created destroyed eternally.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]] (cherubim guard the way to tree of life), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31]] (veil with cherubim separates), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]] (no one can see God's face and live)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 6#Numbers 6 . 25|Numbers 6:25-26]] (The LORD make his face shine upon you), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 17#Psalms 17 . 15|Psalm 17:15]] (when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]] (veil torn in two), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 13#1 Corinthians 13 . 12|1 Corinthians 13:12]] (then face to face), [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 2|1 John 3:2]] (we shall see him as he is)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 4|Revelation 22:4]]'s promise "they will see his face" is the climactic fulfillment of Christ's work in removing the veil that separated humanity from God's holy presence. From [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]] onward, cherubim barred access to God's presence and the tree of life. The veil in the tabernacle, embroidered with cherubim ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31]]), embodied this exclusion. [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]] declared the old covenant's restriction: "you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live." For 1,500 years, only the high priest once yearly could approach God's presence behind the veil, and even then indirectly through protective incense ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 13|Leviticus 16:13]]). But when Christ died, "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]])—God Himself ripping open the barrier. Through Christ's torn flesh (the torn veil, [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]), believers now have "confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19]]). But this access reaches perfect consummation in the new creation: "they will see his face"—the very thing that was death-threatening under the old covenant becomes the redeemed's eternal joy. What Moses was denied—"you cannot see my face... and live"—the redeemed will experience forever. The cherubim that excluded from the tree of life ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]]) are gone; the tree is restored ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 2|Revelation 22:2]]); face-to-face access is granted. Paul's promise "then face to face" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 13#1 Corinthians 13 . 12|1 Corinthians 13:12]]) and John's promise "we shall see him as he is" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 2|1 John 3:2]]) find fulfillment here. Christ's sacrifice accomplished what the law could never do—remove sin's barrier permanently, grant unhindered access to God's presence eternally, and bring the redeemed into face-to-face communion with God forever.
