### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 23#Luke 23 . 45|Luke 23:45]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2246-2247#G2246|ἥλιος]] (*hēlios*) - "sun"
- [[Lexicon/G1587-1588#G1587|ἐκλιπόντος]] (*eklipontos*) - "failing, eclipsing"
- [[Lexicon/G2585-2586#G2585|καταπέτασμα]] (*katapetasma*) - "veil, curtain"
- [[Lexicon/G3485-3486#G3485|ναοῦ]] (*naou*) - "temple, sanctuary"
- [[Lexicon/G4977-4978#G4977|ἐσχίσθη]] (*eschisthē*) - "was torn, split"
- [[Lexicon/G3319-3320#G3319|μέσον]] (*meson*) - "middle, midst"

**Context**: Luke's account of the crucifixion emphasizes the cosmic signs accompanying Jesus's death. Verse 44 records darkness over the whole land from noon until 3 PM. Verse 45 explains the darkness: "the sun's light failed," and immediately adds, "and the curtain of the temple was torn in two." Luke uniquely places the veil's tearing before Jesus's death (v. 46), creating theological emphasis: the veil tears as Jesus is dying, demonstrating that His sacrifice is opening access to God.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31-33]] (God prescribes the veil), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2]] (do not come inside the veil)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]] (parallel account), [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 15#Mark 15 . 38|Mark 15:38]] (parallel account), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-20]] (confidence to enter through the veil)

**Christological Connection**: Luke's account of the torn veil (23:45) uniquely places it before Jesus's death, emphasizing that the veil tears as Jesus is dying—demonstrating that His sacrifice is opening access to God in real time. The darkness covering the land and the sun's light failing signal cosmic significance: Jesus's death is world-altering judgment and redemption. The veil prescribed in [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31-33]] had enforced separation between sinful humanity and holy God for 1,500 years—[[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2]] warned of death for unauthorized entry behind it. Now, as the true Lamb of God is sacrificed, God tears the veil "in two" (*meson*—completely split), announcing that the barrier sin created is destroyed. Hebrews interprets the torn veil as Christ's torn flesh: "the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]). What excluded under the old covenant (veil barring access) becomes what includes under the new covenant (Christ's flesh opening access). The darkness evokes exodus plague ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 10#Exodus 10 . 21|Exodus 10:21-23]]) and eschatological judgment ([[Readable Bible/30 - Amos/Amos 8#Amos 8 . 9|Amos 8:9]]), connecting Jesus's death to both redemption from bondage and satisfaction of divine justice. The veil's tearing announces: the way into God's presence is open; the Most Holy Place is accessible; the blood of Jesus grants "confidence to enter the holy places" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19]]). Luke's placement—veil tears as Jesus dies—creates theological emphasis: atonement is being accomplished; access is being opened; the old covenant is passing away; the new covenant is being inaugurated. What the high priest did once yearly with animal blood behind the veil, Christ does once for all with His own blood, securing eternal redemption and opening the way to God's presence for all who believe.
