### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3598-3599#G3598|ὁδόν]] (*hodon*) - "way, road, path"
- [[Lexicon/G4372-4373#G4372|πρόσφατον]] (*prosphaton*) - "new, fresh, recently made"
- [[Lexicon/G2198-2199#G2198|ζῶσαν]] (*zōsan*) - "living"
- [[Lexicon/G1457-1458#G1457|ἐνεκαίνισεν]] (*enekainisen*) - "inaugurated, dedicated, opened"
- [[Lexicon/G2585-2586#G2585|καταπέτασμα]] (*katapetasma*) - "veil, curtain"
- [[Lexicon/G4561-4562#G4561|σάρξ]] (*sarx*) - "flesh, body"

**Context**: Hebrews 10:20 stands at the theological heart of the epistle's climactic exhortation. After establishing Christ's superior sacrifice (vv. 1-18), the author grounds the invitation to "draw near" (v. 22) in Christ's accomplishment: He has opened "a new and living way... through the curtain, that is, through his flesh." This verse explicitly identifies Christ's flesh with the veil, transforming the old covenant's barrier into the new covenant's access point.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31-33]] (veil separates Holy Place from Most Holy), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 2|Leviticus 16:2]] (do not come inside the veil)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]] (veil torn in two), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]] (Word became flesh and dwelt among us), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]] (I am the way), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 8|Hebrews 9:8]] (way not yet opened)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]] unveils the profound mystery of how Christ's incarnation and death transform the veil from barrier to access. The verse explicitly states: Christ opened "a new and living way... through the curtain, that is, through his flesh"—identifying the veil with Christ's flesh. This identification is multilayered: (1) Christ's incarnation—He took flesh ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]: "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us")—bringing God's presence near, just as the veil marked where God's presence dwelt; (2) Christ's death—His flesh was torn on the cross, and simultaneously "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]]), demonstrating that His torn body is the torn veil; (3) Christ's resurrection—the way is "living" (*zōsan*) because Christ lives, not dead like repeated animal sacrifices. The paradox is glorious: the veil that excluded sinners from God's holy presence becomes, in Christ's flesh, the very means of access to that presence. What barred entry now opens entry. The veil's cherubim ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 31|Exodus 26:31]]) recalled [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]] where cherubim blocked access to the tree of life; Christ's torn flesh defeats that exclusion, opening access to eternal life ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 14|Revelation 22:14]]). The old covenant said, "Stay behind the veil or die"; Christ's new covenant says, "Enter through my flesh and live." The way is "new" (*prosphaton*—freshly inaugurated at the cross) and "living" (*zōsan*—eternally effective through Christ's resurrection). Jesus is Himself "the way" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 6|John 14:6]])—not showing the way but being the way—His flesh the veil through which we pass into God's presence.
