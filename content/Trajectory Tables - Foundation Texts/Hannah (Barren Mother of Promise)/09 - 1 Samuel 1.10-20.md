### [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 10|1 Samuel 1:10-20]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4751|H4751]] מָרָה (mārâ) - "bitter" - in bitterness of soul
- [[Lexicon/H6419|H6419]] פָּלַל (pālal) - "prayed" - Hannah prayed
- [[Lexicon/H1058|H1058]] בָּכָה (bāḵâ) - "wept" - wept abundantly
- [[Lexicon/H5088|H5088]] נֵדֶר (nēḏer) - "vow" - she made a vow
- [[Lexicon/H2142|H2142]] זָכַר (zāḵar) - "remember" - if You will remember me
- [[Lexicon/H8050|H8050]] שְׁמוּאֵל (šəmûʾēl) - "Samuel" - because I asked him from the LORD

**Context**: Hannah, childless and provoked by her rival Peninnah, pours out her soul before the LORD at Shiloh. Her prayer is so intense that Eli mistakes her for drunk. She vows: if God gives her a son, she will dedicate him as a Nazirite for life. God "remembers" her, she conceives, and names her son Samuel—"because I asked him from the LORD" (or "heard by God").

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1]]; 25:21; 30:22 - The matriarchal pattern of barren mothers
- [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 2|Judges 13:2-5]] - The barren mother of a Nazirite deliverer
- [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 10|1 Samuel 1:10-20]] - Hannah's prayer becomes the paradigm for desperate petition
- [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 7|Luke 1:7-25]] - Elizabeth's barrenness ends; [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 36|Luke 1:36-37]] cites the pattern

**Connections**:
- **TO**: The matriarchal barren mother tradition
- **FROM OT**: Samuel's role as prophet-priest-judge, anointer of kings
- **FROM NT**: Luke 1 structures Elizabeth's and Mary's stories after this pattern

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged paradigm of prayer) and Forward-Looking (Hannah's pattern anticipates Elizabeth and Mary)

**Christological Connection**: Samuel, born to the barren mother who prayed, becomes the prophet who anoints Israel's kings—including David, through whom Christ comes. The pattern of desperate prayer answered by the gift of a special son reaches its climax in Zechariah and Elizabeth (who pray for a child) and in Mary (who receives announcement of the greatest Son). Hannah's son anointed kings; Mary's Son IS the King.
