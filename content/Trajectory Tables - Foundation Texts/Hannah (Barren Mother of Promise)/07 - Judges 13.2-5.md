### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 2|Judges 13:2-5]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6135|H6135]] עֲקָרָה (ʿăqārâ) - "barren" - his wife was barren
- [[Lexicon/H3205|H3205]] יָלַד (yālaḏ) - "bear" - had not borne children
- [[Lexicon/H4397|H4397]] מַלְאָךְ (malʾāḵ) - "angel/messenger" - the Angel of the LORD
- [[Lexicon/H5139|H5139]] נָזִיר (nāzîr) - "Nazirite" - separated unto God from birth
- [[Lexicon/H3467|H3467]] יָשַׁע (yāšaʿ) - "save/deliver" - he shall begin to save Israel

**Context**: Israel is in the hand of the Philistines. A man from Dan has a barren wife. The Angel of the LORD appears to her with an annunciation: "You shall conceive and bear a son... he shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb... he shall begin to save Israel." This is the first annunciation narrative to a barren woman—the pattern will be repeated with Elizabeth and Mary.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The matriarchal pattern (Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel) continues
- [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 2|Judges 13:2-5]] adds the annunciation element—angelic announcement to barren woman
- [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 11|1 Samuel 1:11]] - Hannah's Nazirite vow for Samuel echoes this
- [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 13|Luke 1:13-17]], 30-35 - Gabriel's annunciations to Elizabeth and Mary follow this pattern

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]]; 25:21; 30:22-24 - The barren mother pattern
- **FROM OT**: Samson's role as flawed deliverer
- **FROM NT**: Luke 1 structures both annunciations (to Zechariah, to Mary) after this pattern

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged annunciation pattern) and Forward-Looking (anticipating the Lukan annunciations)

**Christological Connection**: The barren mother bearing a Nazirite deliverer points to Mary bearing Jesus. Samson was set apart from birth—Jesus is "the Holy One" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 35|Luke 1:35]]). Samson would "begin to save Israel"—Jesus would complete salvation. The annunciation formula (barren woman, angelic announcement, promised son, special mission) reaches its fulfillment in Luke 1, where both John (to barren Elizabeth) and Jesus (to virgin Mary) are announced using this pattern.
