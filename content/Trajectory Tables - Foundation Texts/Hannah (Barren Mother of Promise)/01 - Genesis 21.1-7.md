### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6485|H6485]] פָּקַד (pāqaḏ) - "visited/attended to" - the LORD visited Sarah
- [[Lexicon/H6213|H6213]] עָשָׂה (ʿāśâ) - "did/fulfilled" - as He had spoken
- [[Lexicon/H6256|H6256]] מוֹעֵד (môʿēḏ) - "appointed time" - the time God had promised
- [[Lexicon/H6711|H6711]] צָחַק (ṣāḥaq) - "laugh" - the root of Isaac's name
- [[Lexicon/H3327|H3327]] יִצְחָק (yiṣḥāq) - "Isaac" - meaning "he laughs"

**Context**: Sarah, the first barren mother of the covenant line, finally conceives at age ninety. God "visited" Sarah as He had promised; she conceived and bore Abraham a son "at the appointed time." Sarah's previous laughter of disbelief (18:12) becomes laughter of joy: "God has made laughter for me; everyone who hears will laugh with me" (21:6). The impossible has happened: the dead womb lives.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]] - "Sarah was barren; she had no child" (the problem stated)
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 18#Genesis 18 . 11|Genesis 18:11-14]] - "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" (the promise renewed)
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]] - The promise fulfilled
- [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 11|Hebrews 11:11]] - "By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive"

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]] - ; 17:15-19 - The barrenness and promise
- **FROM OT**:
  - The pattern continues through Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 11|Hebrews 11:11]] - celebrates Sarah's faith
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 19|Romans 4:19]] - notes Abraham's faith despite Sarah's barrenness
**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged pattern) and Forward-Looking (anticipates the virgin birth)

**Christological Connection**: Sarah's conception from her "dead" womb ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 19|Romans 4:19]]) typifies the virgin birth. What was naturally impossible became reality through God's visitation. Mary, like Sarah, receives God's promise of a miraculous conception: "For no word from God will ever fail" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 37|Luke 1:37]])—echoing "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 18#Genesis 18 . 14|Genesis 18:14]]). Isaac, the child of promise born against nature, points to Jesus, THE Child of Promise born from a virgin through the Holy Spirit's power.
