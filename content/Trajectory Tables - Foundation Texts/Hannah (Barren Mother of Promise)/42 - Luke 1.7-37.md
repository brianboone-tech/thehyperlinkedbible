### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 7|Luke 1:7, 36-37]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4723|G4723]] στεῖρα (steira) - "barren" - Elizabeth was barren
- [[Lexicon/G4260|G4260]] προβαίνω (probainō) - "advanced" - advanced in years
- [[Lexicon/G4773|G4773]] συγγενίς (syngenis) - "relative" - Elizabeth your relative
- [[Lexicon/G101|G101]] ἀδυνατέω (adynateō) - "be impossible" - nothing is impossible
- [[Lexicon/G4487|G4487]] ῥῆμα (rhēma) - "word" - no word from God will fail

**Context**: Zechariah and Elizabeth are old and childless—"Elizabeth was barren, and they were both well along in years" (1:7). Gabriel announces that Elizabeth will bear a son, John. Then to Mary, Gabriel cites Elizabeth as evidence: "Even Elizabeth your relative has conceived a son in her old age, and she who was called barren is in her sixth month. For no word from God will ever fail" (1:36-37).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 18#Genesis 18 . 14|Genesis 18:14]] - "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" (Sarah's conception)
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]]; 25:21; 30:22 - The matriarchal barren mothers
- [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 13#Judges 13 . 2|Judges 13:2-5]] - Barren mother of Samson
- [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 10|1 Samuel 1:10-20]] - Hannah's prayer and conception

**Connections**:
- **TO**: The entire barren mother tradition
- **FROM OT**: Luke explicitly echoes [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 18#Genesis 18 . 14|Genesis 18:14]] ("nothing is impossible")
- **FROM NT**: Elizabeth's conception confirms Mary's—if the barren can conceive, surely the virgin can

**Type Classification**: NT Continuation (the barren mother pattern in its penultimate form) and Forward-Looking (Elizabeth points to Mary)

**Christological Connection**: Elizabeth's barrenness overcome points to Mary's virginity overcome—both by divine power. John, born to the barren mother, is the forerunner; Jesus, born to the virgin, is the One for whom John prepares the way. The barren mother tradition reaches its climax in the virgin birth: if God can open barren wombs, He can cause a virgin to conceive. The impossible becomes possible because "no word from God will ever fail."
