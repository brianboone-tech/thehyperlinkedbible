### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 21|Genesis 25:21]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6279|H6279]] עָתַר (ʿāṯar) - "prayed/entreated" - Isaac pleaded with the LORD
- [[Lexicon/H5227|H5227]] נֹכַח (nōḵaḥ) - "on behalf of/opposite" - Isaac prayed for his wife
- [[Lexicon/H6135|H6135]] עָקָר (ʿāqār) - "barren" - Rebekah was barren
- [[Lexicon/H6030|H6030]] עָנָה (ʿānâ) - "answered" - the LORD answered him
- [[Lexicon/H2029|H2029]] הָרָה (hārâ) - "conceived" - Rebekah conceived

**Context**: The barren mother pattern continues in the second generation. Rebekah, like Sarah before her, cannot bear children. But unlike Abraham (who waited passively, then tried Hagar), Isaac actively prays for his wife. "The LORD answered him" (וַיֵּעָתֶר לוֹ יְהוָה)—using the same root as Isaac's prayer, emphasizing God's response to faithful intercession.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]] - Sarah's barrenness ended by divine visitation
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 21|Genesis 25:21]] - Rebekah's barrenness ended through Isaac's prayer
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 22|Genesis 30:22]] - Rachel's barrenness ended: "God remembered Rachel"
- The pattern continues through Hannah and Elizabeth

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]] - Sarah's precedent
- **FROM OT**: The twins' struggle in the womb and God's reversal of birth order
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 9#Romans 9 . 10|Romans 9:10-13]] cites God's choice of Jacob before birth as evidence of unconditional election

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged pattern) and Forward-Looking (anticipating the ultimate barren mother)

**Christological Connection**: The pattern of barrenness overcome by prayer anticipates both Hannah's petition for Samuel and Zechariah's for John the Baptist. More deeply, the divine choosing of Jacob before birth ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 9#Romans 9 . 11|Romans 9:11]]) points to Christ, the eternally chosen One ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 20|1 Peter 1:20]]) through whom election operates. The children of promise—like Isaac's child Jacob—are born not by natural descent but by God's sovereign choice, ultimately fulfilled in being "born again" through Christ.
