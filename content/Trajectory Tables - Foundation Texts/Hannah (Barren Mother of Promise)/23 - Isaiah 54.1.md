### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6135|H6135]] עֲקָרָה (ʿăqārâ) - "barren one" - sing, O barren one
- [[Lexicon/H3205|H3205]] יָלַד (yālaḏ) - "bear/give birth" - who did not bear
- [[Lexicon/H7442|H7442]] רָנַן (rānan) - "sing/shout for joy" - break forth into singing
- [[Lexicon/H8074|H8074]] שָׁמֵם (šāmēm) - "desolate" - children of the desolate one
- [[Lexicon/H1166|H1166]] בָּעַל (bāʿal) - "married" - the married woman

**Context**: Following the Suffering Servant's atoning work (Isaiah 53), Zion is called to rejoice. Israel in exile was like a barren, abandoned wife—but she will have more children than the fertile one. The barren mother motif becomes a metaphor for Israel's restoration and, ultimately, for the ingathering of Gentiles into God's family.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1-7]]; 25:21; 30:22 - Literal barren mothers
- [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 2#1 Samuel 2 . 5|1 Samuel 2:5]] - "The barren gives birth to seven"
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]] - Barrenness applied metaphorically to exiled Zion
- [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 27|Galatians 4:27]] - Paul applies this to the new covenant church

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - The barren mother tradition throughout Genesis and Samuel
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 18|Isaiah 49:18-21]] - uses similar imagery
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 66#Isaiah 66 . 7|Isaiah 66:7-9]] - shows Zion giving birth
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 27|Galatians 4:27]] - quotes this verse as fulfilled in the church
**Type Classification**: Prophetic Application (the barren mother pattern applied to eschatological restoration)

**Christological Connection**: Paul's citation ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 27|Galatians 4:27]]) makes explicit what Isaiah implied: the barren woman who bears many is fulfilled in the church, born through Christ's death and resurrection. Christ's cross looked like ultimate barrenness—defeat, death, abandonment. But resurrection proved it the most fruitful moment in history. The church born from the cross is the barren woman's many children. All who are in Christ are children of the "Jerusalem above," children of the once-barren mother now rejoicing.
