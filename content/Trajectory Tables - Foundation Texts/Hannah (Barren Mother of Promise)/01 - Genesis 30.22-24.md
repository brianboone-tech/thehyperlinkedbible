### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 22|Genesis 30:22-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2142|H2142]] זָכַר (zāḵar) - "remembered" - God remembered Rachel
- [[Lexicon/H8085|H8085]] שָׁמַע (šāmaʿ) - "listened/heard" - God listened to her
- [[Lexicon/H6605|H6605]] פָּתַח (pāṯaḥ) - "opened" - God opened her womb
- [[Lexicon/H622|H622]] אָסַף (ʾāsap̄) - "taken away" - God has taken away my reproach
- [[Lexicon/H3130|H3130]] יוֹסֵף (yôsēp̄) - "Joseph" - may the LORD add another son

**Context**: Rachel's barrenness was particularly painful—she watched her sister Leah bear child after child while her own womb remained closed. Her desperation drove her to extremes (demanding children from Jacob, using her maidservant). Finally, "God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her and opened her womb." Joseph is born—the son who will become Israel's deliverer.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 29#Genesis 29 . 31|Genesis 29:31]] - "The LORD saw that Leah was unloved, so He opened her womb, but Rachel was barren"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 1|Genesis 30:1]] - Rachel's desperate cry: "Give me children, or I will die!"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 22|Genesis 30:22]] - "God remembered Rachel"
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 50#Genesis 50 . 20|Genesis 50:20]] - Joseph's role: "God meant it for good, to preserve many people alive"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 1|Genesis 21:1]]; 25:21 - The matriarchal pattern
- **FROM OT**: Joseph's role as preserver of the family echoes through the Joseph narrative
- **FROM NT**: Joseph as type of Christ (rejected by brothers, exalted to save them)

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged) and Forward-Looking (Joseph as type of Christ the Deliverer)

**Christological Connection**: Joseph, son of the barren mother, becomes the deliverer who saves his family from death. His brothers sold him into slavery; he rose to become ruler of Egypt; he forgave those who wronged him and preserved them alive. The pattern is christological: rejection, exaltation, salvation, forgiveness. Jesus, born of the ultimate barren mother (virgin), was rejected by His brothers (Israel), exalted at God's right hand, and saves those who rejected Him.
