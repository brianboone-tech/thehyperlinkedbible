### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 43#Isaiah 43 . 16|Isaiah 43:16-19]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1870|H1870]] דֶּרֶךְ (derekh) - "way/path" - God makes a way through obstacles
- [[Lexicon/H2319|H2319]] חָדָשׁ (chadash) - "new" - a new thing God is doing
- [[Lexicon/H4057|H4057]] מִדְבָּר (midbar) - "wilderness/desert" - place of new exodus
- [[Lexicon/H5104|H5104]] נָהָר (nahar) - "rivers/streams" - provision in desolation

**Context**: Isaiah 43 falls within the "Book of Comfort" (Isa 40-55), addressing exiles who will experience Babylonian captivity. The passage frames the return from Babylon as a "new exodus" that surpasses the original deliverance from Egypt, establishing a typological pattern that climaxes in Christ.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Exodus 14-15 - The original exodus through the sea
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 48#Isaiah 48 . 20|Isaiah 48:20-21]] - "Go out from Babylon! Flee from the Chaldeans!"
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 51#Isaiah 51 . 9|Isaiah 51:9-11]] - "Was it not You who dried up the sea... who made the depths of the sea a road for the redeemed to cross over?"

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Exodus 14 - Original sea crossing establishes the pattern
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 77#Psalm 77 . 19|Psalm 77:19-20]] - "Your path led through the sea... You led Your people like a flock"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 1|1 Corinthians 10:1-4]] - "Our fathers... all passed through the sea"
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 16|Hebrews 3:16-4]] - :11 - Rest/promised land typology
**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (new exodus anticipation), linking to both Cyrus's historical deliverance and Christ's ultimate redemption

**Christological Connection**: The new exodus through Cyrus was only partial; the exiles returned but remained under foreign domination. The true "new thing" is Christ's redemption—the ultimate exodus from sin's bondage. Luke deliberately uses exodus language for Jesus' death ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 9#Luke 9 . 31|Luke 9:31]]), and the early church understood baptism as passing through waters into new life, echoing both Red Sea and Jordan crossings.
