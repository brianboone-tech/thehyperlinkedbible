### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 16#Judges 16 . 28|Judges 16:28-30]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2142|H2142]] זָכַר (zāḵar) - "remember" - please remember me
- [[Lexicon/H2388|H2388]] חָזַק (ḥāzaq) - "strengthen" - strengthen me just once more
- [[Lexicon/H4191|H4191]] מוּת (mûṯ) - "die" - let me die with the Philistines
- [[Lexicon/H7227|H7227]] רַב (raḇ) - "many/more" - more than he killed in his life
- [[Lexicon/H2416|H2416]] חַי (ḥay) - "life/living" - in his life

**Context**: In Dagon's temple, blind and humiliated, Samson positioned himself between the central pillars. His prayer: "O Lord GOD, please remember me (זָכְרֵנִי נָא). Strengthen me (חַזְּקֵנִי נָא), O God, just once more" (16:28). God answered. Samson grasped the pillars and said, "Let me die with the Philistines" (תָּמוֹת נַפְשִׁי עִם־פְּלִשְׁתִּים). He pushed with restored strength, collapsing the temple on the lords and people. The narrator notes: "he killed more when he died than while he lived" (וַיִּהְיוּ הַמֵּתִים אֲשֶׁר הֵמִית בְּמוֹתוֹ רַבִּים מֵאֲשֶׁר הֵמִית בְּחַיָּיו).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The prayer "remember me" echoes [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 13#Nehemiah 13 . 31|Nehemiah 13:31]] and anticipates the thief's prayer ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 23#Luke 23 . 42|Luke 23:42]])
- Victory through death anticipates [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 12|Isaiah 53:12]]—"he poured out his soul to death"
- The destruction of Dagon's temple echoes the fall of foreign gods before the LORD ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 5#1 Samuel 5 . 1|1 Samuel 5:1-5]])

**Type Classification**: Providential (Samson's death serves divine purposes), Forward-Looking (the victory-through-death pattern explicitly anticipates the cross)

**Christological Connection**: Samson's death-victory is the clearest typological anticipation of Christ's cross. Both achieved greater victory in death than in life. Samson destroyed the temple of Dagon; Christ destroyed the works of the devil ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 8|1 John 3:8]]). Samson's death brought down the lords of the Philistines; Christ's death "disarmed rulers and authorities" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]). "Through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14]]). What seemed to be Christ's final defeat—crucifixion between criminals—became cosmic victory. The cross, like Dagon's temple, appeared to be the enemy's triumph; both became the enemy's destruction. Samson's "Let me die with the Philistines" shadows Christ's voluntary self-sacrifice: "No one takes my life from me, but I lay it down of my own accord" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 18|John 10:18]]).
