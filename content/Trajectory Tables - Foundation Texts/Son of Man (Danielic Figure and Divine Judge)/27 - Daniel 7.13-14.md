**Context**: Daniel 7 presents a night vision of four beasts arising from the sea (Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome—vv. 1-8), followed by the heavenly throne room scene: the Ancient of Days takes His seat (vv. 9-10), the beasts are judged (vv. 11-12), then 'one like a son of man' comes with clouds, approaches the Ancient of Days, and receives everlasting dominion (vv. 13-14). Later, the angel interprets: the saints of the Most High receive the kingdom (vv. 18, 22, 27), but the vision itself focuses on the individual 'son of man' figure.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Synthesizes Psalm 8 (son of man crowned with dominion) with divine theophany (cloud-riding YHWH—[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 13#Exodus 13 . 21|Exodus 13:21]]; [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 104#Psalm 104 . 3|Psalm 104:3]]; [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 19#Isaiah 19 . 1|Isaiah 19:1]])
- The 'coming with clouds' is divine prerogative throughout OT: only YHWH rides the clouds as His chariot
- This figure receives worship ('all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him'—v. 14), language elsewhere reserved for God alone
- Contrast with beasts (animal kingdoms) highlights this figure's humanity, yet His heavenly origin ('coming with clouds') and divine authority transcend mere humanity

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking type - the vision explicitly looks forward to a future kingdom and coming figure. The 'one like a son of man' is expectational in the original context, awaiting fulfillment. Jesus identifies Himself as this figure, making Daniel 7:13-14 both prophecy and type.

**Christological Connection**: Jesus identifies Himself as the Danielic Son of Man throughout His ministry. At His trial, He declares: 'You will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of heaven' ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62|Mark 14:62]]), directly quoting [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 13|Daniel 7:13]]. The high priest tears his robes, recognizing the claim to divine status. Jesus' use of 'Son of Man' is strategic: it affirms true humanity while claiming transcendent authority. He comes 'with clouds' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 30|Matthew 24:30]]; 26:64; [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 13#Mark 13 . 26|Mark 13:26]]; [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 21#Luke 21 . 27|Luke 21:27]]), receives 'all authority in heaven and earth' ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 18|Matthew 28:18]] = [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 14|Daniel 7:14]] dominion), and exercises divine prerogatives (forgiveness, Sabbath lordship). Stephen's vision confirms Jesus is this figure: 'the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God' ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 56|Acts 7:56]]). Revelation depicts Christ as 'one like a son of man' (1:13; 14:14), coming on clouds (1:7), executing judgment. The suffering servant who was crucified is the exalted Son of Man who will return in glory, fulfilling Daniel's vision of the human-yet-divine figure who receives everlasting dominion over all nations.
