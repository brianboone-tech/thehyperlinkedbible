**Context**: Revelation's opening declares the book's theme: Jesus Christ's revelation (1:1), His second coming (1:7), and His present reality among the churches (1:12-20). Verse 7 announces: 'Behold, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him—even those who pierced Him.' Verses 12-16 describe John's vision of 'one like a son of man' among the seven lampstands (churches), clothed in priestly and royal garments, with cosmic attributes (eyes like fire, voice like many waters, face like the sun).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Verse 7 combines [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 13|Daniel 7:13]] ('coming with clouds') with [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 12#Zechariah 12 . 10|Zechariah 12:10]] ('they will look on me whom they have pierced')
- Verses 12-16 blend [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 13|Daniel 7:13]] ('like a son of man') with [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 10#Daniel 10 . 5|Daniel 10:5-6]] (theophanic vision: man clothed in linen, face like lightning, eyes like torches)
- The white robe (v. 13) echoes [[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 9|Daniel 7:9]] (Ancient of Days clothed in white); combining Son of Man imagery with divine attributes

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking (prophesying Jesus' second coming) and backward-looking (interpreting Jesus' crucifixion through Zechariah 12:10; His exaltation through Daniel 7:13). The imagery is typological: Daniel's vision of the Son of Man finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ's parousia.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 7|Revelation 1:7]],13 brings the Son of Man trajectory to eschatological consummation. The suffering servant who was pierced ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 5|Isaiah 53:5]]; [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 12#Zechariah 12 . 10|Zechariah 12:10]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|John 19:34]]) is the Danielic Son of Man who comes on clouds in glory. This fusion defines Christianity's christological uniqueness: the same Jesus who died returns to judge. Verse 7 universalizes the vision: 'every eye will see Him'—not just the Sanhedrin ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62|Mark 14:62]]), but all humanity. 'Even those who pierced Him' (quoting [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 12#Zechariah 12 . 10|Zechariah 12:10]]) suggests repentance (as in Zechariah's context—mourning leading to cleansing, 13:1) or judgment. The vision in verses 12-16 reveals Christ's present reality: He is not absent, awaiting return, but present among His churches (lampstands), exercising authority. The white robe (priestly), golden sash (royal/priestly), white hair (Ancient of Days—[[Readable Bible/27 - Daniel/Daniel 7#Daniel 7 . 9|Daniel 7:9]]), blazing eyes (penetrating judgment), feet like bronze (refined strength), voice like many waters (sovereign authority), seven stars (control of church leaders), sharp two-edged sword (word of judgment—[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 12|Hebrews 4:12]]), and radiant face (divine glory) all identify Jesus as the divine-human Son of Man. He is worshiped (v. 17 'I fell at his feet as though dead'), identified as 'the first and the last' (divine title—[[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 44#Isaiah 44 . 6|Isaiah 44:6]]; 48:12), and declared 'I died, and behold I am alive forevermore' (resurrection victory). This is the one who will return: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 14|Revelation 14:14]] repeats the imagery ('one like a son of man... seated on a white cloud... with a sharp sickle'), executing harvest judgment. The suffering Son of Man becomes the conquering King.
