### [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14-15]]; [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 3#1 John 3 . 8|1 John 3:8]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G554|G554]] ἀπεκδύομαι (*apekduomai*) - to disarm, strip off
- [[Lexicon/G2358|G2358]] θριαμβεύω (*thriambeuo*) - to triumph over (Roman triumph imagery)
- [[Lexicon/G2673|G2673]] καταργέω (*katargeo*) - to render powerless, destroy
- [[Lexicon/G3089|G3089]] λύω (*luo*) - to destroy, loose, abolish

**Context**: Colossians 2:15 declares Christ "disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them." Hebrews 2:14-15 adds that through death Christ destroyed "the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil" and delivered "those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery." 1 John 3:8 states "The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Joshua conquered physical enemies; Christ conquers spiritual enemies
- Roman triumph imagery: victorious general parading defeated enemies
- Complete victory - not partial like Israel's incomplete conquest

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Joshua's conquest - earthly enemies defeated
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] - seed crushing serpent's head
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] - enemies made footstool
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 20|Ephesians 1:20-22]] - all things under His feet
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 10|Revelation 20:10]] - final defeat of Satan

**Type Classification**: NT Fulfillment - Conquest typology realized in Christ

**Christological Connection**: Christ's cross was His Jericho - apparent weakness that was actually divine power. He "disarmed" the powers (as Joshua disarmed Canaan), "put them to open shame" (public display of victory), and "triumphed over them" (complete conquest). Unlike Israel's incomplete conquest, Christ's victory is total: sin's penalty paid, Satan's power broken, death defeated. The conquest is DONE; believers now enforce the victory.
