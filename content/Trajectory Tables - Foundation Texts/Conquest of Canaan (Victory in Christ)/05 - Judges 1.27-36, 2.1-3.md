### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 1#Judges 1 . 27|Judges 1:27-36]]; [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 1|Judges 2:1-3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3423|H3423]] יָרַשׁ (*yarash*) - to dispossess (negated: did NOT drive out)
- [[Lexicon/H3427|H3427]] יָשַׁב (*yashab*) - to dwell, remain
- [[Lexicon/H4170|H4170]] מוֹקֵשׁ (*moqesh*) - snare, trap
- [[Lexicon/H6654|H6654]] צַד (*tsad*) - side (thorns in your sides)

**Context**: After Joshua's death, the incomplete conquest becomes evident. "Manasseh did not drive out the inhabitants of Beth-shean... When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor, but did not drive them out completely." The Angel of the LORD rebukes: "You have not obeyed my voice... I will not drive them out before you, but they shall become thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare to you."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Warning fulfilled: remaining Canaanites led Israel into idolatry
- Pattern of Judges: sin → oppression → crying out → deliverance → repeat
- Incomplete obedience leads to ongoing struggles

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 2|Deuteronomy 7:2-5]] - commanded to destroy completely
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 23#Joshua 23 . 12|Joshua 23:12-13]] - warned against intermarriage with Canaanites
- **FROM OT**: Judges cycles of apostasy (Judg 2:11-23)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 6|1 Corinthians 10:6-12]] - Israel's failures as warnings
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 1|Hebrews 12:1]] - "lay aside every weight, and sin which clings"

**Type Classification**: Providential Type (negative example - consequence of compromise)

**Christological Connection**: Israel's failure to fully drive out the Canaanites pictures believers' failure to fully "put to death" the deeds of the flesh (Col 3:5). Tolerated sin becomes "thorns in your sides" - ongoing sources of temptation and defeat. Christ has won complete victory (Col 2:15), but believers must actively appropriate it by dealing decisively with sin rather than coexisting with it.
