### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]; [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 13#John 13 . 10|John 13:10]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3670|G3670]] ὁμολογέω (*homologeo*) - to confess, acknowledge
- [[Lexicon/G2511|G2511]] καθαρίζω (*katharizo*) - to cleanse, purify
- [[Lexicon/G3068|G3068]] λούω (*louo*) - to bathe (whole body)
- [[Lexicon/G3538|G3538]] νίπτω (*nipto*) - to wash (part of body)
- [[Lexicon/G4228|G4228]] πούς (*pous*) - foot
- [[Lexicon/G2513|G2513]] καθαρός (*katharos*) - clean, pure

**Context**: 1 John 1:9: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us (καθαρίσῃ) from all unrighteousness."

John 13:10: "Jesus said to him, 'The one who has bathed (λελουμένος) does not need to wash (νίψασθαι), except for his feet (τοὺς πόδας), but is completely clean (καθαρός).'"

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Priests washed before each service (repeated)
- Believers are "bathed" once but need ongoing foot-washing
- Initial cleansing + continuing purification

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 17|Exodus 30:17-21]] - Priests wash hands AND feet
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 4|Leviticus 16:4]] - High priest bathes whole body
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 10|Hebrews 10:10]] - "Sanctified... once for all"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 14|Hebrews 10:14]] - "By a single offering he has perfected"

**Type Classification**: NT Application - Laver pattern applied to Christian life

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' foot-washing teaches the distinction the laver implied. Priests who bathed still washed hands and feet before service. Believers are definitively "bathed" (justified, regenerated) but need ongoing "foot-washing" (confession, sanctification). Christ provides both: once-for-all cleansing through His blood AND ongoing cleansing through confession and His faithful forgiveness. The laver's dual function (initial entry + repeated washing) anticipates justification + sanctification.
