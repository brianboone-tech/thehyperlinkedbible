### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 26#Exodus 26 . 30|Exodus 26:30]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6965|H6965]] הֵקִים (*heqim*, Hiphil of קוּם) - "to erect, establish, set up"; technical term for constructing the tabernacle
- [[Lexicon/H4941|H4941]] מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*) - "ordinance, custom, manner, plan"; the divinely ordained design or specification

**Context**: Exodus 26 provides detailed instructions for the tabernacle structure itself—the curtains, coverings, frames, and veil. After specifying the materials, dimensions, and assembly, God concludes (v. 30) by reiterating that the entire structure must conform to the pattern shown on the mountain. This third repetition of the heavenly pattern principle (after 25:9, 40) demonstrates its foundational importance. The command brackets the instructions: begin by acknowledging the heavenly pattern (25:9), execute each detail faithfully (25:10-26:29), and remember the pattern governs everything (26:30).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 40#Exodus 40 . 16|Exodus 40:16-33]] narrates Moses's obedience: "Moses did everything just as the LORD commanded him" (v. 16), with the phrase repeated nine times in the chapter
- [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 1#Numbers 1 . 54|Numbers 1:54]]; 2:34 affirm Israel's obedience: "Thus did the people of Israel; according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so they did"
- [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 44|Acts 7:44]] (Stephen's speech) references this pattern principle as fundamental to Israel's worship

**Type Classification**: **Direct Type** (God commanded construction according to specific pattern) and **Forward-Looking** (the emphasis on derivative nature implies something greater exists beyond the copy)

**Christological Connection**: The tabernacle structure—curtains, frames, veil—all testified to barriers between God and humanity. Only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, and then only once a year. Christ's death tore the veil from top to bottom (Matt 27:51), demonstrating that he is the true tabernacle who grants access to God's presence. He "erected" (ἔπηξεν, Heb 8:2, from same root as הֵקִים) the true tent—not a structure made with hands but his own incarnation and ascension, opening the way to heaven itself.
