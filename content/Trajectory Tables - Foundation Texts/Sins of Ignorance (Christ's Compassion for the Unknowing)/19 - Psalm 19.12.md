### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 19#Psalm 19 . 12|Psalm 19:12-13]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7601-7700#H7691|H7691]] שְׁגִיאָה (shegi'ah) - error, mistake (plural: שְׁגִיאוֹת)
- [[Lexicon/H5501-5600#H5546|H5546]] נִסְתָּרוֹת (nistarot) - hidden things, secret faults
- [[Lexicon/H5301-5400#H5352|H5352]] נָקָה (naqah) - to be clean, acquit, declare innocent
- [[Lexicon/H2001-2100#H2086|H2086]] זֵד (zed) - presumptuous, arrogant

**Context**: David concludes his meditation on God's revelation (heavens declare, law restores) with a prayer for cleansing. He distinguishes between "errors" (sins he doesn't recognize), "secret faults" (hidden from his awareness), and "presumptuous sins" (willful rebellion). He prays for cleansing from the first two and restraint from the third.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 2|Leviticus 4:2]] - The term שְׁגִיאוֹת (errors) is cognate with שְׁגָגָה (sin of ignorance)
- [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 15#Numbers 15 . 30|Numbers 15:30-31]] - Presumptuous sin (בְּיָד רָמָה, "high hand") contrasted with ignorant sin
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 17#Jeremiah 17 . 9|Jeremiah 17:9]] - "The heart is deceitful above all things"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 2|Leviticus 4:2]] - Cognate vocabulary connecting to Levitical provision
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 20#Proverbs 20 . 9|Proverbs 20:9]] - "Who can say, I have made my heart clean?"
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 8|1 John 1:8]] - "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves"

**Type Classification**: **Providential** - David's prayer applies Levitical categories to personal spiritual experience

**Christological Connection**: David's prayer acknowledges what the Levitical system implied: human self-examination cannot identify all sin. The question "Who can understand his errors?" expects the answer "No one." This prepares for Christ's priesthood, which offers compassion to "the ignorant" (Hebrews 5:2). David cannot cleanse what he cannot see; he must trust God's provision for sins beyond his awareness. Christ's atoning work covers not just the catalog of known sins but the vast terrain of unknown transgressions.
