### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 48#Genesis 48 . 15|Genesis 48:15]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7401-7500#H7462|H7462]] רָעָה (ra'ah) - to pasture, tend, graze, shepherd
- [[Lexicon/H0001-0100#H0430|H0430]] אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) - God

**Context**: Jacob's deathbed blessing over Joseph's sons Ephraim and Manasseh. This is the first time in Scripture that God is explicitly called "the Shepherd" (הָרֹעֶה, ha-ro'eh with the definite article). Jacob reflects on his entire life journey—from Paddan-aram to Egypt—and attributes his preservation to God's shepherding care.

**OT-to-OT Development**:

**Type Classification**: **Backward-Looking Providential Type** - Jacob's testimony was not explicitly predictive in original context, but God providentially arranged this language to prefigure Christ. The NT recognizes Jesus as the ultimate fulfillment of divine shepherding.

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ is the eternal Shepherd whom Jacob encountered. [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11]] claims the divine shepherd role ("I am the good shepherd"). The patriarchs' experience of God's shepherding finds ultimate expression in Christ's incarnate ministry: guiding, protecting, feeding, and laying down His life for the sheep. Jacob's words, "the God who has shepherded me," become the church's confession: "the Lamb will be their shepherd" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 17|Revelation 7:17]]).
