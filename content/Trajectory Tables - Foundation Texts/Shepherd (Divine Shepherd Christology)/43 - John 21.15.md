### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 21#John 21 . 15|John 21:15-17]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0101-0200#G0162|G0162]] ἀρνίον (arnion) - lamb, lambkin (diminutive)
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4168|G4168]] πρόβατον (probaton) - sheep
- [[Lexicon/G1001-1100#G1006|G1006]] βόσκω (bosko) - to feed, pasture (provide food)
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4165|G4165]] ποιμαίνω (poimaino) - to shepherd, tend, rule

**Context**: Post-resurrection appearance at Sea of Galilee. After miraculous catch of fish (21:1-14), Jesus restores Peter through threefold commission matching Peter's threefold denial (18:15-27). The shift from fishing to shepherding recalls Peter's original call (Luke 5:10: "you will catch men") now reframed as pastoral care.

**OT-to-OT Development Fulfilled**:
- Ezekiel 34:2-3 (shepherds should feed the flock) → Ezekiel 34:23 (My servant David will shepherd them) → Jeremiah 3:15 (shepherds after My heart who will feed you) → FULFILLED in John 21:15-17 (Feed My lambs/sheep)

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Ezekiel 34:2-3 (shepherds must feed sheep); Jeremiah 3:15 (shepherds after My heart); 2 Samuel 5:2 (David commissioned to shepherd)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 5#1 Peter 5 . 1|1 Peter 5:1-4]] - Peter's later exhortation: shepherd the flock; Chief Shepherd will appear
  - [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 20#Acts 20 . 28|Acts 20:28]] - Paul to Ephesian elders: shepherd the church
**Type Classification**: Fulfillment and application of Davidic shepherd commissioning (2 Sam 5:2) and prophetic shepherd promises (Jer 3:15; Ezek 34:23).

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ, the Good Shepherd ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11]]), commissions Peter as undershepherd. This scene demonstrates Christ's continuing shepherd role even post-ascension: He cares for His flock through appointed human shepherds. The possessive "MY sheep" is emphatic—the flock belongs to Christ, not Peter. This recovers the proper OT pattern: David was undershepherd under Yahweh (2 Sam 5:2; [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 78#Psalm 78 . 70|Psalm 78:70-72]]); Peter is undershepherd under Christ. The dual verbs βόσκω (feed) and ποιμαίνω (shepherd/tend) echo [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 34#Ezekiel 34 . 2|Ezekiel 34:2-3]]'s requirements: shepherds must both nourish (teaching/doctrine) and protect/oversee (governance/discipline). Peter's later writing (1 Pet 5:1-4) shows he internalized this commission: "Shepherd the flock of God... when the Chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown." The threefold "Do you love Me?" grounds pastoral ministry in personal devotion to Christ—shepherding flows from love for the Chief Shepherd.
