### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 20|Hebrews 13:20]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4166|G4166]] ποιμήν (poimen) - shepherd
- [[Lexicon/G3101-3200#G3173|G3173]] μέγας (megas) - great, large, mighty
- [[Lexicon/G0300-0400#G0321|G0321]] ἀνάγω (anago) - to bring up, lead up (aorist participle: brought up from dead)
- [[Lexicon/G0100-0200#G0129|G0129]] αἷμα (haima) - blood

**Context**: Hebrews' closing benediction (13:20-21) follows exhortations to faithful living and precedes final greetings. This is the only place in Hebrews where Jesus is called "shepherd," bringing to climax the epistle's theme of Christ's superiority over all previous mediators (angels, Moses, Aaron, Levitical priests).

**OT-to-OT Development Fulfilled**:
- Isaiah 63:11 (Moses brought up from sea) → Psalm 23 (Yahweh my shepherd) → Ezekiel 34:23 (one shepherd) → Zechariah 13:7 (strike the shepherd) → FULFILLED in Hebrews 13:20 (great Shepherd brought from dead)

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Isaiah 63:11 (brought up from the sea—Moses); Psalm 23 (Yahweh my shepherd); Ezekiel 34:11-16; 23 (divine and Davidic shepherd)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11]] - Good Shepherd
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 25|1 Peter 2:25]] - Shepherd and Overseer
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 5#1 Peter 5 . 4|1 Peter 5:4]] - Chief Shepherd
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 17|Revelation 7:17]] - Lamb will shepherd
**Type Classification**: Antitype fulfilling multiple types - Moses (brought up from sea), David (shepherd-king), suffering shepherd (Zech 13:7).

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ is "the great Shepherd of the sheep" superior to all previous shepherds. The title μέγας (great) indicates escalation: greater than Moses (Heb 3:3), greater than Aaron (7:11-28), greater than David. The resurrection validates His shepherding work—where [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 13#Zechariah 13 . 7|Zechariah 13:7]] prophesied the struck shepherd, Hebrews declares God "brought again from the dead" the Shepherd who was struck. The phrase "through the blood of the eternal covenant" connects shepherd imagery to priestly sacrifice: Jesus is both Priest who offers and Shepherd who protects. His shepherding is eternal (αἰωνίου), not limited to earthly lifetime like David or Moses. The bringing up "from the dead" (ἐκ νεκρῶν) alludes to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 11|Isaiah 63:11]] but escalates: Moses brought up from Red Sea waters; Jesus brought up from death itself—ultimate deliverance.
