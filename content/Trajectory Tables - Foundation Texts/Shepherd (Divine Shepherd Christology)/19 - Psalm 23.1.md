### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 23#Psalm 23 . 1|Psalm 23:1-6]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7401-7500#H7462|H7462]] רָעָה (ra'ah) - shepherd (Qal active participle: רֹעִי, "my Shepherd")
- [[Lexicon/H3000-3100#H3068|H3068]] יְהוָה (Yahweh) - the covenant name of God
- [[Lexicon/H2600-2700#H2637|H2637]] חָסֵר (chaser) - to lack, be in want (negated: "I shall not want")

**Context**: David's most beloved psalm, expressing intimate trust in Yahweh's shepherding care. Remarkably, David the human shepherd-king (2 Sam 5:2; Psalm 78:71-72) recognizes he himself needs the divine Shepherd. The psalm divides between pastoral imagery (vv. 1-4: green pastures, still waters, paths of righteousness, valley of shadow) and banquet imagery (vv. 5-6: table, anointing, overflowing cup).

**Type Classification**: **Forward-Looking Providential Type** - While David wrote from personal experience, God providentially designed this psalm to prefigure Christ's shepherding. The imagery anticipates deeper fulfillment: "still waters" → living water (John 4:10-14); "paths of righteousness" → Christ as the Way (John 14:6); "valley of death's shadow" → Christ's victory over death (John 11:25-26); "dwell in house of Yahweh forever" → eternal life in Christ (John 10:28).

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ is the Good Shepherd whom Psalm 23 anticipates. He provides abundant life ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 10|John 10:10]] = "I shall not want"); guides into all truth ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 16#John 16 . 13|John 16:13]] = "paths of righteousness"); protects through death itself ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 28|John 10:28]] = "valley of death's shadow—I fear no evil"); prepares a table ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 29|Luke 22:29-30]] = covenant meal); and promises eternal dwelling ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 2|John 14:2-3]] = "I will dwell in the house of Yahweh forever"). [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 17|Revelation 7:17]] brings the typology to consummation: "The Lamb will be their shepherd and guide them to springs of living water"—Psalm 23's "still waters" fulfilled eternally.
