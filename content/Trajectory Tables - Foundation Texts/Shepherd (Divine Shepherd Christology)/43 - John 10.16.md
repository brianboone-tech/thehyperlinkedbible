### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 16|John 10:16]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4166|G4166]] ποιμήν (poimen) - shepherd
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4167|G4167]] ποίμνη (poimne) - flock
- [[Lexicon/G0800-0900#G0833|G0833]] αὐλή (aule) - fold, courtyard, sheepfold

**Context**: Within the Good Shepherd discourse (John 10:1-21), this verse extends shepherd imagery beyond ethnic Israel to include Gentiles. Jesus announces He has "other sheep not of this fold" who must be brought into one unified flock under one shepherd.

**OT-to-OT Development Fulfilled**:
- Ezekiel 34:23 (one shepherd) → Ezekiel 37:24 (one shepherd over reunited Israel/Judah) → Isaiah 56:6-8 (foreigners gathered to God's house) → FULFILLED in John 10:16 (one flock, one shepherd)

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Ezekiel 34:23 ("one shepherd"); Ezekiel 37:24 (one shepherd, one king); Isaiah 56:8 (gather others to Israel); Genesis 12:3 (all nations blessed)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 51|John 11:51-52]] - gather scattered children of God
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-16]] - one new man from Jew/Gentile
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 4#Ephesians 4 . 4|Ephesians 4:4-6]] - one body, one Lord
**Type Classification**: Antitype fulfilling and escalating **Forward-Looking Direct Type** (Ezek 34:23; 37:24).

**Christological Connection**: Jesus Christ fulfills Ezekiel's "one shepherd" at cosmic scale. Where Ezekiel anticipated reuniting Israel and Judah under one Davidic shepherd, Jesus gathers Jew and Gentile into one unified flock. This demonstrates Fairbairn's principle of escalation: the antitype always surpasses the type. The promise "they will listen to My voice" (ἀκούσουσιν τῆς φωνῆς μου) echoes 10:3-4, 14—intimate knowledge between shepherd and sheep regardless of ethnic origin. [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 11#John 11 . 51|John 11:51-52]] interprets: Jesus would die "to gather into one the scattered children of God." Paul's theology of "one new man" (Eph 2:14-16) from Jew and Gentile flows from this shepherd imagery. The singular "one shepherd" (εἷς ποιμήν) emphasizes Christ's exclusive role as mediator—no other shepherd can unify God's worldwide flock.
