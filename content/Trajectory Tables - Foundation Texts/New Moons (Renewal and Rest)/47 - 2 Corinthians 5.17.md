### [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1501-1600#G1536|εἴ τις]] (*ei tis*) - "if anyone"
- [[Lexicon/G1701-1800#G1722|ἐν Χριστῷ]] (*en Christō*) - "in Christ" (locational union)
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2537|καινὴ κτίσις]] (*kainē ktisis*) - "new creation, new creature"
- [[Lexicon/G0701-0800#G0744|ἀρχαῖα]] (*archaia*) - "old things, ancient things"
- [[Lexicon/G3801-3900#G3928|παρῆλθεν]] (*parēlthen*) - "has passed away" (aorist, completed action)
- [[Lexicon/G2201-2300#G2400|ἰδοὺ]] (*idou*) - "behold!" (imperative, draw attention)
- [[Lexicon/G1001-1100#G1096|γέγονεν]] (*gegonen*) - "has become, has come" (perfect, completed with ongoing results)
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2537|καινὰ]] (*kaina*) - "new things"

**Context**: Paul declares that being "in Christ" constitutes radical transformation—a person becomes a new creation. The old has passed away; the new has arrived. This isn't gradual improvement but comprehensive renewal, paralleling God's original creation. Union with Christ inaugurates new creation existence where former realities give way to present newness.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 1#Genesis 1 . 1|Genesis 1:1]] (God created), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 26|Ezekiel 36:26]] (new heart and new spirit)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 15|Galatians 6:15]] (new creation is what counts), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 10|Ephesians 2:10]] (created in Christ Jesus), [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 4#Ephesians 4 . 24|Ephesians 4:24]] (put on new self, created after likeness of God), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]] (I am making all things new)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]]'s new creation fulfills what new moons symbolized—fresh start, complete renewal. The monthly new moon marked temporal renewal; union with Christ accomplishes eternal newness. Christ is the "last Adam" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45]]) who inaugurates new creation, reversing the first Adam's curse. His resurrection is new creation's firstfruits: "God... has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 6|2 Corinthians 4:6]])—echoing Genesis 1's "Let there be light." Being "in Christ" transfers believers from old creation (marked by sin and death) to new creation (marked by righteousness and life). The transformation Paul describes isn't self-improvement but divine recreation: "We are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 10|Ephesians 2:10]]). The "old has passed away" includes not just personal sin history but participation in Adam's fallen humanity. The "new has come" means participation in Christ's resurrection life. Paul elsewhere declares: "For neither circumcision counts for anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 15|Galatians 6:15]])—ethnic distinctions are transcended in Christ's new creation. The monthly renewal new moons provided externally, Christ provides internally and perpetually: "Though our outer self is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 16|2 Corinthians 4:16]]). This present new creation anticipates cosmic renewal when Christ makes "all things new" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 5|Revelation 21:5]])—what believers experience individually now, creation will experience universally then. The new moon's visible monthly change prefigured invisible continuous transformation in Christ. Union with Him means living in permanent newness, not cyclical renewal.
