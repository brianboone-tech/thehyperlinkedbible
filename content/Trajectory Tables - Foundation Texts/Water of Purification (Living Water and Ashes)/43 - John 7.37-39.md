### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 37|John 7:37-39]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5204|G5204]] ὕδωρ (*hydōr*) - "water"
- [[Lexicon/G2198|G2198]] ζάω (*zaō*) - "to live"; ζῶν (*zōn*) - "living"
- [[Lexicon/G4095|G4095]] πίνω (*pinō*) - "to drink"
- [[Lexicon/G4137|G4137]] πληρόω (*plēroō*) - "to fulfill"; as the Scripture has said
- [[Lexicon/G4151|G4151]] πνεῦμα (*pneuma*) - "Spirit, spirit, breath"
- [[Lexicon/G1392|G1392]] δοξάζω (*doxazō*) - "to glorify"

**Context**: Jesus spoke these words on the last day of the Feast of Tabernacles, a festival commemorating Israel's wilderness wanderings when God provided water from the rock (Exodus 17:6). During this feast, priests performed a water-drawing ceremony, making Jesus' proclamation particularly significant. He offers Himself as the source of "living water" (ὕδωρ ζῶν), then John provides the crucial interpretive key: "this he said about the Spirit."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Jesus synthesizes multiple OT water texts: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 17#Exodus 17 . 6|Exodus 17:6]] (water from rock), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 44#Isaiah 44 . 3|Isaiah 44:3]] (water poured on thirsty), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 55#Isaiah 55 . 1|Isaiah 55:1]] (come to waters), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 58#Isaiah 58 . 11|Isaiah 58:11]] (spring whose waters never fail), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 47#Ezekiel 47 . 1|Ezekiel 47:1]] (river from temple), [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 14#Zechariah 14 . 8|Zechariah 14:8]] (living waters from Jerusalem).
- The exact phrase "living water" (ὕδωρ ζῶν) translates Hebrew מַיִם חַיִּים (*mayim ḥayyîm*) used in [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 17|Numbers 19:17]] for the water of purification.

**Type Classification**: **Fulfillment** of **Forward-looking Direct Type** (Numbers 19's commanded ritual using "living water") and **Providential Type** (wilderness water provision).

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 37|John 7:37-39]] reveals Jesus as both the source and substance of the living water required in Numbers 19's purification ritual. The red heifer's ashes had to be mixed with "living water" (מַיִם חַיִּים / ὕδωρ ζῶν)—the identical phrase Jesus uses here. But now John reveals the mystery: the living water is the Holy Spirit, whom Jesus gives to believers. This transforms our understanding of Numbers 19: the ashes represented Christ's completed sacrifice ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13-14]]); the living water represented the Spirit whom Christ sends. Just as neither element alone could purify (ashes needed water; water needed ashes), so Christ's sacrifice and the Spirit's work are inseparable. Jesus gives the Spirit only after being "glorified" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 39|John 7:39]])—His crucifixion and resurrection. This explains [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|John 19:34]]: when the soldier pierced Jesus' side, "blood and water" flowed out. The blood (sacrifice producing the "ashes") and water (Spirit) that were separate in Numbers 19 flow together from Christ's body. He is both the sacrifice and the Spirit-giver, both the Lamb slain and the fountain opened. What Moses' ritual required as two distinct elements, Christ unites in His person. Believers don't need external ashes mixed with external water; they need Christ's blood applied by the Spirit through faith. The living water flows from Christ, into believers ("rivers from his belly"), cleansing them from death's defilement and transforming them into life-giving springs to others.
