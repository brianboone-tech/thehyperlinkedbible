### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 17|Numbers 19:17-18]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4325|H4325]] מַיִם (*mayim*) - "water"
- [[Lexicon/H2416|H2416]] חַיִּים (*ḥayyîm*) - "living, alive" (from חָיָה *ḥayah*, "to live")
- [[Lexicon/H6083|H6083]] עָפָר (*aphar*) - "ashes, dust"
- [[Lexicon/H5079|H5079]] נִדָּה (*niddah*) - "impurity, uncleanness, separation"
- [[Lexicon/H5144|H5144]] נָזָה (*nazah*) - "to sprinkle"
- [[Lexicon/H231|H231]] אֵזוֹב (*ezov*) - "hyssop"

**Context**: Numbers 19 prescribes the unique ritual of the red heifer, whose ashes, when mixed with "living water" (spring water), created the water of purification (מֵי נִדָּה, *mê niddâ*) for cleansing from corpse defilement. Verses 17-18 specify the preparation and application: ashes placed in a vessel, fresh (living) water poured over them, then sprinkled with hyssop on the defiled person or object. This was the only remedy for death-contamination under the Mosaic law.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25-27]] transforms this ritual language into new covenant promise: "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you will be clean... I will put my Spirit in you." The prophetic trajectory moves from external ceremonial cleansing to internal spiritual regeneration, while retaining the sprinkling motif.
- The requirement for "living water" (מַיִם חַיִּים) establishes terminology that later prophets employ eschatologically ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 14#Zechariah 14 . 8|Zechariah 14:8]]: "living waters shall flow out from Jerusalem").

**Type Classification**: **Forward-looking** (the ritual contains textual indicators pointing forward: it is explicitly called "for purification from sin," Numbers 19:9, indicating incompleteness; the paradox of the cleanser becoming unclean signals need for a better mediator) **Direct Type** (divinely commanded ceremonial institution, Numbers 19:1-2).

**Christological Connection**: The water of purification required two elements working in combination: (1) ashes from the red heifer sacrifice, representing Christ's completed atoning work—His once-for-all death that dealt with sin definitively ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13-14]]); (2) living water, representing the Holy Spirit whom Christ sends to apply His finished work to believers ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 38|John 7:38-39]]). Just as neither ashes alone nor water alone could purify, so neither Christ's objective atonement apart from the Spirit's subjective application, nor the Spirit's work apart from Christ's sacrifice, can cleanse the sinner. The type teaches that salvation requires both Christ's blood (producing the "ashes" of His completed offering) and the Spirit (the "living water" that applies it). [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 34|John 19:34]] beautifully fulfills this: from Christ's pierced side flowed both blood and water—the dual elements of purification united in His crucified body. What Numbers 19 separated (sacrifice producing ashes, then water added later), Christ unites in His person and work. He is both the sacrifice and the source of living water, both the Lamb slain and the Spirit-giver, both our justification and sanctification.
