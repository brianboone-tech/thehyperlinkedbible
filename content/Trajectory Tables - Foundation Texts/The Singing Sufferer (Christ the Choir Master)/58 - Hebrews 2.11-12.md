### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 11|Hebrews 2:11-12]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- ἁγιάζω (*hagiazo*) - "sanctify, make holy" (v.11)
- ἀδελφός (*adelphos*) - "brother" (v.11, 12)
- ἐκκλησία (*ekklesia*) - "congregation/assembly/church" (v.12)
- ὑμνέω (*hymneo*) - "sing hymns, sing praise" (v.12)
- ἀπαγγέλλω (*apangello*) - "declare, announce" (v.12)

**Context**: The author of Hebrews argues that Jesus shared our humanity so He could be a merciful high priest. To prove Jesus' solidarity with believers, he quotes Psalm 22:22, putting the words in Jesus' mouth—"I will declare your name to my brothers." This is the clearest NT identification of Christ as the singer of the Psalms.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Quotes Psalm 22:22 (LXX Psalm 21:23) with ἐκκλησία for קָהָל
- The author interprets the psalm christologically: Jesus IS the speaker
- The "brothers" He addresses are "those who are being sanctified" (v.11)
- This hermeneutical move reveals how early Christians read the Psalter

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Hebrews 2:13 (continues quoting—Isaiah 8:17-18); Hebrews 13:15 (sacrifice of praise)
- **FROM OT**: Psalm 22:22 (direct quotation); Psalm 18:49 (similar praise among brothers)
- **FROM NT**: Romans 15:9 (Christ leading Gentile praise)

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking | Direct

**Christological Connection**: This is the trajectory's interpretive key. Hebrews 2:12 explicitly identifies Jesus as the one who speaks Psalm 22:22. Christ declares God's name to His brothers and sings praise (ὑμνέω) in the midst of the congregation (ἐκκλησία). The suffering One (Psalm 22:1-21) is the same as the singing One (Psalm 22:22-31), and that One is Jesus. He is both the subject of the psalms and their singer—the choir master of the redeemed.
