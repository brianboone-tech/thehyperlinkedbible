### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 1|Psalm 22:1-21]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- עֲזַבְתָּנִי (*azabtani*) - "you have forsaken me" (v.1)
- אֵלִי אֵלִי (*eli eli*) - "my God, my God" (v.1)
- כָּארִי (*ka'ari*) - "like a lion" (v.13, 16, 21)
- כָּרוּ (*karu*) - "they have pierced" (v.16)

**Context**: A psalm of David expressing profound suffering—physical, emotional, and spiritual. The first half (vv.1-21) intensifies from cry to complaint to detailed suffering imagery, reaching its nadir before the remarkable pivot at v.22.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Draws on the lament tradition (Psalm 13, etc.) but with unprecedented intensity
- Imagery of bulls, lions, dogs (vv.12-13, 16, 20-21) evokes cosmic conflict
- Physical details (pierced hands/feet, bones out of joint, garments divided) exceed typical lament
- The "Why?" of v.1 echoes throughout Scripture's theodicy tradition

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Psalm 22:22-31 (resolution), Isaiah 53 (Suffering Servant)
- **FROM OT**: Builds on Psalm 13's pattern; echoed in Psalm 69, 88
- **FROM NT**: Matthew 27:46 (Jesus quotes v.1); John 19:24 (garments); Hebrews 2:12 (v.22)

**Type Classification**: Forward-looking | Providential

**Christological Connection**: Jesus quotes this psalm's opening from the cross (Matt 27:46), identifying Himself as its speaker. The vivid crucifixion imagery (pierced hands/feet, garments divided, surrounded by mockers) finds literal fulfillment in Christ's passion. This is not merely a psalm Jesus appropriates but one He authored through David and now fulfills personally.
