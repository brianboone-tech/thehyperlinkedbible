### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]]

**Hebrew/Greek Key Terms**:
- Ἠλὶ Ἠλὶ (*Eli Eli*) - Aramaic/Hebrew "My God, My God" (transliteration)
- λεμὰ σαβαχθάνι (*lema sabachthani*) - Aramaic "why have you forsaken me"
- ἐγκατέλιπες (*enkatelipes*) - Greek "you have forsaken" (from ἐγκαταλείπω)

**Context**: Jesus' cry from the cross, occurring at the ninth hour after three hours of darkness. The only words spoken in Aramaic that Matthew preserves with transliteration, emphasizing their significance. This is the fourth of Jesus' seven last words and the opening line of Psalm 22.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Jesus quotes Psalm 22:1 verbatim (in Aramaic)
- In Jewish practice, quoting a psalm's opening evoked the entire psalm
- Thus Jesus' cry of forsakenness implicitly includes the psalm's resolution of praise
- The cry signals identification with the righteous sufferer tradition

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Hebrews 2:12 (reveals Jesus as singer of Psalm 22's praise)
- **FROM OT**: Psalm 22:1 (direct quotation); Psalm 13:1 (pattern of lament)
- **FROM NT**: Mark 15:34 (parallel account); Hebrews 5:7 (strong cries and tears)

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking | Providential

**Christological Connection**: By quoting Psalm 22:1, Jesus identifies Himself as the psalm's true speaker—not merely one who appropriates ancient words, but the one for whom the psalm was written. The cry of forsakenness is real (He bears the sin that separates from God), yet it begins a psalm that ends in triumph. The singing sufferer experiences ultimate suffering, but His cry initiates ultimate praise.
