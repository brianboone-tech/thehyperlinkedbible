### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7651|H7651]] שֶׁבַע (*sheḇaʿ*) - "seven"
- [[Lexicon/H7657|H7657]] שִׁבְעִים (*shiḇʿîm*) - "seventy"
- [[Lexicon/H5358|H5358]] נָקַם (*nāqam*) - "to avenge"
- [[Lexicon/H2026|H2026]] הָרַג (*hāraḡ*) - "to kill, slay"
- [[Lexicon/H6482|H6482]] פֶּצַע (*peṣaʿ*) - "wound"
- [[Lexicon/H2250|H2250]] חַבּוּרָה (*ḥabbûrâ*) - "stripe, blow"

**Context**: Lamech, seventh from Adam through Cain's line, composes humanity's first recorded poem—a boast of violence. He addresses his two wives (the first recorded polygamist): "Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, listen to my speech. For I have slain a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me. If Cain is avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold." The song reveals three escalations: (1) Cain killed in jealousy; Lamech kills for mere insult. (2) Cain received divine protection; Lamech claims self-protection. (3) Cain's vengeance was 7x; Lamech demands 77x (or 11x7).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 15|Genesis 4:15]]** established God's sevenfold vengeance protecting Cain—divine mercy restraining blood-feud.
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]** perverts this into human boast—Lamech claims greater vengeance than God granted Cain.
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 11|Genesis 6:11-13]]** describes the earth "filled with violence" before the flood—the Lamechian ethic's inevitable fruit.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 4:15 (Cain's protection), Genesis 6:11 (violence filling earth)
- **TO NT**: Matthew 18:21-22 (Christ's deliberate inversion), Matthew 5:38-42 (non-retaliation)

**Type Classification**: Backward-Looking (negative type recognized from NT vantage as what Christ reverses)

**Christological Connection**: Lamech's song is the antithesis of Christ's teaching. Christ's "seventy times seven" (Matt 18:22) deliberately inverts Lamech's "seventy-sevenfold" (Gen 4:24). The verbal parallel is unmistakable—Jesus consciously reverses the Cainite mathematics. Where Lamech demanded escalating vengeance for wounds received, Christ commands unlimited forgiveness for sins committed against us. The first Adam's descendant Lamech boasts of killing; the last Adam forgives those who kill Him.
