### [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 13|Colossians 3:13]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G430|G430]] ἀνέχομαι (*anechomai*) - "to bear with, endure"
- [[Lexicon/G5483|G5483]] χαρίζομαι (*charizomai*) - "to forgive, give freely" (related to χάρις, grace)
- [[Lexicon/G3437|G3437]] μομφή (*momphē*) - "complaint, grievance"
- [[Lexicon/G2532|G2532]] καθώς (*kathōs*) - "just as, even as"
- [[Lexicon/G2962|G2962]] κύριος (*kyrios*) - "Lord"

**Context**: Colossians 3:12-17 describes the "new self" that believers have "put on" in Christ. Verse 13 commands: "Bear with one another and forgive each other if anyone has a grievance against another. Just as the Lord forgave you, so you also must forgive." The ground of Christian forgiveness is not human magnanimity but divine precedent—we forgive because we have been forgiven. The verb χαρίζομαι (to forgive) is related to χάρις (grace)—forgiveness is grace-giving.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]** shows the Lamechian pattern: grievance → escalated retaliation.
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 21#Exodus 21 . 23|Exodus 21:23-25]]** limited retaliation to proportional response.
- **[[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 19#Proverbs 19 . 11|Proverbs 19:11]]** teaches: "A man's wisdom gives him patience; it is to his glory to overlook an offense."
- Paul grounds forgiveness in Christ's prior forgiveness—transforming human relationships through divine grace.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 4:23-24 (pattern reversed), Proverbs 19:11 (overlooking offense as wisdom)
- **PARALLEL**: Ephesians 4:32 (virtually identical formulation)
- **NT CONTEXT**: Matthew 18:21-35 (parable grounds forgiveness in being forgiven)

**Type Classification**: Backward-Looking (Lamechian grievance-response pattern is what Christ reverses)

**Christological Connection**: The ground of Christian forgiveness directly inverts the Lamechian pattern: (1) **Grievance Acknowledged**: Paul doesn't deny that grievances exist—"if anyone has a grievance against another." Christians are wronged, just as Lamech was (allegedly) wounded. (2) **Response Transformed**: Where Lamech responded to grievance with escalated retaliation, Christians respond with forgiveness modeled on Christ. (3) **Divine Precedent**: "Just as the Lord forgave you" provides the ground and measure. Christ forgave infinitely greater offenses against Him than any human has against another. (4) **Grace-Word**: χαρίζομαι connects forgiveness to grace (χάρις). Forgiveness is not earned or deserved—it is grace-giving that flows from grace-receiving. (5) **Unmerciful Servant**: The parable of [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 18#Matthew 18 . 23|Matthew 18:23-35]] illustrates this logic negatively. The servant forgiven millions who demanded repayment of hundreds embodies the Lamechian spirit in someone who has received grace—and faces severe judgment. Those who have received infinite forgiveness must extend finite forgiveness.
