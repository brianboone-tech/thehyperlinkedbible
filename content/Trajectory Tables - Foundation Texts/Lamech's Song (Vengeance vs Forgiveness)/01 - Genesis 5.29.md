### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 29|Genesis 5:29]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5146|H5146]] נֹחַ (*nōaḥ*) - "Noah" (rest, comfort)
- [[Lexicon/H5162|H5162]] נָחַם (*nāḥam*) - "to comfort, console"
- [[Lexicon/H6093|H6093]] עִצָּבוֹן (*ʿiṣṣāḇôn*) - "toil, painful labor"
- [[Lexicon/H779|H779]] אָרַר (*ʾārar*) - "to curse"
- [[Lexicon/H127|H127]] אֲדָמָה (*ʾăḏāmâ*) - "ground, soil"

**Context**: Genesis 5 records the Sethite genealogy from Adam to Noah—ten generations paralleling the Cainite genealogy of chapter 4. This genealogy includes another Lamech (5:25-31), distinct from Cain's descendant. When this godly Lamech fathers Noah, he says: "This one will comfort us in the labor and painful toil of our hands caused by the ground the LORD has cursed." The two Lamechs represent two humanities: Cainite Lamech boasts of vengeance (4:23-24); Sethite Lamech hopes for redemption from the curse.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17-19]]** pronounced the ground cursed because of Adam's sin, bringing toil and painful labor.
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]** shows Cainite Lamech's response: violence and self-exaltation.
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 29|Genesis 5:29]]** shows Sethite Lamech's response: hope for divine comfort and curse-reversal.
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 21|Genesis 8:21]]** records God's response to Noah's sacrifice: "Never again will I curse the ground because of man."

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 3:17-19 (curse on ground), Genesis 4:23-24 (Cainite Lamech), Genesis 8:21 (curse-reversal)
- **TO NT**: Romans 8:20-22 (creation groaning), Revelation 22:3 (no more curse)

**Type Classification**: Forward-Looking (Sethite Lamech's hope points toward ultimate curse-reversal in Christ)

**Christological Connection**: Sethite Lamech's hope for one who would bring "comfort" from the curse finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Jesus is the true Noah who brings rest: "Come to Me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matt 11:28). Where Cainite Lamech responded to the curse with violence, Christ responds with healing. Where Cainite Lamech escalated vengeance, Christ absorbs violence and forgives. The trajectory from Sethite Lamech's hope to Christ's fulfillment stands in deliberate contrast to the Cainite trajectory from violence to judgment.
