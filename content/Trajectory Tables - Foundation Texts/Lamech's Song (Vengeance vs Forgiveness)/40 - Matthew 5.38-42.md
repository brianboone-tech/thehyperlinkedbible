### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 5#Matthew 5 . 38|Matthew 5:38-42]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3788|G3788]] ὀφθαλμός (*ophthalmos*) - "eye"
- [[Lexicon/G3599|G3599]] ὀδούς (*odous*) - "tooth"
- [[Lexicon/G436|G436]] ἀνθίστημι (*anthistēmi*) - "to resist, oppose"
- [[Lexicon/G4190|G4190]] πονηρός (*ponēros*) - "evil, wicked"
- [[Lexicon/G4600|G4600]] σιαγών (*siagōn*) - "cheek"
- [[Lexicon/G4762|G4762]] στρέφω (*strephō*) - "to turn"

**Context**: Matthew 5:38-42 is the fifth of six "antitheses" in the Sermon on the Mount (5:21-48). Jesus addresses the lex talionis: "You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye and tooth for tooth.' But I tell you, do not resist an evil person. If someone strikes you on the right cheek, turn to him the other also." Jesus does not abolish the OT law's justice but transcends it with kingdom ethics. His followers absorb wrong rather than retaliate—breaking the cycle of violence that began with Cain and climaxed in Lamech.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- **[[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 23|Genesis 4:23-24]]** shows Lamechian vengeance: killing for a wound, 77x for 7x.
- **[[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 21#Exodus 21 . 24|Exodus 21:24]]** limits vengeance to proportional response: eye for eye, tooth for tooth.
- **[[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 32#Deuteronomy 32 . 35|Deuteronomy 32:35]]** reserves ultimate vengeance to God alone.
- Jesus transcends all these: His followers do not resist evil persons at all but absorb wrong and trust God.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Exodus 21:24 (lex talionis—directly quoted), Leviticus 24:20 (parallel formulation), Genesis 4:23-24 (Lamechian excess)
- **FROM MATTHEW**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 18#Matthew 18 . 21|Matthew 18:21-22]] (seventy times seven forgiveness)
- **NT PARALLELS**: Romans 12:17-21 (overcome evil with good), 1 Peter 2:23 (Christ's example)

**Type Classification**: Forward-Looking (lex talionis was a middle term pointing toward kingdom transcendence)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus' teaching embodies what He would demonstrate at the cross. Several connections emerge: (1) **Personal Example**: "When He was reviled, He did not revile in return; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but committed Himself to the One who judges righteously" (1 Pet 2:23). (2) **Breaking the Cycle**: From Cain's murder to Lamech's boast, the cycle of violence escalated. Christ breaks it by absorbing violence without retaliation. (3) **Trust in God**: Non-retaliation is not passivity but trust—God will judge (Deut 32:35; Rom 12:19). (4) **Redemptive Suffering**: Jesus' wounds bring healing (1 Pet 2:24); His death secures forgiveness. Lamech killed for a wound; Christ was wounded for our healing. (5) **New Humanity**: The Lamechian ethic characterized the "old Adam"; Christ inaugurates new humanity marked by self-giving love rather than self-protecting vengeance.
