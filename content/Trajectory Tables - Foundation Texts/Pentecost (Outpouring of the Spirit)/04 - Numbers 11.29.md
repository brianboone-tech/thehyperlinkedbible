### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 29|Numbers 11:29]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5030|H5030]] נָבִיא (*nāḇîʾ*) - prophet
- [[Lexicon/H7307|H7307]] רוּחַ (*rûaḥ*) - spirit
- [[Lexicon/H5414|H5414]] נָתַן (*nāṯan*) - to give, put

**Context**: "But Moses said to him, 'Are you jealous for my sake? Would that all the LORD's people were prophets, that the LORD would put his Spirit on them!'" Moses responds to Joshua's concern about Eldad and Medad prophesying in the camp. Rather than viewing the Spirit's work as a threat, Moses expresses a wish that anticipates Pentecost.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 31#Exodus 31 . 3|Exodus 31:3]] - Spirit given to Bezalel for craftsmanship
- [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 17|Numbers 11:17]], 25 - Spirit on Moses shared with seventy elders

**Connections**:

**Type Classification**: Providential Type - Forward-looking (Moses's wish anticipates Pentecost)

**Christological Connection**: Moses's wish in [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 29|Numbers 11:29]] is the seedbed of Pentecost theology. When the Spirit came upon the seventy elders, they prophesied ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 11#Numbers 11 . 25|Numbers 11:25]]), but two men (Eldad and Medad) prophesied in the camp rather than at the tabernacle. Joshua, jealous for Moses's authority, wanted them stopped. Moses's response is remarkable: rather than protecting his unique status, he longs for the Spirit to be given to ALL God's people. "Would that all the LORD's people were prophets!" This wish finds its fulfillment at Pentecost when the Spirit is poured out on "all flesh"—sons and daughters, young and old, servants male and female. What was exceptional in Moses's day (Spirit on select individuals) becomes normative in the new covenant. Moses mediated a limited Spirit; Christ mediates the universal Spirit. The Prophet like Moses ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 18#Deuteronomy 18 . 15|Deuteronomy 18:15]]) fulfills what Moses could only wish for.
