### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 23#Leviticus 23 . 15|Leviticus 23:15-22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- שָׁבֻעוֹת (šāḇuʿôṯ) - weeks (plural of שָׁבוּעַ, šāḇûaʿ, "week")
- חֲמִשִּׁים יוֹם (ḥămiššîm yôm) - fifty days
- מִנְחָה חֲדָשָׁה (minḥâ ḥăḏāšâ) - new grain offering
- לֶחֶם (leḥem) - bread, loaf
- תְּנוּפָה (tənûp̄â) - wave offering
- חָמֵץ (ḥāmēṣ) - leavened, with leaven
- בִּכּוּרִים (bikkûrîm) - firstfruits
- מִקְרָא־קֹדֶשׁ (miqrāʾ-qōḏeš) - holy convocation

**Context**: Leviticus 23:15-22 prescribes the Feast of Weeks (Pentecost), counted as fifty days from the Feast of First Fruits. This feast marks the completion of the spring harvest cycle (barley at First Fruits, wheat at Pentecost) and is one of Israel's three pilgrimage feasts requiring all males to appear before the LORD in Jerusalem (Exodus 23:14-17).

**Christological Connection**: The Feast of Weeks finds ultimate fulfillment in the outpouring of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost (Acts 2). The timing is exact: Christ rose on First Fruits (initiating the harvest), and the Spirit descended on Pentecost fifty days later (gathering the harvest). The two leavened loaves prefigure the church composed of Jew and Gentile—both imperfect, both included. Peter's sermon at Pentecost ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 14|Acts 2:14-41]]) resulted in 3,000 baptized—the firstfruits of the gospel harvest. This reverses Sinai where 3,000 died under the Law ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 32#Exodus 32 . 28|Exodus 32:28]]). Paul contrasts the covenants: "The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 3#2 Corinthians 3 . 6|2 Corinthians 3:6]]). The Law revealed sin; the Spirit transforms hearts. The feast pointed to the Spirit's work: gathering a multinational people, writing the Law on hearts ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 33|Jeremiah 31:33]]), creating one body from many nations.

**Application**: Pentecost teaches that the harvest is not complete until the Spirit gathers God's people. Christ's resurrection (First Fruits) initiated the harvest; the Spirit's outpouring (Pentecost) gathers it. Are you part of the harvest? Have you received the Spirit? Acts 2:38: "Repent and be baptized...and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit." The Spirit is given to all who trust Christ. The two leavened loaves also teach that the church includes imperfect people from diverse backgrounds—Jew and Gentile, slave and free, male and female (Galatians 3:28). Unity comes not from uniformity but from the one Spirit (1 Corinthians 12:13). Finally, Pentecost reminds us to care for the poor (v. 22). Those who celebrate God's harvest must share His provision. True worship includes generosity.
