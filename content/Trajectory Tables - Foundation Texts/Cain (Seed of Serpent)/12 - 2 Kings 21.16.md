### [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 21#2 Kings 21 . 16|2 Kings 21:16]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1818|H1818]] דָּם (dām) - "blood" - innocent blood
- [[Lexicon/H5355|H5355]] נָקִי (nāqî) - "innocent" - blood of the innocent
- [[Lexicon/H8210|H8210]] שָׁפַךְ (šāp̄aḵ) - "pour out/shed" - Manasseh shed blood
- [[Lexicon/H3966|H3966]] מְאֹד (mᵉʾōḏ) - "very/exceedingly" - very much blood
- [[Lexicon/H4390|H4390]] מָלֵא (mālēʾ) - "fill" - filled Jerusalem with blood

**Context**: Manasseh, Judah's most wicked king, is described as having "shed very much innocent blood, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another." This verse comes amid a catalog of Manasseh's sins: idolatry, child sacrifice, sorcery, and the persecution of the righteous. Tradition held that Manasseh killed the prophet Isaiah.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Manasseh continues the Cainite pattern of shedding innocent blood
- [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 24#2 Chronicles 24 . 21|2 Chronicles 24:21]] records Zechariah's murder in the temple court
- The prophets frequently indict Israel and Judah for bloodshed (Jer 2:34; Ezek 22:3-4)

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking (the pattern is recognized as continuing the Cainite trajectory)

**Christological Connection**: Manasseh "filled Jerusalem" with innocent blood; Jesus would shed His own innocent blood in that same city. Where Manasseh killed prophets, Jesus was killed as "a prophet mighty in deed and word" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 19|Luke 24:19]]). Yet Christ's blood does not cry for vengeance but offers forgiveness. The city that killed the prophets becomes the place where "repentance and forgiveness of sins should be proclaimed" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 47|Luke 24:47]]). Christ absorbs the blood-guilt that accumulated from Abel onward.
