### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 3|Genesis 4:3-7]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4503|H4503]] מִנְחָה (minḥâ) - "offering/gift" - both brothers bring offerings
- [[Lexicon/H8159|H8159]] שָׁעָה (šāʿâ) - "regard/look upon" - God's favorable regard toward Abel
- [[Lexicon/H7257|H7257]] רָבַץ (rābaṣ) - "crouch/lie in wait" - sin crouching at the door
- [[Lexicon/H2403|H2403]] חַטָּאת (ḥaṭṭāʾt) - "sin" - personified as a predator

**Context**: Cain and Abel both bring offerings to the LORD. Abel brings "the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions," while Cain brings "an offering of the fruit of the ground." God regards Abel's offering but not Cain's. When Cain becomes angry, God warns him that sin is "crouching at the door" with desire for him, but he must "rule over it."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The contrast between acceptable and unacceptable worship continues throughout Scripture
- Leviticus codifies proper offerings; prophets condemn worship without faith (Isa 1:11-17; [[Readable Bible/30 - Amos/Amos 5#Amos 5 . 21|Amos 5:21-24]])
- The principle that God looks at the heart, not mere externals, runs from Samuel (1 Sam 16:7) to Malachi (Mal 1:10)

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking (the typological significance of Cain's failed worship is clarified by Hebrews 11:4)

**Christological Connection**: Cain's faithless worship stands in contrast to Christ, who perfectly pleased the Father. Where Cain approached God on his own terms and was rejected, Christ offered Himself as the perfect sacrifice "through the eternal Spirit" (Heb 9:14). The "way of Cain" in worship—human effort divorced from faith—finds its antithesis in worship "in spirit and truth" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 4#John 4 . 24|John 4:24]]) made possible through Christ. Those who follow Christ worship through His mediation, not in Cain's presumption.
