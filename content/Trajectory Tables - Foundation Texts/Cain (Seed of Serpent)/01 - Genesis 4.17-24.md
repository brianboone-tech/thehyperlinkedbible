### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 17|Genesis 4:17-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5892|H5892]] עִיר (ʿîr) - "city" - Cain builds the first city
- [[Lexicon/H168|H168]] אֹהֶל (ʾōhel) - "tent" - Jabal, father of tent-dwellers
- [[Lexicon/H3658|H3658]] כִּנּוֹר (kinnôr) - "harp/lyre" - Jubal, father of musicians
- [[Lexicon/H5178|H5178]] נְחֹשֶׁת (nᵉḥōšeṯ) - "bronze" - Tubal-cain's metalwork
- [[Lexicon/H5358|H5358]] נָקַם (nāqam) - "avenge" - Lamech's seventy-sevenfold vengeance

**Context**: After Cain's curse, his descendants develop impressive civilization. Cain builds a city named after his son Enoch. His descendants include Jabal (father of nomadic herdsmen), Jubal (father of musicians), and Tubal-cain (forger of bronze and iron tools). Yet this cultural progress culminates in Lamech's violent boast: he kills a man for wounding him and claims seventy-sevenfold vengeance—exceeding even God's protection of Cain.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The pattern of impressive civilization divorced from God continues in Babel (Gen 11:1-9)
- The prophets critique nations that trust in their achievements apart from God (Isa 2:7-8; Ezek 28)
- The contrast between worldly wisdom and godly wisdom develops in Proverbs and Ecclesiastes

**Type Classification**: Backward-looking (the pattern of godless civilization is recognized typologically from NT vantage)

**Christological Connection**: The "way of Cain" produces culture without covenant, progress without piety. Christ establishes a different kingdom—"not of this world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 18#John 18 . 36|John 18:36]])—characterized not by worldly power but by the cross. Where Lamech boasts of vengeance, Christ teaches forgiveness "seventy times seven" (Matt 18:22)—directly reversing Lamech's seventy-sevenfold boast. The city of God (Heb 12:22; Rev 21) stands in eternal contrast to the city of Cain.
