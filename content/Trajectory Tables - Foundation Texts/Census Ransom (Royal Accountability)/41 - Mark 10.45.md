### [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 10#Mark 10 . 45|Mark 10:45]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3083|G3083]] λύτρον (lytron) - ransom, redemption price
- [[Lexicon/G473|G473]] ἀντί (anti) - in place of, instead of
- [[Lexicon/G4183|G4183]] πολύς (polys) - many

**Context**: Jesus declares the purpose of His coming: "to give his life as a ransom for many." This climactic statement occurs in the context of teaching servant-leadership, contrasting worldly power with sacrificial service. The ransom language directly fulfills the census ransom trajectory.

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 11|Exodus 30:11-16]] - census ransom law
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 10|Isaiah 53:10-12]] - Suffering Servant bearing sin of many
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 24#2 Samuel 24 . 1|2 Samuel 24:1-17]] - David's failure to require ransom
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/54 - 1 Timothy/1 Timothy 2#1 Timothy 2 . 5|1 Timothy 2:5-6]] - ransom for all
  - [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 18|1 Peter 1:18-19]] - ransomed by precious blood
**Type Classification**: Fulfillment text

**Christological Connection**: Christ is both the ransom price and the one who pays it. Where the half-shekel ransom acknowledged that Israel's lives belonged to God, Christ pays the ultimate ransom—His own life—to redeem God's people from bondage to sin and death. The Greek λύτρον corresponds to the Hebrew כֹּפֶר (kopher) of [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 12|Exodus 30:12]].
