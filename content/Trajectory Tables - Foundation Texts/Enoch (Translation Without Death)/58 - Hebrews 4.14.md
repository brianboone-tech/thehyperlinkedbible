### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 14|Hebrews 4:14]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G749|G749]] ἀρχιερέα (archierea) - "high priest" - Christ's priestly office
- [[Lexicon/G3173|G3173]] μέγαν (megan) - "great" - surpassing all others
- [[Lexicon/G1330|G1330]] διεληλυθότα (dielelythota) - "passed through" (perfect active participle) - completed transit
- [[Lexicon/G3772|G3772]] οὐρανούς (ouranous) - "heavens" (plural) - multiple heavenly spheres
- [[Lexicon/G5207|G5207]] υἱὸν τοῦ θεοῦ (huion tou theou) - "Son of God" - divine identity

**Context**: Hebrews develops Christ's superiority over all previous mediators. Having established His superiority to angels (chs. 1-2) and Moses (ch. 3), the author now presents Him as the great High Priest who has "passed through the heavens"—a spatial metaphor for His completed ascension and heavenly session.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- High priests entered the earthly tabernacle/temple once yearly (Lev 16)
- The tabernacle was patterned after heavenly reality (Exod 25:40; Heb 8:5)
- Christ enters not an earthly copy but heaven itself (Heb 9:24)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Enoch and Elijah bypassed death to enter heaven; Christ passed through heavens as victorious High Priest
- **FROM OT**:
  - Levitical high priestly entrance (Lev 16); Melchizedek priesthood (Gen 14; Ps 110:4)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 11|Hebrews 9:11-12]] - entered greater tabernacle
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 4#Ephesians 4 . 10|Ephesians 4:10]] - ascended above all heavens
**Type Classification**: NT Superiority - The antitype exceeds the type

**Christological Connection**: Where Enoch was taken and Elijah was carried up, Christ "passed through" by His own power and priestly right. The difference is crucial: Enoch and Elijah received grace; Christ achieved redemption. They were witnesses to God's power over death; He is the conqueror of death itself. His ascension is not merely translation from earth to heaven but enthronement as priest-king at God's right hand, where He intercedes for all who come to God through Him.
