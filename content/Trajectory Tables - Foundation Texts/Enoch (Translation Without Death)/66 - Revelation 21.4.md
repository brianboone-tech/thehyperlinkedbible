### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 4|Revelation 21:4]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1813|G1813]] ἐξαλείψει (exaleipsei) - "will wipe away" - complete removal
- [[Lexicon/G1144|G1144]] δάκρυον (dakruon) - "tear" - symbol of sorrow
- [[Lexicon/G2288|G2288]] θάνατος (thanatos) - "death" - the last enemy abolished
- [[Lexicon/G3996|G3996]] πένθος (penthos) - "mourning" - grief/lamentation
- [[Lexicon/G4215|G4215]] πρῶτα (prōta) - "former things" - the old order

**Context**: Revelation 21-22 presents the new creation where God dwells with His people in the New Jerusalem. Death's abolition (21:4) and restored access to the tree of life (22:14) complete the trajectory begun with Enoch's translation. What was exceptional (Enoch) and promised (believers' rapture) becomes permanent reality.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 22|Genesis 3:22-24]] - Banishment from tree of life
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 8|Isaiah 25:8]] - "He will swallow up death forever... wipe away tears from all faces"
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 65#Isaiah 65 . 17|Isaiah 65:17-19]] - New heavens and earth; no more weeping

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Enoch's bypassing death (Gen 5:24); Tree of life lost (Gen 3:24)
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 25#Isaiah 25 . 8|Isaiah 25:8]] - ; 65:17-19 - promises of death's defeat and new creation
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 26|1 Corinthians 15:26]] - , 54-55 - death as last enemy destroyed
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 2|Revelation 22:2]] - , 14 - tree of life restored
**Type Classification**: Eschatological Consummation - The ultimate fulfillment

**Christological Connection**: Fairbairn's observation proves true: "The far distant ends of revelation embrace each other." Enoch's translation anticipated the defeat of death; Christ accomplished it; the new creation manifests it permanently. The tree of life, guarded since Eden's fall, is now freely accessible because the second Adam has undone what the first Adam lost. As Fairbairn notes: "The antitype is always higher than the type"—not merely a return to Eden but glorification beyond it, where God Himself is the temple and the Lamb is the light.
