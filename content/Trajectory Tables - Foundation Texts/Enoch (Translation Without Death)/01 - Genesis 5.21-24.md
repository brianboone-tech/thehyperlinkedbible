### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 21|Genesis 5:21-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H2585|H2585]] חֲנוֹךְ (Chanokh) - "Enoch" meaning "dedicated" or "initiated"
- [[Lexicon/H1980|H1980]] הִתְהַלֵּךְ (hithallekh) - "walked" (Hithpael of הָלַךְ) - continuous, habitual walking
- [[Lexicon/H3947|H3947]] לָקַח (laqach) - "took" - God's taking/seizing
- [[Lexicon/H430|H430]] אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) - "God" - the divine name emphasizing power

**Context**: Genesis 5 presents the genealogy from Adam to Noah, structured with the repeated formula: "X lived Y years and became the father of Z... then X died." Enoch breaks this pattern dramatically. While all others "died," Enoch "was not, for God took him." His 365 years (shortest lifespan in the genealogy) contrast with the lengthy lives of his contemporaries, yet his ending is the most glorious.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/13 - 1 Chronicles/1 Chronicles 1#1 Chronicles 1 . 3|1 Chronicles 1:3]] preserves Enoch in the genealogical record without comment
- Sirach 44:16 (LXX tradition): "Enoch pleased God and was translated, an example of repentance to generations"
- The unique phrase "walked with God" appears elsewhere only of Noah (Gen 6:9), linking these two pre-flood saints

**Connections**:
- **TO**: The command to Abraham to "walk before me" (Gen 17:1) uses similar language
- **FROM OT**: Noah "walked with God" (Gen 6:9) echoes Enoch's description
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 5|Hebrews 11:5]] interprets "walked with God" as "pleased God" and attributes the translation to faith

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged), Forward-looking (points to resurrection/ascension hope)

**Christological Connection**: Enoch's translation without death typifies Christ's ascension and the believer's hope of glorification. Owen observes: "Herein God gave us a pledge and assurance that the body itself hath a capacity of eternal blessedness in heaven." What Enoch experienced as an exception, Christ made possible for all who believe. The one who "walked with God" anticipates the One who is God walking among us, then ascending to the Father's right hand.
