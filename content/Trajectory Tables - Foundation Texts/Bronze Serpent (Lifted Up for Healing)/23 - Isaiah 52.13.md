### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 13|Isaiah 52:13]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7701-7800#H7919|יַשְׂכִּיל]] (*yaśkîl*) - "will act wisely, prosper" (Hiphil)
- [[Lexicon/H5701-5800#H5650|עַבְדִּי]] (*ʿabdî*) - "my servant"
- [[Lexicon/H7301-7400#H7311|יָרוּם]] (*yārûm*) - "he will be high, exalted"
- [[Lexicon/H5301-5400#H5375|וְנִשָּׂא]] (*wənissāʾ*) - "and lifted up, raised"
- [[Lexicon/H1401-1500#H1361|וְגָבַהּ]] (*wəgābah*) - "and be high, lofty"
- [[Lexicon/H3901-4000#H3966|מְאֹד]] (*məʾōd*) - "very, exceedingly"

**Context**: Isaiah prophesies that the Suffering Servant will be "raised and lifted up and highly exalted"—using the same verb (רוּם) that describes the bronze serpent being "lifted up" (Numbers 21:8-9). This "lifting up" has dual meaning: exaltation through suffering, glory through shame. The Servant who is "pierced for our transgressions" (Isaiah 53:5) fulfills the bronze serpent pattern on a cosmic scale.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 8|Numbers 21:8]] (set serpent on pole/standard), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 6|Psalm 22:6-8]] (despised suffering one)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 5|Isaiah 53:5]] (pierced for transgressions), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 12|Isaiah 53:12]] (numbered with transgressors, bore sin of many)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14]] (Son of Man must be lifted up), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 32|John 12:32-34]] (when I am lifted up), [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9]] (God highly exalted him)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 13|Isaiah 52:13]]'s "lifted up" language directly connects the bronze serpent typology to Christ's crucifixion. The same Hebrew verb rûm ("lift up") appears when God commands Moses to "lift up" the bronze serpent on a pole ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 8|Numbers 21:8]]). Jesus makes this connection explicit: "as Moses lifted up (ὕψωσεν, *hypsōsen*) the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up (ὑψωθῆναι, *hypsōthēnai*)" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14]]). [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 32|John 12:32-34]] clarifies that "lifted up" means crucifixion: "when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself.' He said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die." The bronze serpent was physically elevated on a pole for dying Israelites to see; Christ was physically elevated on the cross for dying sinners to believe. But "lifted up" carries double meaning—both crucifixion and exaltation. [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 9|Philippians 2:9]] declares "God has highly exalted (ὑπερύψωσεν, *hyperupsōsen*) him," using the same root. The trajectory moves from humiliation (cross) to exaltation (resurrection/ascension). What the bronze serpent foreshadowed (lifted remedy for deadly poison), Isaiah's Servant fulfilled (lifted Savior bearing sin's penalty). The threefold intensification—"high, lifted up, highly exalted"—captures Christ's complete vindication: crucified in weakness, raised in power, seated at God's right hand. The bronze serpent healed temporarily; Christ saves eternally. Both required being lifted up for all to see, but Christ's lifting accomplishes what the bronze serpent only symbolized.
