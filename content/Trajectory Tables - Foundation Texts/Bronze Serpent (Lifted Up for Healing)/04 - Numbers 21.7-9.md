**Context**: The people confess their sin and ask Moses to intercede. God provides a remedy: "Make a fiery serpent and set it on a pole, and everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live" (v. 8). Moses makes a bronze serpent and lifts it up. "If a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" (v. 9). The cure comes through looking to the lifted-up image of judgment.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 7|Numbers 21:7-9]] provides Scripture's clearest Old Testament type of Christ's crucifixion, explicitly confirmed by Jesus Himself in [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14-15]]: "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life." The correspondences are precise and profound. First, the bronze serpent bore the likeness of sin (serpent form—the creature that brought the curse in Genesis 3) but contained no poison. Similarly, Christ was "made to be sin" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]) yet "knew no sin"—He bore sin's form, became sin's substitute, yet remained personally sinless. Second, the serpent was lifted up on a pole for all to see; Christ was lifted up on the cross ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 12#John 12 . 32|John 12:32-33]]: "when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself"). Third, healing came through looking in faith, not through works or merit. The Israelite did nothing but look; the believer does nothing but trust. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 5|Romans 4:5]] echoes this: "to the one who does not work but believes... his faith is counted as righteousness." Fourth, the promise was universal: "everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live." The gospel extends the same universal offer: "whoever believes in him should not perish" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 16|John 3:16]]). Fifth, the bronze serpent healed physical death from serpent venom; Christ heals spiritual death from sin's poison. The bronze serpent could only delay physical death—those healed eventually died. Christ grants eternal life—"whoever believes... may have eternal life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 15|John 3:15]]). Sixth, the remedy's strange nature—looking at the image of judgment for healing—prefigures the cross's scandal: Christ crucified (the image of God's curse, per [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]) becomes the means of blessing. What seemed like weakness and foolishness is "the power of God and the wisdom of God" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 24|1 Corinthians 1:24]]). The bronze serpent incident also teaches the necessity of Christ's death: God commanded the serpent be made and lifted (v. 8); Jesus says the Son of Man "must" be lifted up ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14]])—not optional but divinely ordained. The trajectory moves from Genesis 3 (serpent brings curse) to Numbers 21 (serpent image brings cure) to John 3 (Christ lifted brings salvation) to [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]] ("looking to Jesus"). What Moses lifted in the wilderness prefigured what the Father lifted at Golgotha: the remedy for sin's deadly poison.
