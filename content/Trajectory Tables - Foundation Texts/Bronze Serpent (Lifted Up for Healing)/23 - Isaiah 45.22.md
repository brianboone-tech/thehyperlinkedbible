### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 45#Isaiah 45 . 22|Isaiah 45:22]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6301-6400#H6437|פְּנוּ]] (*pənû*) - "turn, look" (imperative plural)
- [[Lexicon/H3301-3400#H3467|וְהִוָּשְׁעוּ]] (*wəhiwwāšəʿû*) - "and be saved" (Niphal imperative)
- [[Lexicon/H0601-0700#H0657|אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ]] (*ʾapsê-ʾāreṣ*) - "ends of the earth"
- [[Lexicon/H0001-0100#H0410|אֵל]] (*ʾēl*) - "God"
- [[Lexicon/H0001-0100#H0369|וְאֵין עוֹד]] (*wəʾên ʿôd*) - "and there is no other"

**Context**: God's invitation echoes the bronze serpent principle: "Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth! For I am God, and there is no other." Salvation comes through looking to God in faith, not through works or merit. The bronze serpent taught this truth: healing came not through doing but through looking.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 7|Numbers 21:7-9]] (look at bronze serpent and live)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 52#Isaiah 52 . 13|Isaiah 52:13]] (Servant lifted up), [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 12#Zechariah 12 . 10|Zechariah 12:10]] (look on him whom they pierced)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14-16]] (whoever believes has eternal life), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 16#Acts 16 . 31|Acts 16:31]] (believe and be saved), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]] (looking to Jesus)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 45#Isaiah 45 . 22|Isaiah 45:22]] captures the bronze serpent's theological principle: salvation comes through looking to God in faith, not works. The Israelites simply "looked at the bronze serpent and lived" ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 9|Numbers 21:9]]); Isaiah commands "turn to me and be saved." Jesus applies this to Himself: "as Moses lifted up the serpent... so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 14|John 3:14-15]]). The parallel is exact—looking to the lifted serpent becomes believing in the lifted Christ. The verb "turn" (pənû) implies directing one's gaze, precisely what the bronze serpent required. Hebrews echoes this: "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2). The universal scope ("ends of the earth") finds fulfillment in Christ's commission: "you will be my witnesses... to the end of the earth" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 8|Acts 1:8]]). What Isaiah prophesied—all nations turning to God for salvation—Christ accomplishes through His death and resurrection. The exclusivity ("I am God, and there is no other") applies to Christ: "there is salvation in no one else" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 12|Acts 4:12]]). The bronze serpent foreshadowed, Isaiah proclaimed, Christ fulfilled: salvation comes through looking in faith to the one God has lifted up.
