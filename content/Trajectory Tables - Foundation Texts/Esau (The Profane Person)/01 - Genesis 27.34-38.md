### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 27#Genesis 27 . 34|Genesis 27:34-38]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- צְעָקָה (tze'akah) - "cry" (H6818) - [[Lexicon/T6/T6818|outcry, cry for help]]
- גְּדֹלָה (gedolah) - "great" (H1419) - [[Lexicon/G1/G1419|great, large]]
- מָרָה (marah) - "bitter" (H4751) - [[Lexicon/M4/M4751|bitter, distressed]]
- בָּכָה (bakhah) - "wept" (H1058) - [[Lexicon/B1/B1058|to weep, cry]]

**Context**: Esau learns he's lost the blessing. Cries out "with an exceedingly great and bitter cry." Begs father: "Bless me, even me also, O my father!" (27:34). Repeats three times. Isaac can only give secondary blessing. Esau "lifted up his voice and wept" (27:38).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Pattern of weeping without repentance appears elsewhere: Saul wept after Samuel's rebuke but continued in disobedience (1 Sam 15:24-31)
- Contrast with Jacob's weeping at Peniel while wrestling for blessing (Gen 32:24-30; Hos 12:4)—tears of genuine spiritual desire versus tears of regret

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 34|Genesis 25:34]] - despised birthright
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 27#Genesis 27 . 30|Genesis 27:30-33]] - lost blessing
- **FROM OT**:
  - Obadiah 1-21 (Edom's pride and judgment)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 17|Hebrews 12:17]] - "found no place for repentance, though he sought the blessing with tears"
**Type Classification**: Backward-looking Providential Type. Negative type—warning of profane person's fate. Tears without true repentance lead to final loss.

**Christological Connection**: Contrast with Christ, who "offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears" (Heb 5:7) and was heard because of His reverence. Esau's tears were self-centered; Christ's tears were for Father's glory and sinners' salvation. Warning to unbelievers: Many will say "Lord, open to us!" but door will be shut (Matt 25:11-12). Seek Christ while He may be found—not with worldly sorrow, but with godly repentance that values spiritual inheritance above all.
