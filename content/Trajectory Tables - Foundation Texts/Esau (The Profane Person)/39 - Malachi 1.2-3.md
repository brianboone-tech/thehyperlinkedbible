### [[Readable Bible/39 - Malachi/Malachi 1#Malachi 1 . 2|Malachi 1:2-3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- אָהַבְתִּי (ahavti) - "I have loved" (H157) - [[Lexicon/A157|to love, chosen in covenant]]
- שָׂנֵאתִי (saneti) - "I have hated" (H8130) - [[Lexicon/S8/S8130|to hate, reject]]
- שְׁמָמָה (shemamah) - "wasteland, desolation" (H8077) - [[Lexicon/S8/S8077|ruin, devastation]]

**Context**: Post-exilic Israel questions God's love (Mal 1:2a). God answers by pointing to election of Jacob over Esau. Edom (Esau's descendants) lies in ruins; Israel (Jacob's line) restored from exile. Historical vindication of God's elective love/hate.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Malachi interprets [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 23|Genesis 25:23]] and Jacob/Esau narrative as paradigm of covenantal love versus rejection
- "Love" and "hate" are covenant terms—not mere emotion but elective choice and rejection
- Edom's historical judgment (destruction by Nabateans c. 550 BC) proves God's word concerning Esau

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 25#Genesis 25 . 23|Genesis 25:23]] - oracle—"older will serve younger"); Obadiah 1-21 (judgment on Edom
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 7#Deuteronomy 7 . 7|Deuteronomy 7:7-8]] - God's love for Israel based on His choice, not their merit
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 9#Romans 9 . 13|Romans 9:13]] - Paul quotes Malachi to prove unconditional election
**Type Classification**: Backward-looking Providential Type. Malachi interprets historical pattern (Jacob/Esau) as typological of elect/reprobate distinction.

**Christological Connection**: God's elective love reaches fullest expression in Christ. Father's love for Son (Matt 3:17, "This is my beloved Son") extends to all in Christ (Eph 1:6, "accepted in the Beloved"). Esau (rejected) versus Jacob (loved) prefigures reprobates (rejected in Adam) versus elect (loved in Christ). God's hate for Esau demonstrates holy wrath against sin; God's love for Jacob demonstrates sovereign grace triumphing over unworthiness.
