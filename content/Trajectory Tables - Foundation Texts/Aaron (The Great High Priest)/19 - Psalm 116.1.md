### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 116#Psalms 116 . 1|Psalm 116:1]]

**Context**: The psalmist expresses love for God "because he has heard my voice and my pleas for mercy," beginning a thanksgiving psalm for deliverance from death (v. 3: "the snares of death encompassed me"). Hebrews 5:7-9 applies this "prayers of the righteous sufferer" motif to Christ, who "offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence."

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- אָהַב (*ahav*) - "love" (covenant loyalty, devotion)
- שָׁמַע (*shama*) - "hear" (divine response to prayer)
- תַּחֲנוּנִים (*tachnunim*) - "pleas for mercy/supplication" (urgent petition)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 7|Hebrews 5:7-9]] draws on the Psalms' "righteous sufferer heard by God" theme to establish Christ's priestly qualification through suffering. Just as the psalmist loved God because "he heard my voice," so Christ "in the days of his flesh, offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence." This demonstrates Christ's genuine humanity and thus His ability to "sympathize with our weaknesses" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]) as high priest. Aaron could "deal gently with the ignorant and wayward, since he himself is beset with weakness" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 2|Hebrews 5:2]])—his own frailty enabled compassion. Christ achieves superior sympathy through a different route: He experienced human suffering without sinful weakness, learning obedience through suffering (5:8), making Him "the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him" (5:9). The connection to Aaron's trajectory is profound: both priests qualify through identification with those they represent, but where Aaron's weakness required sacrifice for his own sins (5:3), Christ's sinless suffering produced perfect atonement for others. The "loud cries and tears" prove Christ's priesthood wasn't mere formal appointment but was forged in the furnace of actual human anguish, heard by the Father.
