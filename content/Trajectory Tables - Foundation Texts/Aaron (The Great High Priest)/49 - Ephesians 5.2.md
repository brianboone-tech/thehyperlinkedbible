### [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 5#Ephesians 5 . 2|Ephesians 5:2]]

**Context**: Christ's self-offering as a "fragrant offering and sacrifice to God" (ὀσμὴν εὐωδίας) directly echoes the language of Exodus 29:18, where Aaron's consecration offerings produced a pleasing aroma to the Lord. What Aaron offered repeatedly with animal blood, Christ offered once for all with His own blood, fulfilling and ending the Aaronic sacrificial system.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- παρέδωκεν (*paredoken*) - "gave up/delivered" - voluntary self-offering, unlike passive animal victims
- προσφορὰν (*prosphoran*) - "offering" - technical sacrificial term used throughout Leviticus
- θυσίαν (*thysian*) - "sacrifice" - the slaughtered victim, here Christ Himself
- ὀσμὴν εὐωδίας (*osmen euodias*) - "fragrant aroma" - exact LXX phrase from Exodus 29:18; Leviticus 1:9

**Christological Connection**: Paul's use of "fragrant aroma" (ὀσμὴν εὐωδίας) establishes Christ as both the superior priest and the superior sacrifice. In [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 18|Exodus 29:18]], when Aaron was being consecrated for priesthood, the burnt offering produced "a pleasing aroma to the Lord"—but Aaron was not the offering, only the officiant. Christ uniquely combines both roles: He is the great High Priest who offers ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 3|Hebrews 8:3]]) and simultaneously the spotless Lamb who is offered ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]]). The Levitical system required endless repetition because animal blood could never truly remove sin ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 4|Hebrews 10:4]]); Christ's single self-offering accomplished what Aaron's thousands of sacrifices could not—eternal redemption ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]). The fragrance that pleased God temporarily under the old covenant is fulfilled in the infinitely pleasing sacrifice of Christ, whose love-motivated self-offering completely satisfies divine justice and exhausts divine wrath. Where Aaron's offerings pointed forward, Christ's offering looks back to nothing—it is the reality to which all shadows pointed.
