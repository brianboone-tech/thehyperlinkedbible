### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 34|Acts 2:34-35]]

**Context**: Peter at Pentecost argues that Psalm 110:1 applies to Jesus, not David, establishing the foundation for Christ's heavenly priesthood. The enthronement at God's right hand is the position from which Jesus exercises His eternal intercessory ministry as great High Priest, a role Psalm 110:4 explicitly assigns to the one seated there.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- δεξιῶν (*dexion*) - "right hand" - position of completed priestly work and ongoing intercession
- ὑποπόδιον (*hypopodion*) - "footstool" - enemies subjugated under priestly King's authority
- κύριος (*kyrios*) - "Lord" - divine title indicating superior priesthood (Hebrews 7:21)
- εἶπεν (*eipen*) - "said" - the Father's oath establishing the eternal priesthood

**Christological Connection**: Peter's Pentecost sermon establishes the christological and priestly interpretation of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] that becomes foundational for the New Testament's understanding of Christ's work. The apostle argues that David himself did not ascend to heaven, therefore "The Lord said to my Lord, 'Sit at my right hand'" must refer to David's greater Son—Jesus. This enthronement is inseparable from Christ's priesthood, as [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] reveals: "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.'" Where Aaron stood daily offering repeated sacrifices ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]]), Christ sat down—His priestly work of atonement finished ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]). But His session is not retirement; it is the position from which He exercises eternal intercession ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). Peter's citation at Pentecost announces that the Aaronic priesthood has been superseded by a superior priest in a superior position offering superior mediation forever.
