### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11]]

**Context**: The writer references Leviticus 16:27, where the Day of Atonement sacrifices were "burned outside the camp." This finds typological fulfillment when Jesus "suffered outside the gate" (Hebrews 13:12) to sanctify the people—what Aaron performed ritually with animal carcasses, Christ accomplished actually through His crucifixion beyond Jerusalem's walls.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ζῴων (*zoon*) - "animals" - the creatures Aaron sacrificed on Day of Atonement
- αἷμα (*haima*) - "blood" - brought by high priest into Most Holy Place (Leviticus 16:14-15)
- σώματα (*somata*) - "bodies" - burned outside the camp, prefiguring Christ's crucifixion location
- κατακαίεται (*katakaigetai*) - "are burned" - complete consumption, nothing remaining

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11]] unveils profound typology in Leviticus 16's Day of Atonement ritual. Aaron brought the blood of the sin offering into the Most Holy Place to make atonement ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 14|Leviticus 16:14-15]]), but the bodies of those same animals were "burned outside the camp" ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]])—removed entirely from the covenant community, consigned to total destruction beyond the sacred boundary. The writer sees this spatial division—blood inside/body outside—fulfilled in Christ's crucifixion. Jesus' blood entered the true heavenly sanctuary ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]), but His body "suffered outside the gate" of Jerusalem ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]), beyond the walls of the holy city, in the place of cursing and exclusion ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 21#Deuteronomy 21 . 23|Deuteronomy 21:23]]; [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]). What was geographic necessity under Aaron's ministry (bodies burned outside the camp because of ceremonial defilement) becomes theological necessity in Christ's ministry—He bore sin's curse in the place of abandonment, "outside the gate," to "sanctify the people through his own blood" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]). The trajectory escalates from ritual disposal to redemptive suffering, from animal carcasses to the incarnate Son, from temporary cleansing to eternal sanctification. Aaron's annual ceremony pointed to Calvary's unrepeatable sacrifice.
