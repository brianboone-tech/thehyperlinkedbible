### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 2#Psalms 2 . 7|Psalm 2:7]]

**Context**: The Davidic king quotes the divine decree: "You are my Son; today I have begotten you," establishing royal Sonship as the basis of messianic authority. Hebrews 5:5 applies this text to Christ's priestly appointment, demonstrating that legitimate priesthood requires divine calling, not human ambition. Just as Aaron "did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him" (quoting this verse), so Christ's priesthood rests on His unique Sonship declared by God.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- בֵּן (*ben*) - "son" (filial relationship, covenant heir)
- יְלִדְתִּיךָ (*yelidtikha*) - "I have begotten you" (divine generation/appointment)
- חֹק (*choq*) - "decree" (divine statute, unchangeable word)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 5|Hebrews 5:5]] quotes [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 2#Psalm 2 . 7|Psalm 2:7]] to establish that Christ's priesthood flows from His Sonship—He "did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him, 'You are my Son, today I have begotten you.'" This connects divine Sonship to priestly qualification in a way impossible for Aaron. Aaron was appointed as a tribal representative, one among brothers; Christ was appointed as the unique Son, the radiance of God's glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]). Aaron's appointment was through Moses, a human mediator; Christ's appointment was by direct divine decree. Aaron's legitimacy rested on genealogy (tribe of Levi); Christ's legitimacy rests on eternal generation ("today I have begotten you"). The pairing of [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 2#Psalm 2 . 7|Psalm 2:7]] with [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]] in [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 5|Hebrews 5:5-6]] demonstrates that the priest "after the order of Melchizedek" is none other than the divine Son—His Sonship qualifies Him for a priesthood superior to Aaron's in every way. Where Aaron served as a mortal man among mortal brothers, Christ serves as the eternal Son in the Father's house.
