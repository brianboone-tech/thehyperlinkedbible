### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 24#Exodus 24 . 8|Exodus 24:8]]

**Context**: Moses ratifies the Sinai covenant by sprinkling blood on the altar and the people, declaring "This is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you." This covenant-making ceremony establishes the legal framework within which Aaron's priesthood will operate, demonstrating that covenant inauguration requires blood—a principle fulfilled when Christ inaugurates the new covenant through His own blood (Matthew 26:28; Hebrews 9:20).

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- דָּם (*dam*) - "blood" (life-force, atoning medium)
- בְּרִית (*berit*) - "covenant" (binding agreement)
- זָרַק (*zaraq*) - "sprinkle/scatter" (ritual application of blood)

**Christological Connection**: Aaron's entire priestly ministry operated within the covenant Moses established through animal blood. Every sacrifice Aaron offered presupposed this covenant relationship between God and Israel, ratified by blood sprinkled on both altar (representing God) and people (representing Israel). Christ fulfills this typology by establishing the new covenant through His own blood ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14-15]]), serving as both the mediating priest (like Moses) and the sacrificial victim. Where the old covenant required repeated sacrifices administered by Aaron's priesthood, the new covenant was established "once for all" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]) by Christ's single offering. The contrast is explicit in [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 20|Hebrews 9:20]], which quotes this very text to show that if the old covenant needed blood, how much more does the new covenant require better blood—Christ's own.
