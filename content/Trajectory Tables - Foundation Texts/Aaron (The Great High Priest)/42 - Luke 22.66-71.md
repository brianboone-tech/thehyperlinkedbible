### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 66|Luke 22:66-71]]

**Context**: At the Sanhedrin trial, Jesus combines Psalm 110:1 with Daniel 7:13, declaring He will be "seated at the right hand of the power of God." Luke's account emphasizes how this trial itself becomes part of Christ's priestly qualification—through unjust condemnation He learns to sympathize with human weaknesses, fulfilling what Aaron's imperfect priesthood could only foreshadow.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- πρεσβυτέριον (*presbyterion*) - "council of elders" - Israel's religious leadership including high priests
- καθήμενος (*kathemenos*) - "seated" - the enthroned posture of completed priestly work (contrast Aaron's standing service)
- δεξιῶν (*dexion*) - "right hand" - position of mediatorial authority
- δυνάμεως (*dynameos*) - "power" - divine authority undergirding Christ's priesthood

**Christological Connection**: Luke's trial narrative reveals how Jesus' suffering qualifies Him for high priesthood in ways Aaron's ritual purity never could. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 8|Hebrews 5:8]] declares that "although he was a son, he learned obedience through what he suffered," and here we see that learning in action. The religious council's mockery, false accusations, and demand for self-incrimination become the furnace in which Christ's sympathy for tempted humanity is perfected ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 18|Hebrews 2:18]]). When Jesus claims the seat at God's right hand ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]]), He claims it not as one untouched by human weakness but as one who has endured the full weight of unjust human systems. Aaron could sympathize with Israel's weaknesses because he shared their mortality ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 5#Hebrews 5 . 2|Hebrews 5:2]]), but Christ sympathizes infinitely more because He voluntarily descended into their suffering while remaining sinless. The trial that seems to disqualify Jesus actually qualifies Him as the merciful and faithful high priest.
