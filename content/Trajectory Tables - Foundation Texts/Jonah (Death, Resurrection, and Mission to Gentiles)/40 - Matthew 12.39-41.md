### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 39|Matthew 12:39-41]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- **σημεῖον** (*sēmeion*) = "sign" - Authenticating miracle proving divine authority; Jesus offers only ONE sign: Jonah
- **γενεὰ πονηρὰ καὶ μοιχαλίς** (*genea ponēra kai moichalis*) = "evil and adulterous generation" - Covenant unfaithfulness pictured as adultery (cf. Hosea, Ezekiel 16)
- **τὸ σημεῖον Ἰωνᾶ** (*to sēmeion Iōna*) = "the sign of Jonah" - Christ's central self-authenticating sign: death and resurrection
- **τρεῖς ἡμέρας καὶ τρεῖς νύκτας** (*treis hēmeras kai treis nyktas*) = "three days and three nights" - Precise time marker connecting Jonah's fish burial to Christ's tomb burial
- **κοιλίᾳ τοῦ κήτους** (*koilia tou kētous*) = "belly of the sea monster" - LXX term for Jonah's fish; Christ applies to himself
- **καρδίᾳ τῆς γῆς** (*kardia tēs gēs*) = "heart of the earth" - Christ in tomb parallels Jonah in fish's belly
- **ἀναστήσονται** (*anastēsontai*) = "will rise up" - Future resurrection for judgment; Ninevites will condemn unbelieving Israel
- **μετενόησαν** (*metenoēsan*) = "repented" - Nineveh's response to Jonah's preaching; condemns Israel's rejection of greater than Jonah
- **πλεῖον** (*pleion*) = "something greater" - Emphatic: Christ infinitely surpasses Jonah in person and work

**Context**: Pharisees and scribes demand authenticating sign from Jesus (v. 38). Instead of performing another miracle, Jesus declares they will receive only "the sign of Jonah" - his death, burial, and resurrection. He then condemns their unbelief by contrasting Nineveh's repentance at Jonah's minimal preaching with Israel's rejection despite "something greater than Jonah" being present. This becomes Jesus' definitive self-authentication: death-burial-resurrection, explicitly connecting himself to Jonah's three-day experience in fish.

**Christological Connection**: Jesus explicitly identifies himself with Jonah's death-burial-resurrection pattern: "As Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" (v. 40). This becomes THE authenticating sign of Christ's messiahship. But Christ infinitely surpasses Jonah: (1) **Nature of experience**: Jonah was preserved alive in fish, Christ truly died and was truly raised; (2) **Cause**: Jonah deserved death for disobedience, Christ was innocent substitute for guilty; (3) **Purpose**: Jonah delivered from death for personal salvation, Christ died and rose for world's redemption; (4) **Message**: Jonah brought temporal deliverance from temporal judgment, Christ brings eternal salvation from eternal wrath; (5) **Scope**: Jonah reluctantly went to one city, Christ sends disciples to all nations; (6) **Response**: Nineveh repented at reluctant prophet's minimal preaching (five Hebrew words!), Israel rejected enthusiastic Savior's three-year ministry. "Something greater than Jonah is here" (v. 41) - πλεῖον (pleion) is emphatic neuter, suggesting not just greater person but greater everything: greater Prophet, greater Message, greater Salvation, greater Mission. The stinging rebuke stands forever: pagan Ninevites will condemn covenant-breaking Israel because Gentiles believed with less evidence while Jews rejected with overwhelming evidence. This pattern continues: Gentiles receive gospel eagerly ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 48|Acts 13:48]], 17:4) while many Jews resist ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 45|Acts 13:45]], 28:28). Christ's death-burial-resurrection becomes the gospel's foundation ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 1|1 Corinthians 15:1-4]]), explicitly traced back to Jonah's sign.
