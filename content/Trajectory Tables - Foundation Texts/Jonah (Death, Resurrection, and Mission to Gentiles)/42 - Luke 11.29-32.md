### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 11#Luke 11 . 29|Luke 11:29-32]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- **ἐπαθροιζομένων** (*epathroizomenōn*) = "crowding together" - Masses gathering but with wrong motives (sign-seeking not faith)
- **γενεὰ πονηρά** (*genea ponēra*) = "evil generation" - Covenant unfaithfulness (Luke omits "adulterous" from Matthew's version)
- **σημεῖον ζητεῖ** (*sēmeion zētei*) = "seeks a sign" - Demanding authenticating miracle rather than believing Jesus' words/works
- **ἐγένετο σημεῖον** (*egeneto sēmeion*) = "became a sign" - Jonah himself (his experience) was the sign, not additional miracle
- **Νινευῖται** (*Nineuītai*) = "men of Nineveh" - Gentile Assyrians who repented
- **ἀναστήσονται** (*anastēsontai*) = "will rise up" - Future resurrection for judgment
- **κατακρινοῦσιν** (*katakrinousin*) = "will condemn" - Judicial verdict against this generation
- **μετενόησαν** (*metenoēsan*) = "repented" - Nineveh's response to Jonah's preaching
- **κήρυγμα Ἰωνᾶ** (*kērygma Iōna*) = "preaching/proclamation of Jonah" - The message Jonah delivered
- **πλεῖον Ἰωνᾶ** (*pleion Iōna*) = "something greater than Jonah" - Christ's superiority in every dimension
- **πλεῖον Σολομῶνος** (*pleion Solomōnos*) = "something greater than Solomon" - Paired comparison emphasizing Christ's superiority to Israel's greatest king
- **βασίλισσα νότου** (*basilissa notou*) = "Queen of the South" - Queen of Sheba who traveled far to hear Solomon's wisdom

**Context**: Luke's version parallels Matthew 12:38-42 but with variations. Crowds multiply, demanding sign. Jesus responds: only sign of Jonah will be given - but Luke focuses on Jonah as preacher/sign to Ninevites rather than explicitly detailing the three-day burial (though Luke assumes readers know the story). Jesus condemns generation by citing two Gentile examples: (1) Ninevites who repented at Jonah's preaching, (2) Queen of Sheba who traveled far for Solomon's wisdom. Both Gentiles responded to inferior revelation; Jews reject greater revelation (Christ).

**Christological Connection**: Jesus identifies himself as "something greater than Jonah" (πλεῖον Ἰωνᾶ, v. 32) and "something greater than Solomon" (πλεῖον Σολομῶνος, v. 31). The Greek πλεῖον (pleion) is emphatic neuter - not just "someone" greater but "something" greater, suggesting superiority in every dimension: greater Prophet than Jonah, greater King than Solomon, greater Wisdom than Solomon offered, greater Salvation than Jonah brought. The comparisons are devastating: (1) **Jonah vs. Jesus**: Jonah was reluctant, Jesus eager; Jonah preached five words, Jesus taught for three years; Jonah brought temporal deliverance from temporal judgment, Jesus brings eternal salvation from eternal wrath; Jonah went to one city under duress, Jesus commissions disciples to all nations willingly; Jonah was preserved alive in fish, Jesus truly died and truly rose; (2) **Solomon vs. Jesus**: Solomon's wisdom was natural gift, Jesus is Wisdom incarnate ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 1#1 Corinthians 1 . 24|1 Corinthians 1:24]], 30); Solomon built earthly temple, Jesus builds living temple ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]); Solomon's glory was temporal and faded, Jesus' glory is eternal; Solomon fell into idolatry, Jesus remained sinless; Solomon's kingdom divided and fell, Jesus' kingdom has no end ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 33|Luke 1:33]]). Yet Gentiles traveled far for Solomon's wisdom and repented at Jonah's preaching, while Jews had greater than both standing before them and rejected him. The indictment stands: "Men of Nineveh will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at the preaching of Jonah, and behold, something greater than Jonah is here!" (v. 32). This becomes pattern in Acts: Gentiles receive gospel gladly ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 48|Acts 13:48]], "Gentiles were glad and glorified word of Lord") while many Jews oppose ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 45|Acts 13:45]], 28:28). Christ is the greater Jonah who doesn't flee from mission to nations but embraces it, who doesn't resent Gentile salvation but commands it ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 24#Luke 24 . 47|Luke 24:47]], [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 8|Acts 1:8]]).
