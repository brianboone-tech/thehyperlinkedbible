### [[Readable Bible/32 - Jonah/Jonah 1#Jonah 1 . 1|Jonah 1:1-3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- **יוֹנָה** (*Yonah*) = "dove" - Name symbolizing peace, Spirit, gentleness; connects to Noah's dove and Spirit-dove at Jesus' baptism
- **נִינְוֵה** (*Nineveh*) = Capital of Assyria, Israel's enemy; represents hostile Gentile nations
- **קוּם** (*qum*) = "arise, go" - Divine commissioning formula (cf. Abraham, Moses, prophets)
- **בָּרַח** (*barach*) = "flee" - Jonah's rejection of mission to Gentiles mirrors Israel's failure as light to nations
- **תַּרְשִׁישׁ** (*Tarshish*) = Distant western port, opposite direction from Nineveh; flight from God's presence

**Context**: God calls Jonah ben Amittai (historical prophet during Jeroboam II's reign, 2 Kings 14:25) to preach repentance to Nineveh, the brutal Assyrian capital. Instead of obeying, Jonah flees westward to Tarshish (possibly Spain), attempting to escape God's commission. This introduces the central tension: God's mercy toward Gentiles versus prophet's nationalism.

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Abraham called to be blessing to all nations ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]]); Israel's mission as kingdom of priests ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]); Solomon's temple as house of prayer for all peoples ([[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 8#1 Kings 8 . 41|1 Kings 8:41-43]]); Psalms calling all nations to praise Yahweh (Psalm 67, 96, 117)
- **FROM OT**:
  - Isaiah's servant to be light to Gentiles ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 49#Isaiah 49 . 6|Isaiah 49:6]]); Jeremiah sent to nations ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 1#Jeremiah 1 . 5|Jeremiah 1:5]]); Nineveh's repentance becomes standard ([[Readable Bible/36 - Zephaniah/Zephaniah 2#Zephaniah 2 . 13|Zephaniah 2:13-15]])
- **FROM NT**:
  - Jesus cites Jonah as sign ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 39|Matthew 12:39-41]]); Paul sent to Gentiles despite Jewish opposition ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 9#Acts 9 . 15|Acts 9:15]], 22:21); Gospel to all nations ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]])
**Christological Connection**: Jonah's call to preach to Nineveh prefigures Christ's commission to make disciples of all nations ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 28#Matthew 28 . 19|Matthew 28:19]]). Jonah's reluctance mirrors Jewish Christianity's initial resistance to Gentile inclusion ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 11#Acts 11 . 1|Acts 11:1-18]], 15:1-29). But whereas Jonah fled from his mission in self-preservation, Christ embraced his mission unto death. The "dove" (Jonah's name) who fled must give way to the Spirit-dove descending on the true Prophet ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 3#Matthew 3 . 16|Matthew 3:16]]) who willingly goes to hostile nations. Jonah's westward flight to Tarshish (away from Gentile mission) contrasts with Christ's eastward journey to Jerusalem (toward cross and resurrection that enables Gentile salvation). Christ is the faithful Messenger who doesn't shrink from bringing God's word to the nations, even unto death.
