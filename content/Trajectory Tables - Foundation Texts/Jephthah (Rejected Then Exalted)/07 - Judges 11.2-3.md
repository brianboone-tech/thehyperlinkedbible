### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 2|Judges 11:2-3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1644|H1644]] גָּרַשׁ (gāraš) - "drive out/expel" - they drove Jephthah out
- [[Lexicon/H5159|H5159]] נַחֲלָה (naḥălâ) - "inheritance" - you shall have no inheritance
- [[Lexicon/H1272|H1272]] בָּרַח (bāraḥ) - "flee" - Jephthah fled from his brothers
- [[Lexicon/H7386|H7386]] רֵיק (rêq) - "empty/worthless" - worthless men gathered around him

**Context**: Gilead's legitimate sons drove Jephthah out with finality: "You shall have no inheritance in our father's house, because you are the son of another woman." The verb גָּרַשׁ (expel/drive out) is strong—it's used for Adam's expulsion from Eden (Genesis 3:24), Hagar's dismissal (Genesis 21:10), and the Canaanites' dispossession. Jephthah "fled" (בָּרַח) to the land of Tob, where "worthless men" (אֲנָשִׁים רֵיקִים—literally "empty men") gathered to him. The rejection was comprehensive: family, inheritance, homeland.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The expulsion language (גָּרַשׁ) connects to other significant expulsions in Scripture
- "Worthless men" gathering to Jephthah parallels David's cave of Adullam experience ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 22#1 Samuel 22 . 2|1 Samuel 22:2]])
- The denial of inheritance echoes other inheritance conflicts (Esau/Jacob, Ishmael/Isaac)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 10|Genesis 21:10]] - "Cast out (גָּרַשׁ) this slave woman and her son"
  - [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 22#1 Samuel 22 . 2|1 Samuel 22:2]] - distressed/indebted/discontented gather to David
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 37#Genesis 37 . 4|Genesis 37:4]] - Joseph's brothers "hated him and could not speak peacefully to him"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 11|John 1:11]] - "He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him"
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]] - Jesus suffered "outside the camp"
**Type Classification**: Providential (the rejection was humanly motivated but divinely permitted), Backward-Looking (the typological pattern recognized in retrospect)

**Christological Connection**: Jephthah's expulsion by brothers directly anticipates Christ's rejection. Jesus' own brothers "did not believe in him" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 5|John 7:5]]). He was cast out of His hometown ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 29|Luke 4:29]]). He was crucified "outside the camp" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]), expelled from the city. Yet around Him gathered the marginalized—tax collectors, sinners, the sick, the demonized. The pattern is precise: rejected by His own, embraced by outcasts, destined for exaltation. Jephthah among worthless men in Tob foreshadows Jesus among sinners, who would transform the rejected into "a chosen race, a royal priesthood" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 9|1 Peter 2:9]]).
