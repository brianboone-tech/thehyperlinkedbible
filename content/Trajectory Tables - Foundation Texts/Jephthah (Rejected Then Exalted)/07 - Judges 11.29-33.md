### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 29|Judges 11:29-33]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7307|H7307]] רוּחַ (rûaḥ) - "Spirit" - the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah
- [[Lexicon/H5414|H5414]] נָתַן (nāṯan) - "give/deliver" - the LORD delivered them into his hand
- [[Lexicon/H5221|H5221]] נָכָה (nākâ) - "strike/smite" - he struck down twenty cities
- [[Lexicon/H3966|H3966]] מְאֹד (mĕʾōḏ) - "very/exceedingly" - with a very great blow
- [[Lexicon/H3665|H3665]] כָּנַע (kānaʿ) - "subdued" - the Ammonites were subdued

**Context**: Having been installed as head, Jephthah receives divine empowerment: "The Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah" (וַתְּהִי עַל־יִפְתָּח רוּחַ יְהוָה). He then mobilized forces through Gilead and Manasseh and advanced against the Ammonites. The LORD delivered them into his hand, and he struck them "with a very great blow" (מַכָּה גְדוֹלָה מְאֹד)—twenty cities devastated. The result: "the Ammonites were subdued before the Israelites."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Spirit-empowerment marks other judges: Othniel (3:10), Gideon (6:34), Samson (13:25; 14:6, 19; 15:14)
- The phrase "the LORD delivered" (נָתַן יְהוָה) emphasizes divine agency in victory
- The pattern of Spirit → victory anticipates the Messianic promise of [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 2|Isaiah 11:2]]

**Type Classification**: Providential (Spirit-empowerment sovereignly given), Backward-Looking (typological pattern clarified by Christ's Spirit-anointed ministry)

**Christological Connection**: The Spirit-empowered victory of the rejected deliverer anticipates Christ. Jesus began His ministry by declaring: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 4#Luke 4 . 18|Luke 4:18]]). Peter preached: "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power. He went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]]). The rejected Nazarene, empowered by the Spirit, accomplished the ultimate deliverance. As Jephthah, once expelled, subdued Ammon by the Spirit's power, so Christ, crucified as outcast, conquered sin, death, and Satan through the Spirit. The resurrection itself was accomplished "according to the Spirit of holiness" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 4|Romans 1:4]]). The rejected one, Spirit-empowered, achieves victory for those who rejected him.
