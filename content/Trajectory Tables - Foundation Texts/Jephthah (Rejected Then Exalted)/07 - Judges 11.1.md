### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 11#Judges 11 . 1|Judges 11:1]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1368|H1368]] גִּבּוֹר (gibbôr) - "mighty man" - a mighty man of valor
- [[Lexicon/H2428|H2428]] חַיִל (ḥayil) - "valor/strength" - mighty man of valor
- [[Lexicon/H2181|H2181]] זָנָה (zānâ) - "prostitute/harlot" - son of a prostitute
- [[Lexicon/H1568|H1568]] גִּלְעָד (gilʿāḏ) - "Gilead" - Gilead was his father

**Context**: The opening verse of Jephthah's narrative establishes a striking contrast. He is introduced as a גִּבּוֹר חַיִל (mighty man of valor)—the same designation given to Gideon (Judges 6:12) and later to Boaz (Ruth 2:1). This phrase denotes military prowess, wealth, and social standing. Yet immediately Scripture records his stigma: he was "the son of a prostitute" (בֶּן־אִשָּׁה זוֹנָה). This dual description creates narrative tension—heroic capacity combined with shameful origin. His father Gilead also had legitimate sons who would later drive Jephthah out.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The "mighty man of valor" designation connects Jephthah to other deliverers (Gideon, Boaz, David's mighty men)
- The shameful origin parallels other unlikely divine choices: Moses (Levite raised by pagans), David (youngest, overlooked)
- The prostitute connection anticipates Rahab's inclusion in Israel and the Messianic line ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 5|Matthew 1:5]])

**Type Classification**: Providential (Jephthah's circumstances sovereignly arranged), Backward-Looking (typological significance recognized from NT perspective)

**Christological Connection**: Jephthah's combination of heroic capacity with shameful origin anticipates Christ. Jesus was the Son of God yet despised for His human circumstances—"Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 46|John 1:46]]). His opponents questioned His legitimacy: "We were not born of sexual immorality" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 41|John 8:41]]). Yet the despised one became the Savior. The pattern of unlikely origin leading to supreme exaltation finds its fulfillment in Christ who "though he was in the form of God...emptied himself, taking the form of a servant" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 2#Philippians 2 . 6|Philippians 2:6-7]]).
