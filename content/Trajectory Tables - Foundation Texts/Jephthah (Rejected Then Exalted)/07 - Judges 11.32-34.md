### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32-34]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4102|G4102]] πίστις (pistis) - "faith" - through faith
- [[Lexicon/G2610|G2610]] καταγωνίζομαι (katagōnizomai) - "conquer/subdue" - conquered kingdoms
- [[Lexicon/G1343|G1343]] δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē) - "righteousness/justice" - administered justice
- [[Lexicon/G1849|G1849]] ἐπιτυγχάνω (epitynchanō) - "obtain" - obtained promises
- [[Lexicon/G2479|G2479]] ἰσχύς (ischys) - "strength" - became mighty

**Context**: After detailed exposition of earlier faith heroes (Abel through Rahab), the author of Hebrews shifts to summary: "And what more shall I say? Time will not allow me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets." The compressed list then describes what "through faith" (διὰ πίστεως) these figures accomplished: "conquered kingdoms, administered justice, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions, quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in battle, put foreign armies to flight." Jephthah's conquest of Ammon fits "conquered kingdoms" and "put foreign armies to flight."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Hebrews reads the judges through the lens of faith despite their flaws
- The combination of military (conquest) and judicial (justice) achievements fits Jephthah's narrative
- The pattern of weakness-to-strength echoes Jephthah's rejected-to-exalted trajectory

**Type Classification**: Backward-Looking (NT explicitly commends Jephthah as faith exemplar), NT Commendation

**Christological Connection**: Hebrews' commendation of Jephthah's faith prepares for the climax: "Looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]]). Jesus is the ultimate faith hero who endured rejection ("despising the shame") and achieved exaltation ("seated at the right hand"). Every faith hero listed—including Jephthah—pointed forward to Christ's perfect faith. They "conquered kingdoms" by faith; Christ conquered sin, death, and Satan by faith. They achieved temporal deliverance; Christ achieved eternal salvation. Jephthah's faith-driven path from rejection to victory shadows Christ's faith-driven path from cross to crown.
