### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 4#Genesis 4 . 25|Genesis 4:25]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H8352|H8352]] שֵׁת (šēṯ) - "Seth" - meaning "appointed" or "placed"
- [[Lexicon/H7896|H7896]] שִׁית (šîṯ) - "appointed/granted" - the verb from which Seth's name derives
- [[Lexicon/H2233|H2233]] זֶרַע (zeraʿ) - "seed/offspring" - another seed in place of Abel
- [[Lexicon/H8478|H8478]] תַּחַת (taḥaṯ) - "in place of" - substitutionary language
- [[Lexicon/H2026|H2026]] הָרַג (hāraḡ) - "killed" - recalling Abel's murder

**Context**: After the account of Cain's descendants (4:17-24), the narrative returns to Adam and Eve. Eve gives birth to another son and names him Seth, declaring that God has "appointed" another seed to replace the murdered Abel. This marks a theological turning point: the godly line will continue not through Cain but through a divinely appointed replacement.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] promised enmity between seeds; now the "seed of the woman" continues through Seth
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 3|Genesis 5:3]] connects Seth to Adam as bearing his image
- [[Readable Bible/13 - 1 Chronicles/1 Chronicles 1#1 Chronicles 1 . 1|1 Chronicles 1:1]] begins Israel's genealogy with Adam-Seth, not Adam-Cain
- [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 38|Luke 3:38]] traces Jesus through Seth, confirming the theological significance

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] - The seed promise continues through Seth
- **FROM OT**: The line from Seth leads through Noah to Abraham to David
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 38|Luke 3:38]] traces Jesus through "Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God"

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged) and Forward-Looking (Seth appointed to continue the line leading to Christ)

**Christological Connection**: Seth as the "appointed seed" points to Christ, the ultimately appointed Seed. What Seth was provisionally—replacement for the murdered righteous one—Christ is ultimately. Abel's blood cried for vengeance; Christ's blood speaks forgiveness. Seth continued Abel's testimony; Christ fulfills it. The pattern of death-then-appointed-replacement anticipates resurrection: God's purposes are not defeated by the death of the righteous but advanced through divine appointment of a greater successor.
