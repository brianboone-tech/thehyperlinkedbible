### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 29|John 1:29]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0001-0100#G0286|ἀμνὸς τοῦ θεοῦ]] (*amnos tou theou*) - "Lamb of God"
- [[Lexicon/G0101-0200#G0142|αἴρων]] (*airōn*) - "taking away, lifting up, bearing"
- [[Lexicon/G0201-0300#G0266|ἁμαρτίαν]] (*hamartian*) - "sin"
- [[Lexicon/G2801-2900#G2889|κόσμου]] (*kosmou*) - "world"
- [[Lexicon/G2301-2400#G2396|ἴδε]] (*ide*) - "behold, see" (imperative of intense observation)

**Context**: John the Baptist's proclamation "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" stands as the Fourth Gospel's christological thesis statement. Spoken the day after Jewish leaders questioned John's identity and authority, this declaration identifies Jesus publicly for the first time as the long-awaited sacrifice who accomplishes what the entire Levitical system foreshadowed. John's baptism ministry of repentance prepared hearts; now he points away from himself to the One who bears sin. The Baptist merges Passover lamb imagery (deliverance), sin offering typology (atonement), and Isaiah's Suffering Servant (substitution) into a single profound title that defines Jesus's mission and identity.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]] (God will provide the lamb), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 3|Exodus 12:3-13]] (Passover lamb), [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 32|Leviticus 4:32]] (lamb as sin offering), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]] (like a lamb led to slaughter)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 36|John 19:36]] (not a bone broken—Passover lamb), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 8#Acts 8 . 32|Acts 8:32]] (like sheep led to slaughter), [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]] (Christ our Passover), [[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 19|1 Peter 1:19]] (precious blood of lamb without blemish), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6-14]] (Lamb standing as though slain)

**Christological Connection**: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world" encapsulates the entire gospel in eleven words. Every OT lamb sacrifice finds ultimate fulfillment in Jesus Christ, who is simultaneously the Lamb and the Lamb-provider. Abraham's prophetic declaration—"God will provide for himself the lamb" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 22#Genesis 22 . 8|Genesis 22:8]])—reaches consummate realization: God provides the Lamb who is God, the Son offered by the Father in trinitarian love. The substitutionary principle of Isaac's near-sacrifice (ram caught in thicket replaces Isaac) escalates to cosmic substitution: Christ, the beloved Son, dies in sinners' place.

The Passover typology fulfills comprehensively. As the Passover lamb's blood on doorposts averted death-angel's judgment ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 13|Exodus 12:13]]), Christ's blood averts eternal judgment: "there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]). As Israel ate the Passover lamb and immediately began exodus journey, believers "feed on" Christ spiritually ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 6#John 6 . 53|John 6:53-56]]) and begin pilgrimage toward the promised land. As Passover lamb required no broken bones ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 12#Exodus 12 . 46|Exodus 12:46]]), so Christ's legs weren't broken on the cross ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 33|John 19:33-36]]), "that the Scripture might be fulfilled." Paul declares explicitly: "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 7|1 Corinthians 5:7]]).

The sin offering dimension appears in Christ "becoming sin" for us ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]])—the Levitical sin offering was called *ḥaṭṭāʾt* (simultaneously "sin" and "sin offering"). Christ absorbed sin's guilt and penalty, experiencing God-forsakenness ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]]) in sinners' stead. As the sin offering's body was burned outside the camp ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 4#Leviticus 4 . 12|Leviticus 4:12]]), Christ "suffered outside the gate" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]) bearing sin's shame and curse.

Isaiah 53's Servant-Lamb finds complete embodiment in Jesus. The Baptist's proclamation echoes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 7|Isaiah 53:7]]: "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent." Jesus's silent endurance before accusers ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 63|Matthew 26:63]]; [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 14|Matthew 27:14]]) fulfills the Servant's voicelessness. The Servant "bore the sin of many" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 12|Isaiah 53:12]]); the Lamb "takes away the sin of the world"—universal scope. Peter explicitly connects Isaiah 53 to Christ: "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree... by his wounds you have been healed" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]]).

The verb *airō* ("take away") transforms passive lamb imagery into active redemption. Christ doesn't merely suffer; He conquers. "He takes away sin" implies complete removal: "as far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 103#Psalm 103 . 12|Psalm 103:12]]). Where animal sacrifices provided temporary covering requiring repetition—"it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 4|Hebrews 10:4]])—Christ's sacrifice achieves permanent removal: "he entered once for all into the holy places... by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]).

Revelation unveils the Lamb's ultimate triumph. John sees "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]])—bearing crucifixion marks yet alive, now enthroned. Heaven's worship crescendos: "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing!" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 12|Revelation 5:12]]). The slain Lamb becomes the reigning Lion, the sacrifice becomes the Sovereign. The Baptist's proclamation thus bridges old and new covenants: the OT sacrificial system's entire trajectory converges on Jesus, and the NT church worships the Lamb on the throne. "Behold, the Lamb of God"—behold Him crucified for sin, risen in power, returning in glory.
