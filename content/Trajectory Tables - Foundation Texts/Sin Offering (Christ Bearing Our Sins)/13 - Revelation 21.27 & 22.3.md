**Context**: Revelation 21:27 and 22:3 bookend John's vision of the New Jerusalem, describing the eternal state after sin's final removal. Chapter 21 depicts the holy city descending from heaven, God dwelling with humanity, death abolished, mourning ended. Verse 27 emphasizes the city's absolute purity—nothing defiled enters. Chapter 22 continues the vision: the river of life, tree of life, perpetual light from God's glory. Verse 3 declares "no longer will there be any curse"—the Genesis 3 curse fully reversed. These verses represent the sin offering trajectory's consummation: what began with animal sacrifices outside the camp culminates in sinless humanity dwelling with God eternally, curse removed, access unrestricted.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 27|Revelation 21:27]] and 22:3 present the sin offering trajectory's ultimate fulfillment: Christ's substitutionary sacrifice accomplishing what it prefigured—permanent sin removal and eternal communion with God. The absolute exclusion of "anything unclean" (21:27) testifies to atonement's completeness. No defiled thing enters because Christ bore all defilement: "He made him who knew no sin to be sin for us" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]). Believers enter the holy city not because they achieved sinlessness but because Christ's blood cleansed them: "the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]). The perfect holiness required for God's presence is provided, not produced—imputed, not earned.

The "Lamb's book of life" (21:27) identifies Christ as the sovereign determiner of entrance. The diminutive "Lamb" (*arnion*) preserves sacrifice imagery—this is the slain Lamb ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]]: "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain") now enthroned. His sacrificial death purchased redemption: "You were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 9|Revelation 5:9]]). The book records those redeemed by the Lamb's blood—election and atonement converge. Names written "before the foundation of the world" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 13#Revelation 13 . 8|Revelation 13:8]]) testify to eternal purpose; names secured through historical atonement demonstrate temporal accomplishment.

The curse removal (22:3) directly results from Christ's curse-bearing. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 17|Genesis 3:17]]'s "cursed is the ground because of you" finds reversal in "no longer will there be any curse." The mechanism: Christ "became a curse for us" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]), absorbing divine judgment against sin. Hanging on the tree ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 21#Deuteronomy 21 . 23|Deuteronomy 21:23]]: "a hanged man is cursed by God"), Christ experienced curse's fullness: God-forsakenness, physical agony, spiritual death. His three-hour darkness absorbed the eternal curse sinners deserved. The result: "Christ has set us free from the curse" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]), enabling new creation where "no curse remains."

The throne imagery—"throne of God and of the Lamb" (22:3)—unites deity in Christ's person. The Lamb shares God's throne, receiving worship reserved for deity alone. This fulfills [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]]: "Sit at my right hand"—Christ's exaltation following His humiliation. He who bore sin outside the gate now reigns in glory at heaven's center. The trajectory: expelled from Eden → suffered outside Jerusalem → enthroned in new creation. Curse-bearer becomes curse-remover; sin-bearer becomes sovereign.

The perpetual worship—"his servants will serve him" (22:3)—describes redeemed humanity's eternal occupation. The verb *latreuō* denotes priestly service, fulfilling [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]]: "you shall be to me a kingdom of priests." What sin prevented—immediate access, direct service—Christ's atonement restored. Believers don't merely enter God's presence; they minister before Him continuously. The face-to-face communion (22:4) reverses Moses's limitation: "you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 20|Exodus 33:20]]). Christ's mediation enables what sin forbade: "they will see his face"—unveiled glory, unmediated fellowship.

The sin offering trajectory reaches consummation. Leviticus prescribed animal sacrifices bearing sins outside the camp; Revelation unveils eternal reality where sin is permanently expelled. Isaiah prophesied the Servant bearing sin; Revelation shows the Lamb reigning, sin defeated. Hebrews declared Christ's once-for-all sacrifice; Revelation demonstrates its eternal efficacy. The tent of meeting, where God met Israel through veil and sacrifice, gives way to immediate presence: "the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 3|Revelation 21:3]]).

Nothing unclean enters because Christ bore all uncleanness. No curse remains because Christ absorbed all curse. The tree of life, guarded by cherubim since [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 24|Genesis 3:24]], becomes accessible: "blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and that they may enter the city by the gates" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 22#Revelation 22 . 14|Revelation 22:14]]). The robes washed in the Lamb's blood ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]]) grant entrance. What began outside the camp—sin offerings burned, curse borne, Savior suffering—culminates inside the city: sin removed, curse abolished, Lamb reigning, believers worshiping, eternity secured. The sin offering accomplished its purpose: Christ bearing our sins that we might dwell with God forever, curse-free, face-to-face, in holy communion eternally.
