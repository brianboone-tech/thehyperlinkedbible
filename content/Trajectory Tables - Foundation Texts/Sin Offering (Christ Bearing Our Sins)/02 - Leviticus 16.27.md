### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]]

**Context**: Leviticus 16:27 describes the climactic disposal of the Day of Atonement sin offerings—the bull and goat whose blood entered the Most Holy Place. After the high priest completed the blood rituals inside the sanctuary, purifying it from Israel's accumulated defilement, these animals' bodies were carried outside the camp and completely burned. This verse stands within Israel's most solemn annual ceremony, when once yearly the high priest entered God's immediate presence to make atonement for all the nation's sins. The burning outside the camp signals that these sin-bearers, having absorbed the people's guilt through blood ritual, must be removed entirely from the holy community.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]]'s burning of sin offerings outside the camp reaches fulfillment in Christ's crucifixion outside Jerusalem's gates. The geographical parallel is precise: as Day of Atonement sin-bearers were expelled beyond Israel's encampment, Christ "suffered outside the gate" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 12|Hebrews 13:12]]) at Golgotha, beyond Jerusalem's walls ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 20|John 19:20]]: "the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city"). The spatial symbolism reveals theological reality: Christ bore sin's curse and separation from God. As the sin offerings absorbed Israel's impurity through blood ritual then suffered expulsion, Christ absorbed humanity's sin—"the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 6|Isaiah 53:6]])—and endured God-forsakenness on the cross: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]]).

The Day of Atonement pattern involved dual movement: blood entered the Most Holy Place while bodies exited the camp. Christ accomplishes both: He enters "once for all into the holy places... by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]), while simultaneously suffering rejection outside the covenant community. The high priest brought animal blood into the sanctuary; Christ, as both priest and offering, brings His own blood into heaven's true sanctuary ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]]: "Christ has entered... into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf"). The annual repetition of Leviticus 16's ceremony testified to its incompleteness—"it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 4|Hebrews 10:4]])—but Christ's single expulsion-unto-death accomplished what millennia of animal burnings could not: permanent sin removal.

The comprehensive burning (skin, flesh, dung—v. 27) pictures Christ's total sacrifice: nothing withheld, everything consumed. His physical suffering outside Jerusalem ("despised and rejected by men, a man of sorrows," [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 3|Isaiah 53:3]]) fulfilled the social dimension of being expelled beyond the camp—cut off from covenant community, numbered with criminals ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 22#Luke 22 . 37|Luke 22:37]]), subjected to public shame. Yet this expulsion accomplished eternal redemption: "through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 20|Colossians 1:20]]).

Hebrews draws practical application: "Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 13|Hebrews 13:13]]). As Christ was expelled to sanctify His people, believers identify with His rejection, accepting marginalization from worldly systems. The place of curse becomes the location of encounter. What seemed like ultimate defeat—expulsion from the holy city, death beyond sacred space—proved to be victory: "But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 57|1 Corinthians 15:57]]). [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]]'s sin offerings, burned outside the camp after their blood entered God's presence, find consummate fulfillment in Christ who suffered outside the gate, His blood securing eternal access to the Father's throne.
