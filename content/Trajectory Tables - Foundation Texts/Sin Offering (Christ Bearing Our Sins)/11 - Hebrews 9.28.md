### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0501-0600#G0530|ἅπαξ]] (*hapax*) - "once, once for all"
- [[Lexicon/G4201-4300#G4374|προσενεχθείς]] (*prosenechtheis*) - "having been offered" (aorist passive participle)
- [[Lexicon/G0301-0400#G0399|ἀνενεγκεῖν]] (*anenenkein*) - "to bear, carry up, offer"
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4183|πολλῶν]] (*pollōn*) - "many" (genitive plural)
- [[Lexicon/G0201-0300#G0266|ἁμαρτίας]] (*hamartias*) - "sins"
- [[Lexicon/G1301-1400#G1537|ἐκ δευτέρου]] (*ek deuterou*) - "a second time"
- [[Lexicon/G3501-3600#G3700|ὀφθήσεται]] (*ophthēsetai*) - "will appear" (future passive)
- [[Lexicon/G5501-5600#G5565|χωρὶς ἁμαρτίας]] (*chōris hamartias*) - "apart from sin, without reference to sin"
- [[Lexicon/G4501-4600#G4991|σωτηρίαν]] (*sōtērian*) - "salvation"

**Context**: Hebrews 9:28 concludes the author's comparison between Levitical sacrifices and Christ's superior offering. Chapter 9 contrasts the earthly tabernacle (vv. 1-10) with Christ's entrance into heaven itself (vv. 11-14), the annual Day of Atonement repetitions (vv. 6-7, 25) with Christ's once-for-all sacrifice (vv. 12, 26), and animal blood's temporary efficacy (v. 13) with Christ's blood achieving eternal redemption (v. 12). Verse 28 crystallizes the argument: Christ was offered once to bear sins, and will appear a second time not to deal with sin (already finished) but to consummate salvation for believers. The verse establishes the already/not-yet tension: atonement accomplished, consummation anticipated.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]] presents Christ's atoning work through the lens of fulfillment and consummation. The phrase "offered once" (*hapax prosenechtheis*) contrasts with endless Levitical repetitions, demonstrating Christ's sacrifice's finality and sufficiency. Unlike annual Day of Atonement offerings requiring perpetual renewal, Christ's single offering achieved eternal redemption: "when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]). The sitting posture signifies completion—His work finished, He rests. No further sacrifice needed or possible: "there is no longer any offering for sin" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 18|Hebrews 10:18]]).

The sin-bearing language—"to bear the sins of many"—directly quotes [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 12|Isaiah 53:12]], identifying Jesus as the Suffering Servant. Christ didn't merely sympathize with sinners or suffer unjustly; He bore their actual guilt, absorbing divine wrath. The verb "bear" (*anenenkein*) carries sacrificial meaning: offering up, carrying away, removing. Christ "offered himself" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]), voluntarily submitting to death: "I lay down my life... No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 17|John 10:17-18]]). The Father's will and Son's obedience converge: "I have come to do your will, O God" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 7|Hebrews 10:7]]).

The "many" (*pollōn*) for whom Christ bore sins recalls His own words: "the Son of Man came... to give his life as a ransom for many" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 20#Matthew 20 . 28|Matthew 20:28]]). At the Last Supper, He declared: "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 28|Matthew 26:28]]). The "many" contrasts with "all"—not universal salvation but particular redemption: Christ effectively saves those the Father gave Him ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 17#John 17 . 2|John 17:2]]: "you have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to all whom you have given him").

The second advent—"will appear a second time"—fulfills eschatological hope. Christ's first appearing was "to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 26|Hebrews 9:26]]); His second appearing consummates salvation. The phrase "apart from sin" (*chōris hamartias*) doesn't mean He returns sinlessly (He was always sinless) but indicates functional difference: first advent addressed sin through atonement; second advent brings salvation without reference to sin-bearing—that work is finished. Paul similarly contrasts: Christ "died to sin once for all" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 6#Romans 6 . 10|Romans 6:10]]), but lives to God—the sin-bearing phase ended at resurrection.

The high priestly typology illuminates the promise. On the Day of Atonement, the high priest entered the Most Holy Place alone while Israel waited anxiously outside. His emergence signaled atonement's acceptance—if God rejected the offering, the priest died inside. Christ entered "into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]]). His return proves the offering's acceptance. Unlike earthly priests who entered annually, Christ entered "once for all" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 12|Hebrews 9:12]]); unlike their blood which required repetition, His blood secured "eternal redemption."

The "eagerly waiting" phrase describes believers' posture: "our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 20|Philippians 3:20]]). The expectation isn't passive but active—watching, yearning, preparing. Paul describes this as "waiting for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, Jesus who delivers us from the wrath to come" ([[Readable Bible/52 - 1 Thessalonians/1 Thessalonians 1#1 Thessalonians 1 . 10|1 Thessalonians 1:10]]). The second advent brings "salvation"—not initial justification (already received) but eschatological deliverance: glorified bodies ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 21|Philippians 3:21]]), resurrection of the dead ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 52|1 Corinthians 15:52]]), new heavens and earth ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1]]), eternal fellowship with God.

The already/not-yet tension structures Christian existence. Already: justified, sanctified, adopted, sealed. Not yet: glorified, resurrected, perfected. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]] bridges these: Christ bore sins once (past, accomplished); Christ will appear (future, anticipated); believers wait eagerly (present, expectant). The sin offering is complete—"It is finished" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 30|John 19:30]]). The Lamb now stands in heaven "as though it had been slain" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 5#Revelation 5 . 6|Revelation 5:6]]), bearing crucifixion marks glorified. His return consummates what His cross initiated: full salvation for all who eagerly await Him. As the high priest emerged from the Most Holy Place signaling acceptance, Christ will emerge from heaven in glory, vindicating His once-for-all sacrifice, gathering His redeemed, and establishing His eternal kingdom.
