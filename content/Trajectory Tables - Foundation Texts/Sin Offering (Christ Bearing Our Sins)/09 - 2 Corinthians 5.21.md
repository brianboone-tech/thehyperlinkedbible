### [[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 21|2 Corinthians 5:21]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0201-0300#G0266|ἁμαρτίαν]] (*hamartian*) - "sin" (accusative, object of "made")
- [[Lexicon/G4101-4200#G4160|ἐποίησεν]] (*epoiēsen*) - "made, did" (aorist active indicative)
- [[Lexicon/G1001-1100#G1097|γνόντα]] (*gnonta*) - "knowing" (aorist participle, negative: "not knowing")
- [[Lexicon/G1101-1200#G1096|γενώμεθα]] (*genōmetha*) - "we might become" (aorist subjunctive)
- [[Lexicon/G1201-1300#G1343|δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ]] (*dikaiosynē theou*) - "righteousness of God"
- [[Lexicon/G5101-5200#G5228|ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν]] (*hyper hēmōn*) - "on behalf of us, for us" (substitutionary preposition)

**Context**: Second Corinthians 5:21 concludes Paul's exposition of the ministry of reconciliation (vv. 18-20). God has reconciled the world to Himself through Christ, not counting trespasses, and committed this message to apostles. Verse 21 unveils the mechanism: the great exchange whereby sinless Christ becomes sin that sinners might become righteous. This compact statement—perhaps Christianity's most concentrated atonement summary—synthesizes substitution, imputation, and transformation. Paul writes to defend his apostolic ministry against critics, grounding his authority in the gospel's objective reality: God's reconciling work in Christ precedes and enables human response.

**Christological Connection**: Second Corinthians 5:21 presents the gospel's essence: Christ's substitutionary sin-bearing and believers' consequent righteousness. The phrase "made him to be sin" unveils the cross's horror: the sinless One experiences sin's full penalty. Christ didn't merely associate with sinners or suffer unjustly; He became sin in the sense the Levitical sin offering was called "sin"—identifying completely with sin's guilt and curse. Isaiah prophesied: "the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 6|Isaiah 53:6]]). Peter affirms: "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 24|1 Peter 2:24]]). This wasn't symbolic gesture but actual transaction: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]).

Christ's sinlessness—"him who knew no sin"—qualifies Him as the acceptable sacrifice. He "committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 22|1 Peter 2:22]]). He is "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]). Tempted in every way yet without sin ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 15|Hebrews 4:15]]), Jesus lived the perfectly righteous life humanity failed to achieve. His sinlessness makes His sacrifice efficacious—spotless Lamb slain for blemished sheep.

The divine initiative—"God made him"—emphasizes trinitarian love. The Father sent the Son ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 4#1 John 4 . 10|1 John 4:10]]: "God... sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins"); the Son willingly came ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 18|John 10:18]]: "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord"). God didn't spare His own Son but gave Him up for us all ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 32|Romans 8:32]]). The atonement originates in divine love, not human merit: "In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 4#1 John 4 . 10|1 John 4:10]]).

The great exchange reverses the Fall. Adam's sin brought condemnation and death to all ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 18|Romans 5:18]]); Christ's obedience brings justification and life. "As by one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience the many will be made righteous" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 19|Romans 5:19]]). Christ becomes what we are (sin-bearer) that we might become what He is (righteous). He takes our filthy garments and clothes us with His righteousness—Zechariah's vision realized ([[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 3#Zechariah 3 . 4|Zechariah 3:4]]: "Remove the filthy garments... Behold, I have taken your iniquity away from you, and I will clothe you with pure vestments").

The "righteousness of God" believers receive isn't merely legal standing (though it includes that) but active divine righteousness—"the righteousness from God that depends on faith" ([[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 9|Philippians 3:9]]). This righteousness enables believers to "become the righteousness of God in him"—not just declared righteous but increasingly conformed to Christ's image. Justification (forensic declaration) and sanctification (actual transformation) both flow from union with Christ. "In him" (*en autō*) indicates the location of this righteousness—found only in Christ, never in self.

The cross's cry—"My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]])—unveils what "made him to be sin" meant experientially. Christ suffered God-forsakenness, the ultimate consequence of sin: separation from the Father. The Holy One endured hell's essence (abandonment by God) that sinners might experience heaven's essence (fellowship with God). The three hours of darkness symbolized judgment's weight: "God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them" (v. 19)—because He counted them against Christ instead.

The exchange achieves complete reversal. Believers' sins—past, present, future—were imputed to Christ at Calvary; Christ's righteousness—perfect obedience, spotless life—is imputed to believers at conversion. God sees believers "in Christ," covered by His righteousness: "there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]). The basis: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God." The sin offering fulfilled; the great exchange completed; reconciliation accomplished.
