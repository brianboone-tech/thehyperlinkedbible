### [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 21#Judges 21 . 25|Judges 21:25]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4428|H4428]] מֶלֶךְ (*meleḵ*) - king
- [[Lexicon/H3477|H3477]] יָשָׁר (*yāšār*) - straight, right, upright
- [[Lexicon/H5869|H5869]] עַיִן (*ʿayin*) - eye

**Context**: "In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes." This refrain (also 17:6; 18:1; 19:1) serves as the book's theological conclusion. The final chapters narrate horrific events—a Levite's concubine is gang-raped and dismembered, civil war nearly annihilates Benjamin. The refrain diagnoses the problem: without righteous leadership, moral chaos reigns.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 8|Deuteronomy 12:8]] - Warning against doing "what is right in your own eyes"
- [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 5|1 Samuel 8:5]] - Israel demands a king
- 2 Samuel 7 - Davidic covenant established

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 12#Deuteronomy 12 . 8|Deuteronomy 12:8]] - Prohibition of autonomous morality
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 5|1 Samuel 8:5-6]] - Request for king
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]] - Jesus as Son of David
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 32|Luke 1:32-33]] - Christ given David's throne

**Type Classification**: Providential Type - Forward-looking (prepares for Davidic and Messianic kingship)

**Christological Connection**: The refrain "no king in Israel" creates expectation for righteous kingship. The chaos of the judges period demonstrates that human autonomy leads to moral disaster. Israel needed a king—but not just any king. Saul failed; David was flawed; Solomon fell into idolatry. The trajectory points beyond any human king to Christ, who alone rules in perfect righteousness, whose kingdom never ends, and who transforms hearts so that His people delight to do what is right in His eyes, not merely their own.
