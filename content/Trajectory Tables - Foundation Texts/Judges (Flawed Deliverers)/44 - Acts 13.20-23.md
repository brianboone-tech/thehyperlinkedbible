### [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 20|Acts 13:20-23]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2923|G2923]] κριτής (*kritēs*) - judge
- [[Lexicon/G4396|G4396]] προφήτης (*prophētēs*) - prophet
- [[Lexicon/G4990|G4990]] σωτήρ (*sōtēr*) - savior, deliverer
- [[Lexicon/G4690|G4690]] σπέρμα (*sperma*) - seed, offspring

**Context**: Paul's sermon at Pisidian Antioch traces salvation history: "After that [Joshua] he gave them judges until Samuel the prophet. Then they asked for a king, and God gave them Saul... And when he had removed him, he raised up David... Of this man's offspring God has brought to Israel a Savior, Jesus, as he promised." The judges are stepping stones in God's redemptive plan.

- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 2#Judges 2 . 16|Judges 2:16]] - Institution of judges

- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32]] - Judges in faith hall

**Type Classification**: NT Fulfillment - Judges integrated into Christological trajectory

**Christological Connection**: Paul places the judges within the grand narrative leading to Christ. The judges were deliverers (σωτήρ language underlies the Hebrew), but temporary and partial. Samuel marks the transition to monarchy; David receives the eternal covenant; Jesus is the promised "Savior" (σωτήρ) from David's seed. The trajectory is clear: judges → kings → Christ. Each stage anticipates the next; Christ fulfills and transcends all. The judges' role was preparatory—they saved Israel from Midianites and Philistines; Christ saves from sin and death.
