### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 32|Hebrews 11:32-34]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4102|G4102]] πίστις (*pistis*) - faith
- [[Lexicon/G770|G770]] ἀσθένεια (*astheneia*) - weakness
- [[Lexicon/G1743|G1743]] ἐνδυναμόω (*endynamoō*) - to strengthen, empower

**Context**: "What more shall I say? For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah... who through faith conquered kingdoms, enforced justice, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Judges 4-5 - Barak and Deborah
- Judges 6-8 - Gideon
- Judges 11 - Jephthah
- Judges 13-16 - Samson

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Judges passim - Individual judge narratives
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 12#1 Samuel 12 . 11|1 Samuel 12:11]] - Samuel's recollection
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 39|Hebrews 11:39-40]] - All these did not receive promise
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 1|Hebrews 12:1-2]] - Cloud of witnesses; Jesus the perfecter

**Type Classification**: NT Application - Judges as faith examples for believers

**Christological Connection**: Hebrews celebrates the judges' faith without endorsing their sins. The key phrase is "made strong out of weakness" (ἐξ ἀσθενείας ἐδυναμώθησαν)—this perfectly captures the judges' pattern. God used weak, flawed people to accomplish mighty deliverances. The implicit contrast is with Christ: where the judges were made strong despite weakness, Christ was strong in Himself yet willingly became weak ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 13#2 Corinthians 13 . 4|2 Corinthians 13:4]]). The judges' faith was genuine but imperfect; Christ is "the founder and perfecter of our faith" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 2|Hebrews 12:2]]). We look to the judges as examples but to Christ as the ultimate object of faith.
