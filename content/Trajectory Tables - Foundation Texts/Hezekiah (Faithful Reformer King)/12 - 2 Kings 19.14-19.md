### [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 19#2 Kings 19 . 14|2 Kings 19:14-19]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6566|H6566]] פָּרַשׂ (paras) - "spread out" - Hezekiah spreads the letter before God
- [[Lexicon/H3427|H3427]] יֹשֵׁב (yosheb) - "enthroned" - God above the cherubim
- [[Lexicon/H3467|H3467]] יָשַׁע (yasha) - "save/deliver" - the prayer's request
- [[Lexicon/H3045|H3045]] יָדַע (yada) - "know" - all kingdoms will know God is LORD

**Context**: Sennacherib's army surrounds Jerusalem after conquering all Judean cities except Jerusalem. The Assyrian envoy has mocked God's ability to deliver. Hezekiah's response is paradigmatic: he spreads the threatening letter before God and prays.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Isaiah 36-37 - Parallel account with additional prophetic material
- 2 Chronicles 32 - Third account emphasizing God's response
- Psalm 46 - "God is our refuge and strength... though nations rage"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: The crisis recalls Egypt's threat at the Red Sea (Exod 14:10-14)
- **FROM OT**: God's pattern of delivering against impossible odds
- **FROM NT**: Jesus' trust in Gethsemane follows this pattern of prayer in crisis

**Type Classification**: Providential deliverance pattern pointing to Christ's victory

**Christological Connection**: Hezekiah faced a humanly insurmountable enemy and trusted God; Jesus faced sin, death, and hell and trusted the Father. Hezekiah was delivered by the angel of the LORD destroying enemies; Jesus defeated the enemy by submitting to death and rising victorious. Both demonstrate that God's power is made perfect in weakness, and that trust is vindicated even when circumstances appear hopeless.
