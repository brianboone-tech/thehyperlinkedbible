### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 12#Matthew 12 . 6|Matthew 12:6, 41-42]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3187|G3187]] μείζων (meizōn) - "greater" - Jesus' claim to superiority
- [[Lexicon/G2411|G2411]] ἱερόν (hieron) - "temple" - that which Jesus surpasses
- [[Lexicon/G5602|G5602]] ὧδε (hōde) - "here" - emphatic presence of the greater one
- [[Lexicon/G4678|G4678]] σοφία (sophia) - "wisdom" - what Solomon had but Jesus exceeds

**Context**: Jesus defends His disciples for picking grain on the Sabbath. He appeals to David (v. 3-4), the priests (v. 5), and then makes extraordinary claims: "something greater than the temple is here" (v. 6), "something greater than Jonah is here" (v. 41), "something greater than Solomon is here" (v. 42). By implication, He is greater than all Israel's great figures—including Hezekiah.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 5|2 Kings 18:5]] - Hezekiah is incomparable among Judah's kings
- [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 3#1 Kings 3 . 12|1 Kings 3:12]] - Solomon's wisdom is incomparable
- The Davidic kings form a progression pointing to the ultimate king

**Connections**:
- **TO**: The OT comparisons—David, Solomon, Hezekiah—each "greatest" in one dimension
- **FROM OT**: Jesus' claim incorporates all these superlatives: wisdom, trust, righteousness
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 9|Colossians 2:9]] - "In Christ all the fullness of the Deity lives in bodily form"

**Type Classification**: NT Fulfillment - Jesus explicitly claims to fulfill and exceed all types

**Christological Connection**: Hezekiah trusted God more than any king before or after him (2 Kgs 18:5); Jesus IS God incarnate who trusts the Father perfectly. Hezekiah destroyed idols and reformed worship; Jesus IS the object of true worship. Hezekiah was delivered from death for fifteen years; Jesus conquered death permanently. The "greater than" logic means every excellence found in OT figures is present in Jesus without the limitations that marked them.
