### [[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 10#1 Corinthians 10 . 14|1 Corinthians 10:14]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G5343|G5343]] φεύγω (pheugō) - "flee" - urgent command to escape
- [[Lexicon/G1495|G1495]] εἰδωλολατρία (eidōlolatria) - "idolatry" - worship of images/false gods
- [[Lexicon/G27|G27]] ἀγαπητοί (agapētoi) - "beloved" - term of affection intensifying the warning
- [[Lexicon/G1355|H1355]] διόπερ (dioper) - "therefore" - logical consequence of preceding argument

**Context**: Paul addresses Christians tempted to participate in idol feasts. After using Israel's wilderness failures as warnings (10:1-13), he commands: "flee from idolatry." The reform that Hezekiah accomplished nationally, believers must accomplish personally.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 18#2 Kings 18 . 4|2 Kings 18:4]] - Hezekiah destroys idols throughout Judah
- [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 18#1 Kings 18 . 21|1 Kings 18:21]] - Elijah: "How long will you waver between two opinions?"
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 6#Deuteronomy 6 . 14|Deuteronomy 6:14]] - "Do not follow other gods"

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Hezekiah's destruction of idols (2 Kgs 18:4); Josiah's reforms (2 Kgs 23)
- **FROM OT**:
  - The consistent OT call to exclusive worship of YHWH
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 5#1 John 5 . 21|1 John 5:21]] - "Dear children, keep yourselves from idols"
  - [[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 5|Colossians 3:5]] - "Put to death... greed, which is idolatry"
**Type Classification**: NT Application - Believers continue the reform Hezekiah began

**Christological Connection**: Hezekiah destroyed physical idols, including the bronze serpent that had become "Nehushtan." Christ destroys the power of all idols by revealing the true God in Himself. Believers, united to Christ, participate in His kingly work of purifying worship. Where Hezekiah removed false objects of worship, Christians remove false objects of trust, allegiance, and affection—anything that competes with Christ's lordship.
