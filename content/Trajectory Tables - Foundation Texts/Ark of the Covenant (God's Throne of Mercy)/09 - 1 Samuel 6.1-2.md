### [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 6#1 Samuel 6 . 1|1 Samuel 6:1-2]]

**Context**: The ark of the covenant had been captured by the Philistines at Aphek (1 Samuel 4). After seven months in Philistine territory—during which it brought plagues upon Ashdod, Gath, and Ekron—the Philistines desperately seek to return it. Their question "What shall we do with the ark of the LORD?" reveals the ark's power: even in captivity among pagans, God's presence brought judgment on false gods (Dagon fell before it, 5:3-4) and afflicted His enemies. The ark's "exile" and return prefigure a pattern of humiliation and restoration.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- אֲרוֹן יְהוָה (*aron YHWH*) - "ark of the LORD" (the throne-presence of Israel's God)
- שִׁבְעָה חֳדָשִׁים (*shiv'ah chodashim*) - "seven months" (period of captivity/exile)
- מַה־נַּעֲשֶׂה (*mah-na'aseh*) - "what shall we do?" (helplessness before the holy)
- מְקוֹמוֹ (*meqomo*) - "its place" (proper location with God's people)

**Christological Connection**: The ark's seven-month exile among the Philistines typifies Christ's humiliation. The symbol of God's throne-presence was captured by pagans, seemingly defeated—yet it brought judgment on its captors' false god and afflicted them with plagues. So Christ, delivered into enemy hands, seemingly defeated at the cross, in fact conquered the powers and "disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 2#Colossians 2 . 15|Colossians 2:15]]). The ark's return to Israel prefigures Christ's resurrection and exaltation. Psalm 132, which recalls finding the ark "in the fields of Jaar," became a messianic psalm celebrating God's enthronement in Zion—ultimately fulfilled when Christ, risen and ascended, took His seat at God's right hand.
