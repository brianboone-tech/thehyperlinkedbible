### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11]]

**Context**: In the closing exhortations of Hebrews, the author draws a final Day of Atonement typology. Those sin offerings whose blood "is brought into the holy places by the high priest" are burned "outside the camp" (citing Leviticus 16:27). The application follows in v. 12: "So Jesus also suffered outside the gate." The location of Jesus' crucifixion—outside Jerusalem's walls—fulfills the Day of Atonement pattern, connecting Christ's death to the ark/mercy seat typology where the blood was applied.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ζῴων (*zōōn*) - "animals" (the sin offering sacrifices)
- εἰσφέρεται (*eispheretai*) - "is brought" (present passive, regular action)
- τὰ ἅγια (*ta hagia*) - "the holy places" (Most Holy Place with the ark)
- κατακαίεται (*katakaiētai*) - "are burned" (complete destruction outside the camp)

**Christological Connection**: This verse closes the loop on ark/mercy seat typology by connecting the mercy seat ritual to Christ's crucifixion site. The blood sprinkled on the mercy seat came from animals burned outside the camp; Christ's blood applied to the heavenly mercy seat came from His suffering "outside the gate." The parallel is comprehensive: blood inside for atonement → body outside for disposal; Christ's blood offered in heaven → Christ's body crucified outside Jerusalem. The exhortation to "go to him outside the camp" (13:13) calls believers to share Christ's shame, leaving behind the "camp" of old covenant security to embrace the reproach of the cross. The ark's mercy seat required sacrifice outside; access to God's throne of grace requires identifying with Christ's shame.
