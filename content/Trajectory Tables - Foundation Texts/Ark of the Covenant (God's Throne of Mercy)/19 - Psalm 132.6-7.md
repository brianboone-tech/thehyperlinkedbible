### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 132#Psalm 132 . 6|Psalm 132:6-7]]

**Context**: Psalm 132 celebrates David's vow to find a resting place for the ark and God's covenant promises to David. Verses 6-7 recall the ark's recovery from obscurity: "We heard of it in Ephrathah; we found it in the fields of Jaar" (Kiriath-jearim, where the ark remained for twenty years after the Philistines returned it). The psalm transforms historical narrative into liturgical worship, calling Israel to "worship at his footstool"—the ark as the base of God's throne where His feet rest.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- אֶפְרָתָה (*Ephratah*) - (Bethlehem region, near where ark news was heard)
- שְׂדֵי־יָעַר (*sedey-ya'ar*) - "fields of Jaar" (Kiriath-jearim = "city of forests")
- מִשְׁכְּנוֹתָיו (*mishkenotayv*) - "his dwelling places" (where God tabernacles)
- הֲדֹם רַגְלָיו (*hadom raglayv*) - "his footstool" (ark as base of divine throne)

**Christological Connection**: The ark as God's "footstool" reveals it as the earthly extension of God's heavenly throne—where heaven touches earth. Psalm 132's liturgical celebration of finding the ark anticipates the greater finding: "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]). Where Israel found the ark in obscure Kiriath-jearim and brought it to Jerusalem with great joy, God's people now find their rest not in a golden box but in Christ Himself. The call to "worship at his footstool" is now fulfilled as believers, through Christ's blood, boldly approach "the throne of grace" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 16|Hebrews 4:16]]). What the ark symbolized—God's accessible presence—Christ embodies permanently. The ark that wandered from Shiloh to Philistia to Kiriath-jearim to Jerusalem points to Christ who journeyed from heaven to earth to cross to throne.
