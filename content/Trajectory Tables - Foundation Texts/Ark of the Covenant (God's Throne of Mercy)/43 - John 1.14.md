### [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]]

**Context**: John's prologue reaches its climax: "The Word became flesh and dwelt (ἐσκήνωσεν) among us, and we have seen his glory." The verb σκηνόω ("to pitch a tent, tabernacle") deliberately echoes the Hebrew שָׁכַן (shakan) used for God's dwelling in the tabernacle. John declares that what the tabernacle and its ark symbolized—God's glory dwelling among His people—has now become incarnate reality in Christ. The tabernacle was temporary and moveable; the incarnation is permanent and personal.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ὁ λόγος (*ho logos*) - "the Word" (divine self-expression, eternal Son)
- σὰρξ ἐγένετο (*sarx egeneto*) - "became flesh" (genuine incarnation)
- ἐσκήνωσεν (*eskēnōsen*) - "tabernacled/dwelt" (aorist, God pitched His tent among us)
- δόξαν (*doxan*) - "glory" (visible manifestation of divine presence, like Shekinah)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]] is the theological heart of ark/tabernacle typology. The ark dwelt in the tabernacle as God's throne-presence; Christ is "the exact imprint of [God's] nature" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]) dwelling in human flesh. "We have seen his glory" parallels Israel seeing God's glory fill the tabernacle ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 40#Exodus 40 . 34|Exodus 40:34]])—but now the glory is personal, touchable, crucifiable. The ark behind the veil was unapproachable except by the high priest once a year; in Christ, "the veil... that is, his flesh" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 20|Hebrews 10:20]]) opens access to all believers. Where the ark contained the law, manna, and Aaron's rod, Christ is the law fulfilled, the true bread from heaven, and the resurrection. The tabernacle was shadow; "the Word became flesh" is substance.
