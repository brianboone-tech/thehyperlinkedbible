### [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 27|Leviticus 16:27]]

**Context**: After the blood of the Day of Atonement sacrifices has been applied to the mercy seat and altar, the carcasses of the bull and goat must be taken "outside the camp" and completely burned—hides, flesh, and dung. This disposal "outside the camp" is unique to sin offerings whose blood was brought into the Most Holy Place. The location signified removal from God's presence and sacred space, bearing the contamination of the sins that had been transferred to the animal.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- הַפָּר (*happar*) - "the bull" (sin offering for the priest)
- הַשָּׂעִיר (*hassa'ir*) - "the goat" (sin offering for the people)
- מִחוּץ לַמַּחֲנֶה (*michutz lamachaneh*) - "outside the camp" (place of uncleanness and exclusion)
- יִשְׂרְפוּ (*yisrefu*) - "they shall burn" (complete destruction)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 11|Hebrews 13:11-12]] explicitly connects this verse to Christ's crucifixion. Jesus "suffered outside the gate" of Jerusalem, fulfilling the Day of Atonement pattern. The sin offerings whose blood purified the mercy seat had to be burned outside the camp; Christ, whose blood purifies the heavenly sanctuary, was crucified outside Jerusalem's walls. "Outside the camp" signified bearing sin's curse and separation from the holy community—precisely what Christ endured as He became "a curse for us" ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]]). The author of Hebrews then exhorts believers to "go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured" (13:13), participating in Christ's shame now while awaiting "the city that is to come" (13:14).
