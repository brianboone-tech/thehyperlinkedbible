### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 2#1 John 2 . 1|1 John 2:1-2]]

**Context**: After discussing sin and confession (1:8-10), John offers assurance: believers have "an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous." He is also "the propitiation (ἱλασμός) for our sins"—language drawn from the Day of Atonement ritual where blood was applied to the mercy seat (ἱλαστήριον, from the same word family). John combines two ark/mercy seat functions: the high priest entering God's presence as mediator, and the blood satisfying divine justice on the mercy seat.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- παράκλητον (*paraklēton*) - "advocate" (one called alongside to help, legal defender)
- δίκαιον (*dikaion*) - "righteous" (Christ's qualification to represent us)
- ἱλασμός (*hilasmos*) - "propitiation" (sacrifice that turns away wrath, cognate with "mercy seat")
- ὅλου τοῦ κόσμου (*holou tou kosmou*) - "the whole world" (scope exceeds Israel)

**Christological Connection**: First [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 1|John 2:1-2]] unites advocacy and propitiation—the two functions centered on the ark/mercy seat. The high priest entered God's presence once a year to apply blood to the mercy seat; Christ "always lives to intercede" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]) in God's presence perpetually. The earthly ἱλαστήριον (mercy seat) was where propitiation occurred through animal blood; Christ Himself is the ἱλασμός (propitiation) through His own blood. John's statement that He is propitiation "not for ours only but also for the whole world" shows escalation: the Day of Atonement covered Israel's sins; Christ's blood is sufficient for all humanity. The ark's mercy seat was localized, temporal, and exclusive to Israel; Christ's propitiation is universal, eternal, and offered to "whoever believes."
