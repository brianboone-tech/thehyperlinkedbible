### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 28|Hebrews 12:28-29]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4442|G4442]] πῦρ (*pyr*) - fire
- [[Lexicon/G2654|G2654]] καταναλίσκω (*katanalisko*) - to consume utterly
- [[Lexicon/G2999|G2999]] λατρεία (*latreia*) - service, worship
- [[Lexicon/G2124|G2124]] εὐλάβεια (*eulabeia*) - reverence, godly fear
- [[Lexicon/G1169|G1169]] δειλός (*deilos*) - awe
- [[Lexicon/G2143|G2143]] εὐπρέπεια (*euarestos*) - acceptable

**Context**: Hebrews 12:28-29: "Therefore let us be grateful for receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, and thus let us offer to God acceptable worship (λατρεύωμεν εὐαρέστως), with reverence and awe (μετὰ εὐλαβείας καὶ δέους), for our God is a consuming fire (πῦρ καταναλίσκον)."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Direct quotation of [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 4#Deuteronomy 4 . 24|Deuteronomy 4:24]]
- Connects Sinai theophany to New Covenant worship
- God's character unchanged - still consuming fire

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 4#Deuteronomy 4 . 24|Deuteronomy 4:24]] - "The LORD your God is a consuming fire"
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 9#Leviticus 9 . 24|Leviticus 9:24]] - Fire consumes offering
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 10#Leviticus 10 . 1|Leviticus 10:1-2]] - Fire consumes Nadab and Abihu
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 18|Exodus 19:18]] - Mount Sinai wrapped in fire
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 31|Hebrews 10:31]] - "Fearful thing to fall into hands of living God"

**Type Classification**: NT Application - Fire imagery applied to New Covenant worship disposition

**Christological Connection**: The author of Hebrews reminds New Covenant believers that God remains "a consuming fire." Grace does not diminish holiness. The fire that consumed offerings, judged Nadab and Abihu, and fell at Carmel reveals God's unchanging character. Through Christ, believers approach this consuming fire without being consumed - not because fire has changed, but because Christ has absorbed the fire of judgment for us. "Reverence and awe" remain appropriate because the fire remains real.
