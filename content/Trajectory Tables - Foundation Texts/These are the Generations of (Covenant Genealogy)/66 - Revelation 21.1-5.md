### [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-5]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2537#G2537|καινός]] (*kainos*) - "new, fresh, unprecedented" (qualitatively new, not merely recent)
- [[Lexicon/G3772#G3772|οὐρανός]] (*ouranos*) - "heaven, sky, heavens"
- [[Lexicon/G1093#G1093|γῆ]] (*gē*) - "earth, land, world"
- [[Lexicon/G4413#G4413|πρῶτος]] (*prōtos*) - "first, former, previous"
- [[Lexicon/G3928#G3928|παρῆλθεν]] (*parēlthen*) - "passed away, departed, ceased to exist"
- [[Lexicon/G4633#G4633|σκηνή]] (*skēnē*) - "tabernacle, dwelling, tent"
- [[Lexicon/G2992#G2992|λαός]] (*laos*) - "people, nation, people of God"
- [[Lexicon/G2537#G2537|καινὰ ποιῶ πάντα]] (*kaina poiō panta*) - "I make all things new"

**Context**: Revelation 21:1-5 presents the consummation of redemptive history, when God creates "a new heaven and a new earth" (v. 1), fulfilling the trajectory from Genesis 2:4's "generations of the heavens and the earth." After describing final judgment (20:11-15), John sees the eternal state where God dwells permanently with his people. The vision includes: (1) new creation replacing the old (v. 1), (2) new Jerusalem descending from heaven (v. 2), (3) God's permanent dwelling with humanity (v. 3), (4) elimination of all curse effects (v. 4), (5) God's creative declaration making all things new (v. 5). This passage concludes the biblical narrative's arc: from creation (Genesis 1-2) through fall and redemption (Genesis 3–Revelation 20) to new creation (Revelation 21-22).

**Christological Connection**: John's vision of "a new heaven and a new earth" completes the genealogical and cosmic trajectory begun in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 4|Genesis 2:4]]'s "generations of the heavens and the earth," demonstrating that Christ accomplishes universal renewal fulfilling all covenant promises from Adam through Abraham to the new covenant. [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 4|Genesis 2:4]] applied the toledot formula cosmically—even creation has "generations"—and [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 1|Revelation 21:1-5]] completes the cosmic narrative: from first heaven and earth to new heaven and earth, from paradise lost (Genesis 3) to paradise restored (Revelation 21-22), from human genealogies tracing the covenant line to the Lamb's book of life recording the redeemed ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 27|Revelation 21:27]]). The trajectory demonstrates Christ as both Creator and Re-creator. Paul declares: "all things were created through him and for him... and in him all things hold together" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 1#Colossians 1 . 16|Colossians 1:16-17]])—the same Christ through whom the first heaven and earth came into being creates the new heaven and earth. Hebrews teaches that Christ upholds "the universe by the word of his power" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]), and now that same creative word declares: "Behold, I am making all things new" (v. 5). The genealogical narrowing from all humanity (Adam) to one family (Abraham) to one nation (Israel) to one tribe (Judah) to one person (Christ) reverses in new creation into universal expansion: "a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]) dwelling in renewed creation. The promise to Abraham—"in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 12#Genesis 12 . 3|Genesis 12:3]])—finds cosmic fulfillment as the nations "walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 24|Revelation 21:24]]). The covenant formula echoing from Sinai onward—"I will be their God, and they shall be my people"—reaches climactic realization: "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God" (v. 3). This fulfills the pattern established in wilderness tabernacle ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8]]), anticipated in Christ's incarnation—"the Word became flesh and dwelt (*eskēnōsen*, 'tabernacled') among us" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 14|John 1:14]])—and consummated in permanent divine-human fellowship. Christ's death accomplishes the purification enabling this dwelling: "the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]), making believers fit dwelling places for God ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 3#1 Corinthians 3 . 16|1 Corinthians 3:16]]), culminating in eternal communion. The elimination of death, mourning, crying, and pain (v. 4) reverses Genesis 3's curse—accomplished through Christ who "abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" ([[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 1#2 Timothy 1 . 10|2 Timothy 1:10]]). As death entered through the first Adam's sin ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 12|Romans 5:12]]), death is defeated through the last Adam's obedience: "in Christ shall all be made alive" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 22|1 Corinthians 15:22]]). The declaration "the former things have passed away" (v. 4) fulfills Paul's teaching: "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 5#2 Corinthians 5 . 17|2 Corinthians 5:17]])—individual regeneration anticipates cosmic regeneration. God's creative word "I am making all things new" (v. 5) demonstrates that the same power regenerating individuals will renew the entire cosmos. The genealogical theme culminates here: from "these are the generations of the heavens and the earth" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 2#Genesis 2 . 4|Genesis 2:4]]) through "the book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]]) to those "born, not of blood... but of God" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 12|John 1:12-13]]), finally to the Lamb's book of life containing names of the redeemed ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 27|Revelation 21:27]]). The trajectory reveals God's consistent purpose: creating a people for his own possession to dwell with him eternally. Christ accomplishes this through his incarnation (uniting God and humanity), death (removing sin's barrier), resurrection (defeating death), ascension (preparing a place), and return (consummating all things). The pattern from Adam's failed generations (sin and death) to Abraham's promised generations (blessing to nations) to Christ's spiritual generations (new birth) to new creation (cosmic renewal) demonstrates redemptive history's Christ-centered arc. As Genesis opened with God creating heavens and earth, Revelation closes with God creating new heavens and new earth—the Alpha and Omega ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 6|Revelation 21:6]]) completing what he began, bringing many sons to glory ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 10|Hebrews 2:10]]), and establishing his kingdom without end through Christ, the heir of all things ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 2|Hebrews 1:2]]).
