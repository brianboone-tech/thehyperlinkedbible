### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 1|Genesis 5:1-3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H5612#H5612|סֵפֶר]] (*sēp̄er*) - "book, document, written record"
- [[Lexicon/H8435-8436#H8435|תּוֹלְדוֹת]] (*tôlᵉḏôṯ*) - "generations, descendants, genealogy, account"
- [[Lexicon/H0120-0121#H0120|אָדָם]] (*'āḏām*) - "Adam, man, mankind"
- [[Lexicon/H1823#H1823|דְּמוּת]] (*dᵉmûṯ*) - "likeness, resemblance, pattern"
- [[Lexicon/H6754#H6754|צֶלֶם]] (*ṣelem*) - "image, form, representation"
- [[Lexicon/H1254-1255#H1254|בָּרָא]] (*bārā'*) - "to create" (divine creative activity)

**Context**: Genesis 5:1-3 introduces the second toledot formula in Genesis, transitioning from creation's genealogy to humanity's genealogy. Unlike the first toledot (2:4) which traced the cosmos, this one traces the covenant line from Adam through Seth to Noah. The phrase "This is the book of the generations of Adam" opens a genealogy emphasizing both image-bearing continuity and mortality's intrusion. Verse 1 recalls Genesis 1:27 ("in the likeness of God he made him"), while verse 3 shows Adam fathering Seth "in his own likeness, after his image"—image transmission continuing despite sin's entrance.

**Christological Connection**: Genesis 5's genealogy of Adam finds its ultimate fulfillment in Christ, the last Adam who reverses the first Adam's failure. The phrase "This is the book of the generations of Adam" anticipates Matthew's opening: "The book of the genealogy (*genesis*) of Jesus Christ" ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]]). Both trace covenant lineage; Adam's genealogy leads forward to Noah and eventually Abraham and David, while Christ's genealogy looks backward through the same line, demonstrating Jesus as the culmination of covenant history. The image-bearing theology in [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 1|Genesis 5:1-3]] reaches consummation in Christ. Adam was made "in the likeness of God" (v. 1), and he fathered Seth "in his own likeness" (v. 3), transmitting both image and corruption. Christ, however, is "the image of God" ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 4#2 Corinthians 4 . 4|2 Corinthians 4:4]]) perfectly, without sin's distortion—"the exact imprint of his nature" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 3|Hebrews 1:3]]). Believers are being renewed "after the image of its creator" ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 10|Colossians 3:10]]) in Christ, the image-bearing purpose Adam failed to fulfill. Paul develops this in Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15: Adam brought sin and death to all his descendants; Christ brings righteousness and life to all united to him. The genealogy's relentless refrain "and he died" demonstrates the wages of Adam's sin, but Christ "abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" ([[Readable Bible/55 - 2 Timothy/2 Timothy 1#2 Timothy 1 . 10|2 Timothy 1:10]]). Where Genesis 5 records "and he died" nine times (excluding Enoch), Revelation celebrates that in new creation "death shall be no more" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 4|Revelation 21:4]]). Enoch's unique fate—"he was not, for God took him" ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 24|Genesis 5:24]])—prefigures Christ's resurrection and ascension, as Hebrews notes: "By faith Enoch was taken up so that he should not see death" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 5|Hebrews 11:5]]), foreshadowing believers' future transformation. The pattern established in Genesis 5—covenant genealogy leading through chosen seed—culminates in Christ, "the last Adam" who becomes "a life-giving spirit" ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 15#1 Corinthians 15 . 45|1 Corinthians 15:45]]). First Corinthians 15:49 applies [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 5#Genesis 5 . 3|Genesis 5:3]] redemptively: "Just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we shall also bear the image of the man of heaven." Believers participate in new genealogy—"born again" ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 23|1 Peter 1:23]]) not through Adam's corrupted image but through Christ's perfect image. The "book of the generations of Adam" traces humanity under sin and death; the "Lamb's book of life" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 21#Revelation 21 . 27|Revelation 21:27]]) records those redeemed through Christ. Luke's genealogy traces Jesus back to "Adam, the son of God" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 3#Luke 3 . 38|Luke 3:38]]), showing Christ as representative head of renewed humanity, just as Adam was head of fallen humanity. The trajectory from "the generations of Adam" to "the genealogy of Jesus Christ" demonstrates God's faithfulness preserving covenant lineage through millennia of "and he died" entries, until the one came who would conquer death. Christ fulfills the pattern: as Adam fathered a son in his image, God the Father presents the Son who is his exact image; as Adam's descendants inherited corruption and death, Christ's descendants (believers) inherit righteousness and life; as the genealogy narrowed from Adam to Seth to Noah, God's redemptive plan narrows to Christ, then expands to "a great multitude that no one could number" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 9|Revelation 7:9]]) from all nations—the true generations of the last Adam, bearing his image in righteousness and holiness.
