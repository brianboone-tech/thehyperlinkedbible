### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 12#Numbers 12 . 10|Numbers 12:10-15]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6879|H6879]] צָרַע (*tsara*) - "to be leprous, have skin disease"
- [[Lexicon/H7950|H7950]] שֶׁלֶג (*sheleg*) - "snow" (white as snow)
- [[Lexicon/H3637|H3637]] כָּלַם (*kalam*) - "to be humiliated, ashamed"
- [[Lexicon/H7495|H7495]] רָפָא (*rapha*) - "to heal"
- [[Lexicon/H5462|H5462]] סָגַר (*sagar*) - "to shut up, confine"

**Context**: After God vindicates Moses' unique authority (vv. 6-9), His cloud lifts from the tabernacle and Miriam is suddenly leprous "white as snow." Aaron, realizing the gravity of their sin, pleads with Moses not to let sin's consequences fall upon them. Moses intercedes briefly but powerfully: "O God, please heal her!" God responds with seven-day exclusion from camp before healing - a measured discipline showing both justice and mercy.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Leprosy functioned as divine judgment for presumptuous sin ([[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 26#2 Chronicles 26 . 16|2 Chronicles 26:16-21]] - King Uzziah struck for entering temple)
- Seven-day period mirrors purification rituals (Leviticus 13-14) but also pubic humiliation as deterrent
- This becomes paradigmatic warning: [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 24#Deuteronomy 24 . 8|Deuteronomy 24:8-9]] commands Israel to "remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam"
- Despite judgment, [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 6#Micah 6 . 4|Micah 6:4]] still honors Miriam among Israel's leaders - grace after discipline

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - Levitical law for leprosy purification (Leviticus 13-14); leprosy as covenant curse for disobedience ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 26#Leviticus 26 . 16|Leviticus 26:16]], 21)
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 24#Deuteronomy 24 . 8|Deuteronomy 24:8-9]] - memorializes Miriam's leprosy as warning
  - [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 5#2 Kings 5 . 27|2 Kings 5:27]] - Gehazi struck with leprosy for greed
  - [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 26#2 Chronicles 26 . 16|2 Chronicles 26:16-21]] - Uzziah struck for presumption
- **FROM NT**:
  - Leprosy as symbol of sin's defilement ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 8#Matthew 8 . 1|Matthew 8:1-4]]); Christ's power to cleanse leprosy demonstrates authority over sin; exclusion from camp prefigures excommunication discipline ([[Readable Bible/46 - 1 Corinthians/1 Corinthians 5#1 Corinthians 5 . 5|1 Corinthians 5:5]], 13)
**Type Classification**: Backward-looking Providential Type. The incident functions typologically as warning against presumption and illustration of redemptive discipline.

**Christological Connection**: Moses' intercession for Miriam who sinned against him points to Christ's intercession for sinners ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 34|Romans 8:34]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). Just as Miriam needed Moses' mediation for healing, sinners need Christ's advocacy before God. Leprosy as visible defilement symbolizes sin's corrupting power; Christ's power to cleanse lepers ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 8#Matthew 8 . 2|Matthew 8:2-3]]) demonstrates His authority to forgive sins. Exclusion from camp followed by restoration prefigures church discipline's goal: restoration of sinner to fellowship ([[Readable Bible/47 - 2 Corinthians/2 Corinthians 2#2 Corinthians 2 . 5|2 Corinthians 2:5-8]]). God's fatherly discipline of Miriam points to [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 12#Hebrews 12 . 5|Hebrews 12:5-11]] - proof of sonship, producing righteousness.
