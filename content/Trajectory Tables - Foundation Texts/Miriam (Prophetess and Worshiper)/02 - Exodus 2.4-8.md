### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 2#Exodus 2 . 4|Exodus 2:4-8]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H269|H269]] אָחוֹת (*achot*) - "sister"
- [[Lexicon/H5975|H5975]] עָמַד (*amad*) - "to stand, take one's stand"
- [[Lexicon/H3045|H3045]] יָדַע (*yada*) - "to know"

**Context**: Pharaoh has commanded all Hebrew baby boys be thrown into the Nile. Moses' mother hides him three months, then places him in a waterproofed basket among the reeds. Miriam (unnamed here, but identified as Moses' sister in 15:20) stations herself at a distance to observe what happens. When Pharaoh's daughter discovers the baby and shows compassion, Miriam cleverly offers to find a Hebrew nurse - bringing Moses' own mother to care for him.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Miriam's protective watchfulness over Moses anticipates her later role as co-leader with Moses and Aaron ([[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 6#Micah 6 . 4|Micah 6:4]])
- Her quick thinking and initiative foreshadow her prophetic gifting ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 20|Exodus 15:20]])
- The pattern of a faithful woman preserving God's deliverer appears throughout Scripture (Rahab protecting spies, Esther intervening for her people)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - God's promise to preserve Israel ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 13|Genesis 15:13-14]]); pattern of God using women to preserve His purposes ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] - seed of woman)
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 15#Exodus 15 . 20|Exodus 15:20]] - identifies her as "Miriam the prophetess, Aaron's sister"
  - [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 6#Micah 6 . 4|Micah 6:4]] - honors her among Israel's leadership
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 7#Acts 7 . 20|Acts 7:20-21]] - retells Moses' preservation
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 23|Hebrews 11:23]] - commends parents' faith in hiding Moses
**Type Classification**: Backward-looking Providential Type (Miriam's protective role recognized typologically from later revelation of her prophetic office).

**Christological Connection**: Miriam's watchful protection of Moses, who would become Israel's mediator, foreshadows the church's calling to protect and proclaim Christ's work. Just as Miriam ensured Moses' preservation in hostile Egypt, the church guards gospel truth in hostile world. The pattern of faithful women preserving God's redemptive purposes finds ultimate fulfillment in Mary, through whom the greater Moses - Christ - entered the world.
