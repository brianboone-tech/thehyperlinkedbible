### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6135|H6135]] עֲקָרָה (ʿăqārâ) - "barren" - Sarai was barren
- [[Lexicon/H2029|H2029]] הָרָה (hārâ) - "conceive" (implied negation) - she had no children
- [[Lexicon/H3206|H3206]] יֶלֶד (yeleḏ) - "child" - no children

**Context**: This single verse, almost parenthetical in the genealogy of Terah, introduces the fundamental crisis that will drive the next twenty-five years of narrative. Sarai's barrenness is stated as bare fact: "But Sarai was barren; she had no children." In a world where children were wealth, legacy, and blessing, barrenness was catastrophic. Yet this very impossibility becomes the canvas on which God will paint His greatest promises.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 11#Genesis 11 . 30|Genesis 11:30]] establishes the pattern that recurs with Rebekah (25:21), Rachel (30:1), Hannah ([[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 1#1 Samuel 1 . 2|1 Samuel 1:2]]), and the Shunammite woman ([[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 4#2 Kings 4 . 14|2 Kings 4:14]])
- The barrenness motif connects to God's sovereign control over the womb ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 30#Genesis 30 . 2|Genesis 30:2]])
- This sets up the contrast between human impossibility and divine power throughout Genesis

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 15|Genesis 3:15]] - The seed promise now seems threatened by barrenness
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]] - The barren one called to rejoice
  - [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 113#Psalm 113 . 9|Psalm 113:9]] - God makes the barren dwell as a joyful mother
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 19|Romans 4:19]] - "the lifelessness of Sarah's womb"
  - [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 27|Galatians 4:27]] - "Rejoice, O barren woman"
**Type Classification**: Providential (Sarah's historical barrenness), Forward-Looking (sets up promise of supernatural intervention)

**Christological Connection**: Sarah's dead womb typifies the death from which Christ brings life. Just as Isaac came from a womb "as good as dead" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 11#Hebrews 11 . 12|Hebrews 11:12]]), so Christ brings life from the dead. The pattern of life-from-death established here reaches its culmination in the resurrection. Moreover, Christ Himself comes through Sarah's line—the child of promise leads ultimately to the Child of Promise, Jesus, born of a virgin when natural conception was impossible.
