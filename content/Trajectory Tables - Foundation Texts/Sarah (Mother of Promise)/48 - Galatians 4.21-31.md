### [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 21|Galatians 4:21-31]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3814|G3814]] παιδίσκη (paidiskē) - "slave woman" - one by the slave woman

- [[Lexicon/G1242|G1242]] διαθήκη (diathēkē) - "covenant" - represent two covenants
- [[Lexicon/G507|G507]] ἄνω (anō) - "above" - the Jerusalem above
- [[Lexicon/G4723|G4723]] στεῖρα (steira) - "barren" - Rejoice, O barren woman

**Context**: Paul is combating Judaizers who insist Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the law. He uses the Sarah-Hagar narrative allegorically to show that seeking justification through law is bondage (Hagar/Sinai), while trusting in promise is freedom (Sarah/Jerusalem above). The barren woman who produces many children (Isaiah 54:1) represents the new covenant community.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Paul reads Genesis 16-21 (Sarah and Hagar) through [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 1|Isaiah 54:1]] (the barren woman rejoicing)
- The Sinai covenant is identified with Hagar/bondage
- The promise covenant is identified with Sarah/freedom
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 21#Genesis 21 . 10|Genesis 21:10]] ("cast out the slave woman") becomes typological instruction

**Type Classification**: NT Typological Use (Sarah as type of the new covenant; her children as believers)

**Christological Connection**: Sarah's freedom is ultimately Christ's freedom. Believers are "free" because Christ has set them free ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 5#Galatians 5 . 1|Galatians 5:1]]). The "Jerusalem above" is the heavenly city where Christ reigns. The barren woman's many children are the fruit of Christ's redeeming work—Gentiles included in the covenant family. The expulsion of Hagar/Ishmael pictures the supersession of law-based religion by Christ's grace. Paul's allegory thus places Sarah at the center of the gospel: salvation by promise, not performance; by grace through faith in Christ, not by works of the law.
