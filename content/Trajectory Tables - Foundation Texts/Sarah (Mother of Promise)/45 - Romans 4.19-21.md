### [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 19|Romans 4:19-21]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G770|G770]] ἀσθενέω (astheneō) - "be weak" - without weakening in faith
- [[Lexicon/G3498|G3498]] νεκρόω (nekroō) - "dead/deadened" - his own body as good as dead
- [[Lexicon/G3517|G3517]] νέκρωσις (nekrōsis) - "deadness" - the deadness of Sarah's womb
- [[Lexicon/G1252|G1252]] διακρίνω (diakrinō) - "waver/doubt" - he did not waver through unbelief
- [[Lexicon/G1743|G1743]] ἐνδυναμόω (endynamoō) - "strengthen" - was strengthened in his faith
- [[Lexicon/G4135|G4135]] πληροφορέω (plērophoreō) - "fully convinced" - fully persuaded

**Context**: Paul is proving that justification comes by faith, not works. Abraham is the prime example: he believed God's promise and "it was credited to him as righteousness" (4:3). In verses 19-21, Paul focuses on the content of Abraham's faith: he believed that God could give life to his "as good as dead" body and Sarah's dead womb. Abraham's faith was in "the God who gives life to the dead and calls into being what does not yet exist" (4:17).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 4#Romans 4 . 19|Romans 4:19-21]] interprets [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 15#Genesis 15 . 6|Genesis 15:6]] in light of Genesis 17-18
- Paul sees Abraham's faith as specifically resurrection faith—faith in God's power over death
- The pattern connects to [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 51#Isaiah 51 . 2|Isaiah 51:2]] where Israel is told to "look to Sarah who gave you birth"

**Type Classification**: NT Theological Use (Sarah's womb as type of death; Isaac's conception as type of resurrection)

**Christological Connection**: Paul draws explicit connection: the faith that believed God could bring Isaac from Sarah's dead womb is the same faith that believes God raised Jesus from the dead (4:24). Sarah's barrenness typifies death; Isaac's conception typifies resurrection. The pattern of life-from-death established in Genesis reaches its fulfillment in Christ's resurrection. Believers are justified by the same kind of faith Abraham had—faith in the God who gives life to the dead.
