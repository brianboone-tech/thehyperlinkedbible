**Context**: Three visitors appear to Abraham at Mamre—the LORD and two angels. Abraham provides hospitality, and then the LORD delivers the most specific promise yet: "I will surely return to you about this time next year, and Sarah your wife will have a son." Sarah, listening at the tent entrance, laughs in disbelief. The LORD's response—"Is anything too hard for the LORD?"—becomes one of Scripture's foundational affirmations of divine omnipotence.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- This theophany (God appearing in human form) anticipates later theophanies
- The question "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" (הֲיִפָּלֵא מֵיהוָה דָּבָר) echoes in [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 32#Jeremiah 32 . 17|Jeremiah 32:17]], 27 during Jerusalem's fall
- Sarah's laughter becomes Isaac's name—God transforms doubt into joy

**Type Classification**: Providential (the visitation sovereignly arranged), Forward-Looking (specific promise with timeline points to fulfillment)

**Christological Connection**: The question "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" finds its ultimate answer in Christ's virgin birth and resurrection. Gabriel echoes this promise to Mary: "For no word from God will ever fail" ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 37|Luke 1:37]]). What was impossible—a barren ninety-year-old conceiving—points to what was even more impossible: a virgin conceiving, the dead rising. Christ is the answer to the question: nothing is too hard for God, including salvation, resurrection, and eternal life. The theophany itself—God appearing in human form—anticipates the incarnation.
