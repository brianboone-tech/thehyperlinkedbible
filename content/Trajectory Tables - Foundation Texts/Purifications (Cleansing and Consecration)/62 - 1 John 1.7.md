### [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4001-4100#G4043|περιπατέω]] (*peripateō*) - "to walk, conduct one's life"
- [[Lexicon/G5401-5500#G5457|φῶς]] (*phōs*) - "light"
- [[Lexicon/G2801-2900#G2842|κοινωνία]] (*koinōnia*) - "fellowship, communion, sharing"
- [[Lexicon/G0101-0200#G0129|αἷμα]] (*haima*) - "blood"
- [[Lexicon/G2401-2500#G2511|καθαρίζω]] (*katharizō*) - "to cleanse, purify, make clean"
- [[Lexicon/G0201-0300#G0266|ἁμαρτία]] (*hamartia*) - "sin"
- [[Lexicon/G3801-3900#G3956|πᾶς]] (*pas*) - "all, every, the whole"

**Context**: John writes to believers, establishing the foundation of Christian fellowship: walking in the light as God is in the light. This walking in light produces two results: fellowship with one another and continuous cleansing through Jesus's blood. The verse balances corporate (fellowship) and individual (cleansing) dimensions of Christian life.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 51#Psalms 51 . 2|Psalm 51:2]] (wash me thoroughly from iniquity), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 1#Isaiah 1 . 18|Isaiah 1:18]] (though sins are scarlet, white as snow)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 17#Leviticus 17 . 11|Leviticus 17:11]] (blood makes atonement), [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25]] (I will sprinkle clean water)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]] (blood of Christ purifies conscience), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 5|Revelation 1:5]] (freed us from sins by his blood), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]] (washed robes in Lamb's blood)

**Christological Connection**: First [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 7|John 1:7]]'s declaration—"the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin"—is the New Testament's definitive statement of Christ's cleansing power. Where Levitical blood cleansed ceremonially ("sanctify for the purification of the flesh," [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 13|Hebrews 9:13]]), Christ's blood cleanses spiritually and completely. The present tense verb "cleanses" (katharizei) indicates continuous action—as believers walk in the light, confessing sin, Christ's blood keeps on cleansing. This isn't repeated sacrifice (Christ died once, [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 28|Hebrews 9:28]]) but continuous application of His once-for-all sacrifice. The phrase "from all sin" encompasses both sins already committed (past cleansing) and sins presently confessed (ongoing cleansing). Where the bronze basin required priests to wash repeatedly before each service ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 19|Exodus 30:19-20]]), Christ's blood provides perpetual cleansing for believers who daily approach God's throne. The connection to "walking in the light" shows cleansing isn't automatic but appropriated through transparency—confessing sin rather than hiding it. This fulfills Ezekiel's prophecy: "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 25|Ezekiel 36:25]]). John later emphasizes the necessity of confession: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]). The cleansing power flows from Christ's identity—"Jesus his Son"—fully God (possessing infinite merit) and fully human (capable of shedding blood as substitute). Revelation portrays the ultimate application: the redeemed "have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 7#Revelation 7 . 14|Revelation 7:14]]), standing before God's throne perfectly cleansed. What Levitical washings accomplished temporarily, Christ's blood accomplishes eternally.
