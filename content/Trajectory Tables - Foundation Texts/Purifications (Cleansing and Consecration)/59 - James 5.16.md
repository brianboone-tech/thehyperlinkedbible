### [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 5#James 5 . 16|James 5:16]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G1801-1900#G1843|ἐξομολογέω]] (*exomologeō*) - "to confess, admit, acknowledge"
- [[Lexicon/G0201-0300#G0266|ἁμαρτία]] (*hamartia*) - "sin"
- [[Lexicon/G0101-0200#G0240|ἀλλήλων]] (*allēlōn*) - "one another, mutually"
- [[Lexicon/G2101-2200#G2172|εὔχομαι]] (*euchomai*) - "to pray, wish"
- [[Lexicon/G2301-2400#G2390|ἰάομαι]] (*iaomai*) - "to heal, cure"
- [[Lexicon/G1701-1800#G1754|ἐνεργέω]] (*energeō*) - "to be active, work, be effective"
- [[Lexicon/G1301-1400#G1342|δίκαιος]] (*dikaios*) - "righteous, just"
- [[Lexicon/G2401-2500#G2479|ἰσχύω]] (*ischyō*) - "to have power, be strong, be able"

**Context**: James instructs believers on prayer's power, particularly in the context of sickness and spiritual restoration. He commands mutual confession and intercessory prayer, promising healing for those who confess and pray. This practice reflects the corporate nature of purification—sin affects the community, requiring communal cleansing through confession and prayer.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 5#Leviticus 5 . 5|Leviticus 5:5]] (confess the sin he has committed), [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 5#Numbers 5 . 7|Numbers 5:7]] (confess their sin and make full restitution)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalms 32#Psalms 32 . 5|Psalm 32:5]] (I acknowledged my sin... and you forgave), [[Readable Bible/20 - Proverbs/Proverbs 28#Proverbs 28 . 13|Proverbs 28:13]] (Whoever confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]] (If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive), [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 18#Matthew 18 . 15|Matthew 18:15-17]] (if your brother sins, go and tell him his fault)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/59 - James/James 5#James 5 . 16|James 5:16]]'s command for mutual confession and prayer fulfills the Old Testament pattern of cleansing through confession. Where Levitical confession accompanied sacrifice for atonement ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 5#Leviticus 5 . 5|Leviticus 5:5-6]]), Christian confession rests on Christ's completed sacrifice. Believers confess not to earn forgiveness but to appropriate what Christ has already accomplished. John assures, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]). God's faithfulness guarantees cleansing for those who confess. James adds the horizontal dimension—confession "to one another" acknowledges sin's corporate impact. Sin doesn't merely affect the individual but wounds the body of Christ, requiring communal cleansing through mutual confession and prayer. This reflects Jesus's teaching on church discipline and restoration ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 18#Matthew 18 . 15|Matthew 18:15-20]]), where confession leads to reconciliation. The promise of healing shows confession's comprehensive benefits—spiritual restoration, relational reconciliation, sometimes physical healing. The phrase "pray for one another" indicates intercession's importance—believers bear each other's burdens ([[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 6#Galatians 6 . 2|Galatians 6:2]]), including praying for one another's cleansing from sin. Christ is the ultimate intercessor who "always lives to make intercession" for His people ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). Believers share in this intercessory ministry, praying for each other's sanctification. The walking "in the light" that John describes ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]) includes transparency about sin—confessing to God and to fellow believers, experiencing cleansing through Christ's blood applied by the Spirit. What Levitical washings accomplished ceremonially for individuals, Christ's blood accomplishes spiritually for the corporate body through mutual confession, prayer, and forgiveness.
