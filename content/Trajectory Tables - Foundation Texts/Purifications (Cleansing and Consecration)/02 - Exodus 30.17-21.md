### [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 30#Exodus 30 . 17|Exodus 30:17-21]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H3501-3600#H3595|כִּיּוֹר]] (*kiyyôr*) - "basin, laver"
- [[Lexicon/H5001-5100#H5178|נְחֹשֶׁת]] (*nəḥōšet*) - "bronze, copper"
- [[Lexicon/H7201-7300#H7364|רָחַץ]] (*rāḥaṣ*) - "to wash, bathe"
- [[Lexicon/H4101-4200#H4191|מוּת]] (*mût*) - "to die"
- [[Lexicon/H6901-7000#H6944|קֹדֶשׁ]] (*qōdeš*) - "holiness, sacred thing"
- [[Lexicon/H2601-2700#H2706|חֹק]] (*ḥōq*) - "statute, ordinance"
- [[Lexicon/H5701-5800#H5769|עוֹלָם]] (*ʿôlām*) - "forever, perpetuity"

**Context**: God commands Moses to make a bronze basin between the tent of meeting and the altar, where Aaron and his sons must wash their hands and feet before approaching the altar or entering the tent. This washing is not optional but a matter of life and death—"so that they may not die" (v. 20). The basin becomes a perpetual ordinance for Israel's priesthood.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 29#Exodus 29 . 4|Exodus 29:4]] (priests washed at consecration), [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 40#Exodus 40 . 30|Exodus 40:30-32]] (basin placed and used)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 7#1 Kings 7 . 38|1 Kings 7:38]] (Solomon's ten bronze basins), [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 4#2 Chronicles 4 . 6|2 Chronicles 4:6]] (basins for washing burnt offerings)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/56 - Titus/Titus 3#Titus 3 . 5|Titus 3:5]] (washing of regeneration), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 22|Hebrews 10:22]] (bodies washed with pure water), [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 5|Revelation 1:5]] (washed from sins in His blood)

**Christological Connection**: The bronze basin's requirement—"wash... that they may not die"—points to humanity's desperate need for cleansing to approach God. What the basin accomplished ceremonially and temporarily, Christ's blood accomplishes spiritually and eternally. The basin stood between the altar (where sin was atoned) and the tent (where God dwelt), teaching that cleansing follows atonement. Christ's death provides both: His blood shed on the cross (the ultimate altar) cleanses believers, enabling them to enter God's presence (the ultimate holy place). The writer of Hebrews connects these: "let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 22|Hebrews 10:22]]). Where the bronze basin cleansed hands and feet (external), Christ's blood cleanses conscience (internal). The priests washed before every service; believers daily appropriate Christ's cleansing through confession: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 9|1 John 1:9]]). The basin's perpetual ordinance finds fulfillment in Christ's perpetual priesthood—He "always lives to make intercession" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]), continually cleansing His people. John sees the ultimate fulfillment: "To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood... to him be glory and dominion forever and ever" ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 1#Revelation 1 . 5|Revelation 1:5-6]]). The bronze basin warned of death without washing; Christ promises eternal life through His cleansing: "the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 1#1 John 1 . 7|1 John 1:7]]).
