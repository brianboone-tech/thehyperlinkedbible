### [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 26#2 Chronicles 26 . 18|2 Chronicles 26:18]]

**Context**: King Uzziah, after decades of faithful rule and military success, became proud and entered the temple to burn incense on the altar of incense—a privilege reserved exclusively for Aaron's descendants. Eighty brave priests confronted him: "You have no right to offer incense to the LORD. Only the priests, the descendants of Aaron, are consecrated to burn incense." This direct rebuke echoes the judgment of Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:1-2) and the principle of Leviticus 10:10-11. Uzziah's punishment—leprosy until death—demonstrates that unauthorized approach to God's altar of incense, even by a king, brings severe consequences.

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- לְהַקְטִיר (*lehaqtir*) - "to burn incense" (hiphil infinitive, causative)
- מְקֻדָּשִׁים (*mequddashim*) - "consecrated ones" (passive participle, set apart by God)
- מָעַלְתָּ (*ma'alta*) - "you have acted unfaithfully" (covenant violation)
- מִקְדָּשׁ (*miqdash*) - "sanctuary" (holy place where incense altar stood)

**Christological Connection**: Uzziah's failure highlights why Christ's priesthood must follow Melchizedek's order rather than Aaron's. As king of Judah, Uzziah could never legitimately serve as priest—the roles were separate under the Mosaic covenant. But Christ, "a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]]), legitimately combines both offices. He is the true King-Priest who burns acceptable incense—His own intercession—before the Father. What Uzziah arrogantly attempted and was struck down for, Christ accomplishes perfectly. The leprous king cut off from God's house contrasts with Christ who "always lives to intercede" for us ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]), bringing us into God's presence rather than being expelled from it.
