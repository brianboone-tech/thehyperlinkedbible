### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 1|Hebrews 7:1-4]]

**Context**: The author of Hebrews expounds Genesis 14:17-20, demonstrating that Melchizedek's priesthood is superior to Aaron's. Three arguments establish this: (1) Melchizedek blessed Abraham, and the greater blesses the lesser; (2) Abraham tithed to Melchizedek, acknowledging his superior priesthood; (3) Melchizedek has no recorded genealogy, beginning, or end—"like the Son of God, he remains a priest for all time." This exposition grounds Christ's eternal priesthood in a pre-Mosaic type, explaining how Jesus can be high priest though from Judah rather than Levi.

**Greek Key Terms**:
- ἱερεὺς τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ ὑψίστου (*hiereus tou theou tou hypsistou*) - "priest of God Most High" (unique designation)
- βασιλεὺς δικαιοσύνης (*basileus dikaiosynēs*) - "king of righteousness" (etymology of Melchizedek)
- βασιλεὺς εἰρήνης (*basileus eirēnēs*) - "king of peace" (etymology of Salem)
- μένει ἱερεὺς εἰς τὸ διηνεκές (*menei hiereus eis to diēnekes*) - "remains a priest perpetually" (eternal priesthood)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 1|Hebrews 7:1-4]] is the key NT passage explaining why Christ can serve as priest despite being from Judah. The Levitical priests who burned incense at the golden altar were mortal, succeeding one another in death, their intercession always interrupted. But Christ's priesthood "according to the order of Melchizedek" means He is "a priest forever" ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 4|Psalm 110:4]]). What the daily incense offerings symbolized—continual intercession ascending to God—Christ fulfills perfectly because He "always lives to make intercession" ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 25|Hebrews 7:25]]). The twice-daily incense at the golden altar was a shadow; Christ's unceasing advocacy before the Father is the substance. Melchizedek's combination of king and priest anticipates Christ's royal priesthood, where our King intercedes and our Priest reigns.
