### [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 15#1 Samuel 15 . 22|1 Samuel 15:22-28]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H8085|H8085]] שָׁמַע (*shamaʿ*) - "to hear, obey"
- [[Lexicon/H2077|H2077]] זֶבַח (*zevach*) - "sacrifice"
- [[Lexicon/H7181|H7181]] קָשַׁב (*qashav*) - "to attend, give heed"
- [[Lexicon/H2459|H2459]] חֵלֶב (*chelev*) - "fat" (choicest part)
- [[Lexicon/H352|H352]] אַיִל (*ʾayil*) - "ram"

- [[Lexicon/H7167|H7167]] קָרַע (*qaraʿ*) - "to tear"

**Context**: After defeating Amalek, Saul spares King Agag and the best livestock, claiming they were preserved for sacrifice. Samuel's response is the theological climax of Saul's story: "Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams. For rebellion is as the sin of divination, and stubbornness is as iniquity and idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, He has also rejected you from being king." When Samuel turns to leave, Saul seizes his robe and it tears—a visual prophetic sign that the kingdom is torn from Saul.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The principle "obedience better than sacrifice" echoes throughout the prophets: [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 6#Hosea 6 . 6|Hosea 6:6]] ("I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice"), [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 6#Micah 6 . 6|Micah 6:6-8]], [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 1#Isaiah 1 . 11|Isaiah 1:11-17]].
- The comparison of rebellion to divination/idolatry shows Saul's disobedience as covenant violation equivalent to worshiping other gods—the fundamental sin.
- The verb מָאַס ("reject") appears twice: Saul rejected God's word (v. 23); therefore God rejected Saul (v. 26). Measure for measure.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Exodus 19:5 ("if you will indeed obey My voice"), 1 Samuel 13:13-14 (first rejection announcement), Deuteronomy 25:17-19 (command to destroy Amalek)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 6#Hosea 6 . 6|Hosea 6:6]] (mercy not sacrifice), [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 16|Psalm 51:16-17]] (broken spirit, contrite heart), [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 1#Isaiah 1 . 11|Isaiah 1:11-17]] (useless sacrifices)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 9#Matthew 9 . 13|Matthew 9:13]] (Jesus quotes [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 6#Hosea 6 . 6|Hosea 6:6]]), [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 12#Mark 12 . 33|Mark 12:33]] (scribe affirms obedience over sacrifice), [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 5|Hebrews 10:5-7]] (Christ: "a body You have prepared for Me...I have come to do Your will")

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly arranged contrast); Forward-Looking (the principle of obedience over sacrifice anticipates Christ's perfect obedience); Direct (explicit prophetic judgment).

**Christological Connection**: Saul's rejection for disobedience illuminates Christ's acceptance for obedience. (1) **Obedience Better Than Sacrifice**: Christ offers both—perfect obedience ("I have come to do Your will," Heb 10:7) as His sacrifice. What Saul sundered, Christ unites. (2) **Rejection for Rejection**: Saul rejected God's word and was rejected; Christ was "rejected by men but chosen by God" (1 Pet 2:4)—He submitted to rejection that we might be accepted. (3) **Torn Kingdom**: The kingdom torn from Saul is given to David's line, ultimately to Christ. (4) **Religious Cover for Disobedience**: Saul used sacrifice-language to excuse sin; Christ exposes such hypocrisy (Matt 23). (5) **Complete Obedience**: Where Saul partially obeyed (destroying some, keeping some), Christ obeyed completely—"obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" (Phil 2:8).
