### [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 8#1 Samuel 8 . 5|1 Samuel 8:5-7, 19-20]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4428|H4428]] מֶלֶךְ (*melek*) - "king"
- [[Lexicon/H8199|H8199]] שָׁפַט (*shaphat*) - "to judge, govern"
- [[Lexicon/H1471|H1471]] גּוֹי (*goy*) - "nation"
- [[Lexicon/H3988|H3988]] מָאַס (*maʾas*) - "to reject, despise"
- [[Lexicon/H4427|H4427]] מָלַךְ (*malak*) - "to reign, be king"

**Context**: After Samuel's sons prove corrupt (8:1-3), Israel's elders demand a king. Their request is doubly motivated: (1) Samuel's failed succession, and (2) desire to be "like all the nations" (כְּכָל־הַגּוֹיִם). God interprets this demand as rejection not of Samuel but of YHWH Himself: "They have rejected Me from being king over them" (8:7). Though God grants the request, He instructs Samuel to warn of royal oppression (8:10-18). The people persist: "No! But there shall be a king over us, that we also may be like all the nations" (8:19-20).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- This passage reverses the Sinai vision of [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 5|Exodus 19:5-6]]: "You shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation." Israel was to be distinct from the nations; now they want conformity.
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 17#Deuteronomy 17 . 14|Deuteronomy 17:14-20]] anticipated kingship but required a king chosen by YHWH who would not "multiply horses" or "turn away." Israel's demand bypasses these covenant stipulations.
- The pattern of rejecting divine rule echoes [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 14#Numbers 14 . 4|Numbers 14:4]]: "Let us choose a leader and go back to Egypt."

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Exodus 19:5-6 (kingdom of priests), Deuteronomy 17:14-20 (law of the king), Judges 8:22-23 (Gideon refuses kingship: "the LORD will rule over you")
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 12#1 Samuel 12 . 12|1 Samuel 12:12-17]] (Samuel rehearses their sin), [[Readable Bible/28 - Hosea/Hosea 13#Hosea 13 . 10|Hosea 13:10-11]] ("I gave you a king in My anger")
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 21|Acts 13:21]] (Paul notes God "gave them Saul"), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 15|John 19:15]] ("We have no king but Caesar"—same rejection pattern)

**Type Classification**: Providential (sovereignly permitted as precursor to redemptive history); Forward-Looking (anticipates the failure of human kingship and need for divine king).

**Christological Connection**: Israel's rejection of YHWH's kingship establishes the negative pattern Christ will invert. (1) **"Like the Nations" Rejected**: Israel wanted conformity; Christ offers the kingdom "not of this world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 18#John 18 . 36|John 18:36]]). (2) **Visible vs. Invisible**: Israel wanted a visible king; Christ's kingship is spiritual before it becomes visible. (3) **Pattern of Rejection**: "We have no king but Caesar" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 15|John 19:15]]) repeats 1 Samuel 8's error—rejecting divine kingship for human alternatives. (4) **True King Anticipated**: The failure of human kingship (Saul, then Solomon's successors) creates longing for the Messiah-King who rules by divine right, not popular demand. (5) **God Grants, Then Supersedes**: As God gave Saul then replaced him with David, so the pattern prepares for David's greater Son who reigns forever.
