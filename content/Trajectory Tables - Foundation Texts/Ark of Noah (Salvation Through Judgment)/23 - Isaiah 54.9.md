### [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 9|Isaiah 54:9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4325|H4325]] מַיִם (*mayim*) - "waters" (waters of Noah, unique phrase)
- [[Lexicon/H7650|H7650]] שָׁבַע (*šāḇaʿ*) - "to swear, take an oath" (I swore/have sworn)
- [[Lexicon/H5674|H5674]] עָבַר (*ʿāḇar*) - "to pass over, cross" (never again cover the earth)
- [[Lexicon/H7107|H7107]] קָצַף (*qāṣap̄*) - "to be angry, wrathful" (not to be angry with you)
- [[Lexicon/H1605|H1605]] גָּעַר (*gāʿar*) - "to rebuke, reprove" (never to rebuke you)

**Context**: Isaiah 54:9 is the only OT passage outside Genesis to reference "the waters of Noah" (מֵי־נֹחַ, mê-nōaḥ), making it the canonical interpretation of the flood's theological significance. God speaks to exiled Israel, comparing His restoration promise to the Noahic covenant: "To me this is like the days of Noah, when I swore that the waters of Noah would never again cover the earth. So now I have sworn not to be angry with you, never to rebuke you again." The context is God's covenant faithfulness despite Israel's unfaithfulness (vv. 7-8 acknowledge divine discipline, but vv. 9-10 promise permanent mercy).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 11|Genesis 9:11]] records God's oath: "Never again will all life be cut off by the waters of a flood; never again will there be a flood to destroy the earth." Isaiah invokes this as precedent for God's oath-bound promise to limit future wrath against Israel.
- The progression: Genesis establishes universal covenant (no more flood); Isaiah applies the covenant principle to particular people (no more wrath against restored Israel); NT extends to individual believers (no condemnation for those in Christ, [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]).
- [[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 33#Jeremiah 33 . 20|Jeremiah 33:20-21]], 25-26 similarly appeals to creation covenant's stability as guarantee of Davidic covenant's permanence.

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (Isaiah identifies flood as pattern/precedent) and **Forward-Looking** (Jesus and Peter reinterpret it eschatologically).

**Christological Connection**: (1) **Oath-bound promise** - God's sworn commitment not to be angry fulfilled in Christ who bore the anger ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 4|Isaiah 53:4-6]], 10); (2) **Covenant of peace** - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 10|Isaiah 54:10]] calls it "covenant of peace," fulfilled in Christ who is our peace ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14]]); (3) **Mountains may depart** - Even if creation shakes, God's covenant love remains steadfast ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 10|Isaiah 54:10]]), grounded in Christ's finished work; (4) **Atonement precedes assurance** - The sequence (suffering servant chapter 53, then covenant security chapter 54) shows atonement enables eternal security; (5) **Waters of Noah** - Just as God swore the flood would never return, He swears that those in Christ will never face condemnation ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]])—Christ has borne the flood of wrath.
