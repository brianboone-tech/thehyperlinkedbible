### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 11|Genesis 7:11-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4599|H4599]] מַעְיָן (*maʿyān*) - "spring, fountain" (fountains of the great deep burst forth)
- [[Lexicon/H699|H699]] אֲרֻבָּה (*ʾărubbâ*) - "window, floodgate" (floodgates of heaven opened)
- [[Lexicon/H4229|H4229]] מָחָה (*māḥâ*) - "to wipe out, blot out, destroy" (every living thing wiped out)
- [[Lexicon/H7604|H7604]] שָׁאַר (*šāʾar*) - "to remain, be left over" (only Noah remained, v. 23)
- [[Lexicon/H1398|H1398]] גָּבַר (*gāḇar*) - "to prevail, be strong" (waters prevailed, increased greatly)

**Context**: Genesis 7:11-24 describes the flood's execution: "All the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the floodgates of the heavens were opened" (v. 11). The judgment is comprehensive—"everything on dry land that had the breath of life in its nostrils died" (v. 22). Yet simultaneously: "The waters flooded the earth for 150 days... But God lifted up the ark high above the earth, so that it rose above the mountains" (vv. 18-20, 24). The central paradox: the same waters that killed the wicked saved the righteous. Verse 23 provides the stark summary: "Every living thing on the face of the earth was wiped out... Only Noah was left, and those with him in the ark."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 38#Job 38 . 8|Job 38:8-11]] reflects on God's control over waters: "Who shut up the sea behind doors when it burst forth from the womb... when I said, 'This far you may come and no farther'?"
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 29#Psalm 29 . 10|Psalm 29:10]] declares: "The LORD sits enthroned over the flood; the LORD is enthroned as King forever" (מַבּוּל, mabbûl - the technical term for Noah's flood used only in Genesis and Psalm 29).
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 93#Psalm 93 . 3|Psalm 93:3-4]] echoes the rising waters: "The seas have lifted up, O LORD... mightier than the breakers of the sea—the LORD on high is mighty."

**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged event) and **Forward-Looking** (explicitly interpreted by NT as pattern for baptism and eschatological judgment).

**Christological Connection**: (1) **Waters of judgment borne by the ark** - Christ bore the full flood of God's wrath against sin, sheltering believers from condemnation ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]); (2) **Salvation through judgment** - Just as Noah was saved through (not from) the waters, believers are saved through Christ's death (judgment on sin) in baptism ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 3#1 Peter 3 . 21|1 Peter 3:21]]); (3) **Rising above judgment** - The ark lifted higher as judgment intensified prefigures believers raised with Christ above condemnation ([[Readable Bible/51 - Colossians/Colossians 3#Colossians 3 . 1|Colossians 3:1-3]]); (4) **Universal destruction, particular salvation** - Only those in the ark survived, prefiguring Christ as the only name by which we must be saved ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 4#Acts 4 . 12|Acts 4:12]]); (5) **One hundred fifty days** - The waters prevailed 150 days before receding, possibly prefiguring the complete satisfaction of God's justice through Christ's sufficient atonement.
