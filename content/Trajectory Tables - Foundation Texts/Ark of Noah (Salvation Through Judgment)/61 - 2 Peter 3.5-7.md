### [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 5|2 Peter 3:5-7]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G3056|G3056]] λόγος (*logos*) - "word" (by God's word... by the same word)
- [[Lexicon/G4921|G4921]] συνιστάω (*synistaō*) - "to stand together, be established, exist" (the heavens existed)
- [[Lexicon/G2626|G2626]] κατακλύζω (*kataklyzō*) - "to inundate, flood, deluge" (was deluged)
- [[Lexicon/G622|G622]] ἀπόλλυμι (*apollymi*) - "to destroy, perish, lose" (was destroyed)
- [[Lexicon/G5083|G5083]] τηρέω (*tēreō*) - "to keep, reserve, preserve" (are reserved for fire)
- [[Lexicon/G2920|G2920]] κρίσις (*krisis*) - "judgment, decision, condemnation" (day of judgment)
- [[Lexicon/G684|G684]] ἀπώλεια (*apōleia*) - "destruction, perdition, waste" (destruction of the ungodly)

**Context**: 2 Peter 3:5-7 uses the flood as proof of God's ability and intention to judge the world. Peter writes against scoffers who say, "Where is the promise of his coming?" (v. 4). He responds by establishing a threefold pattern: (1) Creation by God's word (vv. 5-6a), (2) Judgment by water (v. 6b), (3) Future judgment by fire (v. 7). The argument: the same God who created by His word and destroyed by water will judge by fire. Those who doubt final judgment willfully ignore the flood's precedent. The parallel is precise: "the world that then existed was deluged with water and perished... the heavens and earth that now exist are reserved for fire, being kept for the day of judgment and destruction of the ungodly."

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 7#Genesis 7 . 11|Genesis 7:11-24]] records the flood's execution: "All the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the floodgates of the heavens were opened" (v. 11), resulting in universal destruction except for those in the ark.
- [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 11|Genesis 9:11]] establishes God's covenant: "Never again will all life be cut off by the waters of a flood; never again will there be a flood to destroy the earth." Peter's argument presupposes this covenant—no more water judgment, but fire judgment remains.
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 54#Isaiah 54 . 9|Isaiah 54:9]] invokes "the waters of Noah" as covenant precedent. Peter applies the same event differently: Isaiah emphasizes covenant mercy; Peter emphasizes judgment certainty.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 7:11-24 (flood destroyed world); Genesis 9:11 (never again by flood); Isaiah 66:15-16 (the LORD will come in fire to judge); Malachi 4:1 (day is coming, burning like a furnace)
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 37|Matthew 24:37-39]] - as days of Noah, so coming of Son of Man
  - [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 17#Luke 17 . 26|Luke 17:26-30]] - likewise as in days of Lot—fire and sulfur
  - [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 27|Hebrews 10:27]] - fearful expectation of judgment and fury of fire
  - [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 9|Revelation 20:9]] - , 11-15 (fire from heaven devours, great white throne judgment
- **RELATED NT**: 2 Peter 2:5 (did not spare ancient world, but preserved Noah); 2 Peter 3:9-10 (Lord is patient... but day of the Lord will come like a thief)
**Type Classification**: **Providential Type** (sovereignly arranged historical event) explicitly applied by Peter as **Forward-Looking** (eschatological pattern for final judgment).

**Christological Connection**: (1) **Christ as Creator and Judge** - The same word that created (v. 5) and judged by water (v. 6) will judge by fire (v. 7). Christ is identified as the creative Word ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 1|John 1:1-3]]) and coming Judge; (2) **Ark prefigures Christ** - As only those in the ark were saved from water judgment, only those "in Christ" will be saved from fire judgment ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]]); (3) **Noahic covenant limits but doesn't eliminate judgment** - [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 9#Genesis 9 . 11|Genesis 9:11]] promises no more flood, but Peter reveals fire judgment remains. Christ's cross fully satisfies wrath for believers, but unbelievers face stored-up wrath ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 2#Romans 2 . 5|Romans 2:5]]); (4) **Pattern of discrimination** - The flood saved eight, destroyed all others. Final judgment will discriminate between sheep and goats ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 25#Matthew 25 . 31|Matthew 25:31-46]]); (5) **God's patience precedes judgment** - [[Readable Bible/61 - 2 Peter/2 Peter 3#2 Peter 3 . 9|2 Peter 3:9]], 15 explain the delay: "The Lord is not slow... but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish." As God waited 120 years in Noah's day ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 6#Genesis 6 . 3|Genesis 6:3]]), He waits now—but judgment will come suddenly when the door closes.
