### [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 64|Matthew 26:64]]

**Context**: Under oath before the Sanhedrin, Jesus responds to the high priest's question "Are you the Christ, the Son of God?" with a qualified affirmation followed by prophetic declaration: "From now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven." This combines Psalm 110:1 (seated at right hand) with Daniel 7:13 (Son of Man coming with clouds), claiming divine authority and vindication. The high priest tears his robes, declaring blasphemy—Jesus has claimed equality with God by announcing His session at God's right hand.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 64|Matthew 26:64]] represents Jesus's explicit claim to sit at God's right hand, applying [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] directly to Himself before the highest Jewish authority. Where [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 44|Matthew 22:44]] used [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] to prove Messiah must be divine, [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 26#Matthew 26 . 64|Matthew 26:64]] declares Jesus is that divine Messiah who will occupy God's right hand. This claim costs Jesus His life—the high priest tears his robes and declares blasphemy, leading directly to crucifixion—yet also establishes the theological foundation for NT proclamation of Christ's session.

The phrase "from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power" marks the transition from incarnate humiliation to exalted session. Though Jesus appears defeated at trial, His vindication is certain and imminent—"from now on" (*ap' arti*) indicates the process begins immediately. Death leads to resurrection; resurrection to ascension; ascension to session at God's right hand. The present participle "seated" (*kathēmenon*) indicates ongoing state—Jesus will be continuously seated at the right hand, not temporarily but permanently established in authority.

This session claim directly confronts the standing ministry pattern of Levitical priesthood. Where priests stood daily offering repeated sacrifices ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]]), Jesus claims He will sit at God's right hand—a position proving finished work and established authority. The high priest, representative of standing Levitical priesthood, condemns Jesus for claiming to sit at God's right hand, not recognizing that Jesus's session would make the standing priesthood obsolete. The irony is profound: the high priest who stands in earthly temple condemns the great High Priest who will sit in heavenly sanctuary.

The trajectory from [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 44|Matthew 22:44]] to 26:64 shows progression: (1) **Scriptural proof** (22:44)—[[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] proves Messiah is divine; (2) **Personal claim** (26:64)—Jesus applies [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] to Himself; (3) **Vindication** (28:1-20)—resurrection proves claim true; (4) **Ascension and session** ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 9|Acts 1:9-11]]; 2:34-35)—Jesus takes His place at God's right hand; (5) **Apostolic proclamation** (epistles)—the church preaches Christ's session. Jesus's claim before the Sanhedrin—"you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power"—finds fulfillment in His resurrection, ascension, and ongoing session, where He sits not temporarily but "until" all enemies are made His footstool ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]]), exercising the authority and ministry that standing priests could never achieve.
