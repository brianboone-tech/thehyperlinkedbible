**Context**: The author declares the "main point" of Hebrews' argument: "we have such a high priest, one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, a minister in the true tabernacle that the Lord set up, not man." This central declaration combines Christ's high priesthood with His session at God's right hand, establishing that the seated Christ ministers in the heavenly sanctuary, making the earthly Levitical system obsolete.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 1|Hebrews 8:1-2]] contains the author's explicit declaration of the epistle's "main point" (*kephalaion*): "we have such a high priest, one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, a minister in the holy places, in the true tabernacle that the Lord pitched, not man." This central truth combines Christ's high priesthood with His session at God's right hand, establishing the revolutionary concept of a seated priest ministering in heavenly sanctuary.

The phrase "seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven" fulfills [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]]'s invitation: "Sit at my right hand." Christ has taken His seat—the aorist "sat down" (*ekathisen*) indicates completed, definitive action. The right hand position signifies supreme honor and authority. The addition "of the throne of the Majesty" emphasizes Christ shares God's own throne, exercising divine sovereignty. The location "in heaven" contrasts with earthly sanctuary where Levitical priests ministered.

The revolutionary claim is that this seated Christ is "a minister in the holy places, in the true tabernacle." No Levitical priest ever sat while ministering—standing was absolute requirement for temple service ([[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 10#Deuteronomy 10 . 8|Deuteronomy 10:8]]; 18:5; [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 5#2 Chronicles 5 . 14|2 Chronicles 5:14]] assumes standing as normal). Yet Christ sits while ministering, proving His priesthood's unique superiority. The contrast is devastating: Levitical priests stood daily in earthly sanctuary offering repeated sacrifices ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]]); Christ sits in heavenly sanctuary having offered one sacrifice for all time ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 12|Hebrews 10:12]]).

The phrase "true tabernacle that the Lord pitched, not man" establishes Christ's ministry location as heavenly reality, not earthly shadow. The earthly tabernacle, though divinely ordained, was constructed by human hands according to heavenly pattern ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 25#Exodus 25 . 8|Exodus 25:8-9]], 40). The heavenly sanctuary is "true" (*alēthinēs*)—the reality of which earthly was copy ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 5|Hebrews 8:5]]; 9:24). Christ ministers in this true sanctuary, at the Father's right hand, in the immediate presence of God.

This combines the two dimensions of Psalm 110: royal (v. 1, sit at my right hand) and priestly (v. 4, priest forever after order of Melchizedek). Christ is the seated King-Priest, exercising royal authority from the throne while performing priestly ministry in the sanctuary. Where Levitical system kept these separate—priests weren't kings, kings weren't priests (cf. King Uzziah's judgment, [[Readable Bible/14 - 2 Chronicles/2 Chronicles 26#2 Chronicles 26 . 16|2 Chronicles 26:16-21]])—Christ combines them perfectly, fulfilling Melchizedek's unique royal-priestly office ([[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 14#Genesis 14 . 18|Genesis 14:18]]).

The standing-sitting contrast is central. The author's identification of this as the "main point" signals its importance for the entire argument. If Christ sits while Levitical priests stand, Christ's priesthood is fundamentally different and superior. Sitting proves: (1) **Completed sacrifice**—work finished, not ongoing; (2) **Accepted offering**—God invited Him to sit, proving satisfaction; (3) **Permanent priesthood**—no need for succession or replacement; (4) **Superior ministry**—effective intercession based on perfect atonement; (5) **Divine authority**—shared throne, not servant posture.

[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 8#Hebrews 8 . 1|Hebrews 8:1-2]] establishes the foundation for the rest of chapter 8: Christ's seated priesthood mediates a better covenant (v. 6), making the old covenant obsolete (v. 13). The seated high priest in the true tabernacle transcends the standing priests in the earthly copy, accomplishing through His finished work and ongoing intercession what the Levitical system could never achieve—eternal redemption, complete forgiveness, and confident access to God's presence.
