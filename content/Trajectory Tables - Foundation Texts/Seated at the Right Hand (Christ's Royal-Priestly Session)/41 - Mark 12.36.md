### [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 12#Mark 12 . 36|Mark 12:36]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G0801-0900#G0846|αὐτὸς Δαυὶδ]] (*autos Dauid*) - "David himself"
- [[Lexicon/G2001-2100#G2036|εἶπεν]] (*eipen*) - "said"
- [[Lexicon/G1601-1700#G1722|ἐν τῷ πνεύματι τῷ ἁγίῳ]] (*en tō pneumati tō hagiō*) - "in the Holy Spirit, by the Holy Spirit"
- [[Lexicon/G2001-2100#G2036|Εἶπεν ὁ κύριος τῷ κυρίῳ μου]] (*Eipen ho kyrios tō kyriō mou*) - "The Lord said to my Lord"
- [[Lexicon/G2501-2600#G2521|Κάθου]] (*Kathou*) - "sit" (imperative)
- [[Lexicon/G1101-1200#G1188|ἐκ δεξιῶν μου]] (*ek dexiōn mou*) - "at my right hand"
- [[Lexicon/G2101-2200#G2193|ἕως ἂν θῶ]] (*heōs an thō*) - "until I place, until I put"
- [[Lexicon/G5201-5300#G5270|ὑποπόδιον τῶν ποδῶν σου]] (*hypopodion tōn podōn sou*) - "footstool for your feet"

**Context**: Mark records Jesus's teaching in the temple during Passion week, where He quotes Psalm 110:1 to demonstrate that the Messiah must be greater than David. The emphasis on David speaking "in the Holy Spirit" establishes scriptural authority, while the quotation itself proves the Messiah is David's divine Lord. The large crowd "listened to him with delight" (v. 37), recognizing Jesus's superior teaching contrasted with the scribes' inadequate understanding.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] (sit at my right hand), [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 7#2 Samuel 7 . 12|2 Samuel 7:12-16]] (Davidic covenant)
- **FROM OT**: None (Jesus's teaching)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 44|Matthew 22:44]] (parallel), [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 20#Luke 20 . 42|Luke 20:42]] (parallel), [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62|Mark 14:62]] (Jesus applies to Himself at trial), [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 34|Acts 2:34-35]] (Peter's Pentecost sermon)

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 12#Mark 12 . 36|Mark 12:36]] presents Jesus's public teaching on [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]], establishing scriptural foundation for His later claim at trial ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62|Mark 14:62]]). By emphasizing David spoke "in the Holy Spirit," Jesus establishes divine authority of the testimony: the Holy Spirit inspired David to call his descendant "my Lord," proving the Messiah's divine nature. The quotation itself—"Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool"—establishes the Messiah's exalted session at God's right hand.

This seated position is fundamentally royal and authoritative. The command "sit" (*kathou*) at God's right hand places the Messiah in the position of highest honor, sharing the divine throne. The right hand is the place of power, favor, and authority in ancient Near Eastern royal protocol. The duration "until I make your enemies your footstool" indicates the session is purposeful and victorious—the Messiah sits in sovereign authority while God subdues all opposition. The footstool imagery depicts total victory: defeated enemies prostrate beneath the Messiah's feet.

Mark's account, though parallel to [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 22#Matthew 22 . 44|Matthew 22:44]], uniquely emphasizes "in the Holy Spirit," stressing divine inspiration. This makes Jesus's argument irrefutable: if the Holy Spirit inspired David to call Messiah "my Lord," the testimony is divinely authoritative. The scribes' inability to answer (implied in Jesus's rhetorical question) reveals their inadequate Christology—they recognize Messiah as David's son but cannot explain why David calls him "Lord." Only incarnation resolves this: the eternal Son (David's Lord) became human (David's son) to accomplish redemption, then returned to His rightful place at the Father's right hand.

The standing-sitting contrast, though not explicit here, undergirds the passage. The Messiah's invitation to "sit" at God's right hand contrasts with Levitical priests who stood daily ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 11|Hebrews 10:11]]). The seated position proves completed work and established authority. Jesus's public teaching on this text prepares for His explicit claim at trial ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 14#Mark 14 . 62|Mark 14:62]]), where He will apply [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 110#Psalm 110 . 1|Psalm 110:1]] directly to Himself, resulting in His condemnation for blasphemy—yet also establishing the theological foundation for the church's proclamation of Christ's session at God's right hand, where He sits as royal-priestly mediator accomplishing what standing Levitical ministry could never achieve.
