### [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 4#Joshua 4 . 19|Joshua 4:19-24]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H68|H68]] אֶבֶן (*'eben*) - stone
- [[Lexicon/H2145|H2145]] זִכָּרוֹן (*zikkaron*) - memorial, remembrance
- [[Lexicon/H1537|H1537]] גִּלְגָּל (*Gilgal*) - "circle" (of stones)
- [[Lexicon/H3372|H3372]] יָרֵא (*yare'*) - to fear, revere

**Context**: After crossing the Jordan, twelve men (one from each tribe) take twelve stones from the riverbed and set them up at Gilgal as a perpetual memorial. Joshua explains these stones will prompt future generations to ask their meaning, providing opportunity to testify of God's mighty act.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- Memorial stones follow pattern of altars marking divine encounters (Gen 28:18 - Bethel)
- Anticipates Passover memorial question: "What does this mean?" (Exod 12:26)
- [[Readable Bible/06 - Joshua/Joshua 4#Joshua 4 . 23|Joshua 4:23]] explicitly links to Red Sea: "as he did to the Red Sea"

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Red Sea crossing (Exod 14) - parallel deliverance
- **TO**: Passover memorial (Exod 12:26-27) - children's questions
- **FROM OT**: Gilgal becomes significant site (1 Sam 11:14-15)
- **FROM NT**: Lord's Supper as memorial (1 Cor 11:24-25) - "do this in remembrance"

**Type Classification**: Providential Type (sovereignly arranged memorial practice)

**Christological Connection**: Just as the twelve stones testified to God's Jordan crossing miracle, the Lord's Supper testifies to Christ's death and resurrection. Both memorials call God's people to faith based on completed mighty acts. The stones from the riverbed (place of judgment) set up in the land (place of rest) picture believers taken from death and placed in life.
