### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 3#Hebrews 3 . 7|Hebrews 3:7-4:11]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G2663|G2663]] κατάπαυσις (*katapausis*) - rest, resting place
- [[Lexicon/G4520|G4520]] σαββατισμός (*sabbatismos*) - Sabbath rest (hapax)
- [[Lexicon/G570|G570]] ἀπιστία (*apistia*) - unbelief
- [[Lexicon/G1525|G1525]] εἰσέρχομαι (*eiserchomai*) - to enter

**Context**: The author of Hebrews uses Israel's wilderness failure and Joshua's conquest as a warning and encouragement. The wilderness generation failed to enter Canaan because of unbelief (3:18-19). But Joshua's generation did enter, yet "there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God" (4:9) - indicating Joshua's rest was incomplete and temporary.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 95#Psalm 95 . 7|Psalm 95:7-11]] quoted - David still speaks of "today" entering rest centuries after Joshua
- If Joshua (4:8 uses Ἰησοῦς = "Jesus" in Greek) had given complete rest, no "other day" needed
- Sabbath rest (σαββατισμός) connects creation rest (Gen 2:2) with eschatological rest

**Connections**:
- **TO**: Wilderness rebellion (Num 14; Ps 95) - unbelief prevents entrance
- **TO**: Joshua's conquest (Josh 21:43-45) - rest given but incomplete
- **TO**: Creation Sabbath (Gen 2:2-3) - God's rest as pattern
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-25]] - confidence to enter holy places
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 14#Revelation 14 . 13|Revelation 14:13]] - "rest from their labors"

**Type Classification**: NT Interpretation showing escalation from earthly to spiritual rest

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 8|Hebrews 4:8]] is crucial: "For if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day afterward." The Greek uses Ἰησοῦς (Jesus) for Joshua, highlighting that only the true Jesus gives the rest that Joshua could not. Christ's rest is spiritual, eternal, and complete; Joshua's was earthly, temporary, and incomplete. Believers enter this rest through faith in Christ.
