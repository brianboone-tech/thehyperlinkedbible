### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 51#Psalm 51 . 11|Psalm 51:11]]

**Context**: Written after Nathan confronted David about his adultery with Bathsheba and murder of Uriah, Psalm 51 is David's penitential prayer. Verse 11 reveals David's deepest fear: not merely punishment, but the loss of God's presence and Spirit. Having witnessed Saul's tragic decline after "the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul" (1 Samuel 16:14), David knew the anointing could be removed. His plea "take not Your Holy Spirit from me" acknowledges that his sin threatened the very empowerment that made him king. This is the only explicit Old Testament reference to "Your Holy Spirit" (רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ).

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ (*ruach qodsheka*) - "Your Holy Spirit" (unique OT designation emphasizing divine holiness)
- אַל־תַּשְׁלִיכֵנִי (*al-tashlikheni*) - "cast me not away" (hiphil jussive, forceful removal)
- מִלְּפָנֶיךָ (*millefanekha*) - "from Your presence" (lit. "from before Your face")
- אַל־תִּקַּח (*al-tiqqach*) - "take not" (qal jussive, removal/withdrawal)

**Connections**:
- **TO**:
  - [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 16#1 Samuel 16 . 14|1 Samuel 16:14]] - The departure of God's Spirit from Saul that David witnessed firsthand
  - [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 33#Exodus 33 . 15|Exodus 33:15]] - Moses' similar plea: "If Your presence does not go with us, do not send us up from here"
- **FROM OT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 63#Isaiah 63 . 10|Isaiah 63:10-11]] - Israel "grieved His Holy Spirit" in the wilderness, echoing the possibility of alienating God's Spirit
  - [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 37#Ezekiel 37 . 14|Ezekiel 37:14]] - Promise of Spirit restoration: "I will put My Spirit in you and you will live"
- **FROM NT**:
  - [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 4#Ephesians 4 . 30|Ephesians 4:30]] - "Do not grieve the Holy Spirit"
  - [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 14#John 14 . 16|John 14:16-17]] - Jesus promises the Spirit "will be with you forever" and "will be in you"

**Christological Connection**: David's anguished plea reveals the precariousness of the Spirit's presence in the old covenant. The anointing oil poured on his head could not guarantee permanent empowerment—his sin threatened to forfeit it. But Christ, the ultimate Anointed One, provides what David could not secure for himself. Jesus "baptizes with the Holy Spirit" ([[Readable Bible/41 - Mark/Mark 1#Mark 1 . 8|Mark 1:8]]), and those baptized into Christ receive an anointing that "abides in you" ([[Readable Bible/62 - 1 John/1 John 2#1 John 2 . 27|1 John 2:27]]). The new covenant promise transforms David's fear into assurance: "I will put My Spirit within you" ([[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 36#Ezekiel 36 . 27|Ezekiel 36:27]]). What David pleaded not to lose, believers in Christ possess permanently—sealed with the Spirit as a "guarantee of our inheritance" ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 1#Ephesians 1 . 13|Ephesians 1:13-14]]). David's prayer, born of genuine fear, becomes a prayer of gratitude for those in Christ.
