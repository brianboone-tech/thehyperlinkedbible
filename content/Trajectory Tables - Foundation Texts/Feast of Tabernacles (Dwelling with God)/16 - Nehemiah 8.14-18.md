### [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 8#Nehemiah 8 . 14|Nehemiah 8:14-18]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H4801-4900#H4672|וַיִּמְצְאוּ]] (*wayyimṣəʾû*) - "and they found" (in the law)
- [[Lexicon/H5401-5500#H5521|בַּסֻּכֹּת]] (*bassukkōt*) - "in booths, tabernacles"
- [[Lexicon/H2101-2200#H2132|זַיִת]] (*zayit*) - "olive branches"
- [[Lexicon/H1901-2000#H1918|הֲדַס]] (*hădas*) - "myrtle"
- [[Lexicon/H8401-8500#H8057|שִׂמְחָה גְדוֹלָה]] (*śimḥāh gədôlāh*) - "very great gladness"

**Context**: After returning from exile, Ezra reads the law publicly, and the people discover the command to observe the Feast of Tabernacles. For the first time since Joshua's era, Israel properly observes the feast, dwelling in booths made from olive, myrtle, and palm branches. The observance is marked by exceptional joy.

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 23#Leviticus 23 . 33|Leviticus 23:33-43]] (original command), [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 16#Deuteronomy 16 . 13|Deuteronomy 16:13-15]] (feast regulations)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 9#Nehemiah 9 . 1|Nehemiah 9:1-38]] (corporate confession follows), [[Readable Bible/15 - Ezra/Ezra 3#Ezra 3 . 4|Ezra 3:4]] (earlier observance at altar dedication)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 2|John 7:2-39]] (Jesus at the feast), [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]] (light of the world declaration)

**Christological Connection**: The Feast of Tabernacles observance in Nehemiah 8 provides crucial background for understanding Jesus' participation in the feast ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 2|John 7:2-39]]). The water-pouring ceremony that developed from this post-exilic period explains Jesus' dramatic declaration: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water'" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 7#John 7 . 37|John 7:37-38]]). Jesus proclaimed this "on the last day of the feast, the great day"—when the water-pouring reached its climax. Similarly, the torch-lighting ceremony illuminating Jerusalem provides context for Jesus declaring, "I am the light of the world" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 8#John 8 . 12|John 8:12]]). What the feast symbolized through water and light, Christ provides in reality—living water through the Spirit and divine illumination. The "very great gladness" of Nehemiah's feast prefigures the joy believers experience in Christ, who is the true temple ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 19|John 2:19-21]]) and the reality toward which the feast's temporary booths pointed.
