### [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 3|Numbers 16:3]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H6951|H6951]] קָהָל (qahal) - "assembly, congregation" (the gathered people of God)
- [[Lexicon/H6918|H6918]] קָדוֹשׁ (qadosh) - "holy, set apart, sacred"
- [[Lexicon/H5375|H5375]] נָשָׂא (nasa) - "to lift up, exalt oneself"
- [[Lexicon/H3068|H3068]] יְהוָה (YHWH) - "the LORD" (covenant name)
- [[Lexicon/H8432|H8432]] תָּוֶךְ (tavek) - "midst, among" (God's presence among His people)

**Context**: Korah, a Levite (descendant of Kohath), along with Dathan, Abiram, and 250 leaders of the congregation, rebels against Moses and Aaron's authority. Their challenge invokes Exodus 19:6: "All the congregation is holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them." Korah correctly understood Israel's corporate holiness but drew a false conclusion—that this universal priestly identity negated the distinct mediatorial role of the Aaronic priesthood. The rebellion represents a premature grasping at what would only be realized through Christ's new covenant mediation.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]] - The foundational text Korah misapplies; Israel is indeed a "kingdom of priests and holy nation," but this corporate identity operates within God's ordained structures
- [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 3#Numbers 3 . 10|Numbers 3:10]] - Aaron and his sons alone are appointed to serve as priests; outsiders who approach shall be put to death
- [[Readable Bible/05 - Deuteronomy/Deuteronomy 10#Deuteronomy 10 . 8|Deuteronomy 10:8]] - The tribe of Levi is separated to carry the ark, stand before the LORD, and minister in His name

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 19#Exodus 19 . 6|Exodus 19:6]] (the text Korah quotes/misapplies)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 16#Numbers 16 . 31|Numbers 16:31-35]] (divine judgment vindicates Mosaic/Aaronic authority)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/65 - Jude/Jude 1#Jude 1 . 11|Jude 1:11]] ("the rebellion of Korah" as paradigm of false teachers who reject God-ordained authority)

**Type Classification**: **Crisis/Negative Example** within the Direct Type trajectory - shows the misapplication of Exodus 19:6 that the new covenant resolves through Christ's perfect mediation.

**Christological Connection**: Korah's rebellion reveals the tension in the old covenant: Israel was indeed a "kingdom of priests," yet required Aaronic mediation. This tension is resolved in Christ, who is both the unique High Priest ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 4#Hebrews 4 . 14|Hebrews 4:14-16]]) and the one who constitutes His people as priests with direct access to God ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 2#1 Peter 2 . 5|1 Peter 2:5]], 9). What Korah grasped prematurely and illegitimately—universal priestly access—becomes reality through Christ's blood. The new covenant fulfills what the old covenant promised: "They shall all know me, from the least to the greatest" ([[Readable Bible/24 - Jeremiah/Jeremiah 31#Jeremiah 31 . 34|Jeremiah 31:34]]). Christ did not abolish the need for priesthood but fulfilled it perfectly, enabling believers to function as priests offering spiritual sacrifices. Korah's error was not in desiring priestly access but in rejecting the divinely appointed means (Aaronic priesthood pointing to Christ) by which that access would be accomplished.
