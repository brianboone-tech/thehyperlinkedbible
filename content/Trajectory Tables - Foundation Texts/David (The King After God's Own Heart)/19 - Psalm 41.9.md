### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 41#Psalm 41 . 9|Psalm 41:9]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7965|H7965]] שָׁלוֹם (*šālôm*) - peace, friendship
- [[Lexicon/H982|H982]] בָּטַח (*bāṭaḥ*) - to trust
- [[Lexicon/H3899|H3899]] לֶחֶם (*leḥem*) - bread
- [[Lexicon/H6119|H6119]] עָקֵב (*ʿāqēḇ*) - heel

**Context**: "Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me." David laments betrayal by a trusted companion—possibly Ahithophel during Absalom's rebellion (2 Samuel 15:12, 31).

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 15#2 Samuel 15 . 12|2 Samuel 15:12]], 31 - Ahithophel's betrayal
- [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 16#2 Samuel 16 . 23|2 Samuel 16:23]] - Ahithophel's counsel "like the oracle of God"
- [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 17#2 Samuel 17 . 23|2 Samuel 17:23]] - Ahithophel's suicide

**Connections**:
- **TO**: [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 15#2 Samuel 15 . 12|2 Samuel 15:12]] - Ahithophel joins Absalom
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 55#Psalm 55 . 12|Psalm 55:12-14]] - Similar betrayal lament
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 13#John 13 . 18|John 13:18]] - Jesus applies to Judas
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 16|Acts 1:16]] - Scripture fulfilled in Judas

**Type Classification**: Providential Type - Forward-looking (David's betrayal prefigures Christ's)

**Christological Connection**: Jesus explicitly applies this verse to Judas ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 13#John 13 . 18|John 13:18]]): "He who ate my bread has lifted his heel against me." The connection is profound: Ahithophel was David's trusted counselor who betrayed him to Absalom and later hanged himself ([[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 17#2 Samuel 17 . 23|2 Samuel 17:23]]); Judas was Jesus' trusted disciple who betrayed Him and later hanged himself ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 5|Matthew 27:5]]). The phrase "ate my bread" gains additional significance from John's context—the Last Supper, where Jesus identifies His betrayer by giving him bread ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 13#John 13 . 26|John 13:26-27]]). What happened to David happened to David's greater Son, but Christ's betrayal led not to defeat but to redemptive victory.
