### [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 69#Psalm 69 . 1|Psalm 69:1-36]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H7068|H7068]] קִנְאָה (*qinʾâ*) - zeal, jealousy
- [[Lexicon/H2781|H2781]] חֶרְפָּה (*ḥerpâ*) - reproach, shame
- [[Lexicon/H2558|H2558]] חֹמֶץ (*ḥōmeṣ*) - vinegar
- [[Lexicon/H8130|H8130]] שָׂנֵא (*śānēʾ*) - to hate

**Context**: Psalm 69 is a Davidic lament of the righteous sufferer, one of the most frequently quoted psalms in the NT. David suffers unjustly, is hated without cause, and endures reproach for God's sake. The psalm moves from desperate plea (vv. 1-21) through imprecation (vv. 22-28) to praise and confidence (vv. 29-36).

**Key Verses for NT Application**:
- v. 4 - "Those who hate me without a cause" (John 15:25)
- v. 9a - "Zeal for your house has consumed me" (John 2:17)
- v. 9b - "The reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me" (Romans 15:3)
- v. 21 - "For my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink" (John 19:28-29)
- vv. 22-23 - Imprecation applied to unbelieving Israel (Romans 11:9-10)
- v. 25 - "Let their camp be desolation" (Acts 1:20, applied to Judas)

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- 1 Samuel 24 - David's innocent suffering under Saul
- Psalm 22 - Parallel righteous sufferer psalm
- Psalm 109 - Imprecatory psalm

**Connections**:
- **TO**: 1 Samuel 24 - David's suffering/innocence pattern
- **FROM OT**: Psalm 22 - Complementary passion psalm
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 2#John 2 . 17|John 2:17]] - Zeal for God's house
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 15#John 15 . 25|John 15:25]] - Hated without cause
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 19#John 19 . 28|John 19:28-29]] - Vinegar given
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 9|Romans 11:9-10]] - Imprecation fulfilled
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 15#Romans 15 . 3|Romans 15:3]] - Christ bears reproach
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 20|Acts 1:20]] - Applied to Judas

**Type Classification**: Providential Type - Forward-looking (Davidic suffering points to Christ)

**Christological Connection**: Psalm 69 is arguably the second most important passion psalm after Psalm 22. Christ speaks through David: His zeal for God's house consumed Him (temple cleansing), He bore the reproaches meant for the Father, He was hated without cause, He thirsted and received vinegar. The imprecatory section (vv. 22-28) is shocking—Paul applies it to unbelieving Israel ([[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 11#Romans 11 . 9|Romans 11:9-10]]), and Peter applies v. 25 to Judas ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 1#Acts 1 . 20|Acts 1:20]]). The righteous sufferer's curse falls on those who reject the Messiah. Yet the psalm ends in praise (vv. 30-36), anticipating Christ's vindication and the salvation of Zion. David's experience was real but prophetic; Christ's was the ultimate fulfillment.
