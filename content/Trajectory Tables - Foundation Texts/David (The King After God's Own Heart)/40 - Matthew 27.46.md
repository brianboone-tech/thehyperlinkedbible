**Context**: At the climax of crucifixion, Jesus cries out: "Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?" (My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?)—quoting Psalm 22:1. This cry reveals the depth of Christ's suffering as sin-bearer, experiencing divine abandonment while maintaining covenant relationship ("My God"). The quotation from David's psalm demonstrates Jesus consciously fulfilling messianic prophecy even in agony, connecting David's suffering to Christ's redemptive work. This moment marks the precise point where Christ bore God's wrath for sin, accomplishing substitutionary atonement.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]] records Christ's climactic cry: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"—quoting [[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 1|Psalm 22:1]]. This reveals the deepest mystery of atonement: the sinless Son of God experiencing divine abandonment as sin-bearer. Second Corinthians 5:21 explains: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin." Christ endured the separation from God that sin produces—the essence of hell—so believers could experience eternal communion with God. [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 3#Galatians 3 . 13|Galatians 3:13]] declares: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us." [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 4|Isaiah 53:4-5]] prophesied: "Surely he has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows... he was pierced for our transgressions... upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace." At the cross, divine wrath against sin fell on Christ instead of sinners. [[Readable Bible/35 - Habakkuk/Habakkuk 1#Habakkuk 1 . 13|Habakkuk 1:13]] states God's eyes are "too pure to look on evil"—when Christ bore sin, the Father turned away. The double address "My God, my God" maintains covenant relationship even while experiencing abandonment—Jesus never ceases trusting despite unfathomable agony. The trajectory completes: David cried feeling forsaken ([[Readable Bible/19 - Psalms/Psalm 22#Psalm 22 . 1|Psalm 22:1]]) → Christ actually was forsaken ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 46|Matthew 27:46]]) → believers will never be forsaken ([[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 13#Hebrews 13 . 5|Hebrews 13:5]]). [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 8#Romans 8 . 1|Romans 8:1]] announces: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"—because condemnation fell on Christ. The cry from cross reveals David's greater Son enduring what David only foreshadowed, accomplishing redemption through substitutionary suffering, transforming abandonment into atonement.
