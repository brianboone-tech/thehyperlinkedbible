**Context**: God rejects Saul and sends Samuel to anoint David: "Fill your horn with oil, and go. I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided for myself a king among his sons... The LORD sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart... Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers. And the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward." David, youngest son and shepherd, is chosen not by appearance but by heart condition. This shepherd-king prefigures Christ, the good shepherd anointed with the Spirit without measure.

**Christological Connection**: First Samuel 16:1-13 records David's anointing as shepherd-king chosen by God's sovereign election, prefiguring Christ's selection and Spirit-anointing. God rejected Saul and chose David—"I have provided for myself a king among his sons." Samuel examined Jesse's impressive older sons, but God chose the youngest shepherd: "man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart." This divine election based on heart condition, not external qualifications, prefigures Christ's selection before creation ([[Readable Bible/60 - 1 Peter/1 Peter 1#1 Peter 1 . 20|1 Peter 1:20]]). David's humble shepherd origins anticipate Christ's incarnation—born in Bethlehem, laid in manger, of lowly estate. The anointing with oil symbolizes Spirit-empowerment: "the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward." Christ's baptism fulfills this—the Spirit descended and remained on Him ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 32|John 1:32-33]]), anointing "without measure" ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 34|John 3:34]]). [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 10#Acts 10 . 38|Acts 10:38]] declares: "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power." Where David was anointed for Israel's kingship, Christ was anointed for universal reign. The shepherd imagery is crucial—David defended sheep from lions and bears (17:34-35), prefiguring Christ the good shepherd who lays down His life for the sheep ([[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 10#John 10 . 11|John 10:11]]). [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1#Matthew 1 . 1|Matthew 1:1]] identifies Jesus as "son of David," claiming Davidic descent and messianic kingship. [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 1#Luke 1 . 32|Luke 1:32-33]] promises: "the Lord God will give to him the throne of his father David, and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever." The trajectory shows: David chosen as shepherd-king (type, temporary, imperfect) → Christ chosen as good shepherd-king (antitype, eternal, perfect). Where David was youngest and overlooked, Christ came in humility, "despised and rejected" ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 3|Isaiah 53:3]]). Where David's anointing brought Spirit's rushing power, Christ's anointing brought Spirit's infinite fullness. Where David's kingdom eventually crumbled, Christ's kingdom endures forever. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 1#Romans 1 . 3|Romans 1:3]] identifies Jesus as "descended from David according to the flesh," while [[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 13#Acts 13 . 23|Acts 13:23]] declares: "Of this man's offspring God has brought to Israel a Savior, Jesus, as he promised." The anointing establishes Christ as the Messiah ("Anointed One"), fulfilling David's typological role perfectly—shepherd protecting His flock, king ruling in righteousness, Spirit-empowered for redemptive mission, chosen by God for eternal reign over all nations.
