**Context**: Isaiah 39:6 records the LORD's judgment through Isaiah to Hezekiah: "Behold, the days are coming when all that is in your house, and that which your fathers have stored up till this day, shall be carried to Babylon. Nothing shall be left, says the LORD." This prophecy follows Hezekiah's foolish display of treasures to Babylonian envoys (vv. 1-4). The phrase "days are coming" (*yāmîm bā'îm*) introduces prophetic future judgment, connecting to "latter days" terminology—though not using *'aḥărît* explicitly, the concept is identical: future eschatological event when God's word will be fulfilled. The Babylonian exile (586 BC) fulfilled this literally, but the pattern of prophetic warning and historical fulfillment establishes confidence in all eschatological prophecies.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 39#Isaiah 39 . 6|Isaiah 39:6]]'s prophecy—"Behold, the days are coming when all that is in your house... shall be carried to Babylon. Nothing shall be left"—establishes the prophetic "days are coming" formula that threads through Scripture's eschatological vision. Hezekiah's pride in displaying treasures to Babylon leads to divine judgment: those same treasures will be plundered. The prophecy fulfilled literally in 586 BC when Nebuchadnezzar destroyed Jerusalem and exiled Judah. Second Kings 24:13 records: "he carried off all the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the king's house." This pattern—prophetic warning, historical fulfillment—validates prophetic certainty for all "latter days" prophecies. The "days are coming" formula recurs throughout prophetic literature: Jeremiah uses it repeatedly (7:32; 9:25; 16:14; 19:6; 23:5, 7; 30:3; 31:27, 31, 38; 48:12; 49:2; 51:47, 52); Amos announces: "Behold, the days are coming... when I will send a famine on the land" (8:11). Each fulfillment strengthens confidence in unfulfilled prophecies. Christ applies this pattern to Jerusalem's second destruction: [[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 2|Matthew 24:2]] prophesies "there will not be left here one stone upon another that will not be thrown down"—echoing Isaiah's "nothing shall be left." [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 21#Luke 21 . 24|Luke 21:24]] extends the timeline: "Jerusalem will be trampled underfoot by the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled"—ongoing exile with eschatological endpoint. The church age exists in this "trampling" period, between inauguration (Pentecost) and consummation (Christ's return). [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 18#Revelation 18 . 21|Revelation 18:21]] pronounces Babylon's ultimate judgment: "Babylon the great... will be thrown down with violence and will be found no more"—reversing the exile, judging the oppressor. The trajectory shows: Isaiah prophesies Babylonian exile ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 39#Isaiah 39 . 6|Isaiah 39:6]]) → fulfilled historically (586 BC) → pattern validates prophetic certainty → Christ prophesies Jerusalem's destruction ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 24#Matthew 24 . 2|Matthew 24:2]]) → fulfilled AD 70 → ongoing "trampling" until Gentile fullness ([[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 21#Luke 21 . 24|Luke 21:24]]) → ultimate Babylon judged ([[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 18#Revelation 18 . 21|Revelation 18:21]]). Each "days are coming" prophecy that finds historical fulfillment confirms confidence that remaining unfulfilled prophecies will likewise certainly occur. The "latter days" understanding in stage 5 involves recognizing this pattern: God's prophetic word always comes to pass, exactly as spoken, in God's appointed time. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 39#Isaiah 39 . 6|Isaiah 39:6]]'s precision—naming Babylon 115 years before the event—demonstrates divine foreknowledge and guarantees eschatological promises will likewise be fulfilled when their "days" come.
