### [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 4#Micah 4 . 1|Micah 4:1-5]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H0319-0320#H0319|אַחֲרִית]] (*'aḥărît*) - "end, latter part, future"
- [[Lexicon/H3117-3118#H3117|הַיָּמִים]] (*hayyāmîm*) - "the days" (definite plural)
- [[Lexicon/H3559-3560#H3559|נָכוֹן יִהְיֶה]] (*nākôn yihyeh*) - "it shall be established" (Niphal participle + imperfect)
- [[Lexicon/H2022-2023#H2022|הַר בֵּית]] (*har bêt*) - "mountain of the house"
- [[Lexicon/H1980-1981#H1980|נֵלְכָה]] (*nēlĕkâ*) - "let us go" (Qal cohortative)
- [[Lexicon/H3384-3385#H3384|יוֹרֵנוּ]] (*yôrēnû*) - "he will teach us" (Hiphil imperfect with suffix)
- [[Lexicon/H8199-8200#H8199|שָׁפַט]] (*šāp̄aṭ*) - "he will judge" (Qal perfect)
- [[Lexicon/H3198-3199#H3198|הוֹכִיחַ]] (*hôkîaḥ*) - "he will decide, arbitrate" (Hiphil perfect)
- [[Lexicon/H7965-7966#H7965|שָׁלוֹם]] (*šālôm*) - "peace, welfare, wholeness"

**Context**: Micah 4:1-5 parallels Isaiah 2:2-4 almost verbatim: "It shall come to pass in the latter days that the mountain of the house of the LORD shall be established... and peoples shall flow to it... He shall judge between many peoples... they shall beat their swords into plowshares... Each shall sit under his vine and under his fig tree, and no one shall make them afraid." Micah adds the beautiful detail (v. 4)—personal peace symbolized by sitting under vine and fig tree without fear. The vision remains: Messianic kingdom, Zion exalted, nations streaming to worship, universal peace established in the "latter days."

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 4#Micah 4 . 1|Micah 4:1-5]]'s vision—"in the latter days the mountain of the house of the LORD shall be established... they shall beat their swords into plowshares... each shall sit under his vine and under his fig tree"—parallels [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 2#Isaiah 2 . 2|Isaiah 2:2-4]], showing consistent prophetic expectation of Messianic kingdom. The "latter days" (*'aḥărît hayyāmîm*) prophesy Zion exalted, nations streaming to worship, universal peace established. Christ inaugurates this through His first coming: [[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-17]] declares He "is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility... he came and preached peace to you who were far off and peace to those who were near." Jews and Gentiles reconciled—nations beginning to flow together. The church becomes the community where nations gather around Christ. The vine-and-fig-tree imagery finds personal application in [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 1#John 1 . 48|John 1:48-49]]: Jesus tells Nathanael "I saw you under the fig tree"—Nathanael responds: "you are the King of Israel!" Possibly evoking Messianic associations. Jesus' cursing the barren fig tree ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 21#Matthew 21 . 18|Matthew 21:18-22]]) symbolizes Israel's failure to produce fruit, anticipating judgment. The eschatological peace—"none shall make them afraid"—awaits Christ's return. [[Readable Bible/66 - Revelation/Revelation 20#Revelation 20 . 1|Revelation 20:1-6]] describes millennial reign: "they came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years... they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him." [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 11#Isaiah 11 . 6|Isaiah 11:6-9]] expands the vision: "wolf shall dwell with the lamb... earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea"—comprehensive transformation. [[Readable Bible/38 - Zechariah/Zechariah 3#Zechariah 3 . 10|Zechariah 3:10]] promises: "In that day... every one of you will invite his neighbor to come under his vine and under his fig tree"—corporate fellowship in security. The trajectory shows: Micah prophesies "latter days" peace ([[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 4#Micah 4 . 1|Micah 4:1-5]]) → Christ inaugurates spiritual peace, reconciling Jew and Gentile ([[Readable Bible/49 - Ephesians/Ephesians 2#Ephesians 2 . 14|Ephesians 2:14-17]]) → church age sees progressive fulfillment (nations entering kingdom) → Christ's return establishes millennial/eternal physical peace (Revelation 20-22). The "latter days" began at Christ's first coming ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 2#Acts 2 . 17|Acts 2:17]]; [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 1#Hebrews 1 . 2|Hebrews 1:2]]) but await complete consummation—believers experience spiritual shalom now through Christ, anticipating universal physical shalom when He returns and establishes perfect kingdom where each sits under vine and fig tree without fear.
