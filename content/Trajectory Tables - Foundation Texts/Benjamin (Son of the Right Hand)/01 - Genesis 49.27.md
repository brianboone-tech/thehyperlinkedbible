### [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 49#Genesis 49 . 27|Genesis 49:27]]

**Hebrew Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/H1144|H1144]] בִּנְיָמִין (*binyamin*) - "Benjamin"
- [[Lexicon/H2061|H2061]] זְאֵב (*zeʾeb*) - "wolf"
- [[Lexicon/H2963|H2963]] טָרַף (*taraph*) - "to tear, rend, devour"
- [[Lexicon/H1242|H1242]] בֹּקֶר (*boqer*) - "morning"
- [[Lexicon/H6153|H6153]] עֶרֶב (*ʿereb*) - "evening"
- [[Lexicon/H7998|H7998]] שָׁלָל (*shalal*) - "spoil, plunder"
- [[Lexicon/H2505|H2505]] חָלַק (*chalaq*) - "to divide, distribute"

**Context**: Jacob gathers his twelve sons for final blessings/prophecies about their futures "in the days to come" (Gen 49:1). Benjamin receives the shortest blessing, characterized by wolf imagery. The wolf devours prey in the morning and divides spoil in the evening—suggesting continuous, relentless activity. This contrasts with the peaceful blessing of Deuteronomy 33:12, creating a dual portrait of the tribe.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- The warrior imagery is fulfilled in [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 5#Judges 5 . 14|Judges 5:14]] (Benjamin among the peoples), [[Readable Bible/07 - Judges/Judges 20#Judges 20 . 16|Judges 20:16]] (700 left-handed slingers who could sling stones at a hair and not miss), and [[Readable Bible/13 - 1 Chronicles/1 Chronicles 12#1 Chronicles 12 . 2|1 Chronicles 12:2]] (ambidextrous archers and slingers).
- King Saul, Israel's first king, was a Benjamite (1 Sam 9:1-2), embodying the tribe's fierce nature.
- The near-extermination of Benjamin in Judges 19-21 shows how the wolf-tribe's violence could become self-destructive.

**Connections**:
- **TO OT**: Genesis 35:16-18 (Benjamin's birth), Judges 5:14 (Benjamin in battle)
- **FROM OT**: [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 9#1 Samuel 9 . 1|1 Samuel 9:1-2]] (Saul the Benjamite), [[Readable Bible/13 - 1 Chronicles/1 Chronicles 12#1 Chronicles 12 . 2|1 Chronicles 12:2]] (Benjamite warriors)
- **FROM NT**: [[Readable Bible/50 - Philippians/Philippians 3#Philippians 3 . 5|Philippians 3:5-6]] (Paul the zealous Benjamite persecutor)

**Type Classification**: Providential Type (tribal character sovereignly shaped); Forward-Looking (anticipates both Saul's kingdom and Paul's zealous ministry).

**Christological Connection**: The wolf imagery finds surprising fulfillment in the apostle Paul. (1) **Morning Devouring**: Saul of Tarsus "ravaged the church" ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 8#Acts 8 . 3|Acts 8:3]])—literally acting as the devouring wolf, breathing threats and murder ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 9#Acts 9 . 1|Acts 9:1]]). His zeal was misdirected but genuine. (2) **Evening Distribution**: After his Damascus Road encounter, Paul spent his life "dividing the spoil"—distributing the riches of Christ to Jew and Gentile alike. The same aggressive energy that once destroyed now built. (3) **Tribal Identification**: Paul twice emphasizes his Benjamite heritage (Phil 3:5; Rom 11:1), suggesting the blessing was not merely tribal but prophetic of his own ministry. (4) **Wolf Transformed**: Jesus warned of wolves in sheep's clothing (Matt 7:15), but here a literal wolf is transformed into a shepherd—an apostle who would later warn against wolves entering the flock ([[Readable Bible/44 - Acts/Acts 20#Acts 20 . 29|Acts 20:29]]). The predator becomes protector.
