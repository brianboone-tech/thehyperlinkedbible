### [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 15#Luke 15 . 5|Luke 15:5]]

**Greek Key Terms**:
- [[Lexicon/G4263|G4263]] πρόβατον (*probaton*) - sheep
- [[Lexicon/G5606|G5606]] ὦμος (*ōmos*) - shoulder
- [[Lexicon/G2147|G2147]] εὑρίσκω (*heuriskō*) - to find
- [[Lexicon/G5463|G5463]] χαίρω (*chairō*) - to rejoice

**Context**: "And when he has found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing." Jesus tells the parable of the lost sheep, depicting the shepherd who leaves ninety-nine to find the one lost sheep and carries it home on his shoulders with great joy.

**OT-to-OT Development**:
- [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 9|Exodus 28:9-12]] - High priest bears Israel's names on his shoulders
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 40#Isaiah 40 . 11|Isaiah 40:11]] - God carries lambs in His arms
- [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 46#Isaiah 46 . 3|Isaiah 46:3-4]] - "I have made you, I will carry you"
- [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 34#Ezekiel 34 . 11|Ezekiel 34:11-16]] - God Himself will seek and rescue His sheep

**Type Classification**: Providential Type - Backward-looking (connection recognized through Christ's parable)

**Christological Connection**: Mather connects [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 15#Luke 15 . 5|Luke 15:5]] to the high priest's shoulder stones: "He is said to do with the lost Sheep... 'I will carry you, I have made you, and I will bear' ([[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 46#Isaiah 46 . 3|Isaiah 46:3-4]])." The high priest bore the names of the twelve tribes on his shoulders, representing Israel before God in strength. Christ, the Good Shepherd, bears His lost sheep on His shoulders—not merely their names but their entire persons. The image combines the priestly (bearing before God) with the pastoral (seeking the lost). The shoulder signifies strength (cf. [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 9#Isaiah 9 . 6|Isaiah 9:6]], "government shall be upon his shoulders"), and Christ's strength is displayed not in abandoning the one for the ninety-nine but in leaving the secure to rescue the lost. Where Aaron bore Israel's names passively, Christ actively seeks, finds, and carries His sheep home rejoicing.
