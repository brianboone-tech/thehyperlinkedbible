### [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]

**Context**: Hebrews 7:26 declares: "For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens." This verse perfectly answers Exodus 28:36-38's golden plate inscribed "Holy to the LORD." Where Aaron wore external holiness, Christ possesses intrinsic holiness. The fivefold description—holy, innocent, unstained, separated, exalted—shows Christ's complete qualification for high priestly ministry. His inherent holiness enables believers' acceptance before God, fulfilling what the golden plate symbolized.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 7#Hebrews 7 . 26|Hebrews 7:26]]'s declaration—"For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens"—fulfills [[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 36|Exodus 28:36-38]]'s golden plate inscribed "Holy to the LORD." Where Aaron wore external holiness symbolically, Christ possesses intrinsic holiness essentially. The fivefold description comprehensively portrays Christ's qualification: (1) "holy" (*hosios*)—devoted to God; (2) "innocent" (*akakos*)—without evil; (3) "unstained" (*amiantos*)—undefiled; (4) "separated from sinners"—ontologically distinct; (5) "exalted above the heavens"—transcendent. First Peter 2:22 applies [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 53#Isaiah 53 . 9|Isaiah 53:9]] to Christ: "He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth"—absolute sinlessness. First [[Readable Bible/43 - John/John 3#John 3 . 5|John 3:5]] declares: "in him there is no sin"—complete purity. Where Aaron needed purification rituals and atoning sacrifices for himself ([[Readable Bible/03 - Leviticus/Leviticus 16#Leviticus 16 . 6|Leviticus 16:6]], 11), Christ needs neither. Second Corinthians 5:21 states: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin"—Christ's sinlessness enabled substitutionary atonement. The golden plate's purpose—"that Aaron may bear any guilt from the holy things... that they may be accepted before the LORD" ([[Readable Bible/02 - Exodus/Exodus 28#Exodus 28 . 38|Exodus 28:38]])—finds ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Aaron's worn holiness rendered imperfect offerings acceptable; Christ's inherent holiness renders sinful people acceptable. [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 10|Hebrews 10:10]] declares: "we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all"—His holiness becomes ours. [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 5#Romans 5 . 19|Romans 5:19]] states: "by the one man's obedience the many will be made righteous"—Christ's perfect righteousness imputed. The phrase "exalted above the heavens" (*hypsēloteros tōn ouranōn*) shows Christ's current position—[[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 24|Hebrews 9:24]] states Christ "entered... into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf." The trajectory shows: golden plate declares external holiness (shadow) → Christ possesses intrinsic holiness (substance) → His holiness enables our acceptance (substitution) → believers made holy in Him (participation) → eternal holiness in new creation (consummation). Aaron's worn holiness gives way to Christ's inherent holiness, which becomes believers' permanent identity.
