**Context**: Based on Christ's once-for-all sacrifice and superior priesthood, believers now possess confident access to God's presence. This passage combines multiple images: entering the holy places through Christ's blood, the new and living way through the veil (His flesh), hearts sprinkled clean, and bodies washed with pure water. These images fulfill the red heifer's purpose—providing purification enabling access to God's presence.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 10#Hebrews 10 . 19|Hebrews 10:19-22]] presents the ultimate fulfillment of the red heifer's purpose—providing purification enabling access to God's presence. Where the purification water cleansed from corpse-defilement, allowing Israelites to rejoin the community and approach worship, Christ's blood provides permanent purification enabling direct access to God's immediate presence. The phrase "confidence to enter the holy places" would have shocked ancient readers—only the high priest entered the Most Holy Place, once yearly, trembling with fear. Yet Christ's sacrifice grants all believers bold, continuous access. The "new and living way" contrasts the old path—marked by death (animal sacrifices), temporary (repeated yearly), limited (high priest only). Christ's way is new (never before available), living (through the resurrected Christ), permanent (never needing repetition), and universal (for all believers). The identification "through the veil, that is, his flesh" reveals that Christ's crucified body is both the sacrifice providing purification and the path granting access. The torn veil at His death ([[Readable Bible/40 - Matthew/Matthew 27#Matthew 27 . 51|Matthew 27:51]]) symbolized the barrier's removal—no longer does curtain separate believers from God's presence. The dual cleansing—"hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water"—fulfills the red heifer's dual provision (blood and water, [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 9|Numbers 19:9]]). Hearts sprinkled indicates internal cleansing from conscience guilt; bodies washed likely references baptism's external sign. Together they represent complete purification—internal and external, spiritual and ceremonial. What the red heifer accomplished temporarily and ceremonially, Christ accomplishes eternally and spiritually—permanent purification enabling perpetual, confident access to God's throne.
