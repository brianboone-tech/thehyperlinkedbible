**Context**: Hebrews 9:14 is the climax of the red heifer comparison. If the ashes of a heifer could sanctify ceremonially, how much more can Christ's blood, offered through the eternal Spirit, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God. This verse reveals the red heifer's ultimate purpose—prefiguring Christ's cleansing from spiritual death (dead works) to enable living service.

**Christological Connection**: [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 9#Hebrews 9 . 14|Hebrews 9:14]]'s declaration that Christ's blood will "purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" reveals the red heifer typology's ultimate purpose. The ashes cleansed from physical death's defilement; Christ cleanses from spiritual death's corruption. The phrase "dead works" is comprehensive—encompassing both sinful rebellion and religious self-effort, both spiritually lifeless, both unable to please God or provide acceptance. Christ's blood purifies conscience from both, removing guilt's paralyzing power and freeing believers for joyful, faith-filled service. The contrast between dead works and serving the living God is absolute—death vs. life, slavery vs. freedom, futility vs. fruitfulness. Where the red heifer enabled ceremonial approach to worship, Christ enables spiritual transformation producing genuine worship. The phrase "through the eternal Spirit" reveals Christ's sacrifice's unique character—not merely human martyrdom but the God-man offering Himself through divine power, rendering His sacrifice eternally efficacious. The adjective "without blemish" connects to the red heifer's requirement ([[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 19#Numbers 19 . 2|Numbers 19:2]])—Christ's sinless perfection qualified Him as the ultimate sacrifice. What the red heifer foreshadowed, Christ fulfilled—providing purification that doesn't merely restore ceremonial fitness but transforms heart, conscience, and service.
