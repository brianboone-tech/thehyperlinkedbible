while he yet .  etc. Heb. being yet shut up , Sometimes , in the East , when a successful prince endeavoured to extirpate the preceding royal family , some of them escaped the slaughter , and secured themselves in an impregnable fortress , or in a place of great secrecy; while others have been known to seek an asylum in a foreign county , from when they have occasioned , from time to time , great anxiety and great difficulties to the usurper of the crown. The expression shut up , so often applied to the extermination of eastern royal families . Deuteronomy 32 . 32. [[Readable Bible/11 - 1 Kings/1 Kings 14#1 Kings 14 . 10|1 Kings 14 . 10]] , 1 Kings 21 . 21. [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 9#2 Kings 9 . 8|2 Kings 9 . 8]] , [[Readable Bible/12 - 2 Kings/2 Kings 14#2 Kings 14 . 26|2 Kings 14 . 26]] , strictly speaking , refers to the two first of these cases; but the term may be used in a more extensive sense , for those who , by retiring into deserts , or foreign countries , preserve themselves from being slain by the men who usurp the dominions of their ancestors. Thus the term is here applied to David , though he did not shut himself up , strictly speaking , in Ziklag. It is described as a town in the country , and was probably an unwalled town; and it is certain that he did not confine himself to it , but , on the contrary , was continually making excursions from thence.

#### Verse 38 

with a perfect heart .  The meaning of this expression may be inferred from that of a double heart in 1 Chronicles 12 . 33. brought .  The Septuagint reads וצוסןם בץפיןע , "brought (to) them" which is probably correct; the Hebrew lahem , "to them" might be easily mistaken for lechem , "bread." The passage will then read , "bought them on asses , on camels , and on mules , and on oxen , meat , meal , cakes of figs ," etc. , which renders the introduction of and unnecessary. From the mention of oil , figs , and raisins , Mr. Harmer thinks that this assembly was held in autumn.
