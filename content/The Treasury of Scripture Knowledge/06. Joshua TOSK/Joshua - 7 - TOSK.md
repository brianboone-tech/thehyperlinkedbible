put dust .  Rending the clothes , beating the breast , tearing the hair , throwing dust upon the head , and falling prostrate , were usual signs of deep affliction and distress among the ancient Israelites. In illustration of this custom , see note on [[Readable Bible/09 - 1 Samuel/1 Samuel 4#1 Samuel 4 . 12|1 Samuel 4 . 12]] , when the messenger brought tidings to Eli of the discomfiture of the armies of Israel by the Philistines; again , in the case of Tamar , [[Readable Bible/10 - 2 Samuel/2 Samuel 13#2 Samuel 13 . 19|2 Samuel 13 . 19]] , and in [[Readable Bible/16 - Nehemiah/Nehemiah 9#Nehemiah 9 . 1|Nehemiah 9 . 1]] , when a whole nation , "assembled with fasting , and with sackcloth , and earth upon them." In the case of Mordecai , see note on Esther 4 . 1. See note on [[Readable Bible/18 - Job/Job 2#Job 2 . 12|Job 2 . 12]] , where his friends abased themselves to comfort him. See note on [[Readable Bible/26 - Ezekiel/Ezekiel 27#Ezekiel 27 . 30|Ezekiel 27 . 30]] , see note on [[Readable Bible/32 - Jonah/Jonah 3#Jonah 3 . 6|Jonah 3 . 6]] , and see note on [[Readable Bible/33 - Micah/Micah 1#Micah 1 . 10|Micah 1 . 10]]. In each of these instances it is worthy of remark , that putting dust on the head generally follows rending of the clothes , and was the usual mode of evincing poignant sorrow.
