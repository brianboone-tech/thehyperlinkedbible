## Scripture's Interconnections

Biblical authors didn't write in isolation. They **quoted, alluded to, and built upon** earlier Scripture—and they **structured their texts** to highlight key theological points. The Hyperlinked Bible makes both visible through inline links.

---

### The Connections Layer

From any verse in the Readable Bible, you can access three types of connections:

![[Resources/Inline-Links-Example.png]]

---

### <span style="color: #7c3aed; font-weight: bold;">IP</span> - Intertextuality Pairs

**What they show**: How biblical authors quoted, alluded to, and interpreted other Scripture.

**Two categories**:
- **OT → OT** (900+ connections) - How later OT writers developed earlier OT texts
- **NT → OT** (12,000+ connections) - How NT authors understood OT passages to point to Christ

**Example**: [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3.14 to Isaiah 65.25|Genesis 3:14 → Isaiah 65:25]]
- Genesis establishes the serpent's curse
- Isaiah 65:25 shows the new creation retains only this curse while removing others
- Reveals how prophets understood the protoevangelium

**Why it matters**: Shows Scripture interpreting Scripture—the best commentary on the Bible is the Bible itself.

---

### <span style="color: #7c3aed; font-weight: bold;">C</span> - Chiasms

**What they show**: Literary structures where ideas mirror each other (A-B-C-B'-A').

![[Resources/Chiasm-Example.png]]

**Why biblical authors used chiasms**:
- **Highlight the center** — The middle element is normally the main point
- **Create unity** — The structure shows the passage is a complete unit
- **Aid memory** — Mirror structures help oral cultures remember texts
- **Signal intent** — Deliberate structure proves deliberate meaning

**The Hyperlinked Bible contains 1,700+ chiasms**, with:
- Color-coded parallel elements (A/A' red, B/B' blue, C/C' teal, etc.)
- Links to Readable Bible for each verse in the structure
- Inline C links from every verse that appears in a chiasm

---

### <span style="color: #7c3aed; font-weight: bold;">TOSK</span> - Treasury of Scripture Knowledge

**What it shows**: R.A. Torrey's comprehensive 1897 cross-reference database with 500,000+ connections.

**How it's organized**: References grouped by keyword/phrase within each verse.

**Example from [[The Treasury of Scripture Knowledge/01. Genesis TOSK/Genesis - 3 - TOSK#Verse 15|Genesis 3:15]]**:
- **enmity**: [[Readable Bible/04 - Numbers/Numbers 21#Numbers 21 . 6|Numbers 21:6-7]], [[Readable Bible/42 - Luke/Luke 10#Luke 10 . 19|Luke 10:19]]
- **her seed**: [[Readable Bible/23 - Isaiah/Isaiah 7#Isaiah 7 . 14|Isaiah 7:14]], [[Readable Bible/48 - Galatians/Galatians 4#Galatians 4 . 4|Galatians 4:4]]
- **it shall bruise**: [[Readable Bible/45 - Romans/Romans 16#Romans 16 . 20|Romans 16:20]], [[Readable Bible/58 - Hebrews/Hebrews 2#Hebrews 2 . 14|Hebrews 2:14-15]]

**Why it matters**: Provides the most comprehensive cross-reference network available, showing thematic connections across all of Scripture.

---

### Why All Three Matter Together

| Type | Level | What It Reveals |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| **IP** | Micro | How authors **connected** their text to other Scripture |
| **C** | Macro | How authors **structured** their message |
| **TOSK** | Comprehensive | Every **thematic parallel** across Scripture |

**Together**, they reveal the author's intent—what they wanted readers to see—and how every text ultimately points to Christ.
