## Tracing Biblical Types: From Shadow to Substance

One of the most powerful ways to see Christ in the Old Testament is through **typology**—recognizing how God designed earlier persons, events, and institutions to foreshadow later, greater realities fulfilled in Christ.

### What Is a Biblical Type?

A **type** is a divinely intended historical correspondence where something in the Old Testament prefigures something greater in the New Testament.

| Category | OT Type (Shadow) | NT Antitype (Substance) |
|----------|------------------|-------------------------|
| **Person** | Adam | Christ, the Last Adam (Rom 5:14) |
| **Person** | Moses | Christ, the Greater Prophet (Deut 18:15) |
| **Event** | Passover | Christ our Passover (1 Cor 5:7) |
| **Event** | Exodus | Redemption from sin (Luke 9:31) |
| **Institution** | Temple | Christ's body; the Church (John 2:21) |
| **Institution** | Priesthood | Christ, our Great High Priest (Heb 4:14) |

**Key principle**: The antitype always **surpasses** the type. Christ is greater than Adam, greater than Moses, greater than the Temple.

### Why Trajectory Tables Matter

Trajectory Tables show that typology isn't random allegory—it's **progressive revelation**. God designed the shadows to prepare His people for the substance. When you trace a type from Genesis to Revelation, you see:

1. **God's unified plan** — One story from creation to new creation
2. **Christ at the center** — Every type finds fulfillment in Him
3. **Escalation** — The antitype always surpasses the type
4. **Application** — What ancient shadows mean for believers today
