## Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Trace a Theme Through Trajectory Tables

**Follow the "Seed Promise" from Genesis to Revelation**:

1. Start with [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 14|Genesis 3:14-15]]
2. Click the **TT⁴** link → [[Trajectory Tables/143 - Seed Promise (Redemption Through Offspring)|Seed Promise Trajectory]]
3. Read the **Trajectory Summary** to understand the theme
4. Browse the **OT to OT** connections to see how later OT developed the seed promise
5. Browse the **NT to OT** connections to see how NT identifies Christ as THE seed

**What you'll discover**: How a single promise in Genesis 3:15 becomes the thread tying all of Scripture to Christ's victory over Satan—from Eve's hope, through Abraham's seed, David's son, to Paul's declaration in Galatians 3:16 that "the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring... who is Christ."

### Exercise 2: Explore a Chiasm

**Understand Literary Structure**:

1. Open [[Chiasm/Chiasm - 01 - Genesis 3 . 1 - 24|Chiasm - Genesis 3:1-24]]
2. **Identify the center** (the most deeply indented level—H in this case)
3. **Read the center carefully**—this is the main point
4. **Compare A with A'**, **D with D'**, **E with E'**—see the parallels
5. **Ask**: What is Moses emphasizing by structuring it this way?

**What you'll discover**:
- The Fall narrative centers on God's sentence on the serpent and the promise of redemption
- D (fig leaves) and D' (animal skins) show human effort vs. divine provision—the first sacrifice
- E (hiding in fear) and E' (naming Eve "mother of all living") show Adam's movement from fear to faith

### Exercise 3: Compare Intertextuality Pairs

**See How Later Authors Interpreted Earlier Texts**:

1. Read [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3#Genesis 3 . 14|Genesis 3:14]] (serpent cursed to eat dust)
2. Click **IP¹** → [[Intertextuality Pairs/OT to OT/01 - Genesis/Genesis 3.14 to Isaiah 65.25|Genesis 3:14 to Isaiah 65:25]]
3. Read how Isaiah uses this text in his new creation vision
4. Notice: Isaiah retains only the serpent's curse while removing creation's other curses
5. **Ask**: What does this tell us about Isaiah's understanding of Genesis 3?

**What you'll discover**: Isaiah understood that God's new creation would reverse Adam's curse on humanity and the ground, but the serpent's curse remains—pointing to ultimate defeat of evil, not rehabilitation.

### Exercise 4: Navigate a Trajectory Table's Foundation Texts

**Go Deep with Hebrew/Greek Analysis**:

1. Open [[Trajectory Tables/005 - Adam (The First and Last Adam)|Adam Trajectory Table]]
2. Scroll to **Foundation Texts** section
3. Click [[Trajectory Tables - Foundation Texts/Adam (The First and Last Adam)/01 - Genesis 1.26-28|Genesis 1:26-28 Foundation Text]]

5. See how NT passages develop these concepts

**What you'll discover**: The Hebrew terms reveal layers of meaning—humanity as God's royal representative, vice-regent over creation, priest-king in God's temple-garden—all pointing to Christ as the true Image who fulfills Adam's calling.
