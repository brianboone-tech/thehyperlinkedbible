## How Promise Shapes Pattern: Genesis 3:15 as Foundation

### The Pattern-Shaping Promise

**Genesis 3:15**:
<span style="color: #1e90ff;">"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed. He will bruise your head, and you will bruise his heel."</span>

This promise **created expectations** that shaped how biblical authors perceived and narrated events:

**Expectation 1**: Those who rebel against Yahweh = seed of the serpent
**Expectation 2**: Those who embrace Yahweh = seed of the woman
**Expectation 3**: Ongoing conflict between these two seeds
**Expectation 4**: The woman's seed will inflict a **head wound** (fatal), but receive only a **heel wound** (non-fatal)

### How Moses Signals the Pattern

Moses shows that **Cain = seed of the serpent** through deliberate parallels between Genesis 3 and Genesis 4:

| **Genesis 3 (The Fall)** | **Genesis 4 (Cain Murders Abel)** |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 3:9 - "Where are you?" (אַיֶּכָּה) | 4:9 - "Where is Abel?" (אֵי) |
| 3:13 - "What have you done?" | 4:10 - "What have you done?" |
| 3:14 - "**Cursed are you**" (to serpent) | 4:11 - "**Cursed are you**" (to Cain) |
| 3:16 - Desire and ruling (woman/husband) | 4:7 - Desire and ruling (sin/Cain) |
| 3:17-19 - Ground cursed | 4:11-12 - Ground will not yield |

**The shocking connection**: Only the **serpent** was cursed in Genesis 3. When Cain hears "cursed are you" in 4:11, Moses identifies him with the serpent—**Cain is seed of the serpent**.
