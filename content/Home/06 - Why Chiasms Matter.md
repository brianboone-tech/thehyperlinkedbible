## The Key Question

How do we know biblical authors **intended** the patterns we see in Scripture? Are we reading meaning into the text, or discovering meaning the author put there?

**Chiasms answer this question.** They are deliberate literary structures that prove authorial intent.

---

## What Is a Chiasm?

A chiasm is a mirror pattern where ideas are presented and then repeated in reverse order:

![[Resources/Chiasm-Example.png]]

The structure itself communicates meaning. **The center is the emphasis.**

---

## A Real Example: Genesis 1:26-28

Open this chiasm from the site: [[Chiasm/Chiasm - 01 - Genesis 1 . 26 - 28|Genesis 1:26-28 Chiasm]]

Here's what you'll see:

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **A** | God said, "Let us make man in our image" |
| **B** | Let them have dominion over fish, birds, creatures |
| **A'** | God created man in his image; male and female |
| **B'** | Have dominion over fish, birds, every living thing |

**What's at the center?** The creation of humanity in God's image.

**What does the structure tell us?** Moses deliberately crafted this passage so that "image of God" and "dominion" are presented together, then repeated together. This is not accidental—it's a statement that human dominion flows from bearing God's image.

---

## Why This Matters

**1. Chiasms prove intentionality**

When you see the same elements in mirror positions, you know the author designed it that way. This isn't interpretation imposed on the text—it's structure embedded in the text.

**2. The center reveals the main point**

The famous Flood narrative (Genesis 6-9) is structured as a chiasm. What's at the center?

> "But **God remembered Noah**" — [[Readable Bible/01 - Genesis/Genesis 8#Genesis 8 . 1|Genesis 8:1]]

The entire flood story builds to this moment and then descends from it. The author wants you to know: the point isn't the flood—it's God's faithfulness to remember His people.

**3. Parallel sections illuminate each other**

When A and A' are placed in corresponding positions, the author invites you to compare them. Insights emerge from reading them together that you'd miss reading linearly.

## The Bottom Line

Chiasms are **macro-level indicators** of what biblical authors intended to communicate. They're not decoration—they're deliberate design that reveals meaning.

When you see a chiasm, you're seeing the author's hand at work, shaping the text to emphasize what matters most.
