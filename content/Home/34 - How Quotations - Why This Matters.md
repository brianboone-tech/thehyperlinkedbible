## Why This Matters for Reading Scripture

### 1. Scripture Interprets Scripture

Later biblical authors teach us **how to read earlier texts** by showing us the connections they intended.

**Example**: When Matthew quotes Hosea 11:1 ("Out of Egypt I called my son") and applies it to Jesus (Matt 2:15), he's not **misreading** Hosea. He's recognizing that:
- Hosea was describing Israel's exodus
- Jesus is **true Israel**—the one who succeeds where Israel failed
- The exodus pattern was always pointing toward Christ

### 2. Recognizing Authorial Intent

Micro-level indicators help us determine **what the author intended to communicate**:

✅ **Valid interpretation**: Recognizes connections the author signaled through quotations, key terms, sequences, and covenantal parallels
❌ **Invalid interpretation**: Imposes connections the author did not intend

### 3. Christ Is the Climax

All these patterns—exodus, seed, ark, covenant, temple, king, priest, sacrifice—**converge in Christ**:

- He is the **Seed** who crushes the serpent (Gen 3:15 → Rom 16:20)
- He accomplishes the **ultimate Exodus** (Luke 9:31; 1 Cor 5:7)
- He is the **True Israel** (Matt 2:15; Hosea 11:1)
- He is the **Second Adam** who succeeds (Rom 5:12-21; 1 Cor 15:45)
- He is the **Greater Moses** (Deut 18:15 → Acts 3:22)
