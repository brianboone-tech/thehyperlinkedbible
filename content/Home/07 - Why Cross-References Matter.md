## The Key Question

How do we know when the New Testament quotes the Old Testament, the connection is intentional? How do we know biblical authors meant to link passages together?

**Cross-references answer this question.** Biblical authors used specific techniques to signal connections between passages.

---

## Four Ways Authors Signal Connections

Biblical scholars call these **micro-level indicators**—textual clues that prove authors intended to connect passages.

### 1. Quotations

The clearest indicator: one passage directly quotes another.

**Example**: [[Intertextuality Pairs/NT to OT/40 - Matthew/Matthew 1.22-23 to Isaiah 7.14|Matthew 1:22-23 quoting Isaiah 7:14]]

> "All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had spoken through the prophet: 'The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel.'"

Matthew explicitly tells you he's connecting to Isaiah. The connection is undeniable.

### 2. Shared Key Words

Authors reuse rare or distinctive terms to create connections.

**Example**: The Hebrew word for "ark" (תֵּבָה) appears in only two places in the entire Old Testament:
- Noah's ark (Genesis 6-9)
- The basket Moses floated in (Exodus 2:3)

This is not coincidence. Moses chose this rare word to connect himself to Noah—both are covenant mediators delivered through an "ark."

### 3. Repeated Event Sequences

Authors structure stories to parallel earlier events.

**Example**: Abraham's journey to Egypt (Genesis 12) parallels Israel's exodus:

| Abraham | Israel |
|---------|--------|
| Goes to Egypt because of famine | Goes to Egypt because of famine |
| Wife taken/oppressed | People enslaved/oppressed |
| Plagues on Pharaoh | Plagues on Egypt |
| Leaves enriched | Leaves with Egyptian wealth |

The pattern proves Moses wanted readers to see Abraham's experience as a preview of the exodus.

### 4. Covenantal Significance

Events with similar roles in God's redemptive plan are connected.

**Example**: Noah and Moses are both:
- Covenant mediators
- Delivered through water
- Given a renewed creation commission

The similarities aren't random—they show God working the same pattern across history.

## Why This Matters

**1. Scripture interprets Scripture**

When you see these connections, you're watching biblical authors interpret earlier Scripture. They show us how to read the Bible.

**2. Patterns reveal God's design**

The same patterns appearing across different authors and centuries show God's unified plan unfolding through history.

**3. Christ is the fulfillment**

These connections trace trajectories that culminate in Jesus. The NT authors saw Christ as the answer to every OT pattern.

## The Bottom Line

Cross-references are **micro-level indicators** of authorial intent. When you see quotations, shared terms, parallel events, or covenantal connections, you're seeing how biblical authors deliberately linked their writings to earlier Scripture.

These connections aren't imposed by readers—they're embedded by authors. Following them leads you to see Christ in all the Scriptures.
